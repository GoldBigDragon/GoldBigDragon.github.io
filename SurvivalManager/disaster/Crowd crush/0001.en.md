# Why Crowd Crushes Kill

- Almost all crowd-crush deaths are compressive asphyxia. People do not die from being trampled; they die because their chest is squeezed and they cannot breathe.
- When someone falls, a gap opens and the crowd behind topples into it like dominoes, and the pressure spikes.
- That is why getting out before anyone falls is the only reliable response.

---

# Before You Go

- At stadiums, concert venues, and festivals, locate the exits, evacuation routes, and first-aid stations first.
- Agree on a reunion point in case your group gets separated.
- Networks fail in crowds - give children a note with their name and a guardian's phone number.
- Wear flat, closed shoes. Sandals make you fall and leave your feet exposed.

---

# Warning Signs - Leave Immediately

- The gaps close until you cannot move your arms freely.
- Your pace slows and your body moves without your deciding to.
- You feel waves of pressure rolling through from behind.
- Screams or shouting reach you from ahead.

> The moment you notice these, move diagonally toward the edge - toward open space, not toward a wall or a pillar.

---

# In a Dense Crowd

## 1. Do not fight the flow
- Bracing head-on against a crowd only drains your strength until you fall.
- Go with the movement, and each time the pressure eases for a second, work diagonally, a little at a time, toward the edge.
- No sudden stops, sudden turns, or moving against the flow.

## 2. Keep room to breathe
- Hug a bag to your chest, or bring both arms up in front of your chest like a boxer's guard. That space is what protects your lungs.
- Set your feet apart and lower your center of gravity.
- Do not raise your arms overhead or let them hang - once they are up or down, you cannot bring them back.

## 3. Never do this
- If you drop something, leave it. Bending down is how people stop getting back up.
- Do not turn back for a companion or an object.
- Do not run, do not push, do not pull.

## 4. If you fall
- Get into the fetal position: arms wrapped around your head, knees pulled toward your chest.
- Lie on your side so your chest is not compressed. Face down, the weight closes your lungs.
- The instant the pressure eases, push up off a nearby person's leg and rise facing the direction of flow.

## 5. With a child
- Lift the child or put them on your shoulders so they are above the level of the crush.
- A stroller becomes a hazard in dense crowds. Carry the child instead.

# The Most Dangerous Places

- Narrow alleys, slopes, stairways, and the area around doorways carry the highest risk.
- On a downhill slope in particular, one person falling starts a chain reaction.
- At a bottleneck, hold your pace and spacing, and call out loudly to the people pushing from behind.

---

# Emergencies in Crowded Venues

- Follow on-site instructions and public announcements first; do not act on your own judgment.
- Do not pass on unverified information - bad information causes a second wave of panic.
- Fire: raise the alarm, cover your nose and mouth with a wet cloth, and stay low. If your clothes catch fire, Stop, Drop, and Roll.
- Blackout: do not move - hold your position and stay calm. Moving through a dense crowd in the dark sharply raises crush risk. Use your phone light and move in sequence as directed.
- Earthquake: while the shaking lasts, protecting your head matters more than moving. When it stops, go to open ground clear of falling objects, and do not re-enter because of aftershocks.
- Explosion or attack: at the sound of a blast, drop flat and cover your ears and head, stay down a little longer in case of a secondary explosion, then evacuate away from the blast point. For a suspicious object: do not touch it, leave at once, and report it (111 / 112).
- Crowded venues are attractive targets, so do not mix into the crowd - move against it or into a nearby secure facility.

---

# Looking After the Vulnerable

- Children, elderly people, and people with disabilities evacuate first.
- If you see someone panicking, work with those around you to calm them.
- If someone goes down, shout to alert the people nearby, hold back the flow to open a space, and then lift them.
