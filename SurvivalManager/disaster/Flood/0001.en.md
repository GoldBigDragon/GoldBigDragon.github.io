# What Each Water Depth Means

| Depth | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 15 cm (ankle) | In a strong current, even an adult is knocked down and cannot move |
| 30 cm (shin) | An adult can no longer climb an underground stairway / a car floats |
| 50 cm (knee) | Walking becomes very difficult, and one person cannot open a door |
| ⅔ of tire height | The limit at which a car's engine will keep running |

> Once rainwater starts entering an underground space, flooding is complete in 5 to 10 minutes. There is no time to deliberate.

---

# What to Do When Heavy Rain Is Forecast

## 1. Residents of low-lying and flood-prone areas
- Monitor the forecast and prepare to leave. When evacuating early, shut off the power and gas on your way out.
- Shops should bring street signage and other electrical fixtures inside.
- Neighborhood leaders should use broadcasts and call trees to advise staying in and to name the shelter location.

## 2. People in underground spaces
- Keep bolt cutters capable of cutting through window security bars ready.
- Keep flotation items - a life vest, an inner tube, a large block of styrofoam - in case escape becomes impossible.
- Learn the emergency exits and escape routes of underground stations and garages you use.

## 3. Apartment building managers
- Stock flood barriers, sandbags, and pumps, and designate one person per entrance to install them.
- When flooding is expected, announce the shelter location and make clear that vehicles can only be moved before the rain arrives.
- Announce when the flood barriers will go in, and that vehicles cannot be moved afterward.
- Notify elderly residents living alone and people with disabilities in advance, and check on them by intercom or phone.

## 4. Drivers
- Keep an escape hammer in the car.
- Avoid underground parking where flooding is expected, and move cars parked near rivers, beaches, or in low ground.
- When an evacuation advisory is issued, move vehicles out of riverside lots and leave your phone number on the car.

# Crossing a Flooded Street

- The rule is simply not to. Murky water hides its depth, and the sharp and heavy objects on the bottom. At night it is worse.
- If water starts flowing toward you, get to the second floor of the nearest building or to higher ground. It usually drains in two to three hours; move only once it is safe.
- If you truly have no choice:
  - Walk slowly and steadily, keeping a hand on building walls rather than the center of the road.
  - Probe ahead with a long stick to find manholes and obstacles.
  - Avoid places where the water runs hard or swirls - a manhole cover may be gone there.
  - Bubbles rising from a manhole cover mean it may blow open. Get well away at once.
  - Stay 2 to 3 meters clear of traffic signals, streetlights, and standing signs.
  - Power lines, poles, metal structures, pipes, and wire fencing are all electrocution hazards.

# Escaping an Underground Space

## 1. Go immediately
- If water is coming in, open the door first.
- The moment water covers any of the floor, or a drain backs up, leave.
- With knee-deep water (50 cm) outside, one person cannot open the door. Cut the power, then push it open together.
- 30 cm of water on a stairway already defeats an adult, and even ankle-deep water stops children and elderly people.
- Hold the handrail as you move, and do not use the elevator - the power can fail at any moment.

## 2. If the door will not open
- Cut the security bars with bolt cutters and climb out.
- In an underground station or mall, take the emergency passage or alternate exit.
- If you cannot get out at all, shut off the power and gas, call 119 or shout for help, and stay afloat with a life vest or tube until rescue arrives.

## 3. What to wear
- Sneakers are best. Dress shoes, heels, and slippers slip, and rubber boots fill with water and become a liability.
- With nothing suitable, go barefoot.

## 4. Underground parking
- The moment water enters, leave the car and get yourself out.
- Once water is running down the ramp, pressure makes the car impossible to move.
- Never go back into an underground garage to check on a vehicle.

# If Your Car Is Flooding

1. Move the car to safety before the water reaches two-thirds of tire height.
2. If you cannot move it, open a window or the sunroof before the engine dies to keep an exit.
3. If water pressure holds the door shut, break a window with the metal stem at the base of the headrest.
4. If you cannot break the glass, wait until the difference between inside and outside water levels is under 30 cm, then push the door open the moment it gives.
5. Once out, get above the water. If there is nowhere else, climb onto the roof of the car, call 119, and wait.

## While driving
- Cross standing water in low gear and get through quickly.
- When abandoning the car, leave the key in plain sight and the doors unlocked.
- Never enter an underpass that has begun to flood. If you are already in it, leave the car and get out on foot.
- Use your hazard lights entering or leaving an underpass to warn the traffic behind you.
- Never drive onto a bridge with a torrent beneath it - water pressure can push the car into the river.
- If a current traps your car, open the door on the side away from the flow, or break that window. On the upstream side the current holds the door shut.

---

# After the Water Goes Down

- Receding floodwater carries oil and animal carcasses - stay well away from it.
- Verify that tap water and stored drinking water are safe before using them, and discard any food that floodwater touched; food poisoning is a real risk.
- In a flooded home, check that the gas and electrical breakers are off, and do not use them until Korea Gas Safety Corporation (1544-4500), Korea Electrical Safety Corporation (1588-7500), or a qualified professional has inspected them.
- Gas may have leaked: ventilate thoroughly with the windows open, and use no match or lighter until you have.
- Pump a flooded basement out slowly - about a third of the depth per day. All at once, the walls collapse and the floor buckles.
- Photograph damaged property, then report it to your city, county, or district office.
- Wear boots, rubber gloves, and goggles while cleaning, and wash your hands with soap afterward.
