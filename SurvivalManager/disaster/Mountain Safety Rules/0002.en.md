# 4. Snake Bite

## 4-1. Was It Venomous?
- A triangular head and two fang punctures at the front of the wound point to a venomous snake (a pit viper).
- A non-venomous bite leaves several rows of small scratches.

## 4-2. What to Do
1. Get away from the snake at once. Do not try to catch it or provoke it again.
2. Keep the person calm and still. Excitement and movement speed circulation and spread the venom faster.
3. Remove bracelets, watches, and other jewelry. Swelling will turn them into tourniquets.
4. Wash the bite with clean water.
5. Place gauze about 10 cm above the bite and wrap a bandage lightly - loose enough to slide one finger under.
   - Bound too tight, circulation is cut off and the skin dies.
6. Keep the bite below heart level. An arm hangs down; for a leg, lie the person down.
7. Vomiting, abdominal pain, and reduced consciousness can follow, so lay the person on their side.

## 4-3. Never Do This

| Wrong treatment | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Cutting the wound open | Damages muscle, vessels, and nerves, and invites secondary infection |
| Sucking the venom out by mouth | Does not work, and endangers the person doing it |
| Binding the limb tightly | Swelling causes tissue death, and the binding cuts into the flesh |
| Applying ice directly | Does not neutralize the venom and risks frostbite and tissue death |
| Cigarette ash or bean paste | Causes inflammation |
| Food or drink | Alcohol especially - it speeds circulation and spreads the venom |
| Rubbing with pork fat | No effect, high infection risk |

## 4-4. If You Have No Signal
- Getting the person to the nearest facility that stocks antivenom is the priority.
- Make a stretcher from branches and clothing to keep their movement to a minimum.
- If they must walk, go as slowly and steadily as possible.

# 5. Meeting a Wild Boar

## 5-1. Know the Animal
- Poor eyesight, but extremely sharp smell and hearing.
- When provoked, it loses its sense of direction and charges straight ahead.
- Turn your back and run, and it reads you as prey and chases.
- Its neck is short, so it cannot look up well, and it is weak on uphill ground.

## 5-2. If It Has Not Noticed You
- Back away quietly to safety.
- With the wind behind you, it will smell you and come. Watch the wind direction.

## 5-3. If You Come Face to Face
1. Do not shout or move suddenly.
2. Stop calmly, keep eye contact, and watch what it does.
3. Never turn your back. Facing it, retreat very slowly to open distance.
4. Move to the far side of a nearby tree or rock, or to higher ground. It is weak uphill and cannot look up.
5. If you sense an attack coming, shield yourself with your pack or whatever you are carrying.

## 5-4. If It Charges
- A boar is far faster than you. Never turn your back and run.
- Get as high as you can - up a tree, onto a rock.
- Keeping a large tree between you and moving as it moves works well.
- Use your pack and jacket as a shield.

## 5-5. Never Do This
- Throwing stones or threatening it
- Shouting or waving your arms
- Approaching it
- Trying to drive it off
- Turning your back and running

# 6. Bee and Wasp Stings

## 6-1. What to Do
1. If the stinger is visible, scrape it off gently with a credit card or a thin edge. Do not try to pull it out.
2. Never rub the area afterwards. It spreads the venom wider.
3. Wash with cold water and apply a cold pack if you can.

## 6-2. Anaphylaxis (Whole-Body Allergic Reaction)

These signs mean life-threatening
- Difficulty breathing
- Cyanosis (lips and fingers turning blue)
- Rapid loss of consciousness
- Hives over the body
- Facial swelling

What to do
1. Lay the person down and raise their legs into the shock position.
2. If an epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen) is available, inject it into the thigh immediately.
3. If they are not breathing, start CPR.
4. Get off the mountain or call for rescue at once.

> A sting can go systemic within minutes. Do not hesitate - reach for the EpiPen and the phone at the same time.
