# 1. Hiking Safety Rules

## 1-1. Before You Go
- Check the weather, the route, and the distance in advance.
- Check whether the area is closed to entry or the trail is shut.
- Wear boots that fit and that breathe and shed water.
- Tell family or a friend your plan - departure and expected return time, and the route.
- Do not go alone. Two people minimum.

What goes in the pack
- Flashlight and spare batteries, rain gear
- Phone and power bank
- First-aid kit
- Emergency food - salt, chocolate, jerky, hard candy: things dense in calories
- Water bottle, compass, map

How to pack it
- Do not carry more than 30 kg.
- Light and bulky at the bottom, heavy at the top.
- Carry nothing in your hands. Everything goes in the pack.

## 1-2. On the Mountain
- Start early and finish one or two hours before sunset.
- Do not exceed eight hours a day, and always hold back 30% of your strength.
- Set the pace by the weakest member of the group.
- At first, walk 15 minutes and rest 5; once warmed up, walk an hour and rest 10 minutes on a regular rhythm.
- Do not eat a lot at once - little and often.
- Never hike after drinking. It sharply raises the risk of hypothermia.
- Stay on marked trails only.
- Note the ID number on the fire service location markers along the trail as you pass them. They let you give an exact position if you have to call for help.
- Do not look at your phone or take photos while walking.
- Cook and camp only at designated campsites and shelters.

# 2. When You Lose the Trail

## 2-1. Stop Where You Are
> Moving blindly in a panic takes you deeper into the mountain. Staying calm comes first.

- If you clearly remember the way you came, go back.
- If you are not sure, do not move.
- If you took a wrong turn, go back to the last place you recognize and reorient from there.

## 2-2. Work Out Where You Are
- Read the landmarks around you - peaks, valleys, the line of the ridge.
- With a compass, take a bearing and compare it with your map.
- Look for trail signs and markers.

## 2-3. Climb to the Ridge
> Avoid valleys, head for the ridge. A ridge gives you a wide view and a sense of direction; valleys are dangerous and easy to get lost in.

## 2-4. Call 119 (If You Have Signal)
- Give your position in as much detail as you can.
- Describe what happened - a fall, a fracture, being lost.
- Then crouch down and conserve body heat while you wait.

| What to tell them | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Where it happened | "Mt. ○○, ○○ trail, fire service marker no. ○○○" |
| What happened | "Snake bite" / "A fall - I think it's a fracture" |
| The injured person's condition | "Conscious, suspected fracture of the right ankle" |
| Your name and number | Your own name and mobile number |
| How many injured | "One of the two of us is hurt" |
| First aid so far | "Controlling bleeding" / "Splint applied" |

## 2-5. If You Have No Signal
- If you stay and wait: body heat is the priority. Put on every layer you have, gloves and a hat, and crouch down.
- To signal for help: from high ground - a ridge or near a peak - shout, or use bright clothing or a reflector.
- If you go down alone: water runs downhill, so following a stream will bring you off the mountain. But not in darkness or heavy rain - stay out of the watercourse then.

## 2-6. Never Do This
- Forcing a descent in darkness or fog
- Walking on without being sure of direction
- Using up all your strength - keep 30% in reserve

# 3. If You Decide to Bivouac

## 3-1. Choosing the Spot
- First check for landslide and rockfall risk.
- Choose near a rock that breaks the wind, below the ridge line, on gentle ground.
- Avoid valley bottoms, low spots where water collects, and the foot of tall trees (lightning).
- A cloudburst overnight can raise the water in moments. Do not pitch on a streambed or at the water's edge.
- If water does come in, leave your gear and get out immediately.

## 3-2. Staying Warm
- Put on every layer you brought, plus gloves and a hat.
- Use plastic bags and rain gear to block rain and wind.
- Insulate against ground cold with your pack, spare clothes, plastic bags, and leaf litter.
- With others, huddle close and share body heat.

## 3-3. Food and Water
- Eat emergency food in small portions, spread out.
- Use running stream water; if it is not drinkable, collect dew and rainwater.
- Never drink alcohol. It dilates blood vessels and strips heat away faster.

## 3-4. Making Yourself Findable
- Tie bright clothing or reflective material to a branch.
- Signal at intervals with a whistle or a shout.
- At night, repeat SOS (··· −−− ···) with your flashlight.
- On open ground, lay out a large SOS in stones and branches.
