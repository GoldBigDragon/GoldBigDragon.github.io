# 7. First Aid by Injury

## 7-1. Sprain - Telling It from a Fracture
| | The state you are in |
| --- | --- |
| Simple sprain | It hurts, but you can bear some weight |
| Suspected fracture | Severe pain, deformity, tenderness directly over bone, no weight bearing at all |

Treatment
1. Stop at once and keep weight off the injury.
2. Do not apply ice directly - wrap it in cloth and ice for about 20 minutes.
3. Compress moderately with a bandage or handkerchief to limit swelling.
4. Raise the injury above heart level.

If you have to move
- If you cannot move right away, rest properly somewhere safe.
- Use a branch or a trekking pole as a crutch.
- If you must walk, wrap the ankle firmly and put your weight on the good foot.
- With a companion, take their shoulder for support.

## 7-2. Fracture
Signs: severe pain, swelling, deformity, tenderness over bone, unable to bear weight

1. Do not move. With a suspected neck or back injury, do not move the person at all.
2. Apply a splint.
   - Long enough to span the joints above and below the break.
   - If there is bleeding, control it with direct pressure first, then splint.
   - Place branches or thick card either side and secure with clothing, bandage, or a handkerchief.
3. If bone is protruding, cover it with clean cloth and never push it back in.
4. Carry the person with the splint secured, gently.

## 7-3. Abrasion
1. Rinse under clean running water or disinfect.
2. Carefully remove any foreign material.
3. Cover with gauze or a plaster.

## 7-4. Laceration
1. Press hard with clean cloth to stop the bleeding.
2. Once it stops, rinse with clean water. If bleeding heavily, do not rinse - maintain pressure.
3. Disinfect if you can, then cover with clean cloth against infection.
4. Raise the wound above heart level.

## 7-5. Head Injury
Danger signs: loss of consciousness · severe headache · nausea and vomiting · unequal pupils

1. A neck or spine injury may come with it, so do not move the person carelessly.
2. If conscious, lay them comfortably and keep them still.
3. If unconscious, open the airway; if they are not breathing, start CPR.
4. If they vomit, turn them on their side to keep the airway clear.
5. Carry them with head and neck immobilized, as carefully as you can.
6. Even if it looks minor, they must be examined properly at a hospital.

## 7-6. Exhaustion
Signs: heavy fatigue and listlessness · headache, dizziness, nausea · pale or cold, clammy skin · fast, weak pulse

1. Stop immediately and lie down in cool, breezy shade.
2. Raise the legs above heart level so blood reaches the brain.
3. Loosen belt, buttons, and collar to ease breathing.
4. If conscious, take salted water or a little salt from your emergency food. If unconscious, give nothing by mouth.
5. On a hot day, wipe the body with a wet towel to cool it; on a cold day, wrap in clothing or plastic to keep heat in.
6. Rest fully, check recovery, and descend slowly once recovered.

Prevention
- Always hold 30% of your strength in reserve.
- Drink and eat little and often.
- Alcohol brings exhaustion on fast, and drinking while exhausted sharply raises the risk of hypothermia.

## 7-7. Difficulty Breathing
Signs: fast, shallow breathing · cyanosis

1. Sit them comfortably or lie them down with the upper body slightly raised.
2. Loosen everything constricting - buttons, belt.
3. Tilt the head back and lift the chin to open the airway.
4. If an object is blocking the airway, perform the Heimlich maneuver.
   - Stand behind them, place both hands between the navel and the base of the sternum, and thrust sharply upward.
5. If they are not breathing, repeat CPR until they come round.
   - Chest compressions: hands stacked on the center of the chest (lower half of the sternum), 100–120 per minute, 5–6 cm deep
   - Rescue breaths: 2 after every 30 compressions (30:2). If rescue breathing is not possible, compression-only CPR is acceptable.

## 7-8. Acute Chest Pain (Cardiac)
Signs: a bursting pain in the chest · a crushing pressure in the chest

1. Sit or lie them down comfortably and keep them still.
2. Loosen everything tight.
3. Have them breathe deeply and slowly.
4. If they carry a prescribed vasodilator (nitroglycerin or similar), have them take it.
5. If it does not improve, descend at once or call for rescue.
6. If they lose consciousness, open the airway; if they are not breathing, start CPR.

## 7-9. Hypothermia
Signs: body temperature below 35°C · violent chills and shivering · clumsy movement · slurred speech · reduced consciousness

1. Remove wet clothing immediately and wrap in dry clothing, a blanket, or plastic.
2. Move them out of the wind and rain.
3. Help them rewarm.
   - Place your hands at the armpits, neck, and groin - where the large vessels run.
   - With others, huddle close and share heat.
   - If conscious, give warm drinks in small sips.
4. Do not rub or chafe vigorously. It dilates vessels and can drop core temperature faster.
5. If unconscious, open the airway; if they are not breathing, start CPR.

> Shivering easing or stopping is not recovery - it is deterioration. Vomiting, diarrhea, and cardiac arrest can occur during rewarming, so watch them for 48 hours.

## 7-10. Frostbite and Trench Foot
- Frostbite: feet, hands, and exposed face are most vulnerable. In a group, check each other's faces often; alone, periodically cover your nose and lower face with a glove.
  - Do not thaw it near a flame. Immerse in 37–42°C water until it thaws, dry it, and keep it warm with body heat.
- Trench foot (immersion foot): hours to days in a wet environment near freezing damages nerve and muscle, and in severe cases leads to amputation. The best prevention is keeping your feet dry - carry spare socks in a waterproof bag, and wash your feet daily and change into dry socks.
