# 6. Sheltering Somewhere Other Than a Designated Site

## 6-1. Basics
- At night, turn off the lights and stop any light escaping.
- Seal the doors, window gaps, and vents with tape, wet towels, newspaper, and aluminum tape to keep CBR contaminants out.

## 6-2. Against Power Loss
- Prepare a flashlight with batteries, candles, a lighter or matches.
- Card payments and cash machines stop working in a blackout - keep cash.
- Keep a battery-powered FM/AM portable radio for government announcements.

## 6-3. Against Water Loss
- Fill the bathtub and buckets while you can.
- Drink sealed bottled water, and use the stored water for washing and dishes.

## 6-4. Against Gas Loss
- Prepare a portable gas stove and butane canisters.
- Never use a gas stove in an enclosed space - carbon monoxide poisoning and asphyxiation.

## 6-5. Food
- With gas and electricity both out, cooking may be impossible. Choose food that needs no cooking and stores easily - cereal, canned goods.
- With infants or elderly family members, add formula, baby food, and retort porridge - soft and easy to digest.

## 6-6. If the Building Is Damaged
1. Get out quickly by the nearest emergency exit or stairwell.
2. Move as far from the building as you can, in case it collapses.
3. Do not re-enter a building that is leaning or cracked. Aftershocks or a further blast can bring it down completely.

# 7. Two Weeks or More After a Nuclear Detonation

## 7-1. Why Two Weeks
Fallout radiation falls off sharply with time. Taking one hour after the detonation as the baseline:

| Time elapsed | Radiation intensity |
| --- | --- |
| About 6 hours | 1/10 |
| 2 days | 1/100 |
| 2 weeks | 1/1,000 |

> So sheltering for about two weeks is necessary, and your supplies must last that long.

## 7-2. Choosing a Leader and Setting Rules
> Without order and rules, disputes over supplies, space, and hygiene make the whole environment worse for everyone.

In a shared shelter, choose a representative and agree on rules for living together.

- Keep a roster with brief personal details - for headcounts and for contacting people if needed.
- Look after the vulnerable - older people, children, the sick.
- Decide the layout - separate sleeping, eating, and toilet areas.
- Divide the roles - cooking, cleaning, security, medical care, childcare.
- Set rules for distributing supplies - priority of use and allocation, and stick to them. With limited supplies, fair and rational criteria are essential.
- Protect privacy - especially for families with infants and children, and for women.
- Arrange security - watches and patrols to prevent crime, with everyone taking part.

Anything else is decided collectively through the representative.

## 7-3. Managing Supplies
- Take stock of what you actually have and what is needed to last more than two weeks.
- Calculate the rate of consumption and plan how to make up shortfalls.
- Set a daily ration of water and food and manage it systematically. Waste and you will not last the period.

> Eat and drink only what is sealed.
> Food left exposed and tap water may be contaminated with radioactivity.

## 7-4. Health
- Seal doors, windows, and vents with plastic, cloth, and aluminum tape to keep contaminated outside air out.
- Treat and cover wounds and burns properly. Infection in an unsanitary environment can be fatal - personal hygiene matters more than usual.
- Eat and drink only what is clean, and never eat spoiled food.
- Keep up fluids and rest to maintain strength.

## 7-5. Psychological Care
> Long-term sheltering causes severe psychological stress.

- Work, exercise, and conversation with the people around you help relieve anxiety and stress.
- Keeping a regular daily routine supports emotional stability.
- Simple games and storytelling reduce children's anxiety.
