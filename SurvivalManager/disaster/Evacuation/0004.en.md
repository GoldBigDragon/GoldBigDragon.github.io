# 8. Sheltering in a Vehicle

## 8-1. Health Risks
- Carbon dioxide builds up in a sealed car over a long stay. Ventilate periodically.
- Watch for economy class syndrome - sitting still for long periods causes blood to clot, and a clot from the leg can block the pulmonary artery and kill.
  - Stretch and move often, and drink plenty of fluids.
- When running the engine for heat, beware of carbon monoxide. Ventilate well and check the exhaust is not blocked.

## 8-2. Emergency Supplies in the Car
- Keep water, food, and emergency items in the car much as you would in an emergency pack.
- Add a window-breaking hammer, repair tools, a fire extinguisher, jumper cables, and a snow shovel.
- In summer the cabin and trunk get hot enough to spoil supplies. Park underground where you can, and rotate medicines and food regularly.
- Do not store lighters, butane canisters, or anything flammable or explosive in the car.

## 8-3. Fuel
- In a disaster, fuel shortages and power cuts make filling stations unusable.
- Keep the tank well filled and never let it drop below half.

# 9. When a State of Emergency Is Declared

## 9-1. What the Government Does
- Where necessary the government declares national mobilization, drawing on personnel, materials, and equipment needed to conduct a war.
- It manages the distribution of essential goods and introduces rationing where needed.

## 9-2. Go Home Immediately
1. When a state of emergency is declared, return home at once.
2. Use public transport. Ordinary traffic may be restricted to let emergency vehicles move.
3. Against loss of power and water, prepare flashlights, batteries, candles, a lighter and matches, and fill the bathtub or large containers with water to use sparingly.
4. Stay inside, keep listening to civil defense broadcasts on TV and radio, and follow government guidance.
5. Keep phone use to a minimum so a surge in calls does not bring the network down.

## 9-3. Under National Mobilization
- Designated personnel, materials, and equipment report to the assembly point assigned in advance.
- Before you go, tell your family when and where you are reporting, and make sure they know where the emergency supplies are and where the nearby shelters are so they can manage without you.
- On arrival, report to the officials, follow instructions, and do not leave the assembly point on your own.
- If equipment is mobilized, bring the spare parts needed to keep it running.
- After reporting, take the training given and carry out the duties assigned.

## 9-4. Do Not Hoard
> Hoarding drives prices up and shortages deeper - more people end up harmed.

The government designates essential goods and manages their supply, and rations items that cannot be distributed because of hoarding or price spikes.

## 9-5. Managing Emergency Supplies
- Keep essentials, medicines, and CBR protective items at home and check expiry dates regularly.
- Choose food that needs no cooking.
- Prepare at least one set of warm clothes and shoes per person.
- Store items that duplicate the emergency pack in another bag or case, and rotate anything with an expiry date.
- Tailor medicines to your family - add what children and older members need. If someone is ill, keep a recent prescription.
- Prepare CBR protective items; if you do not have them, use substitutes: rain poncho, rubber gloves, long clothing, face masks, tape.

# 10. Rescue and Recovery

## 10-1. Helping with Rescue
- Once things stabilize, take part in rescue work within what you can safely do, with your own safety secured.
- If you find an injured person, give what first aid you can and stay with them until professional rescuers arrive.

## 10-2. Reporting Damage
- Report damaged roads, buildings, and power lines to the authorities.
- Never approach unexploded ordnance - report it to a military unit or other authority.
- Do not approach areas or substances you suspect of CBR contamination - report them.

## 10-3. Secondary Harm
- Watch for injury from damaged buildings and debris, electrocution, fire, and disease.
- When power, water, and gas come back after being cut, watch for fire and flooding.

> If the power comes back while gas is leaking, a spark will cause an explosion.
> Check the gas valve is closed, and if you smell gas, ventilate and then report it.

- Stay away from buildings that are leaning or cracked.
- Stay away from downed power lines.

## 10-4. Signaling for Rescue
- SOS in Morse - three short, three long, three short ( · · · ─ ─ ─ · · · ) - repeated with a whistle or a light.
- With no whistle, strike a pipe or other metal object.
- Shouting burns energy fast. Shout only when you judge rescuers are close.
- If a call will not connect, send a text. Texts use less network capacity and are far more likely to get through.
