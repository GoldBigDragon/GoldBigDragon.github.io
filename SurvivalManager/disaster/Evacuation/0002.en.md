# 4. Moving to Shelter

## 4-1. Before You Leave the House
1. Shut the gas valve and throw the main breaker. This stops the home you will return to from burning down while you are gone.
2. Write on the front door where you are going. For anyone who comes looking.
3. At night, turn off the lights and block any leaking light. It makes target identification harder for an air attack.

## 4-2. What to Wear and Carry
- Sneakers or hiking boots, comfortable clothes, a warm outer layer.
- Long clothing and a hat to minimize exposed skin - against debris, chemical agents, and radioactive fallout.
- Gas mask, face mask, gloves - worn, or ready to use instantly.

## 4-3. Route
- Check the safe route and the shelter on radio and phone.
- Work out a route that bypasses dangerous areas in advance.
- Have an alternative route ready in case main roads are blocked or destroyed.

## 4-4. Go on Foot
> Driving causes congestion and delays the whole evacuation.
> Ordinary traffic may be restricted to let emergency vehicles through.

- Use public transport where possible, and walk where it is not.
- If your area evacuates by government vehicle from an assembly point, walk to the assembly point.
- A bicycle with a rack needs no fuel and carries a small load - genuinely useful.

## 4-5. If You Must Drive
- Close the windows and switch to recirculated air to keep CBR contaminants out.
- If you abandon the car, park it on the right side of the road, leave the key in it, and do not lock the doors.
  - A car blocking the road, or one that rescuers cannot move, delays rescue and evacuation.
- In a disaster, fuel shortages and power cuts make filling stations unusable. In normal times, never let the tank drop below half.

## 4-6. Those Who Need Help
- Help older people, children, pregnant women, and people with disabilities evacuate with you.
- Children and older people are safest evacuated early, at the alert warning stage.

# 5. In a Government-Designated Shelter

## 5-1. Follow the Manager
- For everyone's safety and order, follow the manager's instructions.
- Leaving the shelter on your own puts you at risk and unsettles the other people there.

## 5-2. Safety Rules
- Do not block entrances or passageways.
- Cut unnecessary power use, and do nothing that risks fire.
- If someone needs first aid, tell the manager immediately.

> Do not cook with fire inside an enclosed shelter.
> Both fire and carbon monoxide poisoning are real risks.

## 5-3. Shared Facilities and Communications
- Keep toilets and other shared facilities clean and use supplies sparingly. Many people share limited facilities; how each person behaves determines the environment for all.
- Keep phone use to a minimum. Save the network for people who need to make urgent calls or request rescue.

## 5-4. Information
- Listen to government announcements on TV and radio.
- Be skeptical of rumors on social media.
- Share information with family and neighbors who are less able to act on their own.

## 5-5. Sleep
- Shelters are lit and noisy. An eye mask and earplugs help.
- Lack of sleep weakens immunity and judgment. Get as much as you can.

## 5-6. Hygiene
- Wear a mask to prevent infectious disease.
- Waterless shampoo and body wash are useful.
- Wipe yourself with wet wipes or tissue dampened with a little water.
- Stock plenty of plastic bags, disposable gloves, tissue, and a container for waste and toileting.
- Collecting urine in a plastic bottle saves water.
- Sanitary pads and diapers run out fast in a disaster. Prepare more than you think.

## 5-7. Staying Warm
- A foil emergency blanket reflects body heat back. Tiny to carry, big in effect.
- A plastic bottle filled with hot water works as a heat pack.
- Sleeping on the floor strips heat fast. Put something insulating between you and the ground.
  - Substitutes for a mat: cardboard boxes, styrofoam, foil-faced construction insulation, layers of newspaper.

## 5-8. Conflict and Personal Safety
> Crime and conflict arise easily in shelters.

- Keep your supplies out of sight as much as you can.
- Protect women and children, and go with a family member as far as the entrance of public toilets.
- Keep relations with the people around you good, and cooperate.
- When hands are short, help - serving meals, cleaning, looking after children.
- Avoid behavior that could look suspicious.
