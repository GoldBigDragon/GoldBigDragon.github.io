# The 30-30 Rule

- If thunder follows the flash within 30 seconds, the storm is within about 10 km. Get inside a building or a vehicle now.
- Wait 30 minutes after the last clap of thunder before going back out. Lightning continues after the rain stops.

---

# Safe Places and Dangerous Places

| Safe | Dangerous |
| --- | --- |
| Inside a substantial building | Under a tree, beside a utility pole |
| Inside an enclosed metal-roofed vehicle | Gazebos, sheds, open bleachers, convertibles |
| Underground, a cave | Summits, ridgelines, rock faces |
| A dry hollow in the ground | Open fields, shorelines, anywhere near water |

- Inside a car, do not touch any metal.
- Under a tree is the worst choice of all. If you have no alternative, keep at least 10 m away from it.

---

# When Lightning Is Forecast

- Check the forecast and postpone outdoor plans.
- Stop golfing, fishing, or hiking and get to shelter.
- Put down long metal objects - trekking poles, fishing rods, umbrellas - and lay them flat on the ground, away from you.
- Get off your bicycle.
- If clouds build suddenly and the wind gusts - especially on a summit or ridge - descend immediately.

---

# Indoors

- Unplug telephones and appliances, and keep at least 1 m away from lights and electrical equipment.
- Close the windows and stay away from them.
- Do not shower, bathe, or wash dishes. Current can travel through the plumbing.
- Do not use a corded phone. Mobile phones are fine.

---

# Outdoors With Nowhere to Shelter

1. Move to the lowest ground available - a dry hollow is ideal.
2. Crouch with your feet together. Feet apart lets ground current travel up one leg and down the other, through your body.
3. Lower your head and cover your ears.
4. Do not lie down. The more of you touches the ground, the worse it is.
5. If you can, sit on something insulating at least 10 cm off the ground - a pack, a foam mat.
6. In a group, spread out at least 5 m apart, so a single strike cannot injure everyone.

---

# If Someone Is Struck

> A lightning victim carries no residual charge. It is safe to touch them immediately.

1. Move them to safety with help and call 119.
2. Check for response and breathing.
3. If breathing is absent or abnormal, start CPR immediately.
   - Compress the center of the chest 5 cm deep at 100–120 per minute.
   - If rescue breaths are difficult, compression-only CPR is fine.
   - Use an AED as soon as one is available.
4. With multiple casualties, treat the motionless ones first. Lightning reverses the usual triage priority.
5. If they have a pulse and are breathing, look for burns, fractures, hearing and vision damage, and neurological signs. There will be burns where the current entered and exited.
6. Keep them warm with a blanket or clothing.
7. If they are conscious, let them settle into whatever position is most comfortable, and talk to them calmly if they are agitated or shaking.
8. Even somewhere remote like a mountainside, never give up - keep going. Cardiac arrest from lightning has a comparatively high survival rate.
9. Even if they appear fine, burns can run deep inside the body. They must be examined at a hospital.
