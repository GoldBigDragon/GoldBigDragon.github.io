# 1. What Chemical Weapons Do

Chemical weapons (poison gas) use toxic agents in liquid, gas, or solid form to cause respiratory failure, muscle spasms, chemical burns, and pneumonia.

- They carry less political cost than nuclear or biological weapons and have been used in real conflicts up to recent years.
- They can be delivered by multiple rocket launchers, ballistic missiles, special forces, or aircraft.
- You will not know which agent was used, so prepare for all of them together.

> In wartime, if bombing begins or you see suspicious smoke, assume a chemical attack and respond at once.

## 1-1. Heavier Than Air - Why the Shelter Rule Reverses
- Chemical agents are heavier than air and sink.
- Once released, they can reach high concentrations at ground level and underground.
- So in a chemical attack, a high floor is safer than a low floor or a basement.

> This is the exact opposite of sheltering underground in a nuclear or conventional bombing attack.
> Fix this in your memory now.

- Persistent agents can remain for more than a day. Do not approach a contaminated area until the government confirms it is safe.

## 1-2. The Airway Is Far More Vulnerable Than Skin
- Many agents - nerve agents and blister agents especially - are absorbed through skin as well as the airway.
- But the airway is far more vulnerable.

> Protect the airway (gas mask) before the skin (protective suit).

- To block skin absorption, wear a chemical-resistant suit, gloves, and overboots, and tape the gaps in your clothing.
- Without a suit, minimize exposure with rain gear, rubber gloves, and long clothing.

## 1-3. Smell and Symptoms Will Not Tell You
- Many agents have no smell, and depending on the type, symptoms may only appear hours later.
- By the time you have difficulty breathing, eye pain, vomiting, convulsions, or a rash, you have already absorbed a substantial dose.

> Never judge contamination by smell or symptoms.
> If there is an explosion or suspicious smoke, put on the mask and evacuate even with no symptoms at all.

# 2. Signs of an Attack

If you see any of these, put on a gas mask and leave the area immediately.

- Sudden difficulty breathing, vomiting, or skin rash
- Birds dropping and fish dying for no apparent reason
- Suspicious smoke or mist appearing suddenly
- People around you developing the same symptoms at the same time

# 3. Preparing in Advance

> A chemical attack unfolds very fast. Once it starts, there is almost no time to get equipment ready.

## 3-1. Gas Mask
- Get a KS M 6685 certified mask - the Korean national standard, designed for civilian evacuation under chemical and biological contamination in wartime.
- For industrial chemical releases, get a combined-gas filter (organic vapor, acid gas, ammonia, and particulate filters together).
  - Industrial chemicals are enormously varied, and the right mask differs by substance. Even combined filters do not protect against everything - follow the manufacturer's or the government's guidance on specifics.
- If you cannot obtain a KS M 6685 product, get a combined canister with organic vapor, acid gas, and high-grade particulate filters and a full-face piece.
- An expired filter may not filter agents adequately. Check the dates regularly.

> A mask that does not fit lets contaminated air in around the seal and loses most of its protection.
> Prepare a separate mask sized to each family member's face.

## 3-2. Protective Clothing
- If you can, get a chemical-resistant suit, gloves, and overboots.
- As a fallback, keep rain gear, rubber gloves, rubber boots, and long clothing.

## 3-3. Tape
- Get chemical-resistant cloth tape or duct tape.
- It seals the gaps in protective clothing and blocks window gaps, door gaps, and vents when sheltering indoors.
- Depending on your home and windows, you may need 60 m or more.

## 3-4. Know Your Shelter Spot
The best place in a chemical attack is a high floor indoors that can be sealed against outside air. At home, work, and school, find somewhere that meets these conditions in advance:
- A room that seals well (a bathroom, for example)
- A space with few vents
- A space with few or no windows

## 3-5. Protecting Food
- Keep airtight containers, plastic bags, and cling film ready so you can wrap food quickly.
- Check that your water containers seal properly.
