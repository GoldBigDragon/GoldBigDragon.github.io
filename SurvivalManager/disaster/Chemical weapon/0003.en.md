# 7. Oxygen in a Sealed Space

If you have sealed yourself in against a chemical attack, oxygen becomes its own problem.

> In a sealed space 2.3 m high and about 7.6 m³ in volume, one person sitting still can last around 12 hours.
> By then carbon dioxide reaches 3–4% or more, which is dangerous - you must ventilate or get out.

- If your breathing quickens and you get a headache or feel dizzy, ventilate or leave at once.
- More people in the same space consume oxygen faster. The time you can last drops sharply with each additional person.

When you ventilate
- Check whether the agent is still present outside.
- Confirm through government broadcasts that it has dispersed before ventilating.
- If you have no choice, open briefly while wearing the mask, then seal again.

# 8. Decontamination

## 8-1. Contaminated Clothing
1. Keep the mask on while you undress.
2. Undress slowly and carefully. Rushing sends the agent airborne and spreads contamination.
3. Put the clothing in a sealable plastic bag or container.
4. Used filters and protective suits can leak too. Seal them in plastic, label them as chemical contamination, and dispose of them somewhere safe.
5. Follow the authorities' instructions on disposal.

## 8-2. Washing
> Wash with lukewarm water for at least 15 minutes.
> Fifteen minutes is the minimum. Do not rush it - take the full time.

- Use soap if you have it. Otherwise use detergent, or wash thoroughly with water alone.
- Blow your nose gently to clear contamination from the nasal passages.
- Hair holds chemical agents stubbornly. Wash it especially thoroughly.
- Also wash ears, skin folds, under the nails, armpits, and groin - where residue collects.
- Take care not to break the skin. A cut lets the agent in directly.
- After washing, get professional treatment at a clinic or hospital.

## 8-3. If It Gets in Your Eyes
- Rinse with clean water or saline for at least 15 minutes.
- Remove contact lenses immediately.
- Never rub. It worsens corneal damage.

## 8-4. Medical Care Afterwards
> Decontamination alone does not treat the internal damage an agent causes.

- Get professional treatment as soon as possible afterwards.
- Tell the medical staff which agent you were exposed to, for how long, and what symptoms you have - as precisely as you can.

# 9. Food and Water

## 9-1. The Rule
- Never eat food that has been left exposed.
- Eat only what is completely sealed - bottled water, canned goods, packaged snacks.
- Even sealed food has an outside surface that may be contaminated. Wash the packaging with clean soapy water before opening it.

## 9-2. Never Eat Outdoors
> Do not eat outdoors at all. The agent gets into the food while you are eating.

- Eat only in a sealed indoor space, with the mask off.
- Before taking it off, confirm the space is properly sealed and uncontaminated.
- Even indoors, if you smell something or feel any symptom, stop eating and put the mask back on immediately.

## 9-3. Tap Water
- A chemical attack can contaminate water mains or the source.
- Do not drink tap water until it is confirmed safe.
- Sealed bottled water is the safest choice.

# 10. Government Guidance and After the All Clear

## 10-1. Keep Listening
- Follow government guidance continuously on TV, radio, and civil defense alert messages.
- Confirm which agent, the extent of contamination, safe areas, evacuation routes, and decontamination methods through official guidance.

## 10-2. When to Remove the Mask
> Only after the government confirms it is safe.
> Persistent agents can remain for more than a day - never take it off on your own judgment.

## 10-3. Returning to a Contaminated Area
- Do not return until the government confirms it is safe.
- Persistent agents may remain, and touching contaminated ground or puddles lets the agent through your skin.

## 10-4. After the All Clear
- Help with the injured - but always wear a mask and protective gear when handling a contaminated casualty. Bare-handed contact causes secondary contamination.
- An all clear does not rule out further attacks. Keep the mask and protective gear within immediate reach.
- Watch for injury from damaged buildings and debris, electrocution, and fire.
- A conventional bombing attack may have accompanied the chemical one. Do not approach or touch unexploded ordnance.
- If you can, mark areas confirmed or suspected to be contaminated so others stay away.
