# 4. What to Do Immediately

## 4-1. Put On the Mask
- If a CBR warning is issued, or there is an explosion, or you see suspicious smoke - put the mask on at once.
- With no mask, protect your airway with a face mask, a wet towel, or cloth.
  - A wet towel reduces inhalation of some agents but offers very limited protection. It is a stopgap only.

## 4-2. Check the Seal - Wearing It Is Not Enough
How to check
1. Cover the filter with your hand and breathe in.
2. If you feel negative pressure (a vacuum), the seal is good.
3. If air leaks in around your face, adjust the straps or the position.

- Facial hair makes a seal difficult. Manage it with mask use in mind.
- Eyeglass arms break the seal. Use a mask-compatible spectacle insert or keep contact lenses ready.

## 4-3. Protect Your Skin
- After the mask, put on the chemical-resistant suit, gloves, and overboots, and tape the gaps.
- Without them, minimize exposure with rain gear, rubber gloves, and long clothing.
- Tape the gaps between sleeve and glove, between trousers and shoes, and at the neck.

## 4-4. Close and Seal
1. Close every window and switch off ventilation immediately.
2. Stop the air conditioning, extractor fans, and air purifiers - anything that draws outside air in.
3. Tape the window gaps, door gaps, and vents where air comes in most.
4. Seal around anything connected to the outside - air conditioner pipework, extractor fans, drains - with cling film and adhesive tape.

> Taping windows while shelling or explosions continue risks the glass breaking and injuring you.
> While explosions continue, first take cover away from windows, and do the sealing during a lull.

## 4-5. Protect Food
- If there is time, seal food in airtight containers or plastic, and close the lids on water bottles and storage jars.
- Agents that get into food or water cause poisoning when consumed.

# 5. Where to Shelter

## 5-1. Indoors - a High Floor
Chemical agents are heavier than air and sink, so the safe place is a high floor indoors (not the roof) that can be sealed against outside air.

> This is the exact opposite of the rule for nuclear or conventional bombing, where you go underground.
> Identifying the type of attack matters; if you cannot tell, confirm through government broadcasts.

## 5-2. Why Underground Is Dangerous
- Underground parking and subway stations can hold agents pooled at very high concentrations.
- If you have taken shelter underground on an air raid warning and a chemical attack is then confirmed, leave the basement at once for uncontaminated ground or a high floor indoors.
- If you must go underground anyway, wear a KS M 6685 certified mask and a chemical-resistant suit.
- If the basement is crowded, sheltering inside your own car there may be better.

## 5-3. If There Is No Time to Get Out - a Sealable Room
- A bathroom usually has a small window or none, and seals reasonably well with the door shut - a suitable temporary refuge.
1. Close the door and windows.
2. Seal the gaps with wet towels, newspaper, and aluminum tape.
3. Seal every route to the outside - vents, drains, air conditioner pipework - with cling film and adhesive tape.

## 5-4. If You Are Outdoors
- Get inside a building or a vehicle immediately.
- With no building, get in a car, close the windows, and switch to recirculated air.
- If you must move outdoors, wear a mask and consider the terrain and wind direction.

# 6. Which Way to Move, by Wind Direction

## 6-1. The Wind Is Blowing Toward the Contaminated Area
> Move against the wind.
> The wind is pushing the contamination away from you, so moving with the wind at your back takes you clear.

## 6-2. The Wind Is Blowing From the Contaminated Area Toward You
> Move perpendicular to the wind.

- Moving against the wind takes you closer to the source.
- Moving with the wind means travelling alongside the contamination and being exposed for longer.
- So go at right angles and get out of the plume's path quickly.

## 6-3. How to Read the Wind
- Which way leaves and light objects are carried
- Which way a flag streams
- Which way smoke drifts
- Wet a finger and hold it up - the side the wind comes from feels cold
