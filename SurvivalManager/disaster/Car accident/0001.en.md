# Preventing Accidents

## 1. As a pedestrian
- Never jaywalk, and even on a green signal, stop and check both directions before stepping out.
- At a crosswalk with no signal or a flashing signal, cross only after vehicles have come to a complete stop.
- In the rain, hold your umbrella upright rather than tilted down so you can see, and walk on the edge of the road farthest from traffic.
- At night, wear light colors or reflective material.
- Put your phone and earphones away while walking.
- Children and elderly pedestrians should cross with an adult.

## 2. As a driver
- Check for bicycles, motorcycles, and following traffic before opening your door.
- Do not pass or overtake a vehicle that is letting passengers out.
- On roads with no separated sidewalk, keep to the edge.
- Leave generous clearance around pedestrians.

# If You Are in a Collision

1. Stop. Driving off - even as the party who was hit - can be mistaken for a hit-and-run.
2. Turn on your hazard lights and set a warning triangle 100 m behind you by day, 200 m by night.
3. Get people to safety first. On an expressway, do not stay in the car or on the shoulder - move beyond the guardrail.
4. If anyone is injured, you must report it to the police (112) at the scene. For property damage only, exchange details and file with your insurer.
5. While waiting for police or the insurer, photograph the scene.
   - Take wide shots that capture the crash position, both vehicles' tire marks, and the other plate together.
   - Shoot the damage itself from multiple angles and close up, several times.
6. If the car can be moved, shift it to the shoulder or another safe spot to prevent a second crash.
7. Do not move an injured person unnecessarily. Spinal injuries are not visible from outside. The exception is fire - then move them immediately.
8. If a hazardous-materials truck is involved, it can escalate badly. Leave the area at once.

---

# If Your Car Goes Into Water

1. Release your seat belt and shed shoes and heavy outerwear that would hinder swimming.
2. Take three or four deep breaths before you go, then hold one. You will last noticeably longer underwater.
3. Go out a window if it opens. If not, break a side window at its edge using an escape hammer or the metal stem of a headrest. The center of the glass resists far better.
4. If the door will not open, wait until the cabin has partly filled. Once the pressure inside and out equalizes, the door gives way.
5. Grab anything buoyant within reach on your way out.

> The windshield is laminated and extremely hard to break. Target a side window.

---

# If There Is an Accident Inside a Tunnel

1. Pull into the shoulder or an emergency bay, switch off the engine but leave the key in, so responders can move the car.
2. Report it by mobile phone or the tunnel's emergency telephone.
3. If the fire is small, try to knock it down with your in-car extinguisher or the tunnel's extinguisher or hydrant.
4. If it grows, evacuate away from the smoke, following the guide lights, either out of the tunnel or through a cross-passage into the parallel tunnel.
5. Smoke rises, so stay low and cover your nose and mouth as you move.

---

# Afterward

- Even if you feel fine, see a doctor that day or the next. Neck and back symptoms often surface late.
- Keep the medical report, repair estimate, scene photographs, and dashcam footage together.
- Treat unexpected calls asking for personal details during the claim as possible fraud; verify by calling your insurer's main number yourself.
