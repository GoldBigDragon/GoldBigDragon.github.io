# Preparing in Advance

## 1. Information and planning
- Receive alerts through TV, radio, the internet, and the [Safety Stepping Stone app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=kr.go.nema.disasteralert_new).
- Check your area's hazards - avalanche risk, structures liable to collapse - against past damage records.
- Teach children and vulnerable household members the [shelter locations](https://www.safekorea.go.kr/safekorea-kor/main/main.doidsiSFK/neo/sfk/cs/contents/civil_defense/SDIJKM1402.html) and how to get there, and agree on a reunion point.

## 2. Emergency supplies
- Gather first-aid supplies, a flashlight, water, food, a radio, a phone charger, a portable stove and fuel, and blankets in one place.
- Fill the bathtub in case the water supply stops, and ready a flashlight and batteries for the outage.
- Fill your fuel tank in advance; with no car, arrange a ride with a neighbor.
- Winter car kit: snow chains (or spray chains), sandbags, calcium chloride, a shovel.

## 3. Reinforcing structures
- Check the condition of construction sites and slopes in advance.
- Repair greenhouses and livestock barns that could collapse, and install support posts.
- Pull loose greenhouse straps taut.
- Remove in advance greenhouse sheeting, ginseng-house shade cloth, orchard bird netting, and insulation covers.
- Secure boats, nets, and gear, and insulate aquaculture facilities so fish do not freeze.

# During the Snow Advisory

## 1. On foot
- Stay in if you can, and if you go out, wear layers.
- Wear broad-soled sneakers or hiking boots.
- Keep your hands out of your pockets and wear insulated gloves. You need your hands free to catch yourself.
- Walk slowly, watching for snow falling from eaves and manholes and drains hidden under snow.
- Leave a little earlier than usual and take public transport instead of driving.
- Do not ride a bicycle or motorcycle.

## 2. Leaving unsafe buildings
- Aging or unpermitted houses, greenhouses used as homes, and temporary tents can collapse under snow load - move somewhere safe.
- In areas at risk of being cut off, maintain a contact line with police and local offices.
- Keep away from prefabricated panel structures (factories, market canopies, warehouses) and construction sites.
- If a building cracks, a column bows, or finish materials fall, get out at once and call 119.

## 3. Driving
- If you must drive, carry the winter kit, and before a long trip check fuel, food, and the forecast.
- Clear ice and snow off the car before driving. Snow blowing off your roof causes crashes behind you.
- Never pour hot water on the windshield. The glass will crack.
- Slow down and leave generous distance on curves, hill roads, overpasses, bridges, and icy sections.

### When and where the road freezes
- When: within about 24 hours of 1 cm or more of snow / at night and in the morning as the temperature drops (black ice)
- Where
  - Signalized intersections - repeated starting and stopping polishes the surface slick
  - On bridges - they freeze even when the rest of the road has not
  - Tunnel entrances and exits - shade creates localized ice

## 4. If your car is stranded
- Call 119 and wait in the car, following the situation on the radio.
- Stay with the vehicle if at all possible. If you must leave it, leave your contact number and the key in the car.
- Keep warm with your passengers and take turns resting.
- Clap your hands and move your arms and legs to keep circulation going.
- One person must stay awake at all times.
- At night, turn on the interior light or spread brightly colored clothing on the snow so rescuers can find you.
- If the exhaust pipe is buried in snow, carbon monoxide comes into the cabin. Clear the snow around it before running the engine, and keep a window cracked open.

## 5. Farms and fisheries
- Remove greenhouse sheeting, shade cloth, and bird netting in advance, and stay away from them during heavy snow.
- Insulate aquaculture ponds so fish do not freeze.
- Vessel crews should monitor the forecast and avoid sailing in bad weather; if they must sail, check lifesaving equipment first.

# After the Snow

## 1. Checking and reporting
- Confirm family and friends are safe; if someone is unreachable and may be missing, report it to the police.
- Check aging buildings for damage before entering.
- Photograph damaged property, then report it to your community service center or district office.
- If you are cut off, do not try to drive out - call 119 or 112, or ask for help nearby.

## 2. Snow clearing and secondary hazards
- Clear the walkway in front of your home or shop with family and neighbors to prevent slips on refrozen snow.
- Clear snow during daylight, with at least two people, and only once the work is safe. Do not climb onto roofs or otherwise overreach.
- Find the nearest grit box through the Public Data Portal listing [MOIS Snow-Removal Boxes](https://www.data.go.kr/data/15114125/standard.do).
- Street trees, aging buildings, and greenhouses heavy with snow can collapse - keep away.
- Cold usually follows snow and turns it to ice. Dress warmly and watch your footing.
- If gas or electricity was cut, use them only after inspection by Korea Gas Safety Corporation (1544-4500), Korea Electrical Safety Corporation (1588-7500), or a professional.
- Gas may have leaked: open the windows and ventilate thoroughly, and use no match or lighter until you have.
