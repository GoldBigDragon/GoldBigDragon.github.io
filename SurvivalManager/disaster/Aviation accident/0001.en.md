# Before You Board

- Leave prohibited items behind, and keep carry-on bags within the size and weight limits so they do not block an evacuation.
- Tell airline staff or cabin crew about any medical concern involving a child or elderly passenger before boarding.
- Sit in your assigned seat and do not swap. Crew need to know where passengers are for rescue to be fast.
- Statistically, passengers seated within five rows of an exit have better survival odds.
- If you are not in an exit row, count how many seat rows lie between you and the nearest exit. In heavy smoke you can feel your way along the seat backs and count.
- Cotton or wool clothing and low-heeled shoes serve you better in a fire and an evacuation than synthetics.

---

# In Flight

- Accidents cluster in the first 3 minutes after takeoff and the last 8 minutes before landing. During those windows, take your earphones out, keep your shoes on, and stay awake.
- Keep your seat belt fastened whenever you are seated, in case of turbulence.
- Watch the whole safety briefing and actually look at the card in the seat pocket.

---

# In an Emergency

## 1. Loss of cabin pressure - oxygen masks
- When a mask drops, put your own on first, then help children and elderly passengers.
- At cruising altitude you have only seconds of useful consciousness. Helping someone else first can cost you both.
- Pull the mask down to start the oxygen flow, and cover both nose and mouth.

## 2. The brace position
- Bring your seat back fully upright and pull the belt low and tight across your hips.
- Lean forward, press your head against your knees, and cup the back of your head with both hands.
- If you can reach the seat in front, place both hands on it and rest your forehead on them.

## 3. Evacuating
- Leave your belongings. The few seconds you spend retrieving a bag can trap everyone behind you.
- Remove high heels and sharp jewelry - they tear the evacuation slide.
- In smoke, stay low and move along the seat backs.
- Sit and slide - do not jump onto the slide - and move away from the aircraft immediately.

## 4. Landing on water
- Inflate your life vest only after you are outside the aircraft.
- Inflating it inside will pin you to the ceiling as the cabin floods, and you will not be able to reach the exit.

# If the Aircraft Is Hijacked

- Comply with the hijackers' instructions and avoid drawing attention to yourself.
- If they speak to you, answer quietly.
- If a rescue operation begins, stay face down. It is how the rescue team tells passengers from attackers.
- Remember details - the situation, the number of attackers, their weapons - and report them afterward.

---

# After an Accident

- Move at least 100 meters upwind of the aircraft to get clear of fuel fires and toxic smoke.
- Give first aid to the injured and call 119.
- Abroad, contact the Korean Ministry of Foreign Affairs Consular Call Center: 02-3210-0404 domestically, +82-2-3210-0404 from overseas.
