# What Is a Red Tide?

- A red tide is a rapid bloom of marine plankton that turns seawater red or brown.
- Harmful bloom species clog fish gills and can wipe out entire aquaculture pens.
- Korea issues red tide forecasts in escalating stages: Appearance Advisory → Red Tide Advisory → Red Tide Warning.
- Track conditions at the [NIFS Red Tide Information System](https://www.nifs.go.kr/board/actionRedtideInfoList.do).

---

# For the General Public

- Do not swim or play in the water in affected coastal areas.
- Never collect or eat fish found dead or floating in a red tide zone.
- Toxins can accumulate in shellfish, so obey any harvesting ban that is announced.
- If your skin develops a rash or other symptoms after contact, see a doctor.
- Seafood sold commercially is tested before it reaches market - don't act on rumors.

---

# For Fish Farmers - Before a Red Tide

## 1. All operations
- Check the Red Tide Information System and local government notices regularly.
- Stock up on algae-control material (yellow clay and similar) in advance.

## 2. Offshore cage farms
- Inspect liquid-oxygen systems, compressors, and microscopes.
- Ready the boats and spare nets you would need to relocate stock.

## 3. Land-based seawater farms
- Service and test red tide alarms, seawater filtration, oxygen generators, and liquid-oxygen supply units.
- Keep algae-control material staged near intake and discharge points.
- Store as much reserve rearing water as you can.

---

# For Fish Farmers - During a Red Tide

## 1. All operations
- Shift to emergency footing and keep gathering information.
- Apply algae-control material early if conditions call for it.

## 2. Offshore cage farms
- Concentrate agency and cooperative control vessels on the farm cluster.
- Spray algae-control material from control vessels once the bloom reaches the pens.
- Run liquid-oxygen systems and compressors.
- Depending on how the bloom develops, release stock early or move the pens out of the way.

## 3. Land-based seawater farms
- Sample and inspect the surrounding water frequently.
- Run alarms, filtration, oxygen generators, and liquid-oxygen supply.
- If a dense bloom arrives at night, stop water exchange and switch to emergency oxygen supply.
- Once the bloom hits, suspend feeding - digestion raises the fish's oxygen demand.

---

# After a Red Tide Passes

- Report fish kills and other losses to your city, county, or district office.
- Photograph the damage before anything is cleared. Those images support your compensation and recovery claim.
- Do not leave dead fish in place; dispose of them as local authorities direct to prevent secondary pollution.
