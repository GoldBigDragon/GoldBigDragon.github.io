# 6. Wildfires

## 6-1. If You Are at Home
- Shut off the gas and close every window and door.
- Pack your emergency supplies and a bag so you can leave at a moment's notice.
- Leave the lights on, inside and out. Firefighters can then find the house through smoke and darkness.
- Move flammable material away from the house, and wet the ground around it thoroughly against flying embers.
- Take down curtains and move flammable furniture to the middle of the room, away from windows and doors.
- Do not leave sprinklers or hoses running. It drops the water pressure the fire crews need.
- Check your area's emergency plan and your evacuation site and route.
- If an evacuation notice comes, leave at once - and check next door in case a neighbor has not heard.
- On the way out, use roads well away from the mountain and the fire's path.
- Even without an official notice, if you smell smoke or feel unsafe, move away from the mountain.

## 6-2. Livestock Barns
- Clear flammable material away from the barn and wet it down thoroughly.
- In case you have to leave the animals behind, set out plenty of water and feed.
- When you evacuate, leave the barn doors open and loosen the tethers.

## 6-3. If You Are Outdoors
- Check the situation through public announcements and your phone, and tell the people around you.
- Stop hiking or camping and move away from the mountain, avoiding the fire's path.
- Coming down, stay out of valleys and conifer stands. Valleys channel wind and accelerate fire; conifers are resinous and burn ferociously.
- If you are camping, use roads well away from the mountain.
- If you are cut off, put the wind at your back, clear away the leaf litter and branches around you, and lie flat and low.

## 6-4. After the Fire Has Passed
- Stay away from hot ash, burned trees, charred debris, and live embers.
- The ground can hold pockets of heat that cause burns and reignition.
- Confirm with the authorities whether it is safe to return and whether the tap water is safe to drink.

# 7. Everyday Preparation

## 7-1. Building and Equipment
- Know where the extinguishers and hydrants are and how to use them.
- In any building you enter, find the emergency exit first.
- Keep exits and escape routes clear of stored objects.
- Fit smoke alarms and carbon monoxide detectors in the middle of each floor and outside sleeping areas.

## 7-2. Electricity and Ignition Sources
- Keep dust away from flammable materials and electrical equipment.
- Do not draw more than a power strip's rated current.
- Replace any cable with damaged insulation immediately.
- Never use heating appliances next to flammables, and always switch them off when you leave the room.
- During a blackout, light with flashlights only. No candles - they start fires.

## 7-3. Preventing Wildfires
- Stay out of areas closed to entry.
- Do not burn anything near forest, cook and camp only where it is permitted, and always confirm the fire is out afterwards.
- Keep the ground within 2 m of the house in non-combustible material - stone, sand, or soil.
- Do not grow large plants or shrubs right by the windows.
- Clear leaves and twigs off roofs and decks regularly.
- Store combustibles - rubbish, hay, building materials - at least 1.5 m away.
- Keep gas, oil, propane, and yard waste out of the first 9 m of the yard, and thin the vegetation and prune the trees from 9 to 30 m.
- Use fire-resistant building materials where you can, and keep an outside water source with a hose that reaches every part of the yard.

## 7-4. People
- Agree in advance on your contact list, evacuation site and route, and who does what.
- Practice first aid repeatedly.
- Keep emergency supplies and make sure everyone knows where they are and how to use them.
  - Food: water, canned goods, instant noodles - anything edible without heating
  - Medicine: ointment, cold medicine, antacids, any prescriptions you take
  - Daily items: a change of clothes, tissue, wet wipes, a lighter, sanitary products, plastic bags
  - Other: radio, flashlight and batteries, phone, power bank, some cash, contact list
- Keep a good supply of any medication you take, and have mobility aids - cane, wheelchair - ready in advance.
