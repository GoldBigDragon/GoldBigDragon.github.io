# Warning Signs

- The sea suddenly draws back from the shore. If the water pulls out far enough to expose the seabed, run immediately.
- A roaring sound like an approaching train comes from the ocean.
- If you feel an earthquake while on the coast, a tsunami may follow even if no warning has been issued.
- But a tsunami can also arrive with no warning sign at all. After a strong quake, people in low-lying coastal areas should evacuate whether or not an alert has been issued.

---

# On Land

## 1. Move immediately
- Get away from the shore and from river mouths as fast as you can, allowing for traffic congestion, and head for high ground.
- If your area has a designated tsunami evacuation site, go there.
- Shout to alert the people around you.

## 2. If there is no time to reach high ground
- Climb to the third floor or higher of a reinforced-concrete building, or to a hill at least 10 meters above sea level.
- Avoid wooden and low-rise buildings - they can be destroyed.

## 3. What never to do
- Do not go back for family members or valuables. Turning back is the single most common way people die in a tsunami.
- Do not go and watch the wave. A tsunami is hard to see coming; by the time it is visible, it is too late.

---

# At Sea

- Fishing vessels that have time should move to deep water well offshore.
- Inside harbors the wave height builds and the current accelerates sharply - that is where boats are most at risk.
- Once the vessel is secured, anyone who is ashore should head for high ground together with their crewmates.

---

# How a Tsunami Behaves

- Its wavelength is enormous, so waves arrive repeatedly, 5 to 10 minutes apart.
- The first wave is not the biggest. Often the second or third is higher.
- The danger lasts for hours. Do not go back down until the warning is officially lifted.
- A tsunami wraps around landmasses, so a shore that does not face the source is not safe either.
- It also runs up rivers and travels fast inland. Riverbanks are dangerous too.

---

# After a Tsunami

- Floating debris can strike oil tanks and other facilities and start fires.
- Damage to coastal industrial complexes can release hazardous chemicals. Keep listening to the radio.
- Return home or enter flooded buildings only after authorities declare it safe.
