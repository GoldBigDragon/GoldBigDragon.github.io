# Two Things to Remember First

> The moment water begins covering the floor of an underground space, or backs up out of a drain, leave.
> Once the water reaches knee height, the pressure holds the door shut. Hesitate and you will not be able to get out.

> Turn around on a flooded road.
> Fifteen centimeters of moving water is enough to knock a person off their feet; thirty will float a car. Muddy water hides its own depth - and an open manhole beneath it.

# Preparing in Advance

## 1. Information and planning
- Set up TV, radio, internet, and the [Safety Stepping Stone app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=kr.go.nema.disasteralert_new) so disaster alerts reach you.
- Find out your neighborhood's flood history and risk factors.
- Make an evacuation plan with family and neighbors, and teach children the shelter locations (community center, school) and the route.
  - Avoid on the route: riverbanks, mountain paths, the area around utility poles and transformers, and anything already flooded.
- Agree on a reunion point in case the family is separated.

## 2. Inspecting your property
- Clean the drains and gutters around your home and clear any blockage.
- Check slopes, retaining walls, and embankments for cracks, and report anything dangerous to your local office.

## 3. Emergency supplies
- Stock at least three days of food, water, and essentials against loss of power, gas, and water.
- Gather first-aid supplies, a flashlight, radio, phone charger, portable stove, and blanket in one place, in a pack you can carry.
- Fill the bathtub in case the water supply is cut.
- Fill your fuel tank in advance; if you have no car, arrange a ride with a neighbor.

# When a Heavy Rain Advisory Is Issued

## 1. Get out of the risk areas
- Stay in and check the weather often.
- If you are in the mountains, a valley, near a river, or on a breakwater, move to safety with the people around you.
- Stop hiking, camping, swimming, and fishing immediately.
- If you are in a low-lying or repeatedly flooded area, a landslide zone, an underground space, or an aging building, move somewhere safe.
- Check on elderly neighbors, children, and people with disabilities, and tell them how to evacuate.

## 2. Protect property and vehicles
- Place sandbags and flood barriers at underground garages and other flood-prone entrances.
- Move cars parked near rivers, beaches, or in low ground.
  - When an evacuation advisory is issued, riverside parking lots must be cleared - and leave your phone number on the car.
- Moor boats securely or haul them ashore, and move nets and gear.
- Clear farm channels and adjust water gates only before the rain starts (at the pre-advisory stage).

# During the Advisory

## 1. Do not go near
- Flooded roads, underpasses, and bridges are closed to both people and vehicles. Warn others too.
- Get quickly away from valleys, streams, riverbanks, and shorelines where a torrent can sweep you off.
- Keep away from construction sites, streetlights, traffic signals, utility poles, and underground spaces.
- Do not go out to check on paddy embankments or water gates.
- If you are in a mobile home or temporary structure, move to a solid building now.
- In a landslide zone, evacuate on your own judgment if you see the warning signs, even with no alert issued.

## 2. Preventing secondary damage
- Shut off the gas in advance, and never touch electrical equipment with wet hands.
- In a blackout, use a flashlight or your phone, not candles.
- If any water enters the building, move to higher ground immediately.
- Evacuate together with vulnerable people nearby.

## 3. If your car floods
- When the water rises, abandon the car and get out. A vehicle is replaceable.
- If the door will not open, go out a window. If the window will not open, break a side window at its edge with an escape hammer or a headrest stem.
- If the door is stuck, wait until the cabin has partly filled - once the pressure equalizes, push it open.

# After the Rain

- Confirm that family and friends are safe; if someone is unreachable and may be missing, report it to the police.
- Stay out of damaged areas.
- Before re-entering your home, check that it is structurally safe. If you smell gas, see structural cracks, or find warped door frames, do not go in - have it inspected.
- Report damaged property (housing, water and sewer lines, retaining walls, roads) to your city, county, or district office.
- Pump a flooded basement out slowly - about a third of the depth per day. Draining it all at once lets the saturated ground outside collapse the walls and buckle the floor.
- Discard all food and drinking water that floodwater has touched.
- Wear boots, rubber gloves, and safety goggles when you clean up, and wash your hands with soap afterward.
