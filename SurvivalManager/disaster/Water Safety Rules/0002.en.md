# 6. The Sea and Swimming Beaches

## 6-1. Before You Get In
- Low and high tide usually turn every six hours, and with them the current, the waves, and the shape of the bottom change sharply. Know the tide times in advance.
- If the waves look wrong, do not go in.

| Type | What it is | The danger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Swell | A wave that rolls in calmly from open sea and suddenly rears up in the shallows | It drags people out |
| Rip current | A strong seaward flow beneath the surface | It pulls you out to sea in seconds |

## 6-2. If You Go Under
- Do not flail - let the waves carry you. Take a deep breath, hold it, and you will rise naturally.
- If a heavy wave pushes you out, do not fight it - swim at an angle toward sand or gravel shore.
- The deeper you dive when a big wave breaks over you, the safer you are.

## 6-3. If a Rip Current Takes You
> Swimming straight back against the flow will empty your strength first.
> Drift with it until you are clear of the current, then, out of the rip, swim in at 45 degrees to the beach or perpendicular to it.

## 6-4. If Weeds Tangle You
- Struggling wraps them tighter.
- Work your arms and legs loose slowly, then move vertically and swim steadily out.
- If there is a current, let it carry you and wait quietly. As the weeds loosen, shake them off and surface.

## 6-5. If a Jellyfish Stings You
- If you see jellyfish, get out of the water at once.
- Dead jellyfish still sting. Always wear footwear on the beach.
- Remove any jellyfish stuck to skin with chopsticks or gloves. Never rub it with your hand.
1. Rinse thoroughly with seawater or sterile saline to remove the stingers. (No tap water.)
2. Scrape away what remains with tweezers, a shell, a card, or the blunt edge of a knife.
3. Whether heat or cold helps depends on the person and the species, so rinse and then go to the nearest hospital.

# 7. Fishing Spots

## 7-1. On Arrival
- Check the weather beforehand.
- Locate the safety equipment and wear a life jacket.
- Do not fish outside designated spots - not on rock ledges, tetrapods, or reservoirs.
- Tell family or a friend where you are. If someone fishing cannot be reached and may be missing, report it to the nearest police station.

## 7-2. If You Fall In
- Relax completely, keep your chest full of air, and lie back. You will keep breathing even with the body partly submerged.
- If you can swim, do not fight the waves - angle in toward shore.
- In heavy seas or a storm surge, move away from shore instead. Trying to land means being thrown against rock. Hold a survival float, conserve energy, and wait for rescue.

# 8. Ice

## 8-1. When Never to Go Out
- Stay off the ice during the freeze-up (December to early January) and the thaw (mid-February to early March).
- February and March especially - the ice is melting, and however solid it looks you will go through.
- Ice thins toward the middle of a river. Do not head for the center.

## 8-2. Reading the Ice
| Sign | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Struck, it feels like stone and rings clear and high | Thick ice |
| Struck, it gives a dull, low thud and feels soft | Do not step on it |
| Clear-colored ice | Strong |
| Cloudy ice | Layered frozen snow - full of voids and weak |

- As a rule, ice must be at least 10 cm thick to hold one or two people who are not standing together.

## 8-3. If the Ice Feels Wrong Underfoot
- If it feels spongy or starts to crack, turn back the way you came and retreat slowly.
- If it may split, lie down slowly to spread your weight, and roll sideways like a log out of danger.
- Never go under such ice, not even to dive.

## 8-4. If You Break Through
- Do not fight your way up onto the ice.
- Kick your legs as if swimming to float your body, then work for a chance to get up onto the sheet.
- Once out, strip off all wet clothing and change into dry, and take warm drinks. Heat escapes fastest from the head, neck, chest, and groin.

# 9. Snail Gathering and Tetrapods

## 9-1. Gathering Freshwater Snails
- Go in groups of two or more.
- Watch for slips, and do not gather at night.
- Always wear a life jacket, even in shallow water.
- Block the sun with a hat, arm sleeves, and sunscreen.
- Do not wear chest waders. Once water gets inside them, you cannot stand back up.
- When straightening up after bending, raise your head slowly to avoid dizziness.

## 9-2. If You Slip Off a Tetrapod
> Tetrapods are tall and slick - you cannot climb back up by grabbing them.
> Forcing it means scrapes and cuts, and osmotic pressure keeps them bleeding, which can lead to dangerous blood loss.

- Report through the Haerode app or 119.
- The waves can throw you against the concrete, so protect yourself from injury and keep warm until rescuers arrive.
