# 1. The Basics of Water Play

## 1-1. Before You Get In
- Warm up properly and put on a life jacket before entering.
- At beaches and rivers, stay inside the designated safe area.
- Never swim after drinking.
- Children must enter only with an adult.
- To avoid cardiac arrest, check the water temperature and enter slowly.
- If you hear thunder, get out of the water immediately. Water is a target for lightning.

## 1-2. When Something Goes Wrong
1. Shout for help and call 119.
2. Do not jump in without thinking. The most common tragedy is the rescuer drowning alongside the victim.
3. However strong a swimmer you are, use the ring buoy, life jacket, or rescue rope on site. A long branch or a rope will do.
4. If the person is unconscious, begin rescue breathing and CPR.

# 2. If You Go Under - the Back Float

> It applies in every water emergency below.

1. Do not panic - take a deep breath in.
2. Look straight up at the sky, tilt your chin up and your head back until your ears are fully submerged.
3. Straighten your chest and lower back to keep the body horizontal, and let every muscle go slack.
4. Stretch your arms overhead, and bend your legs slightly and spread them wide below you to maximize the surface touching the water.

- Thrashing sinks you and exhausts you. Stay calm, conserve energy, and wait for rescue.
- With no one to help and no swimming ability, stay floating on your back and paddle with your hands only, moving slowly toward shore.
- Grab any structure or floating object - wood, styrofoam - to support yourself.

# 3. Mountain Streams

## 3-1. Before You Get In
- Check whether your phone has signal.
- Rain raises a mountain stream in moments. Check the forecast, and stay out if rain is coming.
- Even after the rain, there is rockfall and landslide risk - avoid swimming and camping.
- Locate rescue tubes and other safety gear, and look for the depth limit line set by the site manager.
- Sound the depth with a long branch, and read the current from drifting leaves and grass.
- If the water is too murky to judge depth, or fast enough to foam like a waterfall, do not go in.
- Water shoes prevent slips.

> Never dive.
> The bottom is shallow and uneven, so you can strike rock, and even deep spots can hold invisible whirlpools.

## 3-2. If You Go Under
- Hold the back float and conserve your strength.
- If you are swept along, do not fight the current. Drift with it while moving diagonally toward the nearest bank.
- Point your feet downstream so rocks and trees do not hit your head.
- In a deep pool where the current is weak, a confident swimmer can check the depth, then dive, push off the bottom, surface to catch a breath, and alternate back floating and kicking toward the shallows.

# 4. Reservoirs

## 4-1. When You Are Nearby
- Do not walk around a reservoir alone. Nobody passes by, so rescue is extremely difficult if you fall in.
- Watch your footing and stay away from the waterline. Be especially careful at reservoirs with no fence.
- If you must enter, wear protective gear.

## 4-2. If You Fall In, or See Someone Fall In
- If it is you, hold the back float and wait for rescue or make your way out slowly.
- If you see someone go in, shout to alert everyone nearby.
- Do not jump in on impulse. Use the rescue box, a long branch, or a rope, or call the fire or police station.

# 5. Tidal Flats

## 5-1. Before You Go Out
- Check the incoming tide, and do not go out within two hours of it.
- Never go alone.
- Always wear a life jacket, and take extra care if you wear chest waders.
- Enter and leave only by the designated path.
- Do not approach or cross a tidal channel (the drainage cut the water runs out through).
- If sea fog rolls in, get out at once, whatever the tide table says.

## 5-2. If You Lose Your Way
- Use the map or compass app on your phone to fix a direction.
- With no tools, read the ripple marks in the mud. Walking at right angles to the ripples, on the side with the gentler slope, leads you toward land.

## 5-3. If You Get Stuck in the Mud
- Sunk below the knee: pull out the leg opposite your center of gravity first and kneel, then free the other foot and crawl out on your knees.
- Sunk above the knee: work your legs back and forth to open a gap, then lie back, pull out one leg at a time, and crawl out on your stomach.
- If you cannot free yourself or are cut off, send an SOS from the Haerode app or call 119 at once.
- While you wait, mark your position with a whistle, lantern, or phone, and keep your body warm.
