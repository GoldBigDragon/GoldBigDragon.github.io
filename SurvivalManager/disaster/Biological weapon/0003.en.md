# 8. Food and Water

## 8-1. Under Biological Attack
- Eat only what is completely sealed - bottled water, canned goods.
- Most biological weapon pathogens are killed by heat. Unsealed water and food should be heated above 100°C for at least 15 minutes.
- Do not drink tap water until it is confirmed safe.

## 8-2. During an Epidemic
> After a major disaster, waterborne diseases such as cholera spread, and many die of dehydration from diarrhea or of the infection itself.

- Eat and drink only what is clean, and never eat spoiled food. In a disaster, throw out anything you suspect without hesitation.
- Water collected outside can carry waterborne disease and parasites. Filter it and boil it before drinking.

## 8-3. Food Safety
- Wash your hands before and after preparing food.
- Store raw and cooked food separately and prevent cross-contamination.
- Seal leftovers and reheat them thoroughly.

## 8-4. Livestock and Pets
- When a pet has been outside, wash it thoroughly, and wear gloves when handling its waste.
- If a pet shows unusual symptoms, consult a vet and isolate it if you can.
- Report mass die-offs or unusual symptoms in livestock to the authorities immediately. Mass livestock deaths are one of the signs of a biological attack, so speed matters.

# 9. Hygiene and Cleanliness

## 9-1. Personal
- Carry hand sanitizer or gloves when you go out.
- Wash your hands with soap when you get back.
- Maintain oral hygiene with regular brushing or mouthwash.
- Waterless shampoo and body wash save water.
- When water is short, wet wipes can clean your body or substitute for washing up.

## 9-2. Environment
- Keep your living space clean and disinfect it regularly.
- Ultraviolet light in sunshine kills many pathogens. Air bedding in the sun often.
- Stock plenty of plastic bags, disposable gloves, tissue, and a container for waste and toileting.
- Waste collection may stop. Seal rubbish and manage it hygienically.

## 9-3. The Isolation Room
- If a family member is infected or suspected, isolate them in a separate room if you can.
- Give them their own towels, dishes, and bedding, and disinfect the door handle and surfaces regularly.
- When caring for them, wear a mask and gloves, and always wash your hands afterwards.
- Seal their waste and used tissues in plastic bags for disposal.

## 9-4. Dishes and Washing Up
- Keep disposable plates and cutlery in case you cannot wash up.
- In an emergency, line dishes with cling film, a plastic bag, or foil, or cut a plastic bottle in half and use that.
- Never share dishes with an infected person, and wash anything they have used thoroughly in hot water with detergent.

## 9-5. Laundry
- Wash an infected person's clothes, bedding, and towels separately in hot water, and dry them in direct sunlight if you can.
- Wear gloves when handling the laundry, and do not shake or flap it while carrying it.

## 9-6. Bathroom and Waste
- Disinfect the toilet, washbasin, and taps regularly.
- Always close the lid before flushing.
- Seal used masks, tissues, and gloves in a plastic bag before disposal.
- Double-bag rubbish containing an infected person's items so no one else comes into contact with it.

# 10. If You Become Infected

## 10-1. Self-Isolation
- At the first suspicion, stay in and avoid all contact with others.
- Isolate in a separate room and minimize contact even with family.

## 10-2. Medical Advice
> Call before you go to a hospital in person - it prevents the infection spreading inside the hospital.

- Contact the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (1339) or your local health center for guidance.
- Give your symptoms, contact history, and movements accurately.
- Follow official guidance to get tested and receive vaccination or other treatment.

## 10-3. Managing Symptoms
- Take your temperature regularly and record how symptoms change.
- Keep fluids up and rest.
- If symptoms worsen, contact a medical facility immediately.
- Persistent high fever or difficulty breathing is an emergency. Call 119, and tell them on the phone that an infectious disease is suspected.

# 11. Government Guidance and Psychological Care

## 11-1. Keep Listening
- Follow government guidance on TV, radio, and civil defense alert messages.
- Confirm the disease, its transmission route, prevention, treatment, vaccination schedule and location, and isolation rules through official guidance.

## 11-2. Vaccination
- Once the pathogen is identified and a vaccine exists, the government announces the schedule and locations.
- When your date and place are set, go promptly as instructed.

## 11-3. Movement Restrictions
> Breaking a movement restriction for your own convenience spreads the infection to more people.

If the government imposes movement restrictions or quarantine, comply.

## 11-4. Rationing
- In a severe epidemic, broken logistics may force rationing of essentials.
- Cooperate with it, and keep disaster supplies at home in normal times so you do not need to hoard.

## 11-5. Anxiety and Stress
- Keep a regular routine, and talk to family and friends - it helps.
- Watching too much news deepens anxiety. Check what you need and do not immerse yourself further.

## 11-6. Solidarity
- Cooperate with neighbors, share what each needs, and check on older people and those living alone. A community response matters.
- Even in isolation, keep social connection through phone calls, video calls, and messaging.
