# 5. Preparing in Advance

## 5-1. Face Masks
Masks are the most basic protection against both epidemics and biological weapons.

- Where airborne transmission is likely, use a KF94 mask or a class 1 respirator, not an ordinary mask.
- Wear it so it covers your nose and mouth fully and extends below the chin, with no gaps for air to leak in.
- Two or more per person per day may be consumed. Stock enough for the whole family.
- A serious, prolonged epidemic can consume hundreds.

## 5-2. Hygiene Supplies
- Stock plenty of soap, detergent, and hand sanitizer.
- Keep disposable gloves (plastic or latex) to avoid contact with contaminated material.

## 5-3. Food and Water
> A serious epidemic breaks the logistics system.
> Even where logistics hold, going out may be dangerous or movement restricted, making it hard to buy food.

- Keep sealed bottled water.
- Stock food that needs no cooking - ready meals and canned goods above all.

## 5-4. Medicines
> If the health service is overwhelmed by an epidemic, you cannot get treatment for anything else either.

- Over-the-counter items to keep at home: antiseptic (povidone iodine, hydrogen peroxide), antibiotic ointment, thermometer, antipyretic painkillers, antacids, anti-diarrheal, cold medicine, pain patches, antiparasitics, antihistamines.
- Anyone with a chronic condition needs spare essential medication and a prescription (or a copy). With a prescription, obtaining more is far easier if you run out.

## 5-5. Gas Mask and Air Purifier
- In a biological attack, wear a KF94 mask or a class 1 or better respirator and stay indoors.
- For higher protection, a KS M 6685 certified gas mask is worth having.
- If you have a HEPA air purifier, check that it is ready to run. HEPA filters are effective at trapping fine biological particles.

> But never run an air purifier during a chemical attack.
> Only run it when a biological attack has been confirmed.

## 5-6. Communication Plan
- Have a plan for checking on family during an epidemic.
- As it worsens you must minimize face-to-face contact, so check in by phone, text, or messenger.

# 6. Under Biological Attack

## 6-1. Protect Your Airway and Stay Indoors
1. Close the windows and switch off ventilation immediately.
2. Wear a KF94 mask or a class 1 or better respirator and stay indoors. A gas mask is safer still.
3. Avoid all contact with contaminated material and with patients, protect your airway with a mask or handkerchief, and leave a contaminated area quickly.

## 6-2. Run the Air Purifier
- If you have a HEPA purifier, turn it on. It filters most biological particles and improves the safety of indoor air.
- This applies only when a biological attack has been confirmed. In a chemical attack it may draw outside air in - establish what has happened before deciding.

## 6-3. Stay Put
- Keep outdoor activity to a minimum and do not travel to other areas.
- People in a contaminated area should go to a designated shelter or home.
- Indoors, keep yourself clean and follow government and local guidance.

> Uncontrolled movement widens the infection enormously.

## 6-4. Insects
- Some pathogens are carried by mosquitoes, fleas, and other insects.
- Check your window screens, use insect repellent, and wear long clothing to minimize exposed skin.

## 6-5. Suspicious Substances and Mail
- If a suspicious liquid or powder leaks from an item, cover it with plastic or wrapping to stop it spreading.
- Do not touch it or smell it - leave the space immediately.
- Do not open suspicious mail. Store it somewhere isolated, then report it promptly to your local health center or the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (1339).

> Anthrax has actually been dispersed through the mail.
> Be especially careful with mail that looks or smells unusual, or has no clear sender.

- If you have been exposed, record the details of the people around you for contact tracing and containment, pass them to the authorities, and isolate those contacts in a room.

# 7. During an Epidemic

## 7-1. Information
- Establish the route, symptoms, and incubation period from government announcements and the news.
- Do not be swayed by rumors on social media, and do not pass unverified information on.

## 7-2. Avoiding Contact
- Stay away from supermarkets, public transport, cinemas and other crowded places where you can.
- Avoid direct contact with patients, and if a family member is a suspected case, isolate them in a separate room.
- If you are ill yourself, stay home and do not come into contact with others.

## 7-3. Masks and Hygiene
- In crowded places, and above all when visiting a hospital, always wear a mask and minimize exposed skin.
- Keep your hands away from your eyes, nose, and mouth - exposed mucous membranes are the easiest route in. If you touch your face unconsciously, make a deliberate effort to stop.
- After touching elevator buttons, door handles, toilets, shopping carts and other shared surfaces, sanitize or wash your hands at once.
- After being out, always wash your hands with soap for at least 20 seconds.

## 7-4. Disinfection
> Some pathogens are not adequately killed by ordinary ethanol sanitizer.
> Effective disinfection differs by disease - follow the specific guidance the government issues.

## 7-5. Cough Etiquette
- Even if you are not sure you are infected, cough away from other people and into a mask, your arm, or a tissue.
- Covering with your hand puts the pathogen on your hand and then on every surface you touch. The inside of your elbow is safer.
- If you used a tissue, throw it away immediately and wash your hands.

## 7-6. Keeping Your Strength Up
A weakened immune system leaves you vulnerable. Regular meals, fluids, enough sleep, appropriate exercise, and managing stress all matter.
