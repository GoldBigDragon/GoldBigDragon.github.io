# 1. What Makes Biological Weapons Different

## 1-1. They Are Extremely Hard to Detect
> The most dangerous thing about a biological weapon is that you cannot tell you have been attacked.

- Chemical and nuclear weapons announce themselves with explosions and smoke. A biological release is usually invisible and odorless at the moment it happens.
- Days to weeks can pass between release and the first patients, through the incubation period - and infection spreads widely in that time.

## 1-2. Incubation Amplifies the Spread
- Depending on the pathogen, incubation runs from as little as one day to several weeks.
- During incubation the infected person has no symptoms and carries on normally, passing the pathogen on as they go.
- That is why a biological attack is so often caught late - by the time it is found, a great many people may already be infected.

# 2. Principal Pathogens

Symptoms vary between individuals and can appear within hours in the fastest cases.

| Pathogen | Incubation | Symptoms and risk |
| --- | :---: | --- |
| Viral hemorrhagic fever | Unknown | High fever, muscle pain, diarrhea, chest pain, hemorrhage. In severe cases about 90% die within a week |
| Ricin | Within 24 hours | Vomiting, diarrhea, difficulty breathing, pulmonary edema. Heavy exposure can kill within 3 days |
| Smallpox | Within 3 days | Pustules, high fever, fatigue. In severe cases about 30% die within 2 weeks |
| Botulism | Within 3 days | Difficulty breathing, muscle paralysis. Severe cases can kill within a day |
| Tularemia | Within 5 days | High fever, chills, muscle pain, swollen lymph nodes, skin ulcers. Can progress to pneumonia and respiratory failure. Highly infectious but almost never transmitted person to person |
| Cholera | Within 5 days | Diarrhea, vomiting, dehydration. Untreated, severe dehydration kills; with proper fluid therapy the fatality rate drops sharply. High risk of waterborne transmission |
| Anthrax | Within 6 days | Persistent cough, difficulty breathing, muscle paralysis. Severe cases can kill within 2 days |
| Plague | Within 6 days | High fever, difficulty breathing, headache, cough. Severe cases can kill within 4 days |
| Marburg | Within 10 days | High fever, chills, muscle pain, rash, hemorrhage. Death from bleeding and organ failure |
| Nipah virus | Within 2 weeks | Fever, headache, drowsiness, coma, encephalitis. Death from central nervous system involvement. Person-to-person transmission possible in some cases |
| Ebola | Within 3 weeks | High fever, muscle pain, vomiting, diarrhea, hemorrhage. Death from multi-organ failure and massive bleeding. Transmitted by contact with body fluids |
| Q fever | Within 3 weeks | High fever, severe headache, muscle pain, pneumonia or hepatitis. If it becomes chronic, complications such as endocarditis. Airborne transmission possible |
| Brucellosis | Within 4 weeks | Fever, sweating, fatigue, joint and muscle pain. If chronic, it involves the liver, spleen, heart and other organs, causing prolonged debility. Low fatality but lasting loss of function |
| Hantavirus | Within 5 weeks | Fever, muscle pain, cough, then acute respiratory failure or hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome. Death from rapid pulmonary edema and hypoxia |

> Sarin is not a biological weapon - it is a chemical nerve agent. For sarin and other chemical agents, see the Chemical Weapons document.

# 3. Signs of an Attack

Suspect a biological attack if you see any of these.

- Large numbers of acute cases with similar symptoms
- A disease that does not fit the region or the season
- Animals or livestock dying en masse with no clear cause

> Even before any government announcement, take personal protective measures at once and wait for official guidance.

# 4. How Infections Spread

Transmission routes vary with the pathogen.

- Airborne droplets (coughing, sneezing)
- Direct contact (physical contact with a patient)
- Vectors (mosquitoes, fleas, and other insects)
- Ingestion (contaminated water or food)
- Blood

When an epidemic begins, use government announcements and the news to establish the route, symptoms, and incubation period, and take the precautions that match the route.

> Many viruses and biological weapon pathogens still have no vaccine and no treatment.
> Prevention is the best response - not becoming infected is what matters most.
