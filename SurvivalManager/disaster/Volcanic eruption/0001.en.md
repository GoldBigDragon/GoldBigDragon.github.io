# Volcanic Hazards

| | What it is and why it is dangerous |
| --- | --- |
| Volcanic ash | Fine powder thrown out by the eruption. It blankets the area and pollutes the air, blocks sunlight enough to cause abnormal cooling, grounds aircraft through ash clouds, and can trigger widespread power failures |
| Pyroclastic material | Solid fragments, large and small, blasted from the vent |
| Lahar (volcanic mudflow) | Water mixed with pyroclastic debris racing downhill. It causes landslides and floods and reshapes the terrain |
| Lava | Molten magma flowing across the surface. It buries land and starts wildfires |

> Most volcanic damage comes from ash.

## Korea's active volcanoes
Geologists classify a volcano as active if it has erupted within the last 10,000 years.
- Mount Paektu - the highest peak on the peninsula, with the caldera lake Cheonji at its summit. Major eruptions in 946–947; most recent in 1903
- Mount Halla - with the crater lake Baengnokdam at its summit. Eruptions recorded in 1002 and 1007
- Ulleungdo - a volcanic island built up by lava from the sea floor. Over the past 20,000 years it has erupted roughly every 3,000–7,000 years

# Preparing in Advance

Continuous ashfall can keep you indoors for hours or days.
- Dust mask and protective goggles (KF94 or better helps)
- At least three days of drinking water and ready-to-eat food
- Radio and batteries
- Tape and plastic wrap (to seal window gaps and protect electronics)
- Portable lamp or flashlight
- Heater and fuel, blankets and clothing
- Medicine and a first-aid kit
- Broom, shovel, and other cleaning tools
- Some cash
- In-car emergency supplies
- Bags for disposing of ash

---

# Escaping the Area Around the Vent

- A volcano can erupt with no warning at all.
- An eruption throws rock, large and small, that can strike and kill directly.
- If you see smoke near the vent, hear rumbling from underground, or notice an unusual smell, leave for a safe area at once and notify the police.
- Get away from the vent as fast as you can, and take cover in the nearest shelter or behind rock.
- Volcanic gas is heavier than air and pools in valleys. Avoid narrow, low ground.
- Wear a helmet and goggles and cover your mouth with a mask or wet towel.
- A pyroclastic flow - a cloud of ash and gas - is extremely hot, extremely fast, and travels far. Escape is very hard: get out by vehicle, immediately.
- A lahar runs down valleys. Move to high ground away from the valley floor.
- Follow emergency broadcasts on your phone or radio and do what they instruct.

# Before the Ash Arrives

- If you are outside, get into a building quickly.
- Seal door gaps and vents with damp towels, and tape the windows shut.
- People with chronic bronchitis, emphysema, or asthma should stay indoors.
- Disconnect downspouts and drainpipes from the roof gutter so drains do not clog.
- If you use collected rainwater for supply, disconnect the pipe between the collector and the tank.
- Remove contact lenses - ash can damage the cornea.
- Wrap electronics in plastic film to keep ash out.
- Listen to emergency broadcasts and learn the response procedures.

---

# While Ash Is Falling

- Stay indoors if at all possible and follow emergency broadcasts.
- If you are outdoors, get into a car or building quickly.
- Cover your nose and mouth with a mask, handkerchief, or clothing. A KF94 or KF80 mask helps.
- Do not drink water that ash has fallen into.
- Wash vegetables thoroughly before eating.
- If your eyesight is poor, wear glasses rather than contacts, and use sealed goggles when the ash is heavy.
- Acid rain is possible - do not let the rain fall on you.
- Ash accumulating on a roof can collapse the building. Clear it at an appropriate point.
- Avoid driving. Ash causes breakdowns and crashes. If you must, use headlights and drive slowly.

# After the Ashfall - Cleaning Up

## 1. General
- Wear goggles and a mask and clean indoors, outdoors, and your car promptly. With no mask, use a wet cloth.
- Change out of the clothes you wore outside and wash yourself thoroughly.
- Dampen the ash lightly with water or use a wet mop so it does not become airborne again.
- Put collected ash in sturdy plastic bags and take it to a designated disposal point.

## 2. Outdoors
- Keep ash out of buildings, machinery, vehicles, and water and sewer systems.
- Keep machinery switched off or sealed until all the ash has been cleared.
- Clean roof ash with water at an appropriate time.
- Wet the lawn before mowing it.
- Shovel off ash more than 1 cm deep.
- Dampen with sprinklers before cleaning.
- Keep ash out of drains and sewers.
- Too much water turns ash into a hard mass - be careful.

## 3. Indoors
- Open all doors and windows to ventilate.
- Cut power to appliances before cleaning them.
- Clean carpets, furniture, and appliances with a vacuum or damp cloth. Take care not to raise dust when emptying the vacuum.
- Replace filters in air conditioners and heaters.
- Do not use brush-head floor vacuums, ventilation fans, or clothes dryers.
- Shake ash out of clothing before washing, and use more detergent than usual.

## 4. Vehicles
- Seal the air intake and electronic components before cleaning.
- Wipers scratch glass - use plenty of washer fluid.
- Change engine oil, the oil filter, and the air filter frequently.
- Clean the interior and also the engine bay and trunk. Brushing scratches the paint - take care.

## 5. Why cleaning is essential
- Ash penetrates televisions, computers, and cameras and causes unrepairable failures.
- The volcanic glass in ash generates static electricity, which can destroy electronics or start a fire.
- In dry air, wind and passing vehicles lift ash back into the air, harming health.
- Ash can drift for months or years after an eruption, so it settles again even where you have cleaned. Cleaning has to continue.
