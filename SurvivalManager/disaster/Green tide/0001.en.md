# What Is an Algal Bloom?

- A "green tide" is a rapid explosion of blue-green algae (cyanobacteria) in rivers and lakes that turns the water a murky green.
- Some of these algae release toxins such as microcystin that are harmful to people and animals.
- Blooms thrive in summer, when sunlight is strong, water is warm, and the current is slow or still.

---

# Algae Alert Levels

| Level | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Watch | Algae counts are climbing | Stay out of the water; check updates |
| Alert | Algae counts are high | No water contact; do not eat local fish or shellfish |
| Bloom Emergency | Severe, widespread bloom | Keep away from intake areas; follow local authorities |

- Check current alerts on Korea's [Water Environment Information System](https://water.nier.go.kr/) and your local government's notices.
- Thresholds differ between drinking-water intake zones and recreational zones.

# What to Do During a Bloom

## 1. Stay out of the water
- Stop all water contact: swimming, water-skiing, fishing, wading.
- Swallowing even a small amount of heavily bloomed water can cause vomiting and diarrhea.
- Breathing in spray or mist carrying toxins can trigger coughing and airway irritation.

## 2. Be careful with fish and shellfish
- Do not catch or eat fish or shellfish from affected waters.
- Toxins build up in the internal organs, and neither cooking nor washing removes them.

## 3. Watch your drinking water
- If tap water develops an earthy or musty smell or taste, stop using it and report it to your local authority.
- Boiling does not destroy these toxins. If you suspect a problem, switch to bottled water.

## 4. If you have been in contact
- Wash immediately with clean water and soap.
- See a doctor right away if you develop a rash, stinging eyes, abdominal pain, or vomiting.

## 5. Protect pets and livestock
- Dogs are especially vulnerable: they drink the water and lick it off their coats, and the dose that sickens a person can kill a dog.
- Never let a pet swim in or drink from bloomed water.
- Do not water livestock from affected sources.

## 6. Stay informed
- Follow TV, radio, emergency text alerts, and instructions from local officials.
