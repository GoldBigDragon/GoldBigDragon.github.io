# The Five Rules for Any Train Emergency

1. Tell - use the intercom or emergency call unit to alert the crew.
2. Cover - put a towel, tissue, or your sleeve over your nose and mouth. Smoke and toxic gas drop people faster than flame does.
3. Move - go to an unaffected car first. Climbing down onto the track is a last resort.
4. Follow - do what the driver and crew announce. Do not act on your own judgment.
5. Yield - when a door opens, let the injured, elderly, and pregnant passengers out first.

> Never cross to the opposite track. That is where another train is coming.
> Do not linger beside the rails. Wait in a safe place for ambulances, a rescue train, or buses.

---

# Opening the Doors Manually

## 1. Subway
1. Open the cover of the manual release under the seat beside the door or on the wall.
2. Pull the emergency cock inside, or turn the red emergency handle 90 degrees clockwise.
3. Wait about 10 seconds, until the hiss of escaping air stops.
4. Push the door open to both sides.

## 2. High-speed rail
1. Push down the air-release button at the upper left of the door.
2. Air hisses out and the door lock button pops outward.
3. Pull the lock button.
4. When the door springs outward, pull the handle sideways to open it.

## 3. If the door still will not open
- Break a window at its edge with the emergency hammer. The center resists far better.
- With no hammer, strike with the bottom corner of a fire extinguisher.
- If a platform screen door will not open, push the handles apart or push the red bar fitted to it.

---

# In a Fire

- Alert the crew, then try to knock the fire down early if you safely can.
  - Subway: two extinguishers in every car
  - High-speed rail: extinguishers in the gangway between cars
- Once the fire grows, abandon firefighting and evacuate.
- Smoke rises - stay low as you move.
- In a power failure, follow the emergency guide lights; if you cannot see them, feel along the wall or follow the tactile paving laid for visually impaired passengers.
- If you cannot reach the surface, evacuate into the tunnel in the direction the train was traveling, as directed.
- Inside the tunnel, keep low and follow the emergency lights to the nearest portal or refuge.

---

# In a Derailment or Collision

- A second impact can follow the first. Confirm the train has stopped before you move.
- Luggage can fall from the overhead racks - protect your head as you get up.
- If the door will not open, break the emergency window with the hammer.
- If the car is tilted, leave through the door on the lower side.

---

# In a Chemical or Nerve-Agent Attack

- Cover your nose and mouth immediately and move to an unaffected car.
- Alert the crew via the intercom.
- Do not touch anything that might be contaminated, and do not step in spilled liquid or powder.
- Once off the train, move into the wind, not with it, to get clear of the contaminated area.
- Stinging eyes or skin, dizziness, vomiting, coughing, or difficulty breathing mean possible exposure: call 119 and get to a medical facility.
- Remove contaminated clothing slowly, seal it in a plastic bag, and wash thoroughly under running water.

---

# Before Anything Happens

- When you board, look for the nearest emergency hammer and call unit so you know where they are.
- In a subway station, note your exit number and direction. It is easy to lose your bearings when the lights go out.
- On the platform, never lean on the screen doors or reach out over the track.
