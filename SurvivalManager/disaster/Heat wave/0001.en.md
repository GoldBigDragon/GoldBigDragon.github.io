# Heat Wave Alert Criteria

| | Issued when either applies |
| --- | --- |
| Advisory | A daily maximum apparent temperature of 33 °C or above expected to persist for two days or more / a sharp rise or prolonged heat expected to cause serious damage |
| Warning | A daily maximum apparent temperature of 35 °C or above expected to persist for two days or more / a sharp rise or prolonged heat expected to cause serious damage across a wide area |

---

# Preparing in Advance

- Follow the forecast and share what you learn with the people around you.
- Learn the signs of heat illness, what to do, and the numbers of nearby hospitals, together with family and neighbors.
- Children, elderly people, and those with cardiovascular or cerebrovascular conditions tolerate heat poorly and need closer attention.
- Service your air conditioner and fans in advance.
- Fit curtains, cloth, or window film to block direct sunlight.
- Ready a wide-brimmed hat, sunshade, and sunscreen for going out.
- Check that you have a flashlight, emergency food and drink, a hand fan, and a portable radio for an outage.
- Keep bottled water for a supply cut and fill the bathtub for washing water.
- In older houses, inspect the transformer - cooling load can overload it.
- Think twice about long drives: heat can deform road surfaces and rail tracks.

## Everyday heat sense
- Keep the difference between indoor and outdoor temperature to about 5 °C. A healthy indoor setting is 26–28 °C.
- Avoid caffeine and alcohol and drink water or electrolyte drinks instead. Both are diuretics and deepen dehydration.
- Between 2 p.m. and 5 p.m. is the hottest window - do not work outdoors then.
- Food spoils fast in heat: do not eat anything that has sat out.

# During a Heat Wave

## 1. At home
- Limit outdoor activity; when you must go out, take a wide-brimmed hat, light clothing, and a water bottle.
- Drink often. Drink before you feel thirsty.
- Never leave an elderly person or child alone in a closed car.
- Without air conditioning, block the sun and open windows on opposite sides to create a cross-draft.
- If you will be out for long and someone frail is home alone, ask a relative or neighbor to check in, and call regularly.
- With dizziness, nausea, headache, or muscle cramps, move somewhere cool, rest, and sip a cool drink slowly.

## 2. At work
- Take short, frequent breaks rather than one long one.
- Postpone outdoor events and sports.
- A 10- to 15-minute nap at lunch restores stamina.
- Dress comfortably to keep your body temperature down.
- Where there is no cooling, block the sun and ventilate with fans and open windows and doors.
- Outdoor sites such as construction must follow water, shade, rest, and implement a scheduled heat break during the vulnerable 2–5 p.m. window.

## 3. At school
- If cooling cannot be provided, consider shortened days or closure, and watch for food poisoning.
- In classrooms without cooling, block the sun and ventilate with fans and open windows.
- Suspend outdoor PE and field trips.

## 4. Livestock and aquaculture
- Open barn windows for continuous ventilation and keep stocking density appropriate.
- Install misting systems on greenhouse and barn ceilings to cut radiant heat.
- Watch farmed fish closely and suppress water temperature - adding ice, for example.
- Report any animal or fish deaths to the veterinary authority promptly.

## 5. Cooling shelters
- If you are out, or your home has no air conditioning, spend the hottest hours at a nearby cooling shelter.
- Find them on the [Safety Stepping Stone app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=kr.go.nema.disasteralert_new) and your [local government website](https://www.safekorea.go.kr/safekorea-kor/main/main.doidsiSFK/neo/sfk/cs/sfc/htw/htweaiList.html?menuSeq=862). Locate the nearest one before you need it.

# Heat Illness

## 1. Heat stroke - the most dangerous
Signs
- The body's temperature control center has failed. Multiple organ damage can follow, and the fatality rate is high.
- Central nervous system dysfunction - confusion or unconsciousness
- Hot, dry skin with no sweating (above 40 °C)
- Severe headache, chills, rapid pulse, rapid breathing, low blood pressure

What to do
1. Call 119 immediately.
2. Move the person somewhere cool.
3. Loosen their clothing and wet their body with cool water, then fan them or use a fan.
4. Place ice packs at the neck, armpits, and groin to bring the temperature down.

> Do not give an unconscious person anything to drink. It goes into the airway.

## 2. Heat exhaustion
Signs - heavy sweating / cool, clammy, pale skin / body temperature not greatly raised (40 °C or below) / extreme weakness and fatigue, muscle cramps / nausea or vomiting, confusion, dizziness

What to do
- Rest somewhere cool or air-conditioned.
- Drink water to replace fluids.
- Take a cool shower or bath.
- If it lasts more than an hour or does not improve, go to a medical facility for intravenous fluids and electrolytes.

## 3. Heat cramps
Signs - muscle cramps (arms, legs, abdomen, fingers) caused by salt loss through sweat. Common with hard labor or exercise in the heat.

What to do
- Rest somewhere cool and drink water.
- Massage the cramping muscle.
- Go to the emergency room right away if:
  - The cramps last more than an hour
  - You have an underlying heart condition
  - You follow a low-sodium diet

## 4. Heat syncope
Signs - a brief loss of consciousness as blood shifts to the skin and less reaches the brain. It happens on standing up suddenly, or after standing for a long time, and comes with dizziness.

What to do
- Lay the person flat somewhere cool.
- Raise their legs above their head.
- If they can respond, have them sip water slowly.

## 5. Heat edema
Signs - swelling of the hands, feet, and legs as fluid moves out of the blood vessels during prolonged standing or sitting.

What to do
- Lay the person flat somewhere cool.
- Elevate the swollen area above the level of the heart.

## 6. Heat rash
Signs - small red bumps or blisters where blocked sweat ducts trap sweat (neck, upper chest, groin, inner arms and legs).

What to do
- Keep the area cool and dry.
- Use a rash powder or ointment.
