# 9. Unexploded Ordnance and Cluster Munitions

Where shelling has stopped, bombs that failed to explode remain. These are a major cause of civilian casualties after the shelling ends.

## 9-1. Unexploded Ordnance
> Never touch it or move it. The slightest impact or vibration can set it off.

- Do not approach. Report it immediately to a military unit or the authorities.
- Going closer out of curiosity, or to take a photograph, is just as dangerous.

## 9-2. Cluster Munitions
- A cluster bomb is a single carrier shell that scatters hundreds of submunitions over an area.
- The dud rate of submunitions is very high. Many remain unexploded on the ground long after the shelling.
- Submunitions often look like cans or toys. This is why children in particular are so often hurt after picking one up.

| If you find one | What never to do |
| --- | --- |
| Leave the area immediately | Touch it |
| Report its location to the authorities | Kick or throw it |
| Keep others away | Move or collect it |

- Apply the same rule to any small metal object, can-shaped object, or brightly colored unfamiliar item you have not seen on that street before.

> Teaching children is the practical prevention. Drill it in advance: "Never touch an unfamiliar object - tell an adult."

# 10. Building Collapse

## 10-1. Escaping a Building
- Find the nearest EXIT or emergency stairwell and move out in an orderly way.
- Get as far from the building as you can. Further collapse follows.
- Hold a cushion, bag, or pillow over your head as you move. Injuries from brick and glass fragments are common.
- Do not use the elevator. Power failure and fire come together.

## 10-2. Trapped in the Rubble

> Do not force your way out. It can trigger further collapse, and it burns the strength you have left first.

- Stay in as comfortable a position as you can to conserve energy and water.
- Signal with sound - tapping a pipe or wall carries further than shouting and costs far less energy.
- If you have a whistle, blow it at intervals. It is far more efficient than your voice.
- Repeat the SOS signal (three short - three long - three short) so rescuers recognize it as human.
- Shine a flashlight and send the alert tone from a phone or radio periodically.

> Do not strike a match or lighter. Leaking gas can explode.

- Cover your nose and mouth with cloth so you do not breathe the dust.

# 11. Loss of Power, Water, and Gas

## 11-1. Power Failure
- Keep a battery flashlight, candles, and a lighter or matches.
- Use candles somewhere safe and put them out completely. Candle fires are common during outages.
- Keep cash. During an outage, card payments and cash withdrawals both stop.
- Keep a battery-powered FM/AM portable radio. It may be your only way to hear government announcements when power and the network are both down.

## 11-2. Water Cut
- Stockpiling bottled water in advance is the surest measure - at least 2.5 liters per person per day.
- Fill the bathtub and buckets in advance for washing and the toilet.
- Keep a 10-liter water container and a cart. To collect a ration or carry water you need a way to move it.
- Locate the civil defense emergency water facilities in advance. Find them on the National Disaster Safety Portal ▶ Civil Defense ▶ Emergency Facilities ▶ Water Supply.
- Always purify water collected outside. Filter out particles and boil it hard for at least one minute to kill waterborne pathogens and parasites.

> In CBRN contamination, boiling does not make water safe. Radioactive material and chemical agents are not removed by boiling. Drink sealed bottled water only.

## 11-3. Gas Cut
- Keep a portable gas stove and canisters.
- Store canisters where ventilation is good.
- Build your stock around food that needs no cooking and stores easily (cereal, canned goods, hard biscuits, energy bars) to conserve gas.

# 12. Once the Shelling Stops

## 12-1. Do Not Go Out Yet
> A pause is not the end. It can resume, so stay where you are safe and keep listening to government broadcasts until the all-clear is officially declared.

## 12-2. Helping the Injured
- After the all-clear, join the rescue of the injured.
- Treat and cover wounds and burns to prevent infection.
- Large-scale shelling produces more casualties than medical facilities can reach in time. Knowing first aid beforehand genuinely saves lives.
- Donate blood to support the treatment of the injured.

## 12-3. Secondary Hazards

| Hazard | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| A leaning or cracked building | Do not go near it |
| A downed power line | Electrocution risk - stay away |
| A gas leak | If you smell gas, shut the valve, open windows to ventilate, and report it |
| The moment power and water return | Watch for fire and flooding |

> If power is restored while gas is leaking, a spark causes an explosion. Check for the smell of gas before restoration.

- Also watch for further injury from debris and for infectious disease carried by contaminated water.

## 12-4. Essential Supplies
- Do not hoard. Cooperate with government rationing.
- After large-scale shelling, logistics collapse and the supply of food and essentials is not reliable.
- Keep emergency supplies in advance and receive rations in an orderly way as directed.
