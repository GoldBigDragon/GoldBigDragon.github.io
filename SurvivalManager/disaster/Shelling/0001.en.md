# 1. What Shelling Means

- There is almost no warning. Shells take seconds to a few minutes from launch to impact, and they land across a wide area at once.
- The secondary damage is worse than the fragments. Building collapse, fire, and window glass shattered by the blast, which sprays like bullets, account for a large share of casualties.
- You cannot know in advance what munitions will be used. Assume conventional explosives may come mixed with chemical or biological weapons, or nuclear warheads, and prepare for all of them together.
- It does not happen once. Shelling repeats, and several types of attack can arrive at the same time.

> The period after the first explosion can be the more dangerous one. Stay alert and keep listening to government broadcasts on radio or emergency text alerts.

## 1-1. The Moment You Hear the Siren

Civil defense alert types and siren signals are covered in the Civil Defense Alert document. For shelling, remember only this sequence.

| Step | Action |
| --- | --- |
| ① | Close every window - this blocks both fragments and blast pressure, and keeps CBRN contaminants out |
| ② | Put on your gas mask (or a face mask) - assume a CBRN attack is possible |
| ③ | Move deep inside the building, away from windows, or go underground |
| ④ | Lie face down and protect your head and body |

# 2. When You Are Indoors

## 2-1. Where to Go
- Choose a space with no windows - bathroom, interior hallway, stairwell, interior storage room.
- The innermost part of the building is best: far from exterior walls, close to interior walls and columns.
- Never use the elevator - take the stairs. A blast-induced power cut traps you inside, and the doors can open onto a floor that is on fire.
- Stay off the roof. It leaves you fully exposed to shellfire.

## 2-2. The Right Floor Depends on the Type of Attack

> This judgment decides whether you live. Listen to government broadcasts to learn what kind of attack it is.

| Type of attack | Safer location | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conventional shelling | Lower floors or underground | Thick structure blocks fragments and blast |
| Confirmed chemical attack | Higher floors | Chemical agents are heavier than air and sink, pooling underground |

- With no information at all, go low or underground as for conventional shelling - but go in wearing a gas mask.

## 2-3. Body Position
- Lie face down. Getting low is the most basic defense against fragments.
- Cover your eyes and ears with both hands.
- Keep your mouth open - this prevents your eardrums from rupturing under the sudden pressure change.
- Keep your abdomen off the floor - this reduces the shock wave transmitted through the ground.

# 3. When You Are Outdoors

Outdoors is the most dangerous place to be: you are fully exposed to fragments and CBRN agents. Get inside immediately.

| Situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| A building is nearby | Get into the nearest building and follow section 2 |
| No building, but a vehicle | Get inside the vehicle, close the windows and switch to recirculated air to block outside air |
| Neither | Use terrain - a ditch, a hollow, the far side of a concrete structure - and lie flat |

- Lie with your body on the side away from the explosion, hands over eyes and ears, mouth open, abdomen off the ground.
- Low ground helps. Fragments mostly fly upward and outward, so the closer to the ground you are, the less likely you are to be hit.
- But if chemical weapons are possible, do not stay long in a ditch or low ground. Heavy agents collect there.

---

# 4. When You Are Driving

- A vehicle offers almost no protection from fragments or blast. Do not stay in it - moving to a solid building or underground is far safer.
- Pull over into a nearby open area or to the right side of the road. A car left in the middle of the road blocks military vehicles, ambulances, and fire engines.
- Do not lock it, and leave the key in the car. Emergency crews must be able to move it.
- Once out, get to the nearest building or underground facility quickly.
- The exception: where there is no building at all, inside the vehicle beats open ground. Close the windows, switch to recirculated air, and get low below the seat line.
