# 5. Preparing for a Combined CBRN Attack

Because you cannot know what the enemy will mix into the shelling, prepare for explosives (fragments), chemical, biological, nuclear, and fire all at once.

## 5-1. Putting On the Gas Mask and Checking the Seal
- Put it on the instant the siren sounds or the explosions begin.
- Many chemical agents have no smell, and depending on the agent symptoms may not appear for hours. Never judge contamination by smell or symptoms.
- Seal check: cover the filter with your hand and inhale. If you feel negative pressure, the seal is good. If air leaks in around your face, adjust the straps and check again.
- Use a combined-gas filter (organic, acid, ammonia, particulate) and check its expiry date.
- Take the mask off only after the government has announced that it is safe.

> Removing it too early exposes you to residual agent. Nerve and blister agents are absorbed through skin too, but the airway is far more vulnerable - protect it first.

## 5-2. When Underground Becomes the Dangerous Place
- Chemical agents are several times heavier than air and sink. In a chemical attack they pool in underground parking garages and subway stations.
- If you must go underground while a chemical attack is possible, go in wearing a KS M 6685 certified gas mask and chemical-resistant protective clothing.
- If the underground space is crowded, your own car parked down there may be better. A vehicle blocks some outside air and gives you a small sealed space.
- Persistent chemical agents remain for more than a day, in high concentration above and below ground.

## 5-3. The Oxygen Limit of a Sealed Space

If you seal yourself in, the air you have to breathe is finite.

| Conditions | How long it lasts |
| --- | --- |
| Sealed space 2.3 m high and about 7.6 m³, one adult at rest | About 12 hours |

- With several people, that time shrinks sharply in proportion to the number.
- Above 3–4% carbon dioxide, the air is dangerous.

> Rapid breathing, headache, and dizziness are the warning. Ventilate at once or leave that space.

## 5-4. A Warning About Sealing Windows
- Taping window frames and door gaps is effective against chemical and biological contamination.
- But do not tape windows while shelling is underway. The risk of being cut by breaking glass is far greater. Do it during a lull.

# 6. Fire During Shelling

In a large-scale disaster many fires break out at once and fire engines cannot reach you in time. You will have to fight the early fire yourself.

## 6-1. Before a Fire Starts
- Move kerosene, gas cylinders, and other fire hazards somewhere safe.
- Shut off the external gas valve.
- Unplug electric heaters. They cause secondary fires when power is restored after an outage.
- Know where the extinguisher is. It may be the only means of early suppression you have.

## 6-2. When Fire Breaks Out
- Cover your mouth and nose and stay low. Smoke rises, so the closer to the floor, the more breathable air there is.
- A hot door handle means fire on the other side. Do not open it - find another route.
- If your clothes catch fire, roll on the ground to put it out.
- Know at least two escape routes in advance, so you can move immediately if fire or smoke reaches your shelter.

> For burn first aid, see the First Aid document.

# 7. Food and Drinking Water

During shelling, CBRN contaminants drift in the air. If they settle on open food, eating it causes internal exposure or poisoning.

## 7-1. What Is Safe to Eat
- Only fully sealed items - bottled water, canned food, packaged snacks.
- Do not eat anything that has been left exposed.
- Do not drink tap water until it is confirmed safe. Sealed bottled water is the safe choice.

## 7-2. Even Sealed Food Must Be Washed
- If the outside of the packaging is contaminated, you eat the contaminant with the food.
- Wash the packaging in clean soapy water before opening it.
- Pay particular attention to the pull-tab of a can and the mouth of a bottle.

## 7-3. Where to Eat
> Never eat outdoors. Contaminants land on the food while you are eating. Eat only in a sealed indoor space.

## 7-4. If You Have Time
- Wrap food well in sealed containers or plastic.
- Cap every bottle and jar of drinking water.
- Fill the bathtub and large containers with water for washing and toilet use.
- Never eat from a can that is swollen or leaking.

# 8. Decontamination

If you have been exposed to CBRN contamination, decontaminate before entering a safe space. Going in contaminated contaminates the whole space.

## 8-1. Removing Clothing and Equipment
- Keep your mask or gas mask on while undressing - you inhale contaminants during that process.
- Undress slowly and carefully so contaminants do not become airborne.
- Put the clothing and shoes into plastic and seal it.
- Used filters and protective suits also leak contaminants. Seal them the same way, label them as CBRN-contaminated, and leave them somewhere safe.
- Follow the authorities' instructions on disposal.

## 8-2. Washing
- Wash thoroughly with lukewarm water and soap (detergent if there is no soap).
- Deliberately wash the places that get missed - hair, ears, skin folds, under the fingernails.
- Blow your nose gently to clear contaminants from the nasal passages.
- Do not scrub with a rough towel.

> Do not break the skin. Any abrasion lets contaminants into your body through the opening.
