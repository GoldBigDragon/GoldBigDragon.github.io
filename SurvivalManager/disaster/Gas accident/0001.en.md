# What You Must Know About the Gas Itself

| | LPG (propane / butane) | City gas, LNG (methane) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compared to air | Heavier | Lighter |
| Where it collects | Floor, basements, drains | Ceiling |
| Where to smell for it | Down low | Up high |
| How to clear it | Sweep it along the floor and out with a broom or cushion | Open windows to vent from above |

- The gas itself is colorless and odorless. The smell you notice is an odorant added deliberately for safety.

---

# Preventing Gas Accidents

## 1. Before use
- LPG appliances and LNG appliances are not interchangeable. Use the appliance matched to your gas supply.
- Before lighting, check for a leak by smell, at the height shown in the table above.
- Open a window while the appliance is running.
- Keep flammable items away from the stove.
- Brush soapy water on pipe and hose connections regularly and watch for bubbles.
- Clean the burner periodically so the flame ports do not clog.

## 2. While in use
- Do not leave the appliance unattended.
- Always confirm the flame has caught. An open cock with no flame leaks gas continuously.
- Adjust the air shutter until the flame burns blue. A red flame means incomplete combustion - it produces carbon monoxide and toxic gas and wastes fuel.
- Keep the stove in one fixed place rather than moving it around.

## 3. Portable butane stoves
- Never use one inside a sealed tent or a small room. The risk of asphyxiation and fire is severe.
- If you must use one indoors, ventilate thoroughly and test it outdoors first.
- Do not put a pot wider than the trivet on it. Radiated heat cooks the butane canister until it explodes.
- Seat the canister fully, with the notch on its rim facing upward.
- If gas leaks during use, lift the locking lever and detach the canister at once.
- Puncture spent canisters to vent residual gas, then dispose of them away from any ignition source.

# Signs of a Leak

- You smell the characteristic odorant.
- A flame goes out by itself, or burns red (incomplete combustion).
- Your gas bill jumps unexpectedly.
- Soapy water on a joint produces bubbles.
- You feel headache, dizziness, or nausea - the early signs of poisoning.

---

# If Gas Is Leaking

1. Make no spark. Light switches, fans, doorbells, and mobile phone calls all produce one. Step outside to make any call.
2. Cover your nose and mouth with a wet towel and move in low to the ground.
3. Close the intermediate valve and the main valve.
4. Open all windows and the front door to bring in fresh air.
5. If LPG is involved, use a broom or cushion to sweep the gas along the floor and out the door.
6. Carry or drag anyone affected outdoors or into well-ventilated space.
7. Call 119, and notify your city gas supplier (for LNG) or your LPG dealer.
8. Do not go back inside until you are certain the gas has fully dispersed.

---

# CPR

1. Check response - tap both shoulders and call to the person.
2. Get help - call 119 and ask someone to bring an AED.
3. Check breathing - look for chest movement, no longer than 10 seconds.
4. 30 chest compressions - hands stacked on the center of the chest (lower half of the breastbone), pressing about 5 cm deep at 100–120 per minute, hard and fast. Let the chest recoil fully between compressions.
5. 2 rescue breaths - tilt the head back, lift the chin to open the airway, pinch the nose, and blow for one second, watching the chest rise.
6. Repeat at 30 : 2 until paramedics arrive or the person recovers.

> If you are not confident giving rescue breaths, or if gas or chemical contamination is suspected, compression-only CPR is acceptable. What matters most is not stopping.

# Gas Fires

## 1. A small fire around the stove
1. Close the gas valve first.
2. Sweep the extinguisher side to side, like a broom. With no extinguisher, smother it with a pot lid or a wet blanket or towel.
3. Never throw water on a cooking-oil fire. The oil scatters and the flame erupts.
4. Once it is out, confirm the valve is fully closed and open a window to ventilate.

## 2. Once the fire grows
1. Shout "Fire!" and hit the alarm.
2. Close the door behind you as you leave, to slow the spread of flame and smoke.
3. Cover your nose and mouth with a wet towel and stay low.
4. Take the stairs, never the elevator.
5. If your clothes catch fire: stop, drop, and roll.
6. If a door handle is hot, do not open it - signal for rescue from a window or balcony.
7. If you cannot escape, seal the door gaps with towels or bedding, tell 119 exactly where you are, and signal from the window.

## 3. If an LPG cylinder is burning
- Do not try to put the fire out first. Extinguishing the flame while gas is still escaping lets the gas pool - and the explosion risk goes up, not down.
- If you can reach it safely, close the valve, then cool the cylinder with wet cloth.
- If you cannot close the valve, get far away immediately.
- Cover your ears as you go; a blast can damage your hearing.

# Carbon Monoxide Poisoning

- Carbon monoxide is colorless and odorless, with no added odorant. You cannot smell it.
- Symptoms: headache → dizziness and nausea → impaired judgment → loss of consciousness. Many victims die in their sleep.
- Prevention: have heater and boiler flues inspected regularly, and never burn charcoal, briquettes, or a portable stove indoors or in a tent.
- Run generators and charcoal grills outdoors only, well away from windows.
- Installing a carbon monoxide alarm at home is the single most reliable safeguard.
- If you suspect exposure, open windows, get outside, and call 119.
