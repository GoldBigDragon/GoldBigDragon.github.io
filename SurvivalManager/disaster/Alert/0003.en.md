# 6. Nuclear Warning - What to Do

Issued when a nuclear attack is imminent or under way, or a detonation has occurred or is expected.
The siren rises for 5 seconds and falls for 3, repeating for one minute.

## 6-1. Get to Shelter
1. Take your emergency pack and move quickly to a civil defense shelter or other underground facility.
   - Underground gives substantial shielding from thermal radiation, blast, initial radiation, and fallout compared with the surface.
2. If you cannot reach one, use a windowless space in a concrete building, fire-protection or evacuation facilities, or a ditch.

## 6-2. If There Is No Time - Get Down Now

> Regardless of the direction of the blast, use whatever terrain and cover is around you and get flat immediately.
> 1. Cover your eyes and ears with both hands.
> 2. Open your mouth. - This keeps the blast's sudden pressure change from rupturing your eardrums.
> 3. Keep your abdomen off the ground. - This reduces the shock wave transmitted through the earth.

- If you see the flash, you have seconds before the blast. Get flat or into a building at once.
- The intense thermal radiation lasts several seconds after the detonation - getting flat immediately reduces the injury.
- Stay down until the blast has passed and debris has stopped falling.
- Never look directly at the flash.

## 6-3. Once Debris Stops Falling - Fallout
- If you can, get out of the affected area before fallout begins.
- Fallout travels a considerable distance on the wind. Prepare even if the detonation was far away.
- On reaching safety, find a sealable space and block the doors and vents with plastic, cloth, and aluminum tape.
- When moving, use rain gear and an umbrella so fallout does not land directly on you.

## 6-4. If You Cannot Leave the Affected Area
- Move to a civil defense shelter, or stay deep underground or in the central part of a building, as sealed from the outside as possible.
- Avoid the top floor. Fallout accumulates on the roof and the radiation there is high. A middle floor is best.

## 6-5. How Long to Stay

Fallout radiation falls off sharply over time. Taking one hour after the detonation as the baseline:

| Time elapsed | Radiation intensity |
| --- | --- |
| About 6 hours | 1/10 |
| 2 days | 1/100 |
| 2 weeks | 1/1,000 |

> So roughly two weeks of sheltering is needed. Your emergency supplies must be enough to last more than two weeks.

## 6-6. EMP
- A nuclear detonation produces a powerful electromagnetic pulse (EMP) that can disable electronics, communications equipment, and the power grid over a wide area.
- Keep a battery-powered radio against blackout and loss of communications.

## 6-7. What Not to Do
- Once fallout or rain begins, never move around outside.
  - The one exception: if the place you are sheltering is at risk of being engulfed by fire, you may have to move.
- Looking directly at the flash
- Eating contaminated water or exposed food
- Drinking tap water - it may be contaminated. Drink sealed bottled water only.

# 7. Self-Decontamination After Radioactive Exposure

If you have been exposed to fallout or other radioactive material, or you have dizziness or vomiting, remove the contamination as quickly as possible to reduce your radiation dose.

## 7-1. Preparation
1. Set aside a space for decontamination and cover the floor with plastic so no contamination remains in your shelter.
2. Remove your clothing, watch, and jewelry.
3. Put them and your belongings in a plastic bag or sealable container, seal it, and leave it somewhere safe outside the house, away from people and pets.

## 7-2. The Order
> ① Open wounds → ② Body openings (eyes, ears, nose, mouth) → ③ Unbroken skin

## 7-3. Wounds
- Irrigate thoroughly with saline, and if necessary rinse again with 3% hydrogen peroxide solution.
- Wipe with gauze and cover with sterile waterproof material to prevent further contamination.

## 7-4. Eyes, Ears, Nose, Mouth
- Eyes: if the eyeball is undamaged, rinse thoroughly with water or saline, flowing from the inner corner outward so nothing enters the nose through the tear duct.
- Ears: check the eardrum before you start.
  - With a perforated eardrum, do not irrigate - go to hospital for treatment.
  - If undamaged, rinse the outer ear canal with water or saline and wipe with a damp cotton swab.
- Nose: if conscious, have the person blow their nose, then wipe with a damp cotton swab, taking care not to damage the lining.
- Mouth: wipe around the mouth and rinse thoroughly with saline or water two or three times or more. Brush the teeth gently so as not to cut the gums, and rinse often.

## 7-5. Showering
- Wash from head to toe with clean, unexposed lukewarm water and neutral soap.
- Shampoo the hair at least twice, washing thoroughly.
- Do not use conditioner. It binds radioactive material to the hair protein and makes decontamination harder.
- Do not wash your hair bent forward. Water gets into your eyes, nose, mouth, and ears.
- Do not use an exfoliating cloth or a rough towel. Micro-tears in the skin let contamination inside.
- If you cannot shower, wipe exposed skin with a wet towel.
- Seal the rinse water and any tools used in plastic and store them somewhere safe.
