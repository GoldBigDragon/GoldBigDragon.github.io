# 1. What the Civil Defense Alert System Is

The Government of the Republic of Korea operates a nationwide civil defense alert system to tell the public immediately when an attack by hostile ground, naval, or air forces is expected or under way.

How it reaches you: sirens · voice broadcasts · radio and TV captions · Cell Broadcast Service (CBS) alert messages on your phone

## 1-1. The Five Levels - and How Each One Arrives

| Alert | When | Siren | How it reaches you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alert warning | An attack is expected | None | Voice broadcast, alert message |
| Air raid warning | An attack is imminent or under way | Yes - 5 seconds rising, 3 seconds falling, repeating for one minute | Siren, voice broadcast, alert message |
| CBR warning | Chemical or biological agents have been detected or confirmed | None | Voice broadcast, alert message |
| Nuclear warning | A nuclear attack is imminent or under way, or a detonation is expected | Yes - 5 seconds rising, 3 seconds falling, repeating for one minute | Siren, voice broadcast, alert message |
| All clear | Just after an attack, with no further attack expected | None | Voice broadcast, alert message |

> No siren does not mean no alert.
> The alert warning, the CBR warning, and the all clear come without any siren - only by voice broadcast and phone alert message. You must check your messages and the broadcast.

> General disaster sirens are different: 2 seconds rising, 2 seconds falling, with a voice broadcast. Keep that apart from the 5-second/3-second pattern of the air raid and nuclear warnings.

The right action changes completely with the type of alert. Learn what each one means in advance - that is what makes a fast, correct response possible when it actually happens.

# 2. Alert Warning - An Attack Is Expected

Nothing has started yet, but a threat has been detected. Concentrate on preparing to evacuate and understanding the situation.

## 2-1. What to Do
1. Turn on the TV or radio immediately and follow the government's instructions.
2. Get out your emergency pack, supplies, gas mask, and protective clothing so they are ready to use.
3. Prevent secondary damage - move kerosene and gas cylinders somewhere safe, shut off the external gas valve, and unplug heating appliances.
4. Send children and older family members ahead to a safe place. Once things become urgent, moving them is difficult.
5. Cover food and drinking water to keep out contaminants.
6. At night, turn off the lights and block any light with curtains or blinds. It makes target identification harder for an air attack.
7. Watch for falling objects and stay out of the open.
8. Public venues - theaters, stadiums, restaurants - should stop trading, pass the alert on to customers, and clear them out in an orderly sequence to prevent crush and injury.
9. If an air attack is expected, move to a civil defense shelter or other underground facility.

## 2-2. What Not to Do
- Unnecessary trips outside
- Unnecessary phone calls - the network must not be overloaded
- Believing or forwarding unverified rumors on social media

# 3. Air Raid Warning - An Attack Is Imminent or Under Way

Evacuate immediately.

The broadcast text is specific to the situation - aircraft incursion, missile launch, artillery fire.
Example: "○○:○○ North Korean aircraft incursion. Air raid warning for ○○ area. Move to an underground facility or inside a building."

## 3-1. What to Do
1. Take your emergency pack and move to the nearest civil defense shelter, underground facility, or a windowless space inside a concrete building.
2. In a high-rise, take the stairs down to the basement or lower floors.
3. Never use the elevator.
4. If you are driving, park in a nearby open lot or on the right side of the road, and leave the key in the car when you evacuate.
   - A car blocking the road obstructs rescue and military vehicles. The key must be there so it can be moved if needed.
5. Once sheltered, listen to government broadcasts, and use the radio to learn the blast location, wind direction, and damage.
6. Help older people, children, pregnant women, and people with disabilities evacuate with you.

## 3-2. Special Conditions
- During the monsoon, typhoons, or any period when heavy rain is expected, do not shelter in underground facilities in flood-prone low-lying areas. Go somewhere safer.
- If you cannot reach an underground facility, use a windowless space in a concrete building, fire-protection or evacuation facilities, or a ditch.

## 3-3. What Not to Do
- Using an elevator
- Unnecessary communications
- Acting on your own judgment - always follow the government's instructions
- Being swayed by rumor
