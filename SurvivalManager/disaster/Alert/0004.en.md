# 8. All Clear

Issued just after an attack, when no further attack is expected. It comes with no siren - by voice broadcast and phone alert message.

> All clear does not mean complete safety. Keep listening to government broadcasts and watching the situation, in case of further attacks.

## 8-1. What to Do
1. Help rescue and treat the injured, and cooperate with recovery work.
2. If there was CBR contamination, wash yourself with soapy water.
3. Seal contaminated clothing and shoes in plastic to prevent secondary contamination.
4. Watch for secondary harm - injuries from damaged buildings and debris, electrocution, fire, and disease.
5. When electricity, water, and gas come back after being cut, watch for fire and flooding.
6. Do not hoard essentials, and cooperate with government rationing.
   - In an emergency the government designates essential goods, manages their supply, and rations items that cannot be distributed normally.

## 8-2. Unexploded Ordnance
> If you find unexploded ordnance, never go near it - report it to a military unit or other relevant authority.
> Cluster munitions deserve particular care: hundreds of submunitions scatter, the dud rate is high, and the submunitions look like cans or toys - civilians are frequently hurt handling them.

## 8-3. What Not to Do
- Approaching unexploded ordnance
- Entering a CBR-contaminated area before the government confirms it is safe
- Spreading unverified rumors
- Hoarding essentials

# 9. Everyday Preparation

## 9-1. Know Your Nearest Shelter
Find the nearest civil defense shelter to the places you spend time - home, work, school - in advance.
Searching "민방위 대피소" (civil defense shelter) in Naver Map, Kakao Map, T map, or the Safety Dideemdol app shows the nearest shelter to your current position.

## 9-2. Where to Shelter, by Type of Attack

| Type of attack | Where to shelter |
| --- | --- |
| Conventional weapons | Civil defense shelters, underground parking of apartment blocks and large buildings, subway stations |
| Chemical | A high floor indoors, sealed against outside air |
| Biological | A designated shelter or your home - any interior space you can seal and stay in |
| Nuclear / radiological | Civil defense shelters, underground parking of apartment blocks and large buildings, subway stations |

> A chemical attack is the one case where you go up, not down. Chemical agents are heavier than air and sink.

## 9-3. The Emergency Pack
- Two to three days of water, food, essentials, medicines, a radio, a flashlight, and CBR protective items, packed in a bag or case.
- One pack per person, so everyone can carry their own.
- For a nuclear scenario, you need supplies for more than two weeks.

## 9-4. Family Plan
- Agree on a meeting place in case communications fail. Set a first, second, and third meeting point.
- Write out a family emergency contact card and keep it in your pack. It should carry:
  - Personal and family details, blood types, medications taken
  - Home, work, and school contact numbers
  - Emergency, relief, and government agency numbers
