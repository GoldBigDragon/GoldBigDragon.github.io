# What a Falling Space Object Means

- Debris from satellites and rockets, or a meteoroid, can survive re-entry and reach the ground.
- Most of it burns up in the atmosphere, but what is left arrives with severe impact force and possible explosive effect.
- Predicted re-entry is announced through emergency broadcasts and government alerts.

---

# If You Are Indoors

## 1. With an underground shelter nearby
- The moment a ground impact is announced, move to an underground shelter.
- Find the nearest one through [Safe Korea 24](https://www.safekorea.go.kr/safekorea-kor/main/main.do) or by searching "civil defense shelter" in a map app.

## 2. With no underground shelter
- Move away from any position that faces a window or door head-on.
- Get behind a solid wall or pillar, or under a desk or table, and protect your head.

---

# If You Are Outdoors

## 1. Shelter is within reach
- Move quickly into a nearby underground structure.
- Avoid buildings that are on fire or that look unstable.

## 2. No shelter available
- Wrap your head with whatever you have - a bag, a jacket.
- Drop low behind any terrain feature or solid object.

---

# The Bracing Position

- Lie face down facing away from windows, doors, or the direction of the fall.
- Cover your eyes and ears with both hands, and keep your mouth open.
  - Covering your eyes protects your sight from an intense flash.
  - An open mouth equalizes pressure so the blast wave does not rupture your eardrums.
- Keep your abdomen off the ground to reduce shock transmitted through the earth.
- Stay down until the shock has passed and debris has stopped falling.

---

# After the Impact

- Keep listening to emergency broadcasts and follow instructions.
- Prepare for secondary hazards: earthquakes, tsunami, and fire.
- Never touch an object you suspect is re-entry debris. Report it to 119 or 112 immediately.
  - Residual propellant can be highly toxic, and some spacecraft carry radioisotope power sources.
- Give first aid to anyone injured and call 119.
