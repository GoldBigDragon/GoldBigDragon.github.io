# Wind Advisory Thresholds

| | Sustained wind | Gusts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Advisory | 14 m/s or more (50.4 km/h) | 20 m/s or more (72 km/h) |
| Warning | 21 m/s or more (75.6 km/h) | 26 m/s or more (93.6 km/h) |

- "High wind" means wind strong enough to cause injury and property damage.

---

# Before the Wind Arrives

## 1. Windows and doors
- Close and lock them so nothing can move.
- Aging windows can bow or shatter - replace or reinforce them in advance.
- A gap between the glass and the frame greatly increases the chance of breakage. Seal the gap so the pane cannot rattle.
- Apply safety film to reduce injury from flying glass.
  - If you use tape, apply it so the glass is held firmly against the frame. An X of tape alone does not stop the pane from breaking.

## 2. Everything outside
- Bring in or tie down anything the wind can lift - pots, bicycles, drying racks.
- Signage and rooftop structures cause serious secondary damage when they come loose: fix and reinforce them.
- Reinforce weak points on greenhouses and clear the area around them.
- Ask your local government office to remove dead trees and branches near your home ahead of time.
- Inspect connections on exposed wiring and replace what needs replacing.

## 3. Get ready
- Have a flashlight on hand for the power cut.
- Households with elderly or disabled members should agree on an evacuation method and a way to stay in contact with family and neighbors.

---

# While It Is Blowing

## 1. Indoors
- Stay inside as much as you can.
- Keep away from windows. When glass breaks, fragments travel well into the room.
- Once glass has broken, put shoes or slippers on before you move.
- Do not work on the roof or outdoors, and do not attempt electrical repairs.

## 2. Outdoors
- Avoid standing under trees or utility poles that could come down, and get into a solid building.
- Stay away from construction sites and anywhere with falling or flying objects.
- Do not go near the shore - waves can sweep you away.
- Suspend construction work and crane operations.

## 3. Driving
- Slow down and drive defensively.
- Watch for oncoming vehicles.
- Keep extra distance from the vehicle in the next lane. A strong gust can push a car out of its lane.
- Grip the wheel firmly where a crosswind hits suddenly: on bridges, at tunnel exits, rounding a mountain spur.
- Passing a truck or bus, be ready for the moment its wind shadow ends.

---

# After the Wind

- Never approach or touch a downed power line. Wet ground and any metal it is touching are equally dangerous.
- If you find damaged electrical equipment, do not touch it - call 119 or your local government office.
- If a power line has fallen on your car, stay inside it. Avoid touching any metal part, warn bystanders to keep away, and call 119.
- Do not repair damaged roofing or signage yourself; report it to a professional or the local authority.
- Photograph the damage, then report it to your city, county, or district office.
