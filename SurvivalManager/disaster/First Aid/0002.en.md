# 4. Severe Bleeding

1. Press hard directly on the wound with your hand, a cloth, or gauze.
2. Hold the pressure for at least 10 minutes. Do not lift it to check.
3. If bleeding continues, use a tourniquet - on limbs only.
4. Apply it closer to the heart than the wound, and write down the time you applied it.

# 5. Choking

> If the person is coughing on their own, the airway is not fully blocked. Encourage them to cough it out first.

## 5-1. Adults and Children - the Heimlich Maneuver (with a helper)
1. Encourage coughing.
2. If they cannot cough, perform abdominal thrusts.
3. Stand behind them, make a fist with one hand, and place the thumb side between the navel and the base of the sternum.
4. Wrap your other hand around the fist and thrust sharply inward and upward.

## 5-2. If You Are Alone
1. Try to cough the object out.
2. Make a fist with your dominant hand.
3. Place it on your abdomen, just above the navel and below the ribs.
4. Wrap your other hand around the fist.
5. Drive the fist up and in, like a hook, under the diaphragm - repeatedly.
6. If nothing comes out, find a fixed chair or table at waist height.
7. Keep both hands pressed to your abdomen and bend over the object, forcing pressure into it.
8. Repeat.

## 5-3. Infants - 5 Back Blows + 5 Chest Thrusts
1. Hold the jaw and cheekbones with your left hand and cradle the back of the head with your right, lifting the baby slowly.
2. Sit down and turn the baby face down, head lower than the body, across your thigh.
3. Keep the baby's body in a straight line.
4. Strike firmly five times between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
5. Holding the jaw and the back of the head, turn the baby over.
6. Place two fingers on the sternum just below the nipple line and press five times, fast and firm.
7. Alternate back blows and chest thrusts until the object comes out.

# 6. Burns

## 6-1. What to Do
1. Remove the heat source or get away from it and make the scene safe.
2. Cool the burn under clean running water for at least 20 minutes.
3. Cover with clean gauze to prevent infection - but do not press on it.
4. If shock (low blood pressure) is suspected, lie them down and raise the legs slightly.
5. Cover with a blanket or clothing to keep body heat in.
6. If they are conscious and not vomiting, give small sips of water.

## 6-2. Never Do This

| Wrong treatment | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Bursting blisters | You are opening a route for infection yourself |
| Applying oil, bean paste, soy sauce, toothpaste, lotion, ointment | Sharply raises the risk of infection |
| Putting ice directly on it | Damages the tissue |
| Pulling off stuck clothing or material | The skin comes away with it |

## 6-3. By Degree

| Degree | What it looks like | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | Red and painful | Cool it and keep it moisturized |
| Second | Blisters form | Protect the blisters without bursting them. Cover with clean cloth. Lie the person down - the severe pain can cause shock. |
| Third | Skin turns white or black. Nerve damage may leave no sensation at all | Keep cooling brief to avoid hypothermia. Cover immediately with clean cloth and maintain body heat with a blanket. Give fluids if conscious. |

> A third-degree burn may not hurt. The absence of sensation means the nerves are destroyed - never read "no pain" as "not serious."
