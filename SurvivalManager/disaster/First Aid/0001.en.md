# 1. Before You Begin

- If the person is conscious, explain what you are about to do and ask for their consent.
- If they are unconscious or in immediate danger, act at once without consent.

> First aid is not treatment. It is the skill of keeping someone alive until rescue arrives.
> Even if things appear to resolve, they must be seen by a medical professional.

# 2. CPR

## 2-1. The Sequence
1. Check for hazards - electrocution, fire, collapse - and approach only once it is safe.
2. Check for response - tap the shoulder and ask "Are you all right?" (For an infant, tap the sole of the foot.)
3. Call for help - point at specific people.
   - "You, in the black jacket - call 119!"
   - "You, in the white shirt - bring the AED!"
4. Check breathing - watch the chest.
   > Abnormal breathing, including gasping, counts as no breathing.
5. Chest compressions - at the position and depth in the table below, 100–120 per minute.
6. Rescue breaths - tilt the head back, lift the chin to open the airway, pinch the nose, and blow for one second, enough to raise the chest. 30 compressions : 2 breaths.
7. Keep going until the paramedics arrive or the person comes round.

## 2-2. What Changes with Age

| | Adult | Child (1 to puberty) | Infant (under 1) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Check response | Tap the shoulder | Tap the shoulder | Tap the sole of the foot |
| If you are alone | Call 119 first, then CPR on speakerphone | Two minutes of CPR first, then call 119 | Two minutes of CPR first, then call 119 |
| Hands | Both hands, fingers interlocked | One hand | Two fingers |
| Position | Center of the chest (lower half of the sternum) | Center of the chest (lower half of the sternum) | Center of the chest (lower half of the sternum) |
| Depth | 5–6 cm | About 1/3 of chest depth (about 5 cm) | About 1/3 of chest depth (about 4 cm) |
| Rescue breaths | If you can. Compression-only is fully effective for a bystander | Always | Always |
| Head position | Tilted back | Tilted back | Kept neutral |
| How to blow | Pinch the nose, mouth to mouth | Pinch the nose, mouth to mouth | Cover mouth and nose together |

> In children and infants, cardiac arrest far more often starts as a breathing problem. That is why rescue breaths are not optional for them.

## 2-3. Easy to Overlook
- Swap rescuers every two minutes. Fatigue makes compressions shallower without you noticing.
- Keep any pause in compressions under 10 seconds.
- With multiple casualties, the one who is not breathing comes first.
- In a cold environment, the principle is "cold but not dead" - consider continuing CPR until body temperature is restored.
- In a confined space or a vehicle, move them to a flat surface if you can.

# 3. Automated External Defibrillator (AED)

1. Switch it on.
2. Pad 1 - below the person's right collarbone.
   - For an infant, on the front (center of the chest).
3. Pad 2 - on the person's left side, below the armpit.
   - For an infant, on the back, aligned with pad 1.
4. Follow the voice prompts.
5. Do not touch the person while it is analyzing.
6. Stand clear before the shock is delivered.
7. Resume chest compressions immediately after the shock.
