# While the Ground Is Shaking - Protect Your Body First

1. Get low and cover your head - Get under a table and hold on to a table leg. With no table, wrap your head and neck in a bag, a jacket, or a cushion.
2. Move as little as possible - Walking or running gets you knocked down or hit by falling objects. Drop to your hands and knees and stay low.
3. Hold on until the shaking stops - Evacuation comes after that.

> Do not bolt out of a high-rise while it is still shaking.
> Tall buildings are engineered to sway rather than collapse, and you will not get out during the tens of seconds of shaking anyway. Forcing your way out means falls, falling debris, and collisions with other people. Immediate escape is only worth considering in low-rise brick or mud-walled buildings with little seismic resistance.

---

# Once the Shaking Stops

1. Shut off the electricity and gas, then open a door to secure your way out. Aftershocks can twist the frame and jam the door shut.
2. Put shoes on - always. The floor is covered in broken glass.
3. Take the stairs. Never use the elevator.
   - If you are already in an elevator, press every floor button and get out at the first door that opens.
4. Outside, shield your head with a bag or your hands, keep your distance from buildings, and watch your surroundings as you move.
   - What falls: roof tiles, air-conditioner units, broken glass, signage, bricks, flower pots, stone cladding.
5. Head for open ground - a sports field or a park. Do not use your car.
6. Stay away from downed power lines and any metal or standing water touching them.
7. At the evacuation site, act on verified information from the radio or official announcements.

# What to Do, by Location

| Where you are | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| At home | Shelter under a table → once it stops, cut power and gas, open the door, get out |
| Outdoors | Shield your head with a bag or hands, stay clear of buildings, head for open ground |
| In an elevator | Press all floor buttons, exit at the first stop, then take the stairs |
| At school | Get under a desk and hold the leg → once it stops, move to the field in an orderly line |
| In a store or mall | Avoid falling stock, move toward stairs or a pillar → once it stops, exit |
| Theater or stadium | Shield yourself with a bag, stay in your seat, then evacuate calmly as directed |
| On a train | Grab a strap or pole so you are not thrown → then follow staff instructions |
| Driving | Hazard lights on, slow gradually, pull over to the right shoulder, listen to the radio, and leave the key in the ignition when you evacuate |
| Mountains or coast | Watch for landslides and cliff collapse. If a tsunami alert is issued at the shore, go high immediately |

# If You Are Trapped

- Tap, don't shout. Rapping steadily on a wall or pipe carries and conserves far more energy than yelling.
- Use a whistle if you have one. Cover your mouth to cut down on dust.
- Send a text or share your GPS location by phone. Ration your battery.

# Preparing Ahead of Time

## 1. Set Up Safe Spots at Home
- Decide in advance where you would take cover - under a sturdy table, for example.
- Identify the dangerous spots - near windows, near furniture that could topple - and stay away from them.
- Keep thick indoor shoes on hand so broken glass does not cut you.
- Handle heaters and flammables with care.

## 2. Secure Anything That Can Fall
- Anchor furniture and appliances - bookshelves, lights, mirrors, TVs, computers, refrigerators - so they cannot tip over.
- Do not put televisions, vases, or other droppable items up high.
- Latch cabinet doors so the contents do not spill out.
- Apply shatter-resistant film to window glass.

## 3. Maintain the Building
- Have the gas and electrical systems inspected.
- Check the structure and any walls regularly and repair anything unsound.
- If you find a crack, consult a professional about repair and reinforcement.

## 4. Talk It Through as a Family
- Make sure everyone knows how to shut off the gas and electricity.
- Learn where the nearest open evacuation areas are.
- Agree on where to meet and how to reach each other in an emergency.
- Practice first aid until it is second nature.

## 5. Emergency Supplies
- Know where supplies are stored and how to use them.
- Fires often follow earthquakes - keep a fire extinguisher and know how to operate it.
- A pry bar is worth having for doors jammed by a warped frame.
- Stock protective gear: helmet, gloves, sturdy boots, safety glasses, mask.

# For Older Adults and People with Disabilities

## 1. If You Have Low Vision
- Check radio or television first to understand what has happened.
- When evacuating, move slowly while checking the floor for debris and obstacles, and ask people nearby for help without hesitating.
- To prevent secondary damage, ask someone to check anything involving flame or heat.

## 2. If You Have Limited Mobility
- Do not go it alone - evacuate with a neighbor.
- In a wheelchair or walker, lock the wheels and lean forward, protecting head and neck with a book, cushion, or pillow.
- If you cannot move, wait for help in the safest spot available.
- If you decide to stay home, tell a neighbor or a municipal worker so you are not overlooked.

## 3. If You Have Hearing Loss
- Gather information from captioned broadcasts and your phone.
- If you become unable to move, make noise with a whistle to mark your position.
- Let people nearby know you are deaf or hard of hearing so they can guide you to safety.
- If you have a speech disability, use pen and paper or another communication aid to follow what is happening.

## 4. If You Have a Developmental or Psychiatric Disability
- Do not rush or run outside.
- Follow what you agreed in advance with family or a support worker.
- If deciding on your own is hard, ask someone nearby for help.

# When You Have Children with You

- Use a baby carrier - strollers are impractical during evacuation. Keep a carrier ready, hold the baby in it, and keep your hands free to protect both of you.
- Shoes on, then pick them up - roads are covered in glass and rubble, so carry even children who can walk. Protect the head when carrying on your back, and put shoes on the child whether you carry them in front or on your back.
- Hold hands tightly - so you do not get separated in the chaos. Even with a child who knows the drill, say what to do next out loud and move together.

# After the Earthquake

1. Check, then report - Check your family for injuries and your home for hazards. If someone is hurt, give first aid with your neighbors' help and call 119.
2. Decide whether to go home - Base the decision on the radio and official information.
3. Survey your surroundings - Check your home and workplace for damage, and get a professional inspection if safety is in doubt. Open closets and cabinets carefully - the contents may pour out.
4. Expect aftershocks - They follow the main shock. Do not re-enter a damaged building.
5. Watch for secondary disasters - Tsunami, large fires, landslides, power and water outages, gas supply cuts, chemical or radiation release, and disease can all follow.
6. Ventilate before anything else at home - There may be a gas leak, so air the place out before using any flame or electrical switch.
7. Verify what you hear - Be skeptical of unfounded rumors; follow TV, radio, and official sources.
