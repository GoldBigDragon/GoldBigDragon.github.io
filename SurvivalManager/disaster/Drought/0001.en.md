# Saving Water at Home

## 1. Indoors
- Fill a basin instead of running the tap for dishes, washing your face, brushing your teeth, and rinsing vegetables. Always use a cup when you brush.
- Collect a full load before running the washing machine.
- Shower with a measured amount rather than filling the tub, and turn the water off while you shampoo.
- Shaving, put a little water in the basin and rinse the razor in it.
- Fit a water-saving showerhead or flow restrictor.
- Check taps and pipes thoroughly for leaks. A leak wastes more than anything else.

## 2. Outdoors
- Water the garden with greywater you have already used once.
- Mulch around trees and large plants to slow evaporation and suppress weeds.
- Sweep pavements and roadsides with a broom instead of hosing them down.
- Wash the car with a hose fitted with an automatic shutoff, soaping from a filled bucket and rinsing sparingly.
- Fix leaks in hoses, taps, and connectors.

## 3. Preparing for rationing
- When rationing is announced, secure drinking water and check when the supply will be running.
- Fill the bathtub and large containers with washing water.
- Collect rainwater for the garden and for cleaning.

# On the Farm

## 1. Securing and saving irrigation water
- Inspect regularly: channels, pumps, seeders, electrical equipment, and delivery hoses.
- Impound water in paddies, and maintain channels and paddy bunds to stop losses.
- Save water with intermittent irrigation, communal seedbeds, and dry direct seeding.
- Regulate seedbed watering to prevent seedlings from aging.
- Where transplanting time has passed, sow a different crop.
- Develop available water sources such as wells in advance.
- Plan ahead for replacement seed and alternative crops.

### When water matters most (priority irrigation)
| Period | Growth stage |
| --- | --- |
| May–June | Establishment |
| June–July | Panicle initiation |
| July–August | Booting |

- Keep at least minimal moisture through the establishment period.

## 2. Limiting crop damage
- Apply plastic mulch in upland fields and use water-thrifty cultivation.
- Use portable sprinklers to keep crops from wilting.
- Sow when soil moisture is right, and manage seedlings including pest control.
- Where the ground can be covered, lay rice straw or plastic to cut evaporation.
- Where sowing or transplanting is delayed, grow alternative crops that can be planted late.

# Action by Drought Stage

## Agricultural water

| Stage | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Watch | Water-thrifty cultivation (intermittent irrigation, plastic mulch) / clear channels and remove weeds / draw up a water-saving farming plan |
| Caution | Watch + inspect and service channels, pumps, and hoses / pump return flow from drains and streams / conserve water / implement the water-saving plan |
| Alert | Caution + develop water sources such as wells and boreholes / manage water gates so nothing runs off into the drains |
| Serious | Alert + draw on any available water - groundwater, nearby streams / grow alternative crops where transplanting is impossible or sowing is delayed |

## Municipal and industrial water

| Stage | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Watch | Check for water being wasted |
| Caution | Watch + practice saving at home and school (a cup for brushing, a basin for dishes, shorter showers) |
| Alert | Caution + install and use water-saving fixtures / use rainwater and recycled water |
| Serious | Alert + take part fully in the national drought response, including rationing |

# Health and Hygiene During Drought

- As reservoirs and streams shrink, the concentration of contaminants rises. Do not drink untreated water.
- Store water collected during rationing with the lid on, and boil anything that has stood for a while.
- Hand washing tends to slip when water is short - keep hand sanitizer on hand as well.
- Drought sharply raises wildfire risk. Do not light fires outdoors.
