# Cold Wave Alert Criteria (October–April)

| | Issued when any of the following applies |
| --- | --- |
| Advisory | Morning low drops 10 °C or more from the previous day to 3 °C or below, and 3 °C below normal / morning low of -12 °C or below for two days or more / a sharp drop expected to cause serious damage |
| Warning | Morning low drops 15 °C or more from the previous day to 3 °C or below, and 3 °C below normal / morning low of -15 °C or below for two days or more / serious damage expected across a wide area |

---

# Preparing in Advance

## 1. Health
- Learn the signs and first response for cold injuries (hypothermia, frostbite), and share nearby hospital numbers with family and neighbors.
- Children, elderly people, and those with cardiovascular or cerebrovascular conditions are the most vulnerable.
- People with diabetes or chronic lung disease should get a flu vaccination.
- With children or elderly people at home, check their body temperature and the room temperature often.
- Never let an infant under one year old sleep in a cold room.
- If someone over 65 lives with you, put an easy-to-read thermometer where they will see it. Older people sense temperature change less well, and their lower metabolism makes cold more dangerous.

## 2. Property and supplies
- Service the boiler, pipes, and heaters in advance, and watch for fire hazards.
- Pack water meters, water pipes, and boiler lines with old clothing or insulation and tape the outside shut against cold air.
- In older houses, inspect the transformer ahead of the heating load.
- Ready a flashlight, emergency food and drink, and a portable radio for the outage.
- Keep bottled water for a supply cut and fill the bathtub for washing water.
- Have thermal underwear, a scarf, a hat, and gloves ready.

## 3. Vehicles
- Icy roads make crashes far more likely - avoid long drives.
- Check your winter kit (chains, sandbags, calcium chloride, shovel) and your antifreeze, battery, and lubricants in advance.
- Running the heater with the windows shut reduces oxygen reaching the brain and makes you drowsy. Ventilate at least every 30 minutes.

# During a Cold Wave

## 1. Going out
- Limit outdoor activity; when you go out, cover every exposed area with thermals, a scarf, a hat, and gloves.
- Use public transport where you can, and tell your family where you are going and when.
- Wash your hands and feet when you get back, and avoid heavy drinking and overexertion.
- If you will be out for long and someone frail is home alone, ask a relative or neighbor to look in.
- Call elderly people and people with disabilities who live alone to check on them.

## 2. Preventing frozen pipes
- Leaving the house for a long period, open a tap slightly so water keeps moving.
- If a pipe freezes, thaw it with lukewarm water or a hair dryer. Never use an open flame.
- If a pipe bursts, contact your local government or water utility.
- Limit heavy use of electric heaters and keep flammable items away from them.
- Report electrical faults to KEPCO (123) or Korea Electrical Safety Corporation (1588-7500).

## 3. Driving
- Clear the frost from the windshield completely before setting off.
- Drive slower than usual and leave generous following distance.
- On slippery, icy, or curving roads, avoid accelerating and braking hard - slow down in advance.

## 4. If your car is stranded
- Call for rescue by any means available, keep warm with your passengers, and take turns resting.
- Clap your hands and move your limbs - light movement keeps circulation going.
- One person must stay awake.
- At night, turn on the interior light or spread brightly colored clothing on the snow.
- Stay with the vehicle if you can. If you must leave it, leave your contact number and the key in the car.
- If the exhaust is blocked by snow, carbon monoxide enters the cabin - clear it before running the engine and keep a window cracked.

# Treating Cold Injuries

## 1. Hypothermia
Signs - slurred speech or memory lapses / clouding consciousness / persistent exhaustion / violent shivering in the limbs

What to do
1. Get to a hospital quickly or call 119.
2. Remove wet clothing and wrap the person in a blanket or sleeping bag.
3. Place heat packs or hot water bottles under the armpits and on the abdomen. With nothing available, direct body contact from another person works.
4. If they are conscious, a warm drink helps - but give nothing at all to someone who is not fully conscious.

> Severe chills, memory loss, disorientation, slurred speech, or overwhelming fatigue mean suspected hypothermia: go to a hospital immediately.

## 2. Frostbite
By degree
- First: stabbing pain, redness and itching, swelling
- Second: skin turns dark red and blisters form
- Third: skin and subcutaneous tissue die, sensation is lost
- Fourth: necrosis reaches muscle and bone

What to do
1. Medical care comes first.
2. Move the person somewhere warm.
3. Immerse the affected area in 38–42 °C water - warm enough to be comfortable for an unaffected hand.
4. Face and ears: apply warm wet cloths and change them often.
5. Hands and feet: place sterile dry gauze between the fingers and toes to absorb moisture and keep them from sticking together.
6. Keep the area slightly elevated to reduce swelling and pain.
7. Carry someone with frostbitten legs or feet on a stretcher. They must not walk, even after rewarming.

> Do not rub the area or put it near a flame. Either causes further tissue damage.

## 3. Falls
What to do
1. If the person cannot move on their own, call 119 quickly.
2. Immobilize them in whatever position is comfortable and get help. Use a splint if needed.

Reducing falls on ice
- Shorten your stride by 10–20 percent.
- Wear low-heeled shoes with non-slip soles.
- Keep your hands out of your pockets, and do not walk looking at your phone.
- Try not to carry things in your hands.
- Avoid shaded patches and do not turn sharply.
- Skip heavy, bulky coats that restrict movement.
- If you start to fall, drop low and sit down or roll sideways to limit the impact.
- If you take sedatives, sleeping pills, or anything that causes dizziness, stay in.
- Watch for curbs, wooden decking, and outdoor courts - they get slick.

# Farms and Fisheries

## 1. Crops
- Cover with plastic, rice straw, or non-woven fabric to prevent frost damage.
- Prepare greenhouse heating, thermal curtains, and heat-storage bags in advance.
- Inspect electric equipment such as heat blowers beforehand.
- If an extreme drop is forecast, harvest early and store.

## 2. Livestock barns
- Prepare insulating covers and heaters against drafts and hold the interior at a suitable temperature.
- Check wiring insulation and service heating equipment to prevent fire.
- Increase feed by 10–20 percent to help the animals maintain body heat.

## 3. Aquaculture
- Set aside at least 1 percent of the pond area as an overwintering space, and insulate it with windbreak netting.
- If a prolonged cold wave is forecast, harvest the fish early to avoid losses.
