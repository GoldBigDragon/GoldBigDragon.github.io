# A Typhoon Brings Wind and Water Together

Typhoon damage comes from high wind, heavy rain, storm surge, and landslides arriving at once. Preparing for only one of them is not enough.

---

# When a Typhoon Is Forecast

## 1. Information and planning
- Receive alerts through TV, radio, the internet, and the [Safety Stepping Stone app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=kr.go.nema.disasteralert_new).
- Work out the track and arrival time and build your evacuation plan around them.
- Pack emergency supplies into a bag, and fill the bathtub in case the water supply is cut.
- Ready a flashlight and batteries for the power outage.
- Check on elderly neighbors, children, and people with disabilities, and tell them how to evacuate.

## 2. Move out of risk areas
- If you are in the mountains, a valley, near a river, or on a breakwater, move to safety with the people around you.
- Stop hiking, camping, swimming, and fishing immediately.
- If you are in a low-lying or flood-prone area, a landslide zone, an underground space, or an aging building, relocate.

## 3. Against the wind
- Replace or reinforce aging windows in advance and apply safety film.
- Seal the gap between frame and glass so the pane cannot rattle. Tape only helps if it holds the glass firmly to the frame.
- Secure and reinforce roofing, signage, and towers.
- Bring in or remove everything outside that the wind could lift.
- Reinforce weak points on greenhouses and clear the area around them.
- Inspect connections on exposed wiring and replace what needs it.

## 4. Against flooding
- Check the drains around the house and clear any blockage.
- Place sandbags and flood barriers at underground garages and similar entrances.
- Move cars parked near rivers, beaches, or in low ground, and leave your phone number on the car.
- Clear farm channels, tidy paddy embankments, and adjust water gates only before the rain starts (at the pre-advisory stage).
- Moor boats securely or haul them ashore, and move nets and gear.

# During the Typhoon Advisory

## 1. Do not go outside
- Once an advisory is in effect, stay in.
- Do not go out to secure a boat or move nets and gear.
- Do not go out to check paddy embankments or water gates. A large share of typhoon deaths happen exactly this way.
- Vessels under way should report their position to nearby ships and the coast guard and move as far from the track as possible.

## 2. Places to keep away from
- Flooded roads, underpasses, submersible bridges, and bridges are closed to people and vehicles, even with shallow water.
- Do not go near valleys, riverbanks, or shorelines - a torrent can sweep you away.
- Keep away from construction sites, streetlights, traffic signals, utility poles, and underground spaces.
- Slopes, retaining walls, and embankments can collapse - stay clear.
- If you are in a mobile home or temporary structure, move to a solid building at once.
- In a landslide zone, evacuate even if no alert has been issued.

## 3. Indoors
- Close doors and windows to prevent damage, and keep well away from glass.
- Move to a windowless room such as a bathroom, or the innermost part of the house.
- Shut off the gas in advance, and never touch electrical equipment with wet hands.
- In a blackout, use a flashlight or your phone, not candles.
- If any water enters the building, move to higher ground immediately.

## 4. Outdoors and driving
- Stop all outdoor activity and get inside.
- Slow down and drive defensively, watching oncoming traffic and the vehicle in the next lane. A gust can push a car out of its lane.
- Suspend outdoor work and crane operations - construction materials topple.
- Keep away from glass and signage, and shelter in an open lot or inside a building.
- If thunder follows the flash within 30 seconds, get to shelter at once.

# After the Typhoon

## 1. Checking and reporting
- Confirm that family and friends are safe; if someone is unreachable and may be missing, report it to the police.
- Flooded roads and bridges may be structurally damaged - stay out of affected areas.
- River embankments and slopes with saturated ground can give way. Do not go near them.
- If you are cut off, do not try to wade across water; call 119 or the community service center, or ask for help nearby.
- Before re-entering your home, confirm it is safe.
- Photograph damaged property before anything is repaired, then report it to your city, county, or district office.

## 2. Preventing secondary damage
- Receding water is often contaminated with oil and animal carcasses - keep well away from it.
- Confirm that tap water and stored water are safe before use, and discard any flooded food or ingredients; food poisoning is a real risk.
- In a flooded home, check the gas and electrical breakers and use them only after inspection by Korea Gas Safety Corporation (1544-4500), Korea Electrical Safety Corporation (1588-7500), or a qualified professional.
- Gas may have leaked: open the windows and ventilate thoroughly, and use no match or lighter until you have.
- Never approach or touch a downed power line. Report damaged electrical equipment to 119 or your local office.
- If a power line has fallen on your car, stay inside it, avoid touching metal parts, warn bystanders, and call 119.
- Stand fallen crops back up; after draining flooded fields, wash soil and debris off the plants and apply emergency pest and disease control.
