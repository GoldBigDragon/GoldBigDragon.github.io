# Forecast Categories

| | Good | Moderate | Unhealthy | Very Unhealthy |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| PM10 (µg/m³) | 0–30 | 31–80 | 81–150 | 151 and above |
| PM2.5 (µg/m³) | 0–15 | 16–35 | 36–75 | 76 and above |
| Ozone (O₃) (ppm) | 0–0.03 | 0.031–0.09 | 0.091–0.15 | above 0.15 |

## What particulate matter is
- A mixture of fine particles - carbon compounds, ions (sulfate, nitrate, ammonium), and mineral matter.
- PM10: particles 10 µm in diameter or smaller
- PM2.5: particles 2.5 µm or smaller - one twentieth to one thirtieth the width of a human hair (about 60 µm)

## How it affects the body
- The smaller the particle, the more it passes the nasal mucosa and reaches the alveoli directly.
- Long-term inhalation can raise the incidence of asthma and lung disease and the rate of premature death.
- Short exposure does not cause sudden symptoms. The danger is prolonged, repeated exposure.
- Sensitive groups - children, older adults, and people with respiratory or heart conditions - are affected more than others and need extra care.

# What to Do at Each Level

## 1. "Unhealthy" (particulate matter advisory)
- Sensitive groups: limit outdoor activity and stay indoors.
- Everyone else: reduce long or strenuous outdoor activity. If your eyes sting or you have a cough or sore throat, stay in.
- Wear a mask if you must go out.
- Avoid traveling through heavy-traffic areas.
- Schools should avoid outdoor classes; public facilities and outdoor sports venues restrict operations.

## 2. "Very Unhealthy" (particulate matter warning)
- Sensitive groups: outdoor activity is prohibited.
- Everyone else: avoid long or strenuous outdoor activity, and stay inside with a cough or sore throat.
- Wear a mask if you must go out.
- Do not travel through heavy-traffic areas.
- Kindergartens and elementary schools prohibit outdoor classes and shorten the day or close. Middle and high schools avoid outdoor classes.
- Public facilities and outdoor sports venues suspend operations.
- Managers of parks, sports facilities, palaces, terminals, railways, and subways advise visitors to limit outdoor exertion.

> Basis: Enforcement Rule of the Clean Air Conservation Act, Table 7 - air pollutant concentration thresholds by alert level (relating to Article 14)

## 3. Choosing a mask
- Use KF80 or better; for heavy fine particulate, KF94 or better.
- It only works if it covers nose and mouth completely with no air leaking around the edges.
- Replace a mask that is wet or soiled. Plan on two or more per person per day.
- If you have difficulty breathing (with a heart or lung condition), consult a doctor before using one.

# Before a Yellow Dust Event

## 1. At home
- Check windows and seals so dust cannot get in.
- Prepare goggles, a mask, and long-sleeved clothing for going out.
- Elderly people and those with respiratory conditions should limit outdoor activity.

## 2. Schools
- Consider adjusting arrival and dismissal times, shortening the day, or moving classes indoors.
- Check the student emergency contact list and keep the chain active.
- Confirm masks and first-aid supplies are stocked and air purifiers work.
- Inform parents of the guidance and brief students.

## 3. Farms
- Prepare to move outdoor livestock into barns.
- Ready plastic sheeting to cover straw and feed stored outside.
- Check power sprayers and other washing equipment.
- Inspect greenhouse doors and vents.

## 4. Care facilities
- Check residents' health and carry out hygiene inspections.
- Confirm masks and first-aid supplies and check air purifiers.
- Inform families of the guidance and brief residents.

---

# During a Yellow Dust Event

## 1. At home
- Close the windows and stay in. At Alert and Serious crisis levels, outdoor activity is prohibited.
- Wear goggles and a mask outside, and wash your hands and feet thoroughly on returning.
- Wash vegetables, fruit, and other produce thoroughly before eating.

## 2. Schools
- Restrict PE, field trips, and other outdoor activity - prohibited at Alert and Serious levels.
- Consider adjusting school hours and shortening the day; at Alert and Serious levels, consider temporary closure.
- Students wear masks and protective gear to and from school.
- Close the windows and run air purifiers.
- Monitor the health of exposed students regularly.

## 3. Farms
- Move outdoor livestock into barns promptly.
- Close doors and windows on greenhouses and barns to shut out outside air.
- Cover stored hay and straw with plastic or tarpaulin.

## 4. Care facilities
- Restrict outdoor activity - prohibited at Alert and Serious levels.
- Close windows and run air purifiers.
- Give special attention to residents with respiratory conditions, including medical review.

---

# After a Yellow Dust Event

- At home: ventilate the indoor air and wash any contaminated items thoroughly before use.
- Schools: disinfect and clean indoors and out, and let students with colds or eye problems rest or go home early.
- Farms: wash and disinfect barns, feed troughs, and any equipment that contacts livestock. Brush dust off exposed animals and disinfect them, watch for disease, and report sick animals immediately. Clear dust from greenhouses.
- Care facilities: disinfect and clean indoors and out, and let residents rest or see a doctor as their condition requires.
