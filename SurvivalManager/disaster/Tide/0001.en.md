# Spring Tides and the Baekjung Tide

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Spring tide (사리) | The period when the difference between high and low water is greatest. It occurs when the moon and sun lie in the same direction or directly opposite across the Earth |
| Daejogi (대조기) | The two to four days following a new or full moon, when tidal currents run comparatively fast |
| Baekjung tide (백중사리) | The spring tide around the full moon of the seventh lunar month (Baekjung) - one of the times when sea level rises highest all year |

> Why a few days after the new or full moon, rather than on the day itself?
> Seawater responds not only to the pull of the moon and sun but to coastal topography and seabed friction. So the actual peak arrives one to three days late.

# Preparing in Advance

## 1. Know Your Own Area
- Look up what damage has occurred in your area in the past, and share it with family and neighbors.
- Identify the low-lying flood-risk areas where you live and work.
- Mark the Baekjung tide and spring tide periods on your calendar in advance.

## 2. Be Able to Receive Warnings
- Have a TV and radio ready, and install the [Safety Dideemdol app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=kr.go.nema.disasteralert_new) on your phone.
- Make sure your family and neighbors are set up to receive hazard information too.

## 3. The Evacuation Plan
- Agree on where to meet in case family members end up moving separately.
- Settle in advance who does what, when, and how.
- Know the local evacuation sites, and make sure children know them too.
  - Check [Safe Korea 24](https://www.safekorea.go.kr/safekorea-kor/main/main.doidsiSFK/neo/sfk/cs/contents/civil_defense/SDIJKM1402.html) or your local authority's site for temporary shelters and emergency housing.
- If an emergency looks likely, contact your family immediately and move together to safety.
- If it is urgent, leave first, then contact your family separately and tell them your route.
- In areas that flood often, move belongings to higher ground in advance to prevent secondary damage.

## 4. Emergency Supplies
Keep them in one place, ready to pick up and go.
- First-aid supplies, flashlight, drinking water, emergency food
- Radio, phone charger
- Portable stove, blankets
- If you have a car, keep the tank full. If you do not, arrange in advance to travel with a neighbor who does.

## 5. Maintenance
- Check your household drains and the gutters around the house so water runs away properly.
- Keep checking expiry dates on emergency supplies and replace them.
- Re-confirm evacuation sites and routes periodically.

# When a Sea Level Rise Is Forecast

## 1. Stay Off the Shore
- When a rise is forecast, stay away from the shore and tell others.
- During a spring tide, cancel tidal flat outings, local festivals, fishing, and sightseeing.
- In areas at risk of flooding, residents and vehicles move to higher ground together.

## 2. Get to High Ground
- If flooding is likely, move to higher ground together with the people around you.
- From the shoreline, rock ledges, breakwaters, and low-lying roads - anywhere that can flood - move to high ground immediately.
- Once flooding or overtopping actually begins, follow emergency broadcasts or the instructions of officials, police, and firefighters and move calmly but quickly to a tall building or high ground.
- Keep order, and put older people and children first.
- If someone is injured, call for help nearby and call 119.

## 3. Do Not Approach Danger Zones
> A sea level rise combined with strong winds and heavy seas brings swell waves and surges.
> Stay off coastal roads, breakwaters, beaches, and rock ledges.

- If you are cut off on a rock ledge or a tidal flat, call 119 or 112 at once or call for help nearby, and get as high as you can.

## 4. Watch for Secondary Harm
> Flooding and overtopping bring a risk of electrocution.
> Do not touch or go near utility poles, streetlights, traffic signals, or a house's electrical fittings.

- On a flooded road, neither drive nor walk.
- Leaving the house, shut the water and gas valves and throw the main breaker.

## 5. Reporting
- Check on the safety of your surroundings together, and report and share any hazard you find.
- Contact anyone you know living in or travelling to a coastal area to confirm they are safe, and report a suspected disappearance to the nearest police station.
- Report hazards through 112, 119, or the Safety Report (안전신문고) app.
