# When It Happens - the Order of Things

1. Raise the alarm - Shout or press the emergency bell to tell the crew, and call 119.
2. Put on a life jacket - They are under the seats or in the cabin. Follow the crew's instructions.
3. Layer thin clothing - It preserves body heat in the water.
4. Move to the escape route - Follow the crew, but do not let yourself be carried along by the crowd.
5. Board the lifeboat or life raft - in order, as directed.

> The leading cause of death after abandoning ship is not drowning - it is hypothermia.
> If you cannot swim or have to wait for rescue, hold the Tuck Float: wrap both arms around your legs and pull your knees to your chest. It slows the loss of heat and energy.

# By Situation

## 1. If the Vessel Lists or Floods
- Cabinets and tables slide and can crush you. Move away from heavy furniture.
- If a door will not open, break the window along its edge with the emergency axe and get out. Edges break far more easily than the center.
- If there is a fire or an injured person, tell the crew.

## 2. If You Must Jump into the Sea
1. Keep your legs straight.
2. Cover your mouth and nose with one hand.
3. Jump as far from the hull as you can.

## 3. If There Is a Fire on Board
1. Shout "Fire!" and press the emergency bell.
2. Try to knock it down with an extinguisher, but if it is too dangerous, leave at once.
3. Cover your mouth and nose and follow the crew to the escape route.
4. Help older and less mobile passengers out with you.

# If You Pull Out Someone Who Is Unconscious

## 1. Immediately After the Rescue
- Call the lifeguards or 119.
- Start rescue breathing. Drowning kills through lack of oxygen, so getting air in comes first. Begin in the water, during the rescue, if you can.
- From there on, treat as standard CPR.

> Do not press on the abdomen or back to "get the water out."
> Water and food in the stomach can come back up and block the airway, and vomit can be drawn into the lungs. Above all, every second rescue breathing is delayed cuts the chance of survival.

## 2. Rewarming
1. Move the person somewhere dry and warm.
2. Remove all wet clothing, and warm the chest with hot water bottles or towels soaked in warm water.
3. Wrap the whole body, head and neck included, in blankets.
   - Heat escapes fastest from the head, neck, chest, and groin.
4. If the person is conscious, give warm drinks. No alcohol.
5. If possible, rewarm by skin-to-skin contact inside a sleeping bag.

## 3. How Hypothermia Progresses
| Stage | What is happening |
| --- | --- |
| Early | Skin feels cold, circulation drops, and hands and feet chill first |
| Developing | Involuntary shivering begins as the body sheds heat |
| Worsening | The core starts to cool, and the body cuts peripheral blood flow further to protect vital organs |
| Dangerous | Shivering weakens or stops and internal organs begin to suffer |
| Critical | The brain cools, coordination fails, consciousness is lost. Without immediate, correct treatment this is fatal |

> Shivering stopping is not recovery - it is deterioration.

# Safety Rules on Board

## 1. Passengers
- Board in an orderly way, without running or horseplay, and hold the handrail on stairs.
- Once aboard, go to your assigned cabin or area and listen to the safety announcement all the way through.
- Learn the escape routes and check where the life jackets are and how to put one on.
- Do not touch machinery or equipment.
- Never ask to board a vessel beyond its capacity.
- Follow the instructions and warnings of the crew and staff.
- Do not drink or otherwise disrupt order on board.
- Do not damage life-saving gear or ship's equipment.
- Do not bring or transport explosives, flammable liquids, or high-pressure gas.

## 2. Fishing Crew
- Wear a life jacket.
- In an emergency, hit the SOS button without hesitating.
- Put out a fire quickly with the onboard extinguisher.
- Keep hands and clothing clear of the net hauler.
