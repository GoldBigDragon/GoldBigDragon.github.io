# 7. Getting Ready

## 7-1. What You Can Decontaminate With

| Item | Use |
| :---: | :--- |
| Saline | Wounds; eyes, ears, nose, mouth |
| 3% hydrogen peroxide | Secondary wound irrigation |
| Soap | Body |
| Shampoo | Body |
| Baking soda | Body, mouth, objects |
| Detergent | Objects |
| Bleach | Objects |

## 7-2. The Decontamination Space
- Set aside a space and cover the floor with plastic so no contamination remains in your shelter.
- Prepare it in advance so the contaminated runoff does not contaminate the shelter itself.

## 7-3. Contaminated Clothing and Belongings
1. Remove outer clothing, watch, and jewelry before entering the shelter space.
2. Seal them in a plastic bag or sealable container, then leave them somewhere safe outside the house, away from people and pets.
3. Undress slowly and carefully so contamination does not become airborne.

> Pulling clothing over your head puts contaminated particles on your face.
> Where possible, cut the clothing off.

# 8. Decontamination by Area

## 8-1. The Order - Strictly This Order
> ① Open wounds → ② Body openings (eyes, ears, nose, mouth) → ③ Unbroken skin
>
> The order exists to minimize what gets inside the body.

## 8-2. Wounds
- Irrigate thoroughly with saline, and if needed rinse again with 3% hydrogen peroxide.
- Wipe with gauze and cover with sterile waterproof material against further contamination.
- Radioactive material enters the body directly through a wound, which is why wounds come first.

## 8-3. Full-Body Shower
- Wash from head to toe with clean, unexposed lukewarm water and neutral soap.
  - Hot water opens the pores and promotes absorption of radioactive material. Use lukewarm water.
- Tilt your head back to wash your hair. Bending forward runs contaminated water down your face.
- Shampoo at least twice. Hair holds radioactive particles stubbornly.
- Never use conditioner. It binds radioactive material to the hair protein and makes decontamination harder.
- Keep water out of eyes, nose, mouth, and ears while washing.
- Do not scrub with an exfoliating cloth or a rough towel. Micro-tears let radioactive material into the body.
- Wash ears, skin folds, and under the nails carefully too.
- Afterwards, dry gently with a clean towel and change into uncontaminated clothes.
- If you cannot shower, wipe exposed skin with a wet towel.

## 8-4. Eyes
- If the eyeball is undamaged, rinse thoroughly with water or saline.
- Direct the flow from the inner corner outward, so nothing travels through the tear duct into the nose.

## 8-5. Ears
> The shock wave may have perforated an eardrum. Check its condition before you start.

- With a perforation, do not irrigate - go to hospital for treatment.
- If undamaged, rinse the outer ear canal with water or saline and wipe with a damp cotton swab.

## 8-6. Nose
- If conscious, have the person blow their nose.
- Wipe with a damp cotton swab, taking care not to damage the lining.

## 8-7. Mouth
- Wipe around the mouth and rinse with saline or water two or three times or more.
- Brush gently with toothbrush and toothpaste so as not to cut the gums, and rinse often.
- Take care not to swallow the rinse.

## 8-8. Waste
- Seal the rinse water and the tools you used in plastic and store them somewhere safe.
- This waste is radioactively contaminated. Do not dispose of it casually - follow the authorities' instructions.
