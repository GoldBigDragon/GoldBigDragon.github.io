# 9. Long-Term Sheltering

## 9-1. Understanding the Situation
> With the infrastructure down, TV, internet, and phones may not work.
> A battery-powered radio becomes your central means of getting information.

- Use radio and other government broadcasts to establish the blast location, wind direction, and damage.
- Do not be swayed by rumors on social media - trust only official announcements.

## 9-2. Managing Supplies
- Take stock of what you have and organize what is needed to last more than two weeks (water, food).
- Use supplies sparingly and plan the distribution.

> Drink only sealed bottled water.
> Municipal treatment plants, including advanced treatment facilities, cannot adequately purify radioactively contaminated water.

> Eat only sealed food. Anything left exposed may be contaminated with fallout.

## 9-3. Rules for Living Together
> People gathered in a shelter are exposed to fear, panic, and anxiety, and serious problems can follow.

In a shared shelter, choose a representative and agree on rules.

- Keep a roster with brief personal details for each person
- Look after the vulnerable - older people, children, the sick
- Decide what goes where, and how it is set up
- Divide the necessary roles and cooperate
- Observe the priority of use and the criteria for distributing supplies
- Protect privacy in the shelter (especially families with infants and children, and women)
- Arrange and take part in shelter security (watches, patrols to prevent crime)
- Anything else is decided collectively through the representative

## 9-4. Shelter Conduct
- Do not block entrances or passageways, and do not cook with fire.
- Keep shared facilities and supplies clean and use them sparingly.
- Reduce unnecessary power use and do nothing that risks fire.
- Keep phone use to a minimum so the network does not collapse.
- Tell the manager immediately if first aid is needed.
- Share information with family and neighbors who are less able to act on their own.
- At night, turn off the lights and stop any light escaping.
- Fill the bathtub or buckets against loss of water supply.
- If the building is damaged, get out quickly by the nearest emergency exit or stairwell and move as far away as possible in case of collapse.

## 9-5. Health
- Treat and cover wounds and burns so fallout cannot get in and infection is prevented.
- An unsanitary environment and weakened immunity make infection likely. Personal hygiene matters more than usual.
- Eat and drink only what is clean, and never eat spoiled food.
- If the surroundings are unsanitary or there is an infectious patient, wear a mask at all times.
- Hand sanitizer is a great help when there is no water or soap.
- Maintain oral hygiene with regular brushing or mouthwash.
- Keep your strength up with adequate nutrition and fluids, and as much sleep and rest as you can get.

# 10. Acute Radiation Syndrome

## 10-1. When It Occurs
- It occurs at exposures of 1,000 mSv (1 Sv) or more.
- At 3,500 mSv (3.5 Sv) or more, roughly half of those exposed may die.
- Even below lethal doses, vomiting, hair loss, fatigue, infection, and loss of appetite can appear.

> Radiation damages the fastest-dividing tissues first - the gastrointestinal lining, bone marrow, blood cells, and hair follicles.

## 10-2. Treatment and Recovery
- Medical care is needed, but after a detonation the sheer number of patients may make it hard to get.
- The six weeks after exposure are especially dangerous.
- Recovery then comes gradually over several months.

## 10-3. If It Develops
> Immunity is severely weakened, so infection prevention and personal hygiene demand particular care.

- If the surroundings are unsanitary or there is an infectious patient, wear a mask at all times.
- Eat and drink only what is clean, and never eat spoiled food.
- Hand sanitizer is a great help when there is no water or soap.
- Disinfect and dress wounds thoroughly. Povidone iodine or a combination antibiotic ointment is useful.

> Platelet count and function drop, so bleeding does not stop easily.
> Avoid cuts and knocks. Intracranial bleeding can be fatal - take particular care not to hit your head.

- Avoid shaving, and take care brushing your teeth and cleaning up after using the toilet.
- The gastrointestinal lining is damaged. Avoid irritating foods and eat soft, easily digested food.

# 11. Radiation Protection Reference

## 11-1. Dose Rate Thresholds

| Level | Threshold | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Natural background | 0.05–0.3 μSv/h | Always measurable in the natural state |
| Warning level | 1 μSv/h or above | Restriction on food consumption is advised |
| Emergency level | 1,000 μSv/h (1 mSv/h) or above | Relocation (leaving the contaminated area short-term) or evacuation is required |

## 11-2. Cumulative Dose Thresholds
Cumulative dose = dose rate × exposure time
(For example, 3 hours at 5 μSv/h gives 15 μSv.)

| Threshold | Dose |
| --- | --- |
| Global average annual natural dose | 2.4 mSv (2,400 μSv) |
| Emergency protective action: shelter in place | 10 mSv or above |
| Emergency protective action: relocation | 50 mSv or above |
| Temporary resettlement | 30 mSv first month, 10 mSv thereafter |
| Emergency protective action: distribution of thyroid blocking agent | 100 mSv or above thyroid absorbed dose |
| Acute radiation syndrome onset | 1,000 mSv (1 Sv) or above |
| Dose at which roughly half die | 3,500 mSv (3.5 Sv) |

## 11-3. Thyroid Blocking Agent
- Potassium iodide (KI) tablets prevent radioactive iodine accumulating in the thyroid and so prevent thyroid cancer.

> Do not take it on your own initiative. Take it only as the government directs.
