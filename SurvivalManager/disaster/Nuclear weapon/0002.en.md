# 4. When an Attack Is Imminent

1. On an air raid or nuclear warning, shut the gas valve and throw the main breaker. This prevents secondary fires from the blast.
2. Take your emergency pack and move quickly to the basement of a reinforced concrete structure - civil defense shelter, subway station, underground mall, tunnel.
3. If you cannot get underground, use the innermost part of a reinforced concrete building, furthest from windows, fire-protection or evacuation facilities, or a ditch.
4. Avoid the top floor. Fallout accumulates on the roof and radiation there is high. A middle floor is best.
5. Put on a gas mask and, if you can, a protective suit. Without one, use rain gear or a long outer garment and a hat to keep radioactive particles off your body and clothes.
6. Take 1 g of vitamin C (adult dose) to mitigate genetic damage from radiation exposure.

# 5. The Moment of Detonation

## 5-1. Get Flat Immediately

> Move behind cover (a concrete wall, heavy furniture, a vehicle) and lie down facing away from the blast center.
> 1. Keep your abdomen off the ground. - Reduces organ damage from the shock wave travelling through the earth.
> 2. Open your mouth. - Prevents eardrum rupture and lung injury from the sudden pressure change.
> 3. Cover your eyes and ears with both hands. - Your eyelids are not thick enough to block the flash. To avoid permanent blindness you must cover your eyes with your hands.

- Thermal radiation lasts several seconds. Getting flat the instant you register the blast reduces the burn injury.

## 5-2. Brace for the Shock Wave
- Depending on distance, the shock wave arrives immediately or tens of seconds later.
- Broken glass and debris fly at bullet speed. Get as far from windows as you can.
- In the seconds before it arrives, get behind cover and flat on the floor, or move quickly to the center of the building.

## 5-3. After the Blast Wind Passes
- Stay flat until the wind has passed and debris has stopped falling.
  - Getting up too soon means injury from the returning reverse wind or further falling debris.
- Once debris stops, prepare for fallout immediately.
  - Put on a KF94 mask, or better, a high-grade respirator or a KS M 6685 certified gas mask.
  - Use a protective suit or rain gear, or a long outer garment and a hat, to keep particles off your body and clothes.

# 6. Sheltering from Fallout

## 6-1. What Fallout Is
- Fallout is radioactive particles melted and refrozen by the extreme heat, beginning to fall about one minute after the blast.
- After a surface burst fallout begins almost at once. Get indoors or underground immediately.
- Fallout travels a considerable distance on the wind. Prepare even if the detonation was far away.
- It falls as snow, rain, and dust over a considerable period, causing radiation exposure and contaminating soil, water, and air for a long time.

## 6-2. The Decay Curve - the Single Most Important Piece of Survival Knowledge

Taking one hour after the detonation as the baseline:

| Time elapsed | Radiation intensity |
| --- | --- |
| About 6 hours | 1/10 |
| 2 days | 1/100 |
| 2 weeks | 1/1,000 |

> This curve applies to nuclear detonations only - not to other radiological releases such as a nuclear power plant accident.

So at least about two weeks of sheltering is needed after a detonation. After two weeks the level has fallen to a thousandth of the initial, minimizing the harm.

## 6-3. Choosing Where to Shelter
- If you can, get out of the affected area before fallout begins.
- If it has already started, get indoors or underground immediately.
- Available in a city: civil defense shelters, subway stations, underground malls, building basements
- If you cannot get underground, a middle floor of a concrete building, the innermost part, a space with no windows is relatively safe.

> Avoid the top floor without exception. Fallout accumulates on the roof and radiation there is high.
> A middle floor - distant from both the fallout on the roof and the fallout on the ground - is safest.

## 6-4. Sealing the Space
1. Block the doors and vents with plastic, cloth, and aluminum tape so fallout cannot get in.
2. Switch off all ventilation - air conditioning, extractor fans, air purifiers.
3. Seal around anything connected to the outside (the air conditioner's outdoor unit, for example) with cling film and adhesive tape.
4. If time is short, move quickly to a building basement, the refuge facility of an apartment or house, or a windowless bathroom inside the building, seal the entrance and vents, and use it as a temporary shelter.

## 6-5. If You Have to Move
> Once fallout or rain begins, never move around outside.

- If you have no choice, use protective clothing, rain gear, and an umbrella to keep fallout off your body.
- Always wear a gas mask or face mask, and minimize exposed skin with a protective suit or long clothing.
- Move as fast as you can, and discard anything exposed to fallout before going indoors.
