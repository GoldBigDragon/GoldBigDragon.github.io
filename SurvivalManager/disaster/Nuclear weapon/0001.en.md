# 1. The Five Effects of a Nuclear Detonation

A nuclear detonation produces five effects simultaneously.

| Effect | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| ① Thermal radiation | Intense light and heat lasting several seconds after the blast. Causes temporary or permanent blindness, burns, and large-scale fires at considerable distances |
| ② Initial radiation | Highly penetrating radiation released instantly. Causes acute radiation syndrome in everything near the detonation point |
| ③ Blast and shock | Powerful wind and shock waves destroy buildings and scatter debris. Reinforced concrete buildings rarely collapse, but flying window glass injures the people inside |
| ④ Residual radiation and fallout | Radioactive particles melted and refrozen by the extreme heat, falling from one minute after the blast. Emits lethal radiation for a long time and causes internal exposure |
| ⑤ EMP (electromagnetic pulse) | A powerful electromagnetic wave released instantly destroys electronic circuits. A high-altitude detonation permanently disables electronics and vehicles over a wide area and causes mass blackout |

Energy distribution: blast 50% · thermal radiation 35% · residual radiation (fallout) 10% · initial nuclear radiation 4% · EMP 1%

# 2. Yield and Damage Radius

Based on the 20 kt bomb dropped on Nagasaki in 1945

| Effect | Range of damage |
| --- | --- |
| Blast | Structural and human casualties within 4 km |
| Thermal radiation | Structural and human casualties within 4 km, total burnout within 2.5 km |
| Radiation | Human casualties within at least 30 km |

> Most intercontinental ballistic missile warheads currently deployed by nuclear states are 150 kt to 800 kt.
> They can cause damage tens of times greater than the Nagasaki bomb.

# 3. Preparing in Advance

## 3-1. Know Your Route and Shelter
> Underground facilities give the most effective shielding from thermal radiation, blast, initial radiation, and fallout.

- Identify the nearest underground facility to home, work, and school - civil defense shelter, subway station, underground parking, underground mall, tunnel - and how to get there.
- Searching "민방위 대피소" (civil defense shelter) in Naver Map, Kakao Map, T map, or the Safety Dideemdol app shows the nearest one.

## 3-2. Stockpile Supplies
- Sheltering for two weeks or more may be necessary. Keep two weeks of supplies underground.
- Emergency food should center on things needing no cooking - canned goods, hard biscuits, energy bars, freeze-dried meals - in several varieties for nutritional balance.
- Water: at least 2.5 liters per person per day.
- Against power loss: flashlight with batteries, candles, a lighter or matches
- Against gas loss: portable gas stove, butane canisters

## 3-3. Emergency Pack and Protective Gear
> In a nuclear attack there is no time to gather anything. The pack must be packed in advance.

- A KS M 6685 certified gas mask, or a KF94 mask or class 1 or better respirator. A gas mask is effective at blocking inhalation of fallout particles.
- Protective suit, first-aid supplies
- Safety glasses - they keep radioactive particles out of your eyes.

## 3-4. Radiation Meter
> Radiation cannot be sensed at all - only a meter will tell you.

It lets you gauge contamination where you are and confirm that skin and equipment have been decontaminated - genuinely valuable equipment for this scenario.

## 3-5. Vitamin C
- Vitamin C reduces genetic damage from radiation exposure.
- Taking 1 g (adult dose) before an expected attack can somewhat mitigate that damage.

## 3-6. Blocking Thermal Radiation
- Thermal radiation enters through windows and sets curtains and furniture alight.
- Applying aluminum foil to windows in advance reflects it and reduces the risk of blindness, burns, and fire inside.

## 3-7. EMP - the Faraday Cage
- Storing important electronics - radio, flashlight - in a sealed metal can in advance reduces EMP damage.
- Use paper or bubble wrap to keep the device away from the can's walls.
- But ordinary materials cannot block EMP completely - some will penetrate.

> A device already destroyed by EMP gains nothing from the can. It has to be stored beforehand.

- EMP can destroy a flashlight. Also keep non-electronic light - light sticks or tea lights (small flat candles).

## 3-8. Family Plan and Clothing
- EMP can paralyze the communications infrastructure completely. An agreement made in advance, one that does not rely on phones or messaging, is essential.
- Set a meeting place and write a family emergency contact card for the pack.
- If an attack is expected, wear long clothing to minimize exposed skin. It guards against thermal burns and direct fallout contact.
