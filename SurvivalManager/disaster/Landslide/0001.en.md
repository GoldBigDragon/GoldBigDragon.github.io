# Warning Signs - Any One of Them Means Go

- The ground rumbles, and water suddenly gushes from the hillside.
- Trees sway or topple with no wind.
- Heavily muddy water surges down from upstream - and a sudden *drop* in the stream's flow is a warning too.
- Soil on the slope gives way, or rocks come rolling down.
- New cracks or distortion appear in the ground, buildings, or walls.
- Doors and windows suddenly stop opening properly.
- You hear collapsing sounds, trees snapping, or a deep rumbling in the ground.

> About a third of Korea's natural-disaster deaths come from slope failures - landslides and collapses. A landslide happens in seconds, so leaving early is the only real response.

---

# Where and When

## 1. Dangerous ground
- Areas designated as landslide-prone, and anywhere a landslide has occurred before
- Steep slopes that drain poorly, and bare steep slopes (burned-over forest)
- Cut slopes (where a hill was carved away), fill slopes (where soil was piled), and retaining walls
- Valleys downstream of any of the above

## 2. Dangerous times
- During typhoons and torrential rain
- Immediately after an earthquake - the risk rises sharply if the quake follows heavy rain
- During the spring thaw

# Preparing in Advance

## 1. Around the building
- Check that the gas and electrical shutoffs are installed and working.
- Look for walls and trees that could come down.
- Buildings close to a hillside should have retaining walls and drainage installed and inspected.
- Report hazards to your city, county, or district office.

## 2. Plan and information
- At a family meeting, agree on an emergency contact chain and each person's role.
- Confirm your shelter and route in advance, and learn basic first aid.
- Install the [Smart Forest Disaster app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=kr.go.forest.smartforestkepper) and check forecasts on the [Landslide Information System](https://sansatai.forest.go.kr/mhms_pub/mhms/forecast/forecastPage.do).
- Fill your fuel tank in advance; if you have no car, arrange a ride with a neighbor.

## 3. Emergency supplies
- Food: water, canned goods, hardtack - anything edible without cooking
- Medicine: a kit with ointment, cold medicine, digestive aids, and any prescriptions you take
- Daily items: a change of clothes, tissue, wet wipes, a lighter, feminine hygiene products, plastic bags
- Other: radio, flashlight and batteries, phone and charger, power bank, cash, contact list
- Keep it always in the same place and make sure the whole family knows how to use it. Check expiry dates regularly and replace what has aged out.

# During Torrential Rain or a Typhoon

- Follow the weather and landslide forecasts in real time on broadcast, internet, and the app.
- Do not hike, camp, or otherwise go into the hills.
- Do not go out to check paddy embankments or water gates near a hillside.
- If you were outdoors, move to a community hall or school away from the slope.
- Residents should hold position and, when an evacuation order comes, move to the designated site.
- If your house adjoins a hillside, clear the drainage and cover the slope with plastic sheeting.
- If evacuating is not feasible, do not sleep - keep monitoring official instructions, sounds, and changes around you.

# When a Landslide Occurs

## 1. Inside a building
- With no evacuation announcement, stay inside and keep in contact with neighbors.
- With an announcement, shut off the gas and power, then move to the designated site.
- Neighbors may not have heard - check next door and warn them.
- Stay out of underground parking. Debris flows into it.
- Avoid high-voltage lines as you move.
- If crossing a stream or channel would be dangerous, do not attempt it - move to a solid building nearby.
- If you cannot leave, go to the highest floor, the side farthest from the mountain, curl up, and protect your head.
- If you become trapped, call 119; if the network is unreliable, shout, blow a whistle, or bang on something.

## 2. In the hills or outdoors
- A landslide travels downhill. Move sideways, perpendicular to its path, and climb the nearest high ground.
- Get out of the valley floor. Valleys are the channel a debris flow takes.
- Stop camping immediately and move to a community hall or other safe place.
- If reaching the shelter means crossing a stream, do not force it - signal for rescue from high ground away from the valley.
- If trapped, climb a rise and shout or wave clothing to mark your position.
- Climbing on top of a reinforced-concrete building is another option.

## 3. In a vehicle
- Drive slowly and keep extra distance.
- Detour around landslide-risk sections and get clear quickly.
- Avoid traffic signals, streetlights, and high-voltage lines on the way.

# Evacuating With People Who Need Help

## 1. Children
- Carry a baby in a sling so your hands are free to protect both your heads.
- Hold hands firmly so you are not separated in the confusion.
- Repeat the instructions aloud so the child can act with you.

## 2. Elderly people and people with disabilities
- Designate a helper in advance and agree specifically on how to reach them.
- Learn the route and shelter location ahead of time.
- Keep enough of any regular medication, and have mobility aids - cane, wheelchair - ready.
- Caregivers should rehearse the procedure with them periodically.
- When evacuating, help the elderly, children, and people with disabilities nearby who are on their own.
- Do not act alone - ask neighbors or the community service center for help. If you cannot move, wait for help in the safest spot available.
- Vision impairment: follow the situation by radio or television.
- Hearing impairment: gather information from captioned broadcasts and your phone.
- Difficulty making decisions independently: ask the people around you for help.

# After a Landslide

## 1. Reporting
- Call 119 immediately.
- Also report to the Central Forest Disaster Situation Room, 042-481-4119.
- You can file through "Report a Forest Disaster" in the Smart Forest Disaster app.
- Inform your local disaster and safety headquarters or community service center.

## 2. Casualties
- If anyone is buried or missing, report it to the police and fire service at once.
- Give first aid to the injured and transport them to the nearest hospital by ambulance.

## 3. Preventing further damage
- More slides can follow, in the same area and nearby. Stay away until it is declared safe.
- If further damage to homes, roads, or hillsides looks likely, report it to your local office.
- Flooding often follows a landslide - the same rain causes both.

## 4. Going home
- Follow the guidance of your local office, and judge whether to return based on the condition of the building and its surroundings.
- Check columns and walls for damage before entering.
- Even if your home is undamaged, follow local instructions where another slide is possible.
