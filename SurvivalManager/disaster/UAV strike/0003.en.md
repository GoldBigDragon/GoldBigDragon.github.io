# 9. Traveling by Vehicle

> Vehicles are a priority target for drones. Insecurity Insight puts it flatly - "a vehicle cannot outrun a drone."

- Up to 80% of FPV drone strikes hit the side or the rear of a vehicle.
- Trying to escape by accelerating and swerving does not work. A doctor in Kherson tried exactly that and was hit anyway.

## 9-1. What Is Recommended
1. Stop the vehicle.
2. Get out.
3. Find cover immediately.

## 9-2. Planning the Trip
- Drive with headlights off at night.
- Drive only up to the edge of the danger area and cover the rest on foot.
- Keep the windows open so you do not miss the sound of a drone.

---

# 10. Staying Indoors

## 10-1. Put Two Walls Between You and Outside
> The building guidance from Insecurity Insight: keep at least two solid walls between yourself and the outside.

- Upper floors are vulnerable to direct hits and falling debris. Where possible, move to the lowest floor or the basement.
- With no basement, use a windowless bathroom or an interior hallway.
- Make sure your shelter space has at least two exits. With only one, the moment it is blocked you are trapped.

## 10-2. Windows and Exposure
- Apply anti-shatter film or tape to windows to reduce flying glass.
- Close curtains and shutters to reduce visual and thermal detection.
- Do not stay near flammable material.

## 10-3. At the Moment of the Blast
- Keep your mouth slightly open. This protects your lungs from the pressure wave.

---

# 11. Crossing Open Ground

- With no solid structure nearby, look for a hollow or low ground in the terrain.
- A blast spreads upward in a "V" shape, so low ground is comparatively safer.
- If impact is imminent, lie face down and wrap your arms around your head.

---

# 12. Countering Thermal Cameras

## 12-1. Basic Principles

> A building blocks your heat signature effectively. Staying indoors is the most effective countermeasure there is.

- A thermal camera cannot see through window glass. An ordinary camera can.
- But leaning against a window conducts heat to the glass surface, where it can be detected.
- For brief concealment outdoors, a mylar blanket held away from the body with an air gap reduces your signature for a short time.
- The head and hands release the most body heat. Cover them first.
- Positioning yourself near objects of similar temperature reduces thermal contrast - a sun-heated rock, a warmed surface.
- Dense fog and smoke (especially smoke containing metal particles) degrade thermal equipment.

## 12-2. Day and Night Are Different Problems

| Time | The threat | What works in your favor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Night | Thermal cameras make body heat visible regardless of darkness, so exposure rises. Spotting a drone visually is impossible | Ambient noise drops, so you hear a drone from farther away |
| Day | Drones see shadows clearly. Visual camouflage matters | Without thermal equipment, FPV pilots strike more accurately by day than by night |

> Schedule anything important outdoors for rainy and overcast days.
> Rain degrades a drone's ability to fly, and overcast skies narrow its field of view.

# 13. Community-Level Defense

## 13-1. Anti-Drone Netting
- Ukraine has installed anti-drone netting over roads, hospitals, commercial streets, and critical infrastructure. The netting tangles a drone's propellers so it cannot get through.
- Installed as a tunnel over a road, it protects moving vehicles substantially.

## 13-2. Mutual Watch and Detection Equipment
- In Ukraine, neighbors spontaneously organized a mutual watch, scanning the sky together and alerting one another when a drone appeared.
- A handheld drone detector intercepts an FPV drone's camera transmission and can show the user the video the pilot is looking at right now.
