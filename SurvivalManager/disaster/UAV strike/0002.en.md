# 4. Electronic Signals - Your Phone Points at You

## 4-1. Drones Listen
- Russia's Orlan-10 reconnaissance drone carries ELINT equipment that detects radio-frequency emissions in the 0.8–26 GHz band within about 6 km.
- It picks up mobile data, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and satellite terminal signals.

## 4-2. What to Do
- In a threatened area, switch every phone to airplane mode.
- Turning off Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and mobile data one by one is not enough. Airplane mode is what is recommended.
- Where the highest level of security is needed, put the phone in a Faraday bag or remove the battery.
- Starlink terminals, handheld radios, and CB radios also give away your position - keep their use to a minimum.

> This is an era in which airplane mode, not a gas mask, can decide whether you live.
> One person behind a concrete wall in airplane mode may be safer than several people in a basement with their phones on.

# 5. When You Hear a Drone

## 5-1. Stop, Listen, Identify
- Judge the type by sound first (the table in section 3-1 on page 1).
- Motorcycle or chainsaw - Shahed type. You have several minutes; find a shelter.
- A loud buzz - FPV. Act within seconds.

## 5-2. How to Move

> Do not simply run for your hiding place. Your route is fully visible to the pilot, and you will be showing him exactly where you intend to hide.

- Move along a concealed route, using natural cover - brush, ditches, tall grass, trees.
- The warning from Ukrainian TTP: "Your shadow on the ground is clearly visible from a drone."
- Shadows lengthen in the morning and evening. At those hours your shadow is spotted before your body is.

# 6. When an FPV Drone Charges You - 3 to 7 Seconds

> This is the most dangerous situation you can be in. You have 3 to 7 seconds. Never stand still.

## 6-1. If You Can Run
- Run in a zigzag, changing direction every 7 to 10 m, toward the nearest solid cover.
- An FPV drone has limited turning ability at speed. Irregular changes of direction make the pilot's tracking far harder.
- If you are in a group, scatter in different directions. Running one way means one warhead takes everyone.

## 6-2. No Cover and Impact Imminent
- Drop flat on the ground on your side, facing away from the drone.
- Protect your head and eyes with your hands.

## 6-3. Last Resort
- Throwing an object at a drone at close range - a stick, a bag - has been recorded as effective in actual combat.

# 7. Right After a Strike - the Double Tap

> Do not run to the impact site right after a drone strike.

## 7-1. What the Double Tap Is
- Pilots deliver a second strike 5 to 20 minutes after the first.
- The target is the rescuers and the civilians who gather at the scene. The tactic is built on waiting for people to collect.

## 7-2. So What Do You Do
- Assign someone to watch the sky before any rescue team approaches.
- The pilot may still be watching the scene through a surveillance drone's camera. The first explosion ending does not end the observation.
- Do the rescue dispersed, with the fewest people, and briefly. Reducing the time people spend clustered is the whole point.

# 8. When You Find a Downed Drone

> A downed drone has a lethal radius of over 7 m. Do not touch any part of it.

- The warhead can still detonate.
- It may be booby-trapped.
- The pilot may still be watching through the drone's camera, and can detonate the warhead remotely when someone approaches.

| Do | Never do |
| --- | --- |
| Leave the area immediately | Touch or lift it |
| Report its location to the authorities | Approach to photograph it |
| Keep others, especially children, away | Take it as a souvenir |

- The same rule applies to unexploded ordnance and cluster submunitions. See the Shelling document as well.
