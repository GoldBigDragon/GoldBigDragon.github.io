# What Is a Space Weather Disaster?

- A solar eruption (flare or coronal mass ejection) sends radiation and charged particles to Earth, disrupting communications, satellite navigation, and power grids.
- It is invisible, can begin without warning, and may last from hours to several days.
- Check conditions at the [Korean Space Weather Center](https://spaceweather.kasa.go.kr/Alarm.do), through the [RRA Space Weather](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=go.kr.rra.spacewxm) app, or via emergency broadcasts, TV, and radio.

---

# General Guidance

- Once you know what is happening, pass the information to family and neighbors.
- Move on the assumption that communications and navigation may be wrong. Do not trust a device reading blindly.
- Postpone important travel or work until the event has passed.

---

# Sector-Specific Guidance

## 1. Watching broadcasts
- If satellite or shortwave broadcasts drop out, switch to terrestrial channels until service is restored.
- DMB and UHD broadcasts that rely on GPS timing signals may fail to lock on.
- Retune once the event is over.

## 2. Air travel
- Flights on polar routes may see increased cosmic radiation exposure and communication failures.
- Listen to the crew briefing and cooperate with the airline's rerouting decisions.

## 3. Using location-based devices
- Drivers: navigation errors grow. Trust road signs first and drive defensively.
- Phone users: location services become less accurate.
- Drone operators: flight control can misbehave - hold off on flying.

## 4. Vessel operations
- Before departure: if shortwave is your only radio, stay in port.
- Under way: expect shortwave dropouts and GPS errors. Navigate by radar and visual landmarks, return to port as early as you can, and if needed call the Korea Coast Guard (122) by radio or mobile phone.

## 5. If the power goes out
- Keep a flashlight, portable radio, emergency food and water, and a power bank ready.
- Use a flashlight for light. Candles are a fire hazard.
- An uninterruptible power supply (UPS) protects computers and other sensitive equipment.
- Unplug appliances you are not using to protect them from surges.
- Households that rely on powered life-sustaining medical equipment should arrange backup power and a relocation plan in advance.
- In a long outage, check refrigerated food with a thermometer - when in doubt, throw it out.

---

# Preparing in Advance

- Keep a battery-powered FM/AM radio. It still works when satellite and internet links are down.
- Keep an offline map app *and* a paper map as a hedge against GPS error.
- Write down family emergency contacts on paper.
- A hand-crank generator or solar charger extends how long you can cope with an outage.
