# If You Are Stung - the Order of Things

1. Get out of the water immediately.
2. Rinse away any remaining tentacles with seawater or sterile saline. Rinse thoroughly.
3. If pain persists, apply heat at around 45°C to relieve it.
4. If there is difficulty breathing, loss of consciousness, or whole-body pain, call 119 at once and get the person to hospital for emergency treatment. Start CPR if needed.

> Do not rinse with tap water.
> Fresh water changes the osmotic balance and makes the remaining nematocysts fire more venom, which makes the injury worse. Use seawater or sterile saline only.

> Never grab or rub the tentacles with bare hands. Rubbing bursts more stinging cells.

---

# Symptoms

| When | What appears |
| --- | --- |
| Immediately | Whip-like welts as if lashed, pain, itching |
| Later | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain |
| Severe cases | Falling blood pressure, difficulty breathing, unconsciousness - can be fatal |

---

# Prevention

- Keep skin covered when you go in the water. A rash guard or long-sleeved swimwear helps.
- Report jellyfish you spot through the mobile web reporting service.
- During a bloom, follow the beach's notices and closures.

# The Main Jellyfish in Korean Waters

## 1. Nomura's Jellyfish (*Nemopilema nomurai*)
![Nemopilema nomurai](./media/Nemopilema_nomurai.png)

- Season: from June around Jeju; mid-August to early December nationwide
- Size: a giant species - over 150 cm across and more than 100 kg
- Appearance: strong muscle inside the bell; the oral arms form a J shape at the sides with many tentacles at their tips
- If stung: whip-shaped wounds with pain and redness

## 2. Lion's Mane Jellyfish (*Cyanea capillata*)
![Cyanea capillata](./media/Cyanea_capillata.png)

- Season: July to November along the south coast
- Size: 30–50 cm
- Appearance: white tentacles coated in mucus and stinging cells, hanging in clusters of hundreds from between the well-developed muscles of the bell
- If stung: red spots and mild pain

## 3. Portuguese Man o' War (*Physalia physalis*)
![Physalia physalis](./media/Physalia_physalis.png)

- Season: June to August around Jeju
- Size: 5–15 cm
- Appearance: blue throughout, with long, bead-like venomous tentacles trailing beneath a triangular float
- If stung: severe pain and red whip-like lines with inflammation

## 4. Mauve Stinger (*Pelagia noctiluca*)
![Pelagia noctiluca](./media/Pelagia_noctiluca.png)

- Season: May to July around Jeju and the south coast
- Size: about 8 cm
- Appearance: pink, yellow, or pale pink, with eight tentacles at the bell margin and four curtain-like oral arms inside. True to its name, it glows when disturbed at night
- If stung: pain and difficulty breathing

## 5. Sea Nettle (*Dactylometra quinquecirrha*)
![Dactylometra quinquecirrha](./media/Dactylometra_quinquecirrha.png)

- Season: mainly summer around Jeju and the south coast, though it can be seen from spring through autumn
- Size: 10–20 cm
- Appearance: pale brown bell with brown radial stripes, brown tentacles trailing from the margin between them
- If stung: severe pain, red rash, and swelling
