### Appendix A. Safety Checklist
#### Prohibited Actions
- Never execute wiring operations with wet hands or on rainy days.
- Never permit a metallic object to bridge the positive (+) and negative (−) battery terminals concurrently.
- Never connect components without integrating appropriate fuses and correctly sized wire gauges.
- Never interconnect components without standardizing them to the identical system voltage tier.
- Never connect the solar panels to the charge controller before connecting the battery bank.

#### Pre-Operational Verifications
- Verify that the power is completely shut off and disconnected.
- Confirm that the positive (+) and negative (−) polarities are correct.
- Verify that all metallic jewelry (such as rings and watches) has been removed.
- Ensure that no moisture, open flames, or volatile combustibles remain in the surrounding area.
- Ensure that the voltage ratings of all system components are fully standardized.

#### Emergency Response Procedures
1. De-energize the power source immediately.
2. Never touch an electrocuted casualty with your bare hands.
3. Isolate the casualty from the live conductor using a non-conductive insulator.
4. Contact emergency medical services immediately.
5. Suppress electrical fires using a CO₂ or dry chemical powder fire extinguisher; never use water.
6. If the fire spreads rapidly, prioritize immediate evacuation and reporting over high-risk suppression attempts.

### Appendix B. 24V System Component Matching
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Operating a 24V solar array mandates the utilization of a 24V charge controller, a 24V battery bank, and a 24V input inverter.  

- Principle of Voltage Standardization: Solar panels, charge controllers, battery banks, and inverters must all be standardized to the identical voltage tier (12V, 24V, or 48V).
- System Voltage Baseline: The nominal voltage of the battery bank serves as the baseline for the entire system; select controllers and inverters that align precisely with this battery voltage.
- Configuring a 24V Bank: Connect two identical 12V batteries in series to construct a 24V battery bank.
- Selecting a Charge Controller: Ensure its nominal voltage matches the battery bank, and that it incorporates a generous safety margin to withstand the peak short-circuit current (Isc) and open-circuit voltage (Voc) generated by the solar array.
- Voltage vs. Efficiency: Elevating the system voltage proportionately scales down the current draw (e.g., to transmit a 600W load, a 12V system draws 50A, a 24V system draws 25A, and a 48V system draws 12.5A). Consequently, higher-voltage topologies permit thinner conductors and drastically reduce line losses.

### Appendix C. Safe Connection and Disconnection Sequence
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The battery bank must always be connected first and disconnected last.

#### Connection Sequence  
1. Connect the battery bank to the charge controller.  
2. Connect the solar panel array to the charge controller.  
3. Connect the inverter (with its power switch turned OFF) to the battery bank.  
4. Turn on the power switch of the inverter.  

#### Disconnection Sequence  
1. Turn off the power switch of the inverter.  
2. Disconnect the turned-off inverter from the battery bank.  
3. Disconnect the solar panel array from the charge controller.  
4. Disconnect the battery bank from the charge controller.  

- For enhanced safety, cover the solar panels with an opaque cloth whenever making or breaking array connections.

### Appendix D. Compiled Formulas
#### D-1. Ohm's Law
> V = I × R  (Voltage = Current × Resistance)
> - I = V ÷ R (Current)
> - R = V ÷ I (Resistance)
> - Example: 12V, 6Ω → I = 12 ÷ 6 = 2A

The VIR Triangle (Cover the variable you want to solve for to reveal the formula)
```
   ┌─────────┐
   │    V    │   Cover V → I × R
   ├────┬────┤   Cover I → V ÷ R
   │ I  │ R  │   Cover R → V ÷ I
   └────┴────┘
```

#### D-2. Power
> P = V × I  (Power, Unit: W)
> - Example: 220V × 5A = 1,100W

#### D-3. Energy Consumption
> Wh = W × h  (Energy = Power × Time)
> - Example: 10W × 5h = 50Wh

#### D-4. Battery Storage Capacity / Ampere-Hour Conversion
> Stored Energy (Wh) = Voltage (V) × Capacity (Ah)
> Capacity (Ah) = Energy (Wh) ÷ Voltage (V)
> - Example: 12V × 100Ah = 1,200Wh / 2,500Wh ÷ 24V ≈ 104Ah

#### D-5. Solar Panel Output Relationship
> Pmax ≈ Vmp × Imp
> - Example: 18V × 11.1A ≈ 200W

#### D-6. Series and Parallel Connections
| Configuration | Voltage | Current (Capacity) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Series | Aggregates (Adds) | Remains Constant |
| Parallel | Remains Constant | Aggregates (Adds) |

#### D-7. Charge Controller Amperage Sizing (MPPT Baseline)
> Controller Amperage (A) ≈ (Array Wattage (W) ÷ Battery Voltage (V)) × 1.25
> - Example: 600W ÷ 24V × 1.25 ≈ 31A → Select a 40A Charge Controller

### Appendix E. Capacity Sizing Calculations
#### E-1. The 5-Stage Sizing Protocol
1. Daily Load (Wh) = Σ(Appliance Wattage (W) × Operating Hours (h))  
2. Add Safety Buffer for System Conversion Losses = Daily Load (Wh) ÷ 0.75  
3. Determine Battery Capacity = Sized Daily Load (Wh) × Days of Autonomy ÷ Depth of Discharge (DoD) ÷ System Voltage = Battery Capacity (Ah)  
4. Calculate Solar Array Capacity = Sized Daily Load (Wh) ÷ Peak Sun Hours (Approx. 3h for Winter) = Array Capacity (W)  
5. Size Charge Controller Amperage = (Array Capacity (W) ÷ System Voltage (V)) × 1.25 = Controller Current (A)

### Appendix G. Conductor Gauge and Ampacity Reference
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The data tabulated below represents approximate reference points. Elevated ambient temperatures and extended conductor lengths (inducing voltage drop) mandate scaling up to thicker wire gauges; when a calculation sits on a threshold, always step up to the next thicker wire size. The conductor bridging the battery bank and the inverter must be the thickest gauge in the entire system.  

| Conductor Gauge (SQ) | Closest AWG | Approximate Ampacity | Application Segment (Example) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.5 mm² | 16 AWG | Approx. 15 A | Solar Array ↔ Controller |
| 2.5 mm² | 14 AWG | Approx. 20 A | Solar Array ↔ Controller |
| 4 mm² | 12 AWG | Approx. 28 A | Solar Array ↔ Controller ↔ Battery |
| 6 mm² | 10 AWG | Approx. 36 A | Controller ↔ Battery |
| 10 mm² | 8 AWG | Approx. 50 A | Controller ↔ High-Capacity Battery |
| 16 mm² | 6 AWG | Approx. 65 A | Battery ↔ Inverter (1,000W Tier) |
| 25 mm² | 4 AWG | Approx. 85 A | Battery ↔ Inverter (1,500W and above) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;All fuses implemented in a solar power setup must be explicitly rated for DC current and integrated into the positive (+) line close to the battery terminal. Overcurrent protection sizing must strictly obey the rule: Normal Operating Current < Overcurrent Device Rating < Conductor Ampacity.

### Appendix J. Glossary of Technical Terms
#### Electrical Fundamentals
| Term | Definitions |
| --- | --- |
| Voltage (V, Volts) | The pressure that pushes electricity forward (Water tank height). |
| Current (I, Amperes) | The actual amount of electricity flowing through a circuit (Volume of water). |
| Resistance (R, Ohms) | The degree to which the flow of electric current is opposed (Narrowness of pipe). |
| Ohm's Law | V = I × R |
| Power (W, Watts) | The rate of electrical consumption at any given moment; P = V × I |
| Energy Consumption (Wh) | The cumulative power used over a specific duration; Wh = W × h |
| Direct Current (DC) | Electric current that flows in a single, continuous direction; used in panels and batteries. |
| Alternating Current (AC) | Electric current that alternates directions back and forth; used in standard 220V wall outlets. |
| Series Connection | A configuration wired end-to-end where total voltage aggregates. |
| Parallel Connection | A configuration wired side-by-side where total current and capacity aggregate. |
| Conductors / Insulators | Materials through which electrons flow easily / Materials that oppose the flow of electrons. |

#### Solar Array Definitions
| Term | Definitions |
| --- | --- |
| Pmax (Wp) | Maximum Rated Power output (Watts). |
| Vmp | Maximum Power Voltage under load. |
| Imp | Maximum Power Current under load. |
| Voc | Open-Circuit Voltage; the highest voltage measurable when under no load. |
| Isc | Short-Circuit Current; the maximum current achievable when terminals are directly shorted. |
| STC | Standard Test Conditions; the ideal laboratory parameters used to establish rated metrics. |
| Peak Sun Hours (PSH) | The daily effective full-power solar irradiance window (not raw daylight duration). |

#### System Components
| Term | Definitions |
| --- | --- |
| Charge Controller | A device that defends against overcharging and manages the battery charging profile. |
| PWM / MPPT | Charge controller topologies (Simple and affordable / High-efficiency conversion tracking). |
| Capacity (Ah) | Ampere-hours; the volume of electrical energy a battery can store. |
| Depth of Discharge (DoD) | The usable percentage ratio of a battery's total capacity (High for Lithium, restricted to 50% for Lead-Acid). |
| BMS | Battery Management System; the integrated protection circuitry mandatory for lithium cell management. |
| Inverter | An electronic device that transforms DC power into AC power. |
| Pure / Modified Sine Wave | Inverter output waveform characteristics (Pure sine wave is heavily recommended). |

#### Design & Safety Terminology
| Term | Definitions |
| --- | --- |
| System Voltage | The standardized nominal voltage configuration shared across all components (12V/24V/48V). |
| Short Circuit | Direct contact between positive and negative paths, triggering massive current surges, fire, or explosion hazards. |
| Electrical Arc | A highly stable self-sustaining luminous discharge or spark (requires specialized care in DC circuits). |
| Fuses / Circuit Breakers | Overcurrent protection devices that interrupt faults (must be explicitly rated for DC). |
| Grounding (Earthing) | Physically connecting a circuit to the earth to safely dissipate fault currents. |
| Voltage Drop | The loss of voltage incurred across long or undersized conductors due to internal resistance. |
| Electrical Shock | Physiological injury caused by electric current traversing body tissues (absolutely avoid moisture). |
