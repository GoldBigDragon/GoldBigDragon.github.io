## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;After a blackout, more fires and electric shocks come from makeshift wiring that people rig up in a hurry (extended cords, many devices on one outlet, outdoor wires left in the rain) than from the blackout itself.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This document covers how to carry, interrupt, and protect the electricity you produce safely. The basic relationships among voltage, current, and resistance, and the composition and design of solar systems, are in the Solar panel installation document. Here we cover wire thickness standards, what a fuse protects, how grounding and ground-fault breakers work, and what happens when current flows through the body.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There are two key points.

- Fuses and breakers protect the wire, not the device.
- What determines the danger of electric shock is not the voltage but the current that flows through the body.
