## 1. Electricity and Wiring
### 1-1. Wire Resistance
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The relationships among voltage, current, and resistance and the definitions of power and energy are in the Solar panel installation document. What you need to add for wiring is that the wire itself is a resistance.

- When current flows, the wire eats part of the voltage (voltage drop). The voltage reaching the device is lower by that much.
- All the power consumed by the wire becomes heat. Voltage drop and heating are two sides of the same phenomenon.
- Heating is proportional to the square of the current. Double the current gives four times the heat, and half gives a quarter. This square relationship decides most wiring design judgments.

---
### 1-2. Voltage and Wiring
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When sending the same power, raising the voltage reduces the current, and because heating is proportional to the square of the current, doubling the voltage cuts the heating in the same wire to a quarter.

| System voltage | Current when sending 500W | Loss comparison in the same wire |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 12V | 41.7A | 100 |
| 24V | 20.8A | 25 |
| 48V | 10.4A | 6.3 |
| 220V | 2.3A | 0.3 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It is the same reason power lines carry electricity at tens of thousands of volts. A 12V system makes parts easy to find and lets you use car accessories as they are, but the wire must be thick and you cannot run wiring far. Once the wiring distance exceeds a few meters, raising to 24V or 48V is advantageous in both wire cost and loss.

### 1-3. Wiring Differences Between DC and AC
| Item | DC | AC |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Storage in a battery | Possible as is | Conversion needed |
| Voltage conversion | A separate circuit is needed | Easy with a transformer |
| Long-distance transmission | Disadvantageous | Advantageous |
| When a switch is opened | The arc does not go out easily | The arc goes out by itself |
| Corrosion | Galvanic corrosion proceeds at one pole | Relatively less |
| Shock hazard | Relatively less dangerous at the same current | More dangerous at low current |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;What leads to actual accidents is the fourth item. AC passes through zero current more than a hundred times a second, so the arc formed when contacts separate goes out by itself. DC current never passes through zero, so even when the contacts separate the arc continues across the air.

> **Caution:** If AC switches and breakers are used in a DC circuit, the arc continues even after the contacts separate, so the contacts melt and fuse together and the circuit does not break. Always use parts marked with a DC rating in DC circuits such as batteries and solar.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In a disaster it is reasonable to build around DC. Batteries and solar panels are DC, and LEDs, radios, and chargers also run on DC inside, so eliminating the path of converting to AC and back to DC reduces both losses and parts.

---
### 1-4. Factors That Determine Wire Resistance
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A wire's resistance is proportional to its length and inversely proportional to its cross-sectional area. Drawing out double the length doubles the resistance, and doubling the cross-sectional area halves it. The material multiplies on top of this.

| Conductor | Relative resistance | Notes |
|:---|---:|:---|
| Silver | 0.95 | Lowest, but not realistic |
| Copper | 1.00 | Reference |
| Aluminum | about 1.6 | Light and cheap, but must be thicker for the same performance |
| Iron | about 6 | Unsuitable as wire |

- When temperature rises, a metal's resistance also rises. When a wire heats up from heavy current, the resistance grows, more heat is produced at the same current, and that heat enlarges the resistance again. When this feedback passes a threshold, the sheath begins to melt and leads to a short circuit.
- Wire pulled from a building or a scrapped car may already have suffered overheating. A wire whose sheath has hardened or changed color has damaged insulation even if it looks perfectly fine. If the sheath cracks or crumbles when bent, do not use it.
