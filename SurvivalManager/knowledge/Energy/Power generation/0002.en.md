## 1. Questions to Ask Before Generating Electricity
### 1-1. Can This Be Done Without Electricity?
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is a question that must be passed before starting generation. Tasks that are merely convenient with electricity and tasks that are impossible without electricity belong to entirely different categories; spending limited generation capacity on the former leaves no margin to handle the latter.

| Purpose | Method Without Electricity | Assessment |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Lighting | Oil lamps, candles, working by day | LEDs consume very little, making electricity exceptionally advantageous |
| Cooking | Fire, charcoal, rocket stoves | Using electricity is a massive waste |
| Heating | Firewood, ondol (underfloor heating), insulation retrofits | Electric heating is the worst choice |
| Boiling Water | Fire | Fire is overwhelmingly advantageous |
| Pumping Water | Water bucket, manual pump, water lifting wheel | Possible without electricity |
| Refrigeration | Underground storage, evaporative cooling, salting & smoking | Electricity is advantageous, but power consumption is high |
| Communication | None | Possible only with electricity |
| Receiving & Recording Information | None | Possible only with electricity |
| Medical Devices | None | Possible only with electricity |
| Charging Flashlights & Radios | None | Possible only with electricity |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The bottom four lines of the table represent the entire reason to generate electricity. And all four of these have very low power consumption.

### 1-2. Calculate Power Consumption First
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The plan of deciding on a power generation setup first and tailoring consumption to it almost always fails. The sequence must be to first list and total the required energy amounts, then select equipment matching that total.

| Appliance | Power Consumption | Daily Usage | Daily Energy Consumption |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 LED Bulb | 3–7W | 4 hours | 12–28Wh |
| Radio | 1–5W | 4 hours | 4–20Wh |
| Mobile Phone Charging | 5–15W | 2 hours | 10–30Wh |
| Two-Way Radio Charging | 5–10W | 2 hours | 10–20Wh |
| Laptop | 30–65W | 3 hours | 90–195Wh |
| Small Fan | 20–40W | 6 hours | 120–240Wh |
| Small Refrigerator | Compressor 60–100W | Intermittent | 300–800Wh |
| Electric Kettle | 1,500–2,000W | 10 minutes | 250–330Wh |
| Electric Heater | 1,000–2,000W | 1 hour | 1,000–2,000Wh |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;What should be read from this table is not individual numbers, but the underlying structure. The combined electricity needed to run lights, a radio, and communication devices for an entire day is around 100Wh, yet turning on an electric kettle for just 10 minutes consumes more than that. Appliances that generate heat occupy a larger share than all other loads combined, and whether a single heat-generating appliance is included or excluded shifts the required power generation capacity to an entirely different dimension.

> **The moment you attempt to generate heat with electricity, the generation plan becomes a tenfold problem. Heat should be generated with fire, and electricity should be reserved for tasks that can only be done with electricity.**

### 1-3. Adding Losses Yields the Actual Requirement
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Calculated consumption does not directly equal the required generation amount, because electricity coming from a generator decreases step by step before reaching appliances.

| Stage | Approximate Efficiency |
|:---|---:|
| Wiring and Connections | 95–98% |
| Charge Controller | 90–97% |
| Battery Charge/Discharge | 80–85% |
| Inverter Conversion | 85–90% |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Passing through all paths—storing in batteries and creating AC via an inverter—drops overall efficiency down to around 70%. Therefore, if you intend to consume 200Wh per day, generating 280–300Wh per day makes the math work. On top of this, a buffer must be added for overcast days, dry seasons, and days when generation stops due to failures and maintenance.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The easiest way to save energy is bypassing the inverter. Batteries, LEDs, radios, and chargers all operate natively on DC; eliminating the path of converting DC to AC and back to DC inside appliances removes two entire loss stages. The standby power consumed simply by leaving the inverter turned on cannot be ignored either.

### 1-4. Criteria for Selecting a Power Generation Method
| Method | Continuity | Difficulty of Construction | Fuel | Primary Hazard |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Hydropower | Very High | Medium | None Needed | Drowning, flooding |
| Solar Power | Daytime Only | Low | None Needed | Low |
| Wind Power | Unstable | High | None Needed | Rotating assembly failure, strong winds |
| Steam | High | Very High | Firewood | Boiler explosion |
| Wood Gas | High | High | Firewood | Carbon monoxide, fire |
| Human Power | Only When Needed | Low | Food | Low |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The criterion when selecting a method is not maximum peak output, but the actual daily energy yield obtained. Solar power may seem to have a large rated capacity, but actual generation time per day is merely 3 to 5 hours, whereas a hydropower setup with the exact same rating runs 24 hours continuously. A single 100W hydropower setup produces roughly the same daily energy as a 400W solar power setup.
