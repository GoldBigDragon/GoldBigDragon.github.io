## 1. Power Generation Planning: Required Power and Choosing a Method
### 1-1. Sorting the Uses That Need Electricity
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Jobs that are easier with electricity are different from jobs that are impossible without it. If you spend limited generating capacity on the former, you have no capacity left to cover the latter.

| Use | How to do it without electricity | Judgment |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Lighting | Oil lamps, candles, working in daylight | LED draws very little, so electricity is the exception that is advantageous |
| Cooking | Fire, charcoal, rocket stove | Doing it with electricity is a great waste |
| Heating | Firewood, ondol (heated floor), better insulation | Electric heating is the worst choice |
| Boiling water | Fire | Fire is overwhelmingly advantageous |
| Pumping water | Well bucket, hand pump, pumping water wheel | Possible without electricity |
| Refrigeration | Underground storage, evaporative cooling, pickling and smoking | Electricity is advantageous but consumption is large |
| Communication | None | Only electricity |
| Receiving and recording information | None | Only electricity |
| Medical devices | None | Only electricity |
| Charging flashlights and radios | None | Only electricity |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The bottom four rows of the table are the whole reason to generate electricity, and all of them draw little power.

---
### 1-2. Calculating Power Consumption
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;First write down the energy you need and add it up, then choose equipment that fits the total. Plans that fix the equipment first and fit usage to it almost always fail.

| Device | Power consumption | Daily use | Daily energy |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 LED bulb | 3-7W | 4 hours | 12-28Wh |
| Radio | 1-5W | 4 hours | 4-20Wh |
| Mobile phone charging | 5-15W | 2 hours | 10-30Wh |
| Two-way radio charging | 5-10W | 2 hours | 10-20Wh |
| Laptop | 30-65W | 3 hours | 90-195Wh |
| Small fan | 20-40W | 6 hours | 120-240Wh |
| Small refrigerator | Compressor 60-100W | Intermittent | 300-800Wh |
| Electric kettle | 1,500-2,000W | 10 minutes | 250-330Wh |
| Electric heater | 1,000-2,000W | 1 hour | 1,000-2,000Wh |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The energy used by running lighting, a radio, and communication devices all day totals around 100Wh. An electric kettle uses more than this in just 10 minutes. A single heat-producing device is larger than the sum of all other loads, so whether you put it on the list or leave it off changes the scale of the equipment you need.

> **Caution:** Making heat with electricity multiplies the generation scale by ten. Make heat with fire, and use electricity only for work that only electricity can do.

---
### 1-3. Accounting for Losses
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;You must add the losses in transmission to the calculated consumption to get the required generation.

| Stage | Approximate efficiency |
|:---|---:|
| Wiring and connections | 95-98% |
| Charge controller | 90-97% |
| Battery charge and discharge | 80-85% |
| Inverter conversion | 85-90% |

- Passing through both a battery and an inverter, overall efficiency is around 70%. To use 200Wh a day, you must generate 280-300Wh.
- Leave a margin by adding cloudy days, dry spells, and days when the system stops for breakdowns or maintenance.
- If you skip the inverter, two loss stages disappear. Batteries, LEDs, radios, and chargers all run on direct current, so use DC as it is.
- An inverter consumes standby power just by being switched on.

---
### 1-4. Criteria for Choosing a Generation Method
| Method | Continuity | Difficulty of building | Fuel | Main hazard |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Hydro | Very high | Medium | None needed | Drowning, floods |
| Solar | Daytime only | Low | None needed | Low |
| Wind | Unstable | High | None needed | Rotor breakage, strong winds |
| Steam | High | Very high | Firewood | Boiler explosion |
| Wood gas | High | High | Firewood | Carbon monoxide, fire |
| Human power | Only when needed | Low | Food | Low |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The criterion is not maximum output but the energy actually obtained per day. Even if solar looks large in rating, it runs only 3-5 hours a day, while hydro with the same rating runs 24 hours. A 100W hydro setup produces about the same daily energy as a 400W solar setup.
