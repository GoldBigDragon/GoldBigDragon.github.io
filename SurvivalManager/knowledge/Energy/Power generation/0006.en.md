## 5. Batteries and Charge Control
### 5-1. The Need for Storage
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The time electricity is generated and the time it is used almost always differ. Solar generates only in the daytime while lighting is needed at night, and hydro generates steadily but a radio is used in occasional bursts. The battery is the device that bridges this mismatch, and it is the most expensive and the first part of the system to fail. Most batteries die not from defects but from how they are used.

---
### 5-2. Depth of Discharge and Lifespan
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The number of times a lead-acid battery can be used varies greatly with how deeply it is discharged before recharging. The figures below are general trends and differ by product.

| Depth of discharge | Approximate number of charge-discharge cycles |
|---:|---:|
| 10% | 3,000 or more |
| 30% | 1,000-1,500 |
| 50% | 400-600 |
| 80% | 150-250 |
| 100% | A few dozen |

- If you use a 100Ah battery down to only half, the usable capacity is 50Ah. Prepare twice the capacity you need. Using it shallowly for five years is better than using it deeply and throwing it away after one.
- The worst thing is leaving it fully discharged for days. The lead sulfate formed on the plate surfaces during discharge dissolves again on charging, but if left for a long time the crystals become large and hard and do not go back (sulfation). Capacity is permanently reduced, and in winter the electrolyte can freeze and break the plates.

---
### 5-3. Overcharge and Overdischarge
| State | What happens |
|:---|:---|
| Overcharge | Water in the electrolyte decomposes, producing hydrogen and oxygen. The liquid drops and the plates are exposed, and the hydrogen is explosive |
| Overdischarge | Lead sulfate hardens and capacity is permanently reduced |
| High temperature | For every 10 °C above 25 °C, lifespan is roughly halved |
| Low temperature | Usable capacity falls. It decreases by around 20% at 0 °C, and a discharged battery can freeze |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Preventing the first two is the job of the charge controller. It keeps watching battery voltage, reduces or cuts off charging current when it passes the limit, and disconnects the load when it falls below.

- For a 12V lead-acid battery: raise the voltage to around 14.4-14.8V during charging, then lower it and hold at 13.5-13.8V. Set the load cutoff between 11.8-12.0V. At low temperatures, raise these reference voltages slightly.
- Without a controller, at least fit a voltmeter and have someone watch it. Even without that, building a circuit with a single relay that cuts off charging at a set voltage is far better than nothing.

> **Caution:** Hydrogen released during charging can explode at concentrations over 4% in air, and it tends to collect above the battery. Do not keep batteries in a sealed room or a small box. Avoid even the small sparks of switching on and off around the battery.

---
### 5-4. Connecting Batteries
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For how voltage and capacity change with series and parallel connection, see the Solar panel installation document, and for the changes from a wiring point of view, see the Wiring safety document.

| Connection | Result | Problem that arises |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Series | Voltage adds, capacity stays the same | The weakest battery determines the whole performance |
| Parallel | Capacity adds, voltage stays the same | A voltage difference causes an instantaneous large current |

> **Caution:** Do not mix batteries that differ in capacity, type, manufacturing date, or state of charge. In series, the weak cell is overdischarged and dies first; in parallel, the higher-voltage side pours a large current into the lower side and both are ruined together.

- Before connecting in parallel, fully charge each battery separately to match their voltages.
- For parallel wiring, do not draw everything from one end; draw diagonally so that the cable length to each battery is equal. If the lengths differ, the resistance differs, and only the nearer battery is used more and ages faster.

---
### 5-5. Checking Battery State
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Remaining capacity is easily checked by voltage. During charging or discharging, the voltage reads differently from the actual state, so disconnect all loads and measure after letting it rest at least 30 minutes.

| 12V lead-acid open-circuit voltage | Approximate state of charge |
|---:|---:|
| 12.7V or more | 100% |
| 12.5V | 75% |
| 12.2V | 50% |
| 12.0V | 25% |
| 11.8V or less | Effectively empty |

- For open-type batteries that can be topped up, it is far more accurate to measure the electrolyte's specific gravity with a hydrometer. It is around 1.265 when fully charged and around 1.120 when fully discharged. If the specific gravity differs greatly between cells, there is a problem with that cell.
- Use only distilled water to top up. Minerals in tap water or stream water ruin the plates.
- A battery discharges by itself even when not in use. A lead-acid battery loses 3-5% a month, and faster at high temperatures. Charge spare batteries at least once every few months. If left as they are, they reach a state that cannot be revived.
