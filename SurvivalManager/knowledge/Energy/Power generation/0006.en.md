## 5. Batteries and Charge Control
### 5-1. Power Generation Without Storage Cannot Stand
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The time when power is generated and the time when electricity is used almost always mismatch. Solar power generates only during the day while lighting is needed at night, and hydropower generates constantly while two-way radios are used in bursts. The battery is the device that bridges this mismatch, as well as the most expensive and most vulnerable component in the system.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Most batteries fail not because of defects, but because of how they are used. Therefore, knowing how to extend battery life is more valuable than building an additional power generation setup.

---
### 5-2. Depth of Discharge Determines Lifespan
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For lead-acid batteries, the number of usable charge-discharge cycles changes dramatically based on how deeply they are discharged. Below is a general trend, which varies by product.

| Depth of Discharge (DoD) | Approximate Cycle Life |
|---:|---:|
| 10% | 3,000+ cycles |
| 30% | 1,000–1,500 cycles |
| 50% | 400–600 cycles |
| 80% | 150–250 cycles |
| 100% | A few dozen cycles |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;What this table signifies is clear: if you decide to discharge a 100Ah battery only down to 50%, the actual usable capacity is 50Ah, meaning you must prepare twice the required capacity. While this might seem wasteful at first, using it shallowly for 5 years is far more advantageous than discharging it deeply and discarding it in 1 year.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Leaving a battery in a fully discharged state for several days is the worst possible handling. The lead sulfate formed on the plate surfaces during discharge normally dissolves back into solution upon charging, but if left for too long, it crystallizes into hard, large crystals that cannot be reversed. Once this sulfation progresses, capacity is permanently reduced; in winter, the diluted electrolyte may freeze as well, physically destroying the plates.

---
### 5-3. Overcharging and Overdischarging Are the Same Mistake in Opposite Directions
| State | What Happens |
|:---|:---|
| Overcharging | Water in the electrolyte decomposes into hydrogen and oxygen. Electrolyte levels drop, exposing plates, and hydrogen is explosive |
| Overdischarging | Lead sulfate hardens, permanently reducing capacity |
| High Temperature | For every 10°C rise above 25°C baseline, lifespan is halved roughly |
| Low Temperature | Usable capacity decreases; drops by around 20% at 0°C, and can freeze when discharged |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Preventing both of these is the job of the charge controller. The principle is simple: it continuously monitors battery voltage, reducing or cutting off charging current when voltage exceeds threshold, and disconnecting the load when it drops below threshold. For a 12V lead-acid battery, voltage during charging is raised to around 14.4–14.8V, then lowered and maintained at 13.5–13.8V, with low-voltage disconnect set between roughly 11.8–12.0V. If ambient temperature drops, these voltage setpoints must be adjusted slightly upward.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If a charge controller is absent, at a minimum, a voltmeter should be installed and monitored manually. If even that is unavailable, building a circuit with a single relay that cuts off charging at a specific voltage is far better than having nothing at all.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Hydrogen gas emitted during charging presents a distinct hazard. If air concentration exceeds 4%, it becomes explosive and tends to accumulate above the battery. Never house batteries in sealed rooms or cramped boxes, and avoid even tiny sparks from toggling switches around batteries.

---
### 5-4. Mixing Different Batteries Damages Both
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Because changes in voltage and capacity from series and parallel connections are covered in the solar panel installation and basic electricity documents, this document addresses only the problems that arise when connecting multiple batteries.

| Connection | Result | Problem Created |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Series | Voltage adds up while capacity stays the same | The weakest battery dictates overall performance |
| Parallel | Capacity adds up while voltage stays the same | Voltage differences cause massive instantaneous cross-currents |

> **Batteries with differing capacity, chemistry, manufacture date, or state of charge must never be mixed together. In series, the weakest cell becomes overdischarged and dies first; in parallel, the higher-voltage battery dumps massive current into the lower-voltage one, destroying both together.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When wiring in parallel, each battery must be fully charged individually beforehand to balance voltages, and wiring should not be drawn from one end but diagonally so wire lengths to each battery are identical. Different wire lengths create different resistance, causing closer batteries to be worked harder and age faster.

---
### 5-5. How to Read Battery Status
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The simplest method to determine remaining capacity is reading open-circuit voltage. However, because voltage readings during charging or discharging deviate from true state, all loads must be disconnected for at least 30 minutes before measuring.

| 12V Lead-Acid Open-Circuit Voltage | Approximate State of Charge (SoC) |
|---:|---:|
| 12.7V or higher | 100% |
| 12.5V | 75% |
| 12.2V | 50% |
| 12.0V | 25% |
| 11.8V or lower | Effectively empty |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For flooded open-cell batteries where fluid can be replenished, measuring electrolyte specific gravity with a hydrometer is far more accurate. Full charge measures around 1.265, while full discharge measures around 1.120; if specific gravity varies significantly between cells, a fault exists in that specific cell. When topping up fluid, distilled water must strictly be used; minerals in tap or stream water ruin the plates.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Finally, batteries self-discharge even when not in use. Lead-acid batteries drain by 3–5% per month, faster at higher temperatures. Reserve batteries stored as spares must be charged every few months; leaving them untouched results in an unrecoverable state when retrieved later.
