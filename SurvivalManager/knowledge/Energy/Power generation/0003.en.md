## 2. Common Principles of Generation and Generators
### 2-1. Electromagnetic Induction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Apart from solar, the final stage of nearly all generation is electromagnetic induction. When a conductor and a magnetic field move relative to each other, a voltage appears across the ends of the conductor. Hydro, wind, and steam are all ways of producing this relative motion.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The voltage is proportional to the rate at which the magnetic field changes. There are three ways to increase it.

- Use stronger magnets.
- Wind more turns of coil.
- Spin it faster.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The easiest thing to change in the field is the rotation speed, so the design of generating equipment mostly comes down to matching the rpm.

---
### 2-2. Using a Motor as a Generator
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Feeding in electricity to turn the shaft makes it a motor; turning the shaft to draw out electricity makes it a generator. However, not every motor works well as a generator.

| Type | Use as a generator | Characteristics and conditions |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Permanent-magnet DC motor | Works very well | DC output proportional to rpm. Obtainable from power drills, treadmills, electric scooters |
| Brushless motor | Works | Outputs three-phase AC, so rectification is needed. Obtainable from drones, electric bicycles |
| Stepper motor | Works at small scale | Produces voltage even at very slow rotation, but output is small |
| Automobile alternator | Works conditionally | Current must first be fed to the field, and it must turn above 1,500rpm |
| Ordinary AC induction motor | Difficult | Needs a capacitor for self-excitation, and the rpm conditions are strict |

- Permanent-magnet machines start generating by themselves without external power, which is an overwhelming advantage in a disaster.
- An automobile alternator is common and robust and has a built-in rectifier circuit. However, it consumes electricity to excite the field coil, so at slow rotation it consumes more than it produces. It is usable only if stepped up greatly with a belt and pulleys, and the step-up adds losses.

---
### 2-3. Rotation Speed and Voltage
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A permanent-magnet generator produces voltage almost exactly proportional to rotation speed.

- If rotation is too slow, the generator voltage is lower than the battery voltage and no current flows. To charge a 12V lead-acid battery, 13.5V or more must be produced, and until that rpm is reached, the generator does not charge even though it is turning.
- If rotation is too fast, overvoltage boils the battery and burns the equipment.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A power source that turns very slowly, like a water wheel, needs a step-up mechanism (pulley diameter ratio, gear tooth ratio). Losses grow with each stage, so finish in a single stage if possible. A belt slips and causes loss, but under overload it slips and protects the machine.

| Problem | Symptom | Remedy |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Too little rpm | Voltage lower than the battery, so no charging | Raise the step-up ratio or wind more coil |
| Too much rpm | Voltage soars and the battery gasses | Slow down, or limit with a charge controller |
| Overload | The generator drags heavily and rotation drops | Reduce the load or enlarge the turbine |
| No load | Rotation runs away | Always keep a load connected |

---
### 2-4. Blocking Diode
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If a generator and battery are simply connected, at night or in a dry spell when the generator has stopped, the battery pushes current into the generator. The generator becomes a motor, spinning idle while draining the battery, and the battery is empty within days.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fit a blocking diode that lets current flow only from the generator to the battery. The diode causes a voltage drop of around 0.5V, so the generator voltage needs that much margin.

---
### 2-5. Dump Load
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Unlike solar, hydro and wind do not stop by themselves. When the battery is full and the charge controller cuts off charging, the generator suddenly loses its load and its rotation speed soars. In wind power the blades break, and in hydro the overvoltage damages wiring and equipment.

> **Caution:** A continuously running generating system must have a load (dump load) to get rid of surplus electricity as heat. Fitting a heating element or resistor immersed in a water bucket lets the generator carry a stable load even after the battery is full.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The heat from a dump load can be used to warm water or dry grain. This is electricity that would be lost because there is nowhere to store it, so it is better to use it even at low efficiency.
