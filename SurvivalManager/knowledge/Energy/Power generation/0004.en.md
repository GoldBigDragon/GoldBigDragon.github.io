## 3. Hydropower
### 3-1. Advantages of Hydropower
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A stream flows at night and on cloudy days. There is no need to carry fuel, and once built, nobody has to stay with it. While solar works 3-5 hours a day, hydro works 24 hours, so at the same rating the daily output is 4-5 times as much.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;You do not need a big river. A stream as thick as a human forearm and a height difference of a few meters is enough. If a small stream or valley water is nearby, consider it before other methods.

---
### 3-2. Calculating Output
> **Output (W) ≈ head (m) × flow (L/s) × 9.81 × efficiency**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;9.81 is the acceleration of gravity. Efficiency, with all turbine, generator, and wiring losses combined, is in practice 0.4-0.7, and for hand-built equipment it is more realistic to calculate with 0.4.

| Head | Flow | Output at efficiency 0.5 | Daily energy |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2m | 5 L/s | about 49W | about 1.2kWh |
| 5m | 10 L/s | about 245W | about 5.9kWh |
| 10m | 5 L/s | about 245W | about 5.9kWh |
| 20m | 2 L/s | about 196W | about 4.7kWh |
| 30m | 1 L/s | about 147W | about 3.5kWh |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Head and flow are tied together by multiplication, so if one falls short, the other can make up for it (in the table, 5m and 10 L/s and 10m and 5 L/s give the same output). Of the two, the one people can work on is head.

---
### 3-3. Creating Head
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;People cannot increase flow, but they can create head. Take water from upstream, lead it a long way sideways in a channel of gentle slope, and then drop it all at once at the point you want. If the stream falls 5m per 100m, simply running a diversion channel 200m gives a 10m head.

- Obtaining the same output from head reduces the water needed, making the turbine smaller and the pipe thinner.
- The rotation speed is high, so it can be connected directly to the generator without a step-up mechanism. The whole setup becomes small and simple.
- What can actually be used is the effective head, after subtracting friction loss in the penstock. The longer and thinner the pipe, the larger the loss, so choose the pipe diameter so that the loss does not exceed 10-15% of the gross head.

---
### 3-4. Measuring Flow
- Bucket method: for a small stream, build a weir to gather the water in one place, catch it in a bucket of known volume, and time how long it takes to fill. If a 20L bucket fills in 10 seconds, that is 2 L/s.
- Float method: on a straight stretch of channel, set a distance of 10m, float a piece of wood, and time its passage. Multiply the surface velocity obtained this way by a correction factor to get the average velocity (0.85 for a smooth-bottomed channel, 0.6 for a natural stream with much gravel and rock). Multiplying by the cross-sectional area gives the flow.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The design basis is not the usual flow but the flow in the dry season, when water is lowest in the year. Equipment designed for monsoon-season flow stops in winter and spring.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Do not divert all of the stream's water. Let a certain amount flow on for the people and fish downstream. The problems caused by the downstream channel drying up are often greater than the output gained by increasing the intake.

---
### 3-5. Turbine Types
| Type | Suitable head | Suitable flow | Efficiency | Characteristics |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Undershot water wheel | 0.5-2m | Large | 20-35% | Easiest to build. Receives only the force of the current |
| Overshot water wheel | 2-10m | Small | 60-85% | Uses the weight of the water. Rotation is very slow, 5-20 per minute |
| Crossflow | 2-20m | Medium to large | 70-80% | Can be built from sheet metal. Efficiency holds up when the flow changes |
| Pelton | 20m or more | Small | 80% or more | Cup-shaped buckets receive a jet of water from a nozzle. Fast rotation |
| Propeller | 1-5m | Large | 70-85% | Suited to low head, but the blade shape is difficult to make |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A water wheel can be made of wood, but it turns far too slowly. Connecting a shaft turning at 10 per minute to a generator that needs 1,500 per minute requires a 150-fold step-up, and losses and failures increase together. A Pelton turbine turns fast by itself and can be connected directly to the generator or finished with a single belt stage. If head is available, Pelton is far more advantageous.

---
### 3-6. Intake Structures
| Structure | What it does | Without it |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Weir | Gathers water and sends it to the channel | Flow becomes erratic |
| Screen | Blocks leaves and twigs | The nozzle and turbine clog |
| Settling basin | Lets sand and gravel settle | Turbine blades and nozzle are ground away and become useless |
| Channel | Carries water at a gentle slope to create head | Head cannot be secured |
| Forebay | Keeps the penstock inlet always under water | Air enters the pipe and output drops |
| Penstock | Converts head into water pressure | Force is not delivered to the turbine |
| Spillway | Safely passes overflowing water | The equipment is swept away in a flood |

- The settling basin is the structure most often omitted and most often regretted. Sand in the water strikes the turbine blades and nozzle at high speed and grinds away the metal, and it is common for the nozzle hole to widen within months so that only water is wasted. Make the channel wide and deep partway along to slow the current, and provide a drain to flush out the sand that builds up on the bottom periodically.
- Plan for floods from the start. A day will certainly come when tens of times the usual flow runs, so open a path in advance for the water to go over, so that the intake weir and channel are not lost entirely.
