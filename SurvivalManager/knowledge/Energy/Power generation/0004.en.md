## 3. Hydropower, the Most Realistic Continuous Power Source
### 3-1. Advantages of Hydropower
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In a disaster situation, the primary advantage of hydropower is not peak output, but consistency. Streams flow through the night and flow on overcast days. There is no need to haul fuel, and once constructed, it runs unattended. While solar power works 3 to 5 hours a day, hydropower works 24 hours continuously; for the same power rating, its daily energy yield is 4 to 5 times greater.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Therefore, if a small stream or mountain creek is nearby, it is worth evaluating before any other method. A massive river is not required; a stream as wide as a human forearm and a height drop of a few meters are more than enough.

---
### 3-2. Power Output Is the Product of Head and Flow Rate
> **Power Output (W) ≈ Head (m) × Flow Rate (L/s) × 9.81 × Efficiency**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Here, 9.81 represents acceleration due to gravity, and efficiency accounts for combined losses from the waterwheel, generator, and wiring, typically falling between 0.4 and 0.7 in practice. For homemade equipment, calculating based on 0.4 is realistic.

| Head | Flow Rate | Power Output at 0.5 Efficiency | Daily Energy Yield |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2m | 5 L/s | Approx. 49W | Approx. 1.2kWh |
| 5m | 10 L/s | Approx. 245W | Approx. 5.9kWh |
| 10m | 5 L/s | Approx. 245W | Approx. 5.9kWh |
| 20m | 2 L/s | Approx. 196W | Approx. 4.7kWh |
| 30m | 1 L/s | Approx. 147W | Approx. 3.5kWh |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Note that the second and third lines in the table produce the exact same power output. Because head and flow rate are linked by multiplication, a shortfall in one can be compensated for by the other. And of these two variables, head is the one humans can actively manipulate.

---
### 3-3. Head Can Be Created; Flow Rate Cannot
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Increasing flow rate requires a larger river, a natural condition beyond human control. Conversely, head can be engineered. Water is collected upstream, conveyed laterally along a flume with a very gentle slope over a distance, and then dropped all at once at the desired spot. If the river's natural slope is 5m per 100m, running a 200m flume yields a 10m head.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Maximizing head is advantageous for reasons beyond power output alone. Achieving the same output through head reduces the required water volume, allowing for smaller waterwheels and thinner pipes, while producing higher RPMs that connect directly to a generator without step-up gearing. The entire setup becomes smaller and simpler.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;However, friction losses caused by water rubbing against the penstock walls must be deducted. Because longer and thinner pipes increase friction losses, the standard guideline is sizing pipe diameter so friction loss does not exceed 10–15% of total gross head. What can actually be utilized is net effective head after deducting these losses, rather than total gross head.

---
### 3-4. Measuring Flow Rate
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For small streams, the most accurate method is constructing a temporary weir to consolidate flow, collecting water into a container of known volume, and timing how long it takes to fill. If a 20L bucket fills in 10 seconds, the flow rate is 2 L/s.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For streams where bucket collection is impractical, the float method is used. A straight 10m stretch of watercourse is selected, a floating wood chip is released, and the time to traverse the distance is measured. This yields surface velocity; due to friction along the bed and banks, average velocity is slower. Multiply surface velocity by 0.85 for smooth artificial channels, or by roughly 0.6 for natural rocky stream beds with gravel. Multiplying this average velocity by cross-sectional area yields flow rate.

> **Design criteria must be based on low-flow dry season volume—the time of year with the least water—rather than normal flow. A system designed around summer monsoon flow will stall completely in winter and spring.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Furthermore, 100% of a river's water should never be diverted. A minimum environmental flow must remain for downstream residents and aquatic life; problems arising from a dried-up downstream channel often outweigh the minor power gain from maximizing intake.

---
### 3-5. Head and Flow Rate Determine Waterwheel Type
| Type | Suitable Head | Suitable Flow Rate | Efficiency | Characteristics |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Undershot Waterwheel | 0.5–2m | High | 20–35% | Easiest to construct; relies solely on current impulse |
| Overshot Waterwheel | 2–10m | Low | 60–85% | Uses water weight; rotates very slowly at 5–20 RPM |
| Crossflow Turbine | 2–20m | Medium to High | 70–80% | Fabricable from sheet metal; maintains efficiency across variable flow rates |
| Pelton Turbine | 20m+ | Low | 80%+ | Nozzle shoots water jet into cup-shaped buckets; fast rotation |
| Propeller Turbine | 1–5m | High | 70–85% | Suitable for low head, but blade fabrication is difficult |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Waterwheels are simple to build and can be crafted from timber, but their rotation is excessively slow. Connecting a shaft spinning at 10 RPM to a generator requiring 1,500 RPM demands a 150-fold speed multiplication, multiplying mechanical losses and failure points in the process. Conversely, Pelton turbines spin rapidly by nature, allowing direct coupling to a generator or a simple single-stage belt drive. Provided sufficient head is secured, Pelton turbines hold a clear advantage.

---
### 3-6. Intake Structures Determine Equipment Lifespan
| Structure | Function | Consequence if Omitted |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Weir | Consolidates water and directs it to the flume | Flow rate becomes erratic |
| Trash Rack / Screen | Blocks leaves and twigs | Nozzles and turbines get clogged |
| Settling Basin | Settles out sand and gravel | Turbine blades and nozzles wear out from abrasion |
| Flume / Canal | Conveys water at a gentle slope to build head | Unable to secure required head |
| Forebay | Keeps penstock inlet fully submerged at all times | Air enters the pipe, dropping power output |
| Penstock / Pressure Pipe | Converts head into water pressure | Power fails to transmit to the turbine |
| Spillway | Safely channels away excess floodwater | Equipment gets washed away during floods |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Among these, the settling basin is the structure most frequently omitted and most frequently regretted. Sand suspended in water is hardly noticeable, yet hitting turbine blades and nozzles at high speed grinds away metal like an abrasive. It is common for nozzle apertures to enlarge within months, wasting water. A settling basin is created by widening and deepening a segment of the flume to drop water velocity, fitted with a drain outlet to periodically flush out accumulated sand.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Flood prevention must also be incorporated into plans from the outset. Days when flow rates swell to tens of times normal volume will inevitably arrive; to prevent the intake weir and flume from being washed away entirely, dedicated spillways must be opened in advance.
