## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Electricity is merely a form of energy that is convenient to carry and handle precisely. Converting energy into electricity and back into heat or light adds a loss at every step, so cooking, heating, and boiling water, which can be done with firewood, are not done with electricity.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The uses that truly require electricity are communication, receiving and recording information, medical devices, and charging flashlights and radios. All of them draw little power. The energy used by running LED bulbs, a radio, and a mobile phone all day is less than that used by running an electric kettle for 10 minutes. Plan power generation in this order: first cut down the list of uses that need electricity, then choose the smallest equipment that fits.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This document covers calculating required power, the principles of generators, hydro, wind, steam, wood gas, and human power generation, and batteries and charge control.
