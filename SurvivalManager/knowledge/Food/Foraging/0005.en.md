## 4. Poisonous Mushrooms and Mistaken Folk Beliefs
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Among foraged foods, mushrooms have the worst ratio of risk to reward. At 20-40 kcal per 100 g they are no different from leafy greens, yet the consequences of a wrong pick are more lethal than for any other forage. The reason mushroom poisoning never stops is that nearly all of the widely circulated rules for telling mushrooms apart are wrong.

---
### 4-1. Why the Folk Beliefs Are All Wrong
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The following are the most widespread methods in Korea for telling poisonous mushrooms apart, and none of them is true. The Ministry of Food and Drug Safety, the Korea Forest Service, and the Rural Development Administration repeat the same warnings every year because these beliefs are the cause of actual deaths.

| Folk belief | Fact |
| --- | --- |
| A mushroom that tears easily lengthwise is edible | The deadly destroying angel (Amanita virosa) and fool's mushroom (Amanita verna) also tear cleanly lengthwise. The direction of tearing is a physical property that comes from the arrangement of the gills and has nothing to do with toxicity |
| A mushroom eaten by worms is safe | Insects and slugs are unaffected because the target enzyme of amatoxin differs from that of humans. Deadly mushrooms also carry bite marks from worms |
| A brightly colored mushroom is poisonous | Most mushrooms of the deadliest amatoxin group are pure white. Conversely, the bright orange dalgyalbeoseot (Amanita hemibapha) is edible |
| A mushroom that turns a silver spoon black is poisonous | Silver tarnishes by reacting with sulfur compounds. It does not react to amatoxin, and ordinary foods such as garlic and eggs tarnish silver instead |
| Boiling or salting removes the poison | Amatoxin is very heat-stable; it does not break down when boiled and remains after drying |
| Cooking with eggplant or perilla oil neutralizes it | There is no basis whatsoever |
| A mushroom without a ring on the stalk is safe | The ring is only a clue for narrowing down the species, not an indicator of toxicity |

> **There is no general rule for picking out safe mushrooms. There is no way other than knowing each species one by one.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There is a reason these beliefs do not die out. Most mushrooms sold in stores really are free of poison, so in the overwhelming majority of cases a wrong rule gets used with nothing happening. People take this success as evidence for the rule, but in fact they simply have not met a deadly mushroom. The moment they do, all the earlier successes mean nothing.

---
### 4-2. Amatoxin: A Poison That Comes Late
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Most deaths from mushroom poisoning are caused by a single group, the amatoxins. In Korea this group includes the destroying angel (Amanita virosa), fool's mushroom (Amanita verna), and Amanita subjunquillea; all are common in oak forests and look similar to edible mushrooms.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Amatoxin is especially dangerous because of its mode of action rather than the strength of its toxicity. This toxin blocks RNA polymerase II, which a cell needs to read genetic information and make proteins. The cell then does not die at once but gradually loses function, unable to make new proteins. Liver cells and the tubular cells of the kidney, which have the highest protein turnover, collapse first, and this delay gives the clinical course its unusual shape.

| Stage | Timing | Condition |
| --- | :---: | --- |
| Latent period | 6-24 hours after ingestion | No symptoms. During this time the toxin has already entered the liver |
| Gastrointestinal phase | After 6-24 hours | Violent vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, and severe dehydration begin |
| Apparent recovery | 24-36 hours after ingestion | Symptoms subside and the person seems to have recovered. Stopping treatment at this stage is fatal |
| Liver failure phase | 3-5 days after ingestion | Liver cell necrosis progresses, leading to jaundice, bleeding, and multiple organ failure |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The existence of an apparent recovery phase is the most dangerous feature of this poisoning. When the vomiting and diarrhea stop, people judge that they have gotten better, and cases have been reported of medical settings mistaking it for simple gastroenteritis and sending the patient home. But during this period the liver cells are already being destroyed, and by the time symptoms return, a liver transplant is often the only option.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The lethal dose is also extremely small. The lethal dose of amatoxin is about 0.1 mg per kg of body weight, and a single mushroom can contain up to 15 mg. Since the lethal dose for a 60 kg adult is 6 mg, it really happens that several people who shared one mushroom are poisoned at the same time.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Growing alongside edible mushrooms also magnifies the problem. If even one or two of the mushrooms picked from a cluster are a different species, the whole batch becomes dangerous. So mushrooms must be checked individually, not by the cluster, and the rule is not to mix them in the same container.

---
### 4-3. Mushroom Toxins of Other Groups
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Besides amatoxin there are several other groups, each with different symptoms and timing. Fast-onset toxins usually allow recovery, but cases in which symptoms begin after more than 6 hours almost always belong to dangerous groups.

| Group | Representative mushrooms | Symptom onset | Main damage |
| --- | --- | :---: | --- |
| Amatoxin | Destroying angel, fool's mushroom | 6-24 hours | Liver and kidneys; high fatality rate |
| Orellanine | Some webcaps (Cortinarius) | 2-20 days | Kidney failure; the longest latent period |
| Gyromitrin | False morel (Gyromitra esculenta) | 6-12 hours | Liver and nerves |
| Muscarine | Fiberheads (Inocybe), funnel caps (Clitocybe) | 15 minutes-2 hours | Sweating, drooling, slow heart rate |
| Ibotenic acid and muscimol | Amanita species (gwangdae-beoseot, magwi-gwangdae-beoseot) | 30 minutes-2 hours | Confusion, hallucinations, convulsions |
| Gastrointestinal irritants | Sulphur tuft (Hypholoma fasciculare), Gymnopilus (hwagyeong-solbat-beoseot), and many others | 30 minutes-3 hours | Vomiting and diarrhea; usually recovered from |
| Coprine | Common ink cap (Coprinopsis atramentaria) | When eaten with alcohol | Flushing and palpitations |
| Trichothecenes | Poison fire coral (Podostroma cornu-damae) | Within 30 minutes | Fatal even in small amounts; avoid even touching it |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;One practical rule follows from this. If symptoms appeared within 6 hours of eating a mushroom, it was usually a group from which people recover; if the first symptoms appeared after more than 6 hours, it is likely a group that targets the liver or kidneys. However, this rule is only a reference for diagnosis and cannot serve as a standard for foraging, because it concerns what happens after the mushroom has already been eaten.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most reasonable judgment about mushrooms in a survival situation is not to touch them. Mushrooms provide calories at the level of leafy greens, and picking acorns in the same time yields dozens of times more. Since what can be lost and what can be gained are so far apart in size, unless it is a few species you know for certain, it is better to leave mushrooms off the foraging list.
