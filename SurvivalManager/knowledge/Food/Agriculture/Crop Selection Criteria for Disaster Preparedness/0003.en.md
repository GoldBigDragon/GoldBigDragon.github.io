## 3. Productivity
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Productivity is viewed through three items: calories (how much is produced), protein (how much protein is in it), and micronutrients (what is missing if one eats only that).

---
### 3-1. Calories per Area (g DM/㎡/year) `[E2]`
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Of the 25 items, this is the **one with the largest weight.**

```
Edible dry-matter yield D_e = yield (g fresh weight/㎡/year) × dry-matter fraction
 = calories per area (kcal/㎡/year) ÷ 3.55  ← back-calculated when the dry-matter fraction is unknown
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**3.55 kcal/g DM** is the median dry-matter energy density of 711 entries in the Korean Standard Food Composition Table. Its 10-90% range of 2.78-4.21 spans only a factor of 1.5, whereas kcal/100g on a fresh-weight basis spans a factor of 238. 83% of the fresh-weight calorie value is explained by moisture content, not by energy density.

| Grade | Self-sufficiency multiple | Threshold (g DM/㎡/year) | (t DM/ha) | Meaning |
|:--:|:--:|---:|---:|:---|
| SS | 1.50× or more | **1,106 or more** | 11.1 | One person fed, with a 50% surplus |
| S | 1.00× or more | **737 or more** | 7.4 | **The line at which one-person self-sufficiency holds** |
| A | 0.70× or more | **516 or more** | 5.2 | Covers 70% |
| B | 0.50× or more | **369 or more** | 3.7 | Covers half |
| C | 0.30× or more | **221 or more** | 2.2 | Supplementary calorie source |
| D | 0.15× or more | **111 or more** | 1.1 | Supplementary food |
| E | Below that | - | - | Does not qualify as a food crop |

> **Yield and energy density must not be set up as separate items.**
> If they are separated, moisture content becomes the score itself, so Chinese cabbage ties with potato and rice scores lower than potato.

---
#### 3-1-1. Harvest Index (HI) - A Diagnostic Indicator
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is a device to catch the phenomenon in which a crop that occupies a large amount of ground per plant but has little edible part scores high when tallied per 100 g.

```
HI = edible dry matter ÷ total above-ground dry matter
Total above-ground dry-matter requirement B_req = D_e ÷ HI
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**HI hardly changes even when the cultivation system changes.** Across the 8 treatments of DOK winter wheat, HI was 0.358-0.413 (coefficient of variation 4.4%), while total above-ground dry matter ranged from 6.65 to 13.07 t DM/ha, a 97% change. The entire difference between unfertilized and conventional comes from total biomass.

| Crop group | HI upper limit |
|:---|---:|
| Tuberous roots and tubers (potato, sweet potato, yam, cassava, Jerusalem artichoke, kudzu) | 0.80 |
| Heading and leafy vegetables (Chinese cabbage, cabbage, lettuce, kale) | 0.55 |
| Cereal grains | 0.50 |
| Legume seeds | 0.45 |
| Bulbs and stem-leaf crops (onion, garlic, chive, green onion) | 0.40 |
| Fruits and fungi | 0.35 |
| Fruit vegetables (tomato, cucumber, squash, eggplant) | 0.30 |
| Wild greens and shoots (only tender parts harvested) | 0.20 |
| Nuts and oilseeds (walnut, pine nut, almond, sesame) | 0.15 |

---
#### 3-1-2. Light-Budget Validation Gate
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There is a physical upper limit on the amount of matter a plant can make in a year, and an input value beyond it means the data are wrong.

```
Physical upper limit of total annual above-ground dry matter
 = annual global horizontal irradiation 4,734 MJ/㎡ × realized conversion efficiency 1.0% ÷ heat of combustion of dry matter 17.5 MJ/kg
 = 2,705 g DM/㎡/year (27.1 t DM/ha/year)

If B_req = D_e ÷ HI_max exceeds this value: reject the input value (do not truncate)
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The reason for rejecting rather than truncating is that the upper limit is not an estimate. Cutting to the limit would assign an unfounded optimistic value. A rejected crop is substituted with the median grade of the same crop group and marked as unconfirmed.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**This gate is not applied to fungi,** because they do not photosynthesize. Instead, edible dry matter ÷ substrate dry matter (biological efficiency) is examined, and since the productivity of imported substrate cannot be regarded as land efficiency, the grade is capped at A.

---
#### 3-1-3. Minimum-Yield Gate
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Physical impossibility on the upper side is blocked by the light-budget gate, and unit errors on the lower side are blocked by this gate.

```
Yield < 50 kg/1,000㎡ (= 50 g/㎡): reject the input
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;50 g/㎡ is of the same order as grain seeding rates (wheat 15-20 g/㎡, rice 6-9 g/㎡), so reproduction does not hold. Values in this range are regarded as unit errors, such as writing a per-log or per-bag value as a per-1,000㎡ value. In fact, all 5 species caught by this gate were mushrooms.

### 3-2. Protein - Protein-to-Energy Ratio `[E1]`

```
P:E (%) = protein (g) × 4 kcal/g ÷ total calories (kcal) × 100
```

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 25% or more | 18% or more | 12% or more | **10% or more** | 8% or more | 6% or more | Below 6% |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Because the numerator and denominator of P:E come from the same sample, the value is the same whether fresh or dry. If protein content per 100 g of fresh weight were used, moisture content would become the score itself and crops with a lot of water would be structurally disadvantaged. **B (10%) is the line, set to the WHO/FAO adult protein requirement (about 10-12% of total energy), at which the requirement is met by eating only this crop.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Bonus**: crops with an essential amino acid score of 90 or higher (potato, buckwheat, soybean, amaranth, hemp, quinoa) are raised by one grade.

| Food | Total protein (share of energy) | Available protein | P:E | Grade |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|:--:|
| Potato | 10.0% | **5.9%** | 10.6% | **A** (bonus) |
| Wheat | 13.4% | **5.9%** | 15.4% | A |
| Rice | 9.0% | 4.9% | 8.2% | C |
| Maize | 11.0% | **4.7%** | 10.3% | B |

> **By total protein the order is wheat > maize > potato, but once amino acid quality is corrected it reverses to potato = wheat > rice > maize.**
> The limiting amino acid of grains is lysine, and potato is rich in lysine.

### 3-3. Micronutrients - Actual Intake Basis `[E1]`

```
Intake = min( weight equivalent to 2,100 kcal, 2,500 g )
Micronutrient grade = number of nutrients (out of 21) that meet 10% or more of the RDA at that intake
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**The 2,500 g cap** is the physiological limit of the weight of food an adult can eat in a day. Without the cap, Chinese cabbage, which needs 13 kg to supply 2,100 kcal, would be unfairly advantaged.

> The 21 nutrients judged: vitamins A, C, D, E, K, B6, and B12, thiamin, riboflavin, niacin, pantothenic acid, folate, calcium, iron, magnesium, phosphorus, potassium, zinc, copper, manganese, and selenium. Sodium, which carries no risk of deficiency, is excluded.

|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 18 or more | 16 or more | 14 or more | 12 or more | 9 or more | 6 or more | Fewer than 6 |

| Soybean | 401 | 524 g | 524 g | 17 | S |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;On this basis the correlation between protein and micronutrients is -0.056, so the two items measure different things. Measured per 100 g of edible portion, potato with 82% moisture would be structurally disadvantaged, and the fact that it is a source of vitamin C that grains cannot replace would be erased.
