## 8. Self-Reliance, Added Value, and Palatability
### 8-1. Seed Saving `[E2]`

| Grade | Criterion |
|:--:|:---|
| SS | Unlimited multiplication of genetically identical plants by vegetative propagation |
| S | Self-pollinating, true-breeding type |
| A | Cross-pollinating, care needed against crossing |
| B | F1 hybrid - segregation of traits and reduced yield |
| C | F1 hybrid - sharp deformities and reduced survival |
| D | High difficulty of seed saving (biennial, cold vernalization, long-distance isolation) |
| E | Cannot germinate or reproduce (triploid, seedless, terminator) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Seed reserve correction**: in a disaster, the real constraint is what percentage of the harvest must be set aside as seed for planting the next year.

| Crop | Seed : harvest ratio | Seed reserve relative to harvest |
|:---|:---|---:|
| Amaranth | 1,000-1,500 : 1 | 0.07-0.1% |
| Maize | About 500 : 1 | 0.2% |
| Wheat | About 30 : 1 | 3.3% |
| **Potato** | **4.2-8.5 : 1** | **12-24%** |

> **If the seed requirement exceeds 10% of the harvest, lower the grade by one.**
> Potato requires leaving 1/8 to 1/4 of the harvest as seed potatoes, which is 30-100 times that of grains. Ten vegetatively propagated crops (potato, garlic, ginger, sweet potato, taro, yam, Jerusalem artichoke, apios, lotus root, Chinese artichoke) fall in this category.

### 8-2. Seed Longevity (Sealed Room-Temperature Storage, Period Until Germination Drops to 50%, in Years) `[E2]`

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 5 or more | 3 or more | 2 or more | 1.5 or more | 1 or more | 0.5 or more | Seed cannot be stored |

| Standard storage life | Crops |
|:--:|:---|
| 1 year | Onion, leek |
| 2 years | Maize, chili pepper, okra, spinach |
| 3 years | Kidney bean, pea, carrot, broccoli, arugula |
| 4 years | Beet, Chinese cabbage, kale, kohlrabi, tomato, squash, mustard |
| 5 years | Artichoke, celery, cucumber, lettuce, melon, radish, turnip |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The table above assumes a temperature below 10℃ and humidity below 40%. This scale assumes unpowered room temperature (15-25℃), so the values are converted conservatively.

```
Room-temperature longevity ≒ standard storage life × 0.4
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Vegetatively propagated crops rated SS for seed saving often score E on this item, so the two items must be read as complementary.

### 8-3. Domestic Cultivation Spread (Number of Provinces With Commercial Cultivation, Out of 8) `[E2]`

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 8 provinces + wild stands | 6-8 provinces | 5-7 provinces | 3-4 provinces | 1-2 provinces | Trial cultivation | No domestic cultivation |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This item follows from the premise that on the very day a disaster strikes, the only people holding seed and seedlings are farmers. However good the evaluation, a crop cannot be planted if there is no seed in the country.

### 8-4. Non-Food Value (Number Satisfied) `[E3]`
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Count how many of the following eight are satisfied.

- Medicinal use
- Timber/pulp
- Sap
- Household tools
- Soil nutrient supply
- Pest control
- Soil purification
- Attraction of beneficial insects

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 6 or more | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In a disaster, food value comes first, so the weight is kept low, and it is normal for non-food crops to receive low grades.

> **This item is the most vulnerable to evidence gaps.**
> Crops with shallow evidence score 13.1 points higher on average than crops with full evidence (the largest among the 25 items), because a claim that something is used as medicine is counted as it is. One source is required for each entry, and without it the entry is not counted.

### 8-5. Taste `[E3]`
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Even if calories and nutrition are sufficient, food that is hard to eat cannot be eaten over the long term, and satisfaction at meals is tied directly to maintaining the will to live and vitality.

| Smell/aroma | Texture | Sweetness | Grade | Definition |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:---|
| ○ | ○ | ○ | **SS** | Universal sweetness, with no off-putting smell, aroma, or texture |
| ○ | ○ | × | **S** | Everyday food with no off-putting smell, aroma, or texture |
| ○ | × | ○ | **A** | Universal sweetness, with no off-putting smell or aroma |
| ○ | × | × | **B** | Everyday food with no off-putting smell or aroma |
| × | ○ | ○ | **C** | Sweetness with no off-putting texture |
| × | ○ | × | **D** | Everyday food with no off-putting texture |
| × | × | - | **E** | Food that is avoided |

```
Sweetness bonus = (measured sugar value in the composition table ≥ 8.0 g/100g, as eaten)
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Measured sugar is used because Brix is a refractive index and also reads things that are not sugar. The high Brix of Jerusalem artichoke and yacon comes from inulin and fructans, which people do not perceive as sweet and which are not absorbed in the small intestine. The "sugars" of the composition table measure only free sugars, so they are closer to what the tongue perceives.

| **Oriental melon** | **8.82** | ○ |
| **Wild raspberry** | **7.80** | **×** |
| **Watermelon** | **7.12** | **×** |
| **Jerusalem artichoke** | **6.37** | **×** |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The grade is not assigned directly; the three attributes are kept as data so that the basis for the judgment can be traced. If it is left not as "mugwort = D" but as "off-putting aroma / acceptable texture / sugars 2.0 g → D," then when there is disagreement, one can point to which attribute is the problem.

## 9. Calculating the Overall Score
### 9-1. The Full Formula

```
Scenario score = Σ(grade score × effective weight) ÷ Σ(effective weight)
Effective weight = design target weight × evidence attenuation coefficient c(E)

Overall score = √(short-term score × long-term score) × K(calories) × corroboration coefficient
 However, 0 if caught by an exclusion gate
Supplementary food has a different role, so it is not added in but read separately
```

---
### 9-2. Weights by Scenario
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The differences between scenarios are determined only by **the temporal nature of the items.**

| Item | Short-term (0-1 year) | Long-term (1 year or more) | Supplementary |
|:---|--:|--:|--:|
| Calories per area | **3.50** | 3.00 | 0.50 |
| Yield retention without input | 1.00 | **3.50** | 1.50 |
| Fertilization | 1.75 | **3.00** | 1.75 |
| Continuous-cropping tolerance | 1.00 | **3.00** | 1.50 |
| Seed saving | 1.25 | **3.00** | 2.00 |
| Pest and disease pressure | **2.50** | 2.50 | 1.50 |
| Protein density | 1.50 | 2.00 | 2.00 |
| Storability | 1.25 | 2.00 | 0.75 |
| Yield stability | **2.00** | 1.75 | 0.75 |
| Micronutrients | 1.25 | 1.25 | **3.50** |
| Sunlight | 1.25 | 1.75 | 1.00 |
| Water consumption | 1.50 | 1.50 | 1.25 |
| Harvest speed | **2.00** | 0.75 | 1.50 |
| Perenniality | 0.75 | 1.50 | 1.50 |
| Seed longevity | 0.75 | 1.50 | 1.00 |
| Cooking and processing | 1.50 | 0.75 | 1.50 |
| Taste | 1.25 | 0.60 | 1.50 |
| Non-food value | 0.50 | 0.75 | 1.00 |
| All other items | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In the crop-selection literature, reference weights are without exception derived from expert surveys, and no weights have been validated against empirical performance data. Removing unfounded weights leaves equal weighting, but equal weighting is itself a single assumption that calories are equivalent to seed longevity. So multipliers are applied only to items whose order is clear by time horizon.

---
### 9-3. Productivity Coefficient K - Enters by Multiplication
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In disaster preparedness, production is not an additive factor that other items can substitute for but a **precondition,** so it is reflected by multiplication.

| Calorie grade | Self-sufficiency multiple | K |
|:---:|:---|---:|
| SS | ≥1.50 | **1.00** |
| S | ≥1.00 | **1.00** |
| A | ≥0.70 | 0.93 |
| B | ≥0.50 | 0.87 |
| C | ≥0.30 | 0.79 |
| D | ≥0.15 | 0.70 |
| E | <0.15 | **0 (excluded)** |

> **If K is removed, the correlation between the calorie grade and the overall score collapses from +0.662 to +0.080.**
> Because 9 of the 25 items are structured so that the less a crop needs, the higher it scores, without K a wild green with calorie grade D would sweep SS on the low-input items and overtake grains.

---
### 9-4. Corroboration Coefficient - Reduces Evidence Gaps

```
Corroboration coefficient = 1 - 0.35 × (number of cells substituted from a lower tier ÷ 25)
```

| Substituted cells | Corroboration coefficient | Attenuation |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1.000 | 0% |
| 3 | 0.958 | 4.2% |
| 7 | 0.902 | 9.8% |
| 14 | 0.804 | 19.6% |
| 25 | 0.650 | 35% |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This coefficient is not a penalty but an incentive to improve the data. If the evidence is filled in, it returns to 1.00.

---
### 9-5. Exclusion Gates
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A weighted sum alone cannot express a fatal disqualification on a single item, so gates are used.

| Gate | Condition | Exemption | Basis |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Calories | Calories per area E (below 15% of the self-sufficiency line) | - | Cannot qualify as a food crop |
| Seed saving | Seed saving E | - | Cannot reproduce |
| Storage | Storability E | Perenniality A or higher | Cannot be stockpiled. Exempt, however, for perennials harvested from the field as needed |
| Pests and diseases | Pest and disease pressure E (unprotected loss rate over 80%) | - | Cannot be grown |

> **Exclusion and grade E are different.**
> An excluded crop also has its 25-item evaluation and the score it would have had without the gate recorded. Exclusion does not mean a low score but a fatal disqualification on one item, so as soon as that item improves, the crop enters the valid set.

---
### 9-6. Conversion to Final Grade - Fixed by Percentile
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If threshold scores were fixed, the standard would shift every time a crop was added, so grades are defined by percentile rather than by score.

| Grade | Percentile (valid sample excluding exclusions) | Current corresponding score (183 species) |
|:--:|:---|---:|
| SS | Top 2% | 57.6 or more |
| S | Top 7% | 50.5 or more |
| A | Top 18% | 48.2 or more |
| B | Top 35% | 44.3 or more |
| C | Top 60% | 40.6 or more |
| D | Top 85% | 36.0 or more |
| E | Below that | - |

**Calorie floor band rule**

```
An overall grade of SS or S is given only to crops with calories per area of C (30% of the self-sufficiency line) or higher.
If not met, demote by 2 grades and always list the supplementary-food grade alongside.
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;With percentiles alone, crops used only as supplementary food (dandelion, mugwort, ixeris, and so on) would rise into the top overall bands. They clearly have value as supplementary SS but are not staple candidates, so this rule separates and shows the roles.
