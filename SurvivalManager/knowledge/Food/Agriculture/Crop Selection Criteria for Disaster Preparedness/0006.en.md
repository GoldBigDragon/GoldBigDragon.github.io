## 8. Self-Reliance, Added Value, and Palatability
### 8-1. Seed Saving `[E2]`

| Grade | Criteria |
|:--:|:---|
| SS | Propagated vegetatively; genetically identical plants can be multiplied indefinitely |
| S | Self-pollinating; maintains a true-breeding line |
| A | Cross-pollinating; cross-contamination must be guarded against |
| B | F1 hybrid — traits segregate and yield declines |
| C | F1 hybrid — sharp deformity and reduced survival |
| D | Seed saving is difficult (biennial, needs cold vernalization, long isolation distance) |
| E | Cannot germinate or reproduce (triploid, seedless, terminator) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Seed-reserve-rate correction:** in a disaster, the real constraint is "what percentage of the harvest must be set aside as seed for next year."

| Crop | Seed:Yield Ratio | Seed Reserved as Share of Harvest |
|:---|:---|---:|
| Amaranth | 1,000-1,500 : 1 | 0.07-0.1% |
| Maize | about 500 : 1 | 0.2% |
| Wheat | about 30 : 1 | 3.3% |
| **Potato** | **4.2-8.5 : 1** | **12-24%** |

> **If seed requirement exceeds 10% of the harvest, the grade drops by 1 level.**  
> The potato must have 1/8 to 1/4 of its harvest set aside as seed potatoes, 30-100× that of cereals. This applies to the 10 crops propagated by vegetative organs (potato, garlic, ginger, sweet potato, taro, Chinese yam, Jerusalem artichoke, apios, lotus root, Chinese artichoke).

### 8-2. Seed Longevity (Sealed Storage at Room Temperature, Years Until Germination Rate Falls to 50%) `[E2]`

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 5↑ | 3↑ | 2↑ | 1.5↑ | 1↑ | 0.5↑ | seed cannot be stored |

| Standard Storage Life | Crops |
|:--:|:---|
| 1 yr | Onion, leek |
| 2 yr | Maize, chili pepper, okra, spinach |
| 3 yr | Kidney bean, pea, carrot, broccoli, arugula |
| 4 yr | Beet, napa cabbage, kale, kohlrabi, tomato, squash, mustard |
| 5 yr | Artichoke, celery, cucumber, lettuce, melon, radish, turnip |

> **The table above assumes "below 10°C and below 40% humidity."**  
> Since this scale assumes unpowered room temperature (15-25°C), the figures are converted conservatively.

```
Room-temperature life ≈ standard storage life × 0.4
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Vegetatively propagated crops that score SS on seed saving often score E on this criterion, so **the two must be read as complementing each other.**

### 8-3. Prevalence in South Korea (Number of Provinces, out of 8, with Commercial Cultivation) `[E2]`

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| all 8 provinces + naturalized in the wild | 6-8 provinces | 5-7 provinces | 3-4 provinces | 1-2 provinces | trial cultivation only | not grown in South Korea |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This criterion follows from the premise that **on the day a disaster strikes, only farming households will actually have seeds or seedlings in hand.** No matter how well a crop scores otherwise, it cannot be planted if no seed exists in South Korea.

### 8-4. Non-Food Value (Number of Uses Met) `[E3]`

- Medicinal use
- Timber/pulp
- Sap
- Household tools
- Soil nutrient supply
- Pest control
- Soil remediation
- Attracting beneficial insects

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 6↑ | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In a disaster, food value takes priority, so the weight is kept low, and **it is normal for a non-food crop to receive a low grade here.**

> **This criterion is the most vulnerable to evidence gaps.**  
> Crops with shallow evidence score 13.1 points higher on average than crops with complete evidence — the largest gap of all 25 criteria. This is because a mere "said to be used medicinally" gets counted as is. Each checklist item now **requires one citable source**; without one, it is not counted.

### 8-5. Taste `[E3]`
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Even with enough calories and nutrition, **a food that is hard to eat cannot be consumed long-term,** and satisfaction from meals is directly tied to sustaining the will to live and vitality.

| Smell/Aroma | Texture | Sweetness | Grade | Definition |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:---|
| ○ | ○ | ○ | **SS** | No off-putting smell, aroma, or texture, with a universally recognizable sweetness |
| ○ | ○ | × | **S** | An everyday food with no off-putting smell, aroma, or texture |
| ○ | × | ○ | **A** | A universally recognizable sweetness with no off-putting smell or aroma |
| ○ | × | × | **B** | An everyday food with no off-putting smell or aroma |
| × | ○ | ○ | **C** | A sweetness with no off-putting texture |
| × | ○ | × | **D** | An everyday food with no off-putting texture |
| × | × | - | **E** | An avoided food |

```
Sweetness bonus = (measured sugars in composition table ≥ 8.0 g/100g, as-consumed basis)
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Why measured sugars are used:** Brix is a refractive index, so it also picks up substances that are not sugar. The high Brix of Jerusalem artichoke and yacon comes from inulin and fructans, which people do not perceive as sweet and which are not absorbed in the small intestine. The "sugars" figure in composition tables measures only free sugars, so it is **closer to what the tongue actually tastes.**

| Crop | Sugars g/100g | Bonus |
|:---|---:|:--:|
| Jujube (fresh) | 24.34 | ○ |
| Grape (Kyoho) | 14.33 | ○ |
| Apple | 10.62 | ○ |
| Sweet potato | 9.83 | ○ |
| **Oriental melon** | **8.82** | ○ |
| **Wild raspberry** | **7.80** | **×** |
| **Watermelon** | **7.12** | **×** |
| **Jerusalem artichoke** | **6.37** | **×** |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The reason the three attributes are kept as data rather than assigning a grade directly is that **the basis for the judgment stays traceable.** Instead of "mugwort = D," the record reads "off-putting aroma / unobjectionable texture / sugars 2.0g → D," so that when there is disagreement, you can point to exactly which attribute is at issue.

## 9. Overall Score Calculation
### 9-1. The Full Formula

```
Scenario score = Σ(grade score × effective weight) ÷ Σ(effective weight)
Effective weight = design target weight × evidence decay factor c(E)

Overall score = √(short-term score × long-term score) × K(calories) × corroboration coefficient
 except that it is 0 if an exclusion gate is triggered
Supplementary has a different role, so it is read separately rather than added in
```

---
### 9-2. Weights by Scenario
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The differences between scenarios are determined **solely by the time-related nature of each criterion.**

| Criterion | Short-term (0-1 yr) | Long-term (1+ yr) | Supplementary |
|:---|--:|--:|--:|
| Calories per area | **3.50** | 3.00 | 0.50 |
| Yield retention without synthetic inputs | 1.00 | **3.50** | 1.50 |
| Fertilizer demand | 1.75 | **3.00** | 1.75 |
| Continuous-cropping tolerance | 1.00 | **3.00** | 1.50 |
| Seed saving | 1.25 | **3.00** | 2.00 |
| Pest & disease pressure | **2.50** | 2.50 | 1.50 |
| Protein density | 1.50 | 2.00 | 2.00 |
| Storability | 1.25 | 2.00 | 0.75 |
| Yield stability | **2.00** | 1.75 | 0.75 |
| Micronutrients | 1.25 | 1.25 | **3.50** |
| Sunlight | 1.25 | 1.75 | 1.00 |
| Water use | 1.50 | 1.50 | 1.25 |
| Speed to harvest | **2.00** | 0.75 | 1.50 |
| Perenniality | 0.75 | 1.50 | 1.50 |
| Seed longevity | 0.75 | 1.50 | 1.00 |
| Cooking & processing | 1.50 | 0.75 | 1.50 |
| Taste | 1.25 | 0.60 | 1.50 |
| Non-food value | 0.50 | 0.75 | 1.00 |
| All other criteria | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Weight design:** in the crop-selection literature, baseline weights are, without exception, derived from expert surveys, and no weighting scheme has been validated against empirical outcome data. Removing weights that lack evidence leaves equal weighting as the remaining option, but **equal weighting is itself one assumption too — that "calories are equivalent to seed longevity."** So multipliers are applied only to criteria whose ranking is clear given the time horizon.

---
### 9-3. Productivity Coefficient K — Enters as a Multiplier
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In disaster preparedness, yield is not an additive factor that other criteria can substitute for — it is a **precondition** — so it is applied as a multiplier.

| Calorie Grade | Self-sufficiency Multiple | K |
|:---:|:---|---:|
| SS | ≥1.50 | **1.00** |
| S | ≥1.00 | **1.00** |
| A | ≥0.70 | 0.93 |
| B | ≥0.50 | 0.87 |
| C | ≥0.30 | 0.79 |
| D | ≥0.15 | 0.70 |
| E | <0.15 | **0 (excluded)** |

> **Remove K, and the correlation between the calorie grade and the overall score collapses from +0.662 to +0.080.**  
> Without this coefficient, a wild green with a calorie grade of D can sweep SS on the low-input criteria and overtake cereals, **because 9 of the 25 criteria are structured so that "needing less scores higher."**

---
### 9-4. Corroboration Coefficient — Reducing Evidence Gaps

```
Corroboration coefficient = 1 - 0.35 × (number of lower-tier imputed cells ÷ 25)
```

| Imputed Cells | Corroboration Coefficient | Decay |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1.000 | 0% |
| 3 | 0.958 | 4.2% |
| 7 | 0.902 | 9.8% |
| 14 | 0.804 | 19.6% |
| 25 | 0.650 | 35% |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**This coefficient is not a penalty; it is an incentive to improve the data.** Filling in the evidence brings it back to 1.00. The goal of the scale is not to mark crops down but to get the evidence filled in.

---
### 9-5. Exclusion Gates
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A weighted sum alone cannot express **"a single criterion's fatal disqualification."**

| Gate | Condition | Exemption | Basis |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Calories | Calories per area = E (below 15% of the self-sufficiency line) | - | Cannot qualify as a food crop |
| Seed saving | Seed saving = E | - | Cannot reproduce |
| Storage | Storability = E | Perenniality A or better | Cannot be stockpiled — except perennials harvested as needed straight from the field, which are exempt |
| Pests & disease | Pest & disease pressure = E (yield loss without control over 80%) | - | Cannot be grown |

> **Excluded and grade E are not the same thing.**  
> An excluded crop is still recorded with its full 25-criterion evaluation and "what the score would have been without the gate." Exclusion does not mean a low score — it means a fatal disqualification on one criterion — so **as soon as that criterion improves, the crop re-enters the valid set immediately.**

---
### 9-6. Final Grade Conversion — Fixed by Percentile
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fixing a threshold score would make the standard shift every time a crop is added. **Grades are defined by percentile, not by score.**

| Grade | Percentile (valid sample, excluded crops removed) | Current Corresponding Score (183 species) |
|:--:|:---|---:|
| SS | top 2% | 57.6↑ |
| S | top 7% | 50.5↑ |
| A | top 18% | 48.2↑ |
| B | top 35% | 44.3↑ |
| C | top 60% | 40.6↑ |
| D | top 85% | 36.0↑ |
| E | below that | - |

**Calorie Floor-Band Rule**

```
An overall grade of SS or S is given only to a crop whose calories-per-area grade is C (30% of the self-sufficiency line) or better.
If it falls short, downgrade it by 2 levels, and the supplementary grade must be noted alongside it.
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;By percentile alone, crops that only work as a supplementary food (dandelion, mugwort, ixeris, and the like) would land in the top overall band. Their value as a supplementary-grade SS is clear, but they are not staple-food candidates. This rule keeps **their roles marked separately.**
