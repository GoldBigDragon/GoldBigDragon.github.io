## 1. The Problem This Scale Solves
### 1-1. Disaster Is Already a Constant
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This scale calculates not "if disaster strikes" but **"if external inputs to the current structure are cut off."** The numbers below are not assumptions; they are values already observed.

| Indicator | Value |
|:---|---:|
| Grain self-sufficiency rate (2023~25 average) | 19.2% |
| Projected grain self-sufficiency rate (2035) | 16.8% |
| Projected rice production | 3.62 million t → 3.17 million t (-12.4%) |
| Farmland area (2025) | 1,499,911 ha (13th consecutive year of decline) |
| **Farmland per person** | **293.0 m²** |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In a structure that depends on imports for 80% of its grain, if logistics are cut off, all that is left is 293m² of land per person. Whether one person can live on that land is the question this scale asks.

---
### 1-2. The Self-Sufficiency Line — the Basis for Every Threshold
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Every threshold in the evaluation is derived from a single value: the **self-sufficiency line (S₀).**

```
Farmland per person = 1,499,911 ha × 10,000 ÷ 51,200,000 people = 293.0 m²/person
Minimum annual calorie requirement = 2,100 kcal/day × 365 days = 766,500 kcal/person/yr
Self-sufficiency line S₀ = 766,500 ÷ 293.0 = 2,616 kcal/m²/yr
Converted to dry matter D₀ = 2,616 ÷ 3.55 = 737 g DM/m²/yr = 7.4 t DM/ha/yr
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;2,100 kcal is the joint minimum planning figure that WHO, UNHCR, UNICEF, and WFP use for general emergency rationing. In other words, the self-sufficiency line is the boundary for **"does planting only this crop produce one person's food supply?"**

---
### 1-3. Zero-Input Conditions
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The DOK long-term trial run by Switzerland's FiBL has maintained a plot given no fertilizer at all (NOFERT) for 42 years since 1978. It is the only long-term measured data that is, in effect, identical to disaster conditions.

| Crop | Conventional (t DM/ha) | Zero-input (t DM/ha) | Retention |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| Silage maize | 19.31 | 9.74 | 50.4% |
| Winter wheat | 5.05 | 2.51 | 49.7% |
| Soybean | 2.84 | 1.80 | 63.4% |
| **Potato** | **10.61** | **1.87** | **17.6%** |

> **Under zero-input conditions, feeding one person on cereals, tuber crops, and legumes alone requires 2.9~4.1× the current farmland per person (293m²).**
> Only crops that use their entire above-ground biomass reach the self-sufficiency line, and even that is not in a form people can eat directly. This is not a flaw in the scale; it is the conclusion the scale is supposed to state.

---
### 1-4. The Three Scenarios
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Even for the same crop, the evaluation changes completely depending on its intended use.

| Use | Time Horizon | Question |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Short-term** | 0~1 yr | How much food do you get within this year from planting now? |
| **Long-term** | 1+ yr | How many years can it be sustained while saving your own seed and maintaining soil fertility? |
| **Supplementary** | - | How useful is it in a role that adds nutrition and palatability rather than serving as a staple? |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The supplementary scenario has a different character, so it is not added into the overall score; it is **read separately.** Because the supplementary scenario gives micronutrients the highest weight and lowers the weight on calories, it is normal for vegetables and wild greens to cluster near the top.

---
### 1-5. Common Measurement Assumptions

| Assumption | Value |
|:---|:---|
| Reference area | 1,000m² (10a) |
| Reference period | 1 yr (cumulative annual total for multi-harvest crops) |
| Reference weight | Edible-portion basis. For cereals and legumes, after drying and cleaning; for rice, milled grain |
| Resource conditions | A situation in which chemical fertilizer, pesticides, irrigation, electricity, and fuel are all restricted |
| Composition basis | **Unified to the state at harvest/consumption.** For crops eaten raw, raw values; for crops distributed dried, dried values |
| Moisture-basis consistency | Yield and composition values must use the same moisture basis |
| Handling missing data | Impute with the scoring group's median grade and attach a `?` flag |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Area means ground-projected area.** The growing area of multi-tier or vertical cultivation is not divided by the number of tiers. Dense planting only pushes the shading fraction closer to 1, and natural-light multi-tiering only shades the lower tiers without increasing total light capture. Artificial-light multi-tiering needs about 250 kWh of electricity per kg of plant dry matter, so it is not viable to begin with once electricity is cut off.

## 2. Grade System
### 2-1. Converting Grades to Scores
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Grades come in seven levels — SS, S, A, B, C, D, E — and the conversion to scores is **nonlinear.**

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 100 | 84 | 68 | 52 | 36 | 18 | 0 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;An ordinal scale must not be averaged as the integers 0-6. There is no basis for the SS→S gap and the D→E gap being equal, and in reality **E is close to a fatal disqualification, so the lower end must drop off more steeply.**

---
### 2-2. Numeric Criteria Are Derived from Threshold Tables
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Assigning a grade by hand is limited to descriptive criteria. For numeric criteria, the raw value is entered into the threshold table and the grade is derived mechanically.

```
dir = desc (higher is better) → if value ≥ threshold, that grade applies
dir = asc (lower is better) → if value ≤ threshold, that grade applies
When the source evidence is given as a range, the lower bound is adopted conservatively
```

---
### 2-3. Evidence Strength Is Recorded Alongside the Grade
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Every grade is marked together with **where the value came from.** A criterion with weak evidence is not excluded from scoring; instead, the score is discounted at the crop level.

| Tag | Meaning | Decay Factor |
|:--:|:---|---:|
| **E1** | Primary measurement - uses the figure from a recognized database, long-term trial, or literature table as is | 1.00 |
| **E2** | Literature-derived - calculated from a standard or definition in the literature, or follows an institutional classification | 0.85 |
| **E3** | Self-estimated - judgment based on conventional-cultivation knowledge, with no primary source | 0.60 |

> **If a criterion is excluded for lack of evidence, "unknown" gets mislabeled as "not applicable."**  
> Then, even after the data is filled in, there is no way to know whether the scale has improved. So a criterion with shallow evidence should not be deleted — it should be **flagged and discounted.**
