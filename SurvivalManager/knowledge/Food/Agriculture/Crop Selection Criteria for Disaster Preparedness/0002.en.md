## 1. The Scale
### 1-1. Disaster
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This scale calculates not "if a disaster comes" but **"if external inputs are cut off under the present structure."** The numbers below are not assumptions but values that have already been observed.

| Indicator | Value |
|:---|---:|
| Grain self-sufficiency rate (2023-25 average) | 19.2% |
| Grain self-sufficiency rate outlook (2035) | 16.8% |
| Rice production outlook | 3.62 million t → 3.17 million t (-12.4%) |
| Cultivated area (2025) | 1,499,911 ha (13 consecutive years of decline) |
| **Cultivated area per person** | **293.0 ㎡** |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In a structure that depends on imports for 80% of its grain, if logistics are cut off, all that remains is 293㎡ of land per person. Whether one person can live on that land is the question this scale asks.

---
### 1-2. Self-Sufficiency Line
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Every threshold in the evaluation is derived from a single value, the **self-sufficiency line (S₀)**.

```
Cultivated area per person = 1,499,911 ha × 10,000 ÷ 51,200,000 people = 293.0 ㎡/person
Minimum annual energy requirement = 2,100 kcal/day × 365 days = 766,500 kcal/person/year
Self-sufficiency line S₀ = 766,500 ÷ 293.0 = 2,616 kcal/㎡/year
Dry-matter conversion D₀ = 2,616 ÷ 3.55 = 737 g DM/㎡/year = 7.4 t DM/ha/year
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;2,100 kcal is the joint minimum planning figure that WHO, UNHCR, UNICEF, and WFP use in general emergency ration planning. The self-sufficiency line is the boundary of **"does planting only this crop produce food for one person?"**

---
### 1-3. No-Input Conditions
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The DOK long-term trial of FiBL in Switzerland has kept a plot that receives no fertilizer at all (NOFERT) for 42 years since 1978. It is the only long-term measurement that is practically the same as disaster conditions.

| Crop | Conventional (t DM/ha) | No input (t DM/ha) | Retention rate |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| Silage maize | 19.31 | 9.74 | 50.4% |
| Winter wheat | 5.05 | 2.51 | 49.7% |
| Soybean | 2.84 | 1.80 | 63.4% |
| **Potato** | **10.61** | **1.87** | **17.6%** |

> **To feed one person from grains, tubers, and legumes under no-input conditions, 2.9-4.1 times the present cultivated area per person (293㎡) is needed.**
> Only crops whose entire above-ground part is used reach the self-sufficiency line, and even then it is not in a form that people can eat as it is. This is not a flaw of the scale but the conclusion the scale delivers.

---
### 1-4. Scenarios
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The same crop is evaluated completely differently depending on its use.

| Use | Time horizon | Question |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Short-term** | 0-1 year | If planted now, how much food is obtained within this year? |
| **Long-term** | 1 year or more | How many years can one last while renewing seed oneself and maintaining soil fertility? |
| **Supplementary food** | - | How useful is it in a role that adds nutrition and palatability rather than serving as the staple? |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Supplementary food differs in nature, so it is not added into the overall score and is **read separately.** In the supplementary scenario, micronutrients get the largest weight and calories a small one, so it is normal for vegetables and wild greens to occupy the top ranks in large numbers.

---
### 1-5. Common Measurement Assumptions

| Assumption | Value |
|:---|:---|
| Reference area | 1,000㎡ (10a) |
| Reference period | 1 year (cumulative per year for crops harvested several times) |
| Reference weight | Edible portion. Grains and legumes after drying and cleaning; rice as milled rice |
| Resource conditions | A situation in which chemical fertilizer, pesticides, irrigation, electricity, and fuel are all restricted |
| Composition basis | **Unified to the harvested or eaten state.** Raw values for crops eaten raw, dry values for crops distributed dry |
| Moisture-basis consistency | Yield and composition values must use the same moisture basis |
| Missing data | Substituted with the median grade of the scoring group and marked with a `?` flag |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Area is the projected ground area.** The cultivated area of multilayer or vertical cultivation is not divided by the number of layers. Dense planting only raises the shading rate, and natural-light multilayer cultivation only casts shade on the lower layers without increasing the total light received. Artificial-light multilayer cultivation needs about 250 kWh of electricity per kg of plant dry matter, so it cannot work at all when electricity is cut off.

## 2. Grading System
### 2-1. Score Conversion
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There are seven grades, SS, S, A, B, C, D, and E, and the conversion to scores is **nonlinear**.

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 100 | 84 | 68 | 52 | 36 | 18 | 0 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Grades must not be averaged as integers from 0 to 6. There is no basis for the gap from SS to S being equal to the gap from D to E, and E is close to a fatal disqualification, so the downside should be steeper.

---
### 2-2. Numeric Items
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Grades are written by hand only for descriptive items. For numeric items, the raw value is put into the threshold table and the grade is derived mechanically.

```
dir = desc (larger is better): the grade is met if value ≥ threshold
dir = asc (smaller is better): the grade is met if value ≤ threshold
Evidence given as a range: the lower bound is adopted conservatively
```

---
### 2-3. Evidence
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Every grade is marked with **where the value came from.** Items with weak evidence are not excluded from scoring; instead the score is attenuated crop by crop.

| Mark | Meaning | Attenuation coefficient |
|:--:|:---|---:|
| **E1** | Primary measurement - figures from an official database, long-term trial, or literature table used as they are | 1.00 |
| **E2** | Derived from literature - calculated from criteria or definitions in the literature, or following an institutional classification | 0.85 |
| **E3** | Own estimate - judgment based on conventional cultivation knowledge, no primary source | 0.60 |

> **If an item is excluded because it lacks evidence, "unknown" is wrongly recorded as "not applicable."**
> Items with weak evidence must be marked and attenuated rather than deleted, so that one can tell whether the scale improved when the data are filled in.
