## 4. Time
### 4-1. Speed to Harvest (Transplanting/Sowing → First Harvest) `[E2]`

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 30 days or less | 60 days | 90 days | 120 days | 180 days | 2 yr (730 days) | more than 2 yr |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**What this measures is "the time until you hold the first harvest in your hand," not "how long one harvesting session takes."** Picking a leaf itself may take seconds, but if growing the tree that leaf hangs from takes 3 years, this criterion's value is 3 years.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The D band is wide, 180 days to 2 years, **in order to separate overwintering crops from woody plants.** Wheat, barley, rye, garlic, and onion are harvested within the same year, around 240 days, while a chestnut tree takes 7 years. Putting the two in the same grade would strip the criterion of its power to discriminate.

| Crop | Transplanting → First Harvest | Grade |
|:---|:---|:--:|
| Goji berry | same year as cutting (about 200 days) | C |
| Gomchwi (Ligularia fischeri) | 1 yr | D |
| Bracken fern · Korean black raspberry | 2 yr | D |
| Mulberry | 2-3 yr | E |
| Kiwifruit | 3 yr | E |
| Fig | 4-5 yr | E |
| Pomegranate · pistachio | 5-6 yr | E |
| Ginkgo | 5-8 yr | E |
| **Chestnut** | **7-8 yr** | **E** |
| Sansuyu (Cornus officinalis) | 7-8 yr | E |

> **Planting woody fruit trees in the first year of a disaster is an investment, not a way to secure food.**  
> It is well worth planting them, but you should not expect food from them this year.

### 4-2. Perenniality (Period Sustained Without Replanting) `[E2]`

| Grade | Criteria |
|:--:|:---|
| SS | Harvests continuously for 5+ years after a single transplanting |
| S | Harvests continuously for 2-4 years |
| A | Repeated harvests within 1 year, then resown |
| B | 3 or more harvests within 1 year |
| C | 2 harvests within 1 year |
| D | 1 harvest per year |
| E | Takes 2+ years to first harvest, or cannot be grown again after harvest |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The number of harvests per year is already reflected in calories per area, so this criterion looks only at **"how many years can pass without replanting."**

## 5. Input Resources
### 5-1. Fertilizer Demand (Annual N+P+K, g/m² + Off-take) `[E2]`

```
Fertilizer grade = application-rate grade × 0.5 + off-take grade × 0.5 (when both are available)
Off-take = Σ(dry weight of removed parts × N-P-K content of each part) / area
 For crops whose residue is returned to the field, only the removed parts are counted
```

**① Application rate** (conventional farming, annual N+P+K, g/m²)

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 10 or less | 16 | 20 | 26 | 34 | 44 | more than 44 |

**② Off-take** (removed N+P₂O₅+K₂O, g/m²/yr)

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 4 or less | 7 | 11 | 16 | 22 | 30 | more than 30 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Application rate and off-take rank crops in different orders.**

| Crop | Application Grade | Dry Matter Removed | N Off-take | K Off-take |
|:---|:--:|---:|---:|---:|
| Maize (grain) | **D** (40 g/m²) | 516 kg | 7.2 kg | **2.1 kg** |
| Potato (tuber) | **C** (32 g/m²) | 600 kg | 8.4 kg | **12.6 kg** |

(Per 1,000m²) Maize looks worse by application rate, but by off-take — especially potassium — **the potato is 6× worse.** Returning maize residue to the field brings back a substantial share of nutrients, while potato removes the whole tuber.

> **The nitrogen-fixation bonus is conditional.**  
> Nitrogen off-take in soybean seed (59.2 kg/t) is 4.5× that of maize. Even if atmospheric nitrogen is fixed, if more is removed in the seed than that, the soil nitrogen balance is still negative. So the bonus applies **only when the leaf and stem residue is returned to the field.**

### 5-2. Water Use (Total Annual Evapotranspiration (ET), L/m²) `[E2]`

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 100 or less | 250 | 450 | 700 | 1,000 | 1,500 | more than 1,500 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**This is based on total requirement, including rainfall.** Reading it as "irrigation requirement" would put every crop that rainfall alone can cover into SS, erasing the criterion's power to discriminate. Ordinary open-field crops fall in the 400-800mm range, so the real discriminating band is A-C; SS and S are reserved for drought-tolerant C4 crops, and D and E for aquatic and subtropical crops.

> **Correlates +0.613 with the fertilizer-demand criterion**: crops that need heavy fertilizing are usually also water-hungry. Both are independent physical constraints, so no correction is applied, but you should read the scores bearing in mind that **the overall score of a crop that is low on both criteria is inflated by roughly this correlation.**

### 5-3. Sunlight — Relative Yield at 40% Shade (%) `[E1]`
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is the axis that separates crops in **disasters where light is cut first** — volcanic eruption, nuclear winter, prolonged overcast skies.

| Crop group | 20% shade | **40% shade** | 60% shade |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| Berries | 114% | **114%** | insufficient data |
| Tree fruits | 114% | **113%** | insufficient data |
| Fruit vegetables | 108% | **102%** | insufficient data |
| Forage crops | 103% | **93%** | about 85% |
| C3 cereals | about 97% | **about 88%** | about 75% |
| Leafy vegetables | 100% or more | **86%** | about 70% |
| Tubers & root crops | about 95% | **about 80%** | about 60% |
| Grain legumes | about 85% | **50%** | about 30% |
| **C4 crops (maize)** | about 80% | **45%** | about 25% |
| Fungi | - | *not dependent on photosynthesis* | - |

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 110%↑ | 100%↑ | 90%↑ | 80%↑ | 65%↑ | 50%↑ | below 50% |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**C4 crops and legumes are the most vulnerable.** At 40% shade, maize loses 55% of its yield and grain legumes lose 50%. Berries and tree fruits, by contrast, actually gain yield.

| Soot Injection | Solar Radiation Decrease | Temperature Drop | Cereal Calorie Output |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 Tg (regional conflict) | about 5% | 1.5°C | -7% |
| 27 Tg | - | - | about -35% |
| 47 Tg | about 40% | - | about -50% |
| 150 Tg (all-out war) | **about 65%** | 14.8°C | **about -90%** |

> **These values must not be applied directly to volcanic winter or nuclear winter.**  
> Shade-cloth trials reduce **only light.** Relative yields above 100% for berries and tree fruits occur because shading also relieves heat and moisture stress at the same time, whereas a real soot scenario brings **reduced solar radiation and low temperature together.** The cold-hardiness criterion covers the low-temperature side, so the two criteria must be read together.

### 5-4. Infrastructure Needs `[E3]`
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is defined not as labor calories but as **the annual burden amortized over the years of use after installation.**

| Grade | Criteria |
|:--:|:---|
| SS | No infrastructure needed |
| S | Only small materials, used for several years |
| A | Stakes, shade netting, etc. renewed every 3-5 years |
| B | Structure lasts 5+ years after initial installation |
| C | Needs annual repair |
| D | Large structure, renewed every 3-5 years |
| E | Large structure, entirely consumed every year |

### 5-5. Cooking & Processing Requirements `[E3]`
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**In a disaster, fuel is the scarcest resource of all.**

| Grade | Criteria |
|:--:|:---|
| SS | Eaten raw immediately, just washed |
| S | Eaten raw after tool-free handling such as peeling or cutting |
| A | Heated for 10 minutes or less |
| B | Heated for 30 minutes or less |
| C | Heated for 60 minutes or less, or a brief soak followed by heating |
| D | Long soak (6 hr+) plus heating for 1 hour or more |
| E | **Requires dedicated equipment** such as threshing, milling, or grinding, or multi-stage processing is essential to remove toxins |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Toxicity flag** (noted separately from the grade): solanine, lectins, cyanogenic glycosides, trypsin inhibitors, oxalic acid, wisterin, thujone, favism, and the like.

> **Correlates -0.672 with the pest & disease pressure criterion**: this reflects the real pattern that "the more processing a storable grain needs, the more resistant it tends to be to pests and disease," so no correction is applied.
