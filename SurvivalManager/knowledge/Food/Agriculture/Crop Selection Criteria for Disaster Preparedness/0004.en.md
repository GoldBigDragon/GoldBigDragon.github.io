## 4. Time
### 4-1. Harvest Speed (Transplanting (Sowing) → First Harvest) `[E2]`

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 30 days or less | 60 days | 90 days | 120 days | 180 days | 2 years (730 days) | Over 2 years |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;What is measured is **the period until the first harvest is in hand,** not the time it takes to harvest once. Picking a leaf may take a few seconds, but if it takes 3 years to establish the tree that bears the leaf, the value is 3 years. The D range (180 days to 2 years) is wide in order to separate overwintering crops that are harvested within the same year (wheat, barley, rye, garlic, onion; about 240 days) from woody plants such as the chestnut tree, which takes 7 years.

| Crop | Planting → first harvest | Grade |
|:---|:---|:--:|
| Goji berry | Year of cutting planting (about 200 days) | C |
| Gomchwi (Fischer's ligularia) | 1 year | D |
| Bracken, Korean black raspberry | 2 years | D |
| Mulberry | 2-3 years | E |
| Kiwi (hardy kiwi) | 3 years | E |
| Fig | 4-5 years | E |
| Pomegranate, pistachio | 5-6 years | E |
| Ginkgo | 5-8 years | E |
| **Chestnut** | **7-8 years** | **E** |
| Cornelian cherry | 7-8 years | E |

> **Planting woody fruit trees in the first year of a disaster is an investment, not a way of securing food.**
> They are well worth planting, but do not expect anything to eat from them this year.

### 4-2. Perenniality (Period of Continuing Without Replanting) `[E2]`

| Grade | Criterion |
|:--:|:---|
| SS | Harvested continuously for 5 years or more after a single planting |
| S | Harvested continuously for 2-4 years |
| A | Harvested repeatedly within a year, then resown |
| B | Harvested 3 or more times within a year |
| C | Harvested twice within a year |
| D | Harvested once a year |
| E | Takes 2 years or more to harvest, or cannot be grown again after harvest |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The number of harvests per year is already reflected in calories per area, so this item looks only at **how many years the crop can go without being replanted.**

## 5. Input Resources
### 5-1. Fertilization (Annual N+P+K, g/㎡ + Nutrient Removal) `[E2]`

```
Fertilization grade = input grade × 0.5 + removal grade × 0.5 (when both are available)
Removal = Σ(dry matter of removed parts × N, P, K content of each part) / area
 For crops whose residue is returned to the field, only the removed parts are counted
```

**① Input** (annual N+P+K under conventional cultivation, g/㎡)

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 10 or less | 16 | 20 | 26 | 34 | 44 | Over 44 |

**② Removal** (removed N+P₂O₅+K₂O, g/㎡/year)

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 4 or less | 7 | 11 | 16 | 22 | 30 | Over 30 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Input and removal give a different order. (per 1,000㎡)

| Crop | Input grade | Dry matter removed | N removal | K removal |
|:---|:--:|---:|---:|---:|
| Maize (grain) | **D** (40 g/㎡) | 516 kg | 7.2 kg | **2.1 kg** |
| Potato (tuber) | **C** (32 g/㎡) | 600 kg | 8.4 kg | **12.6 kg** |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;By input, maize is worse, but by removal, especially of potassium, **potato is 6 times worse.** If maize residue is returned to the field, a considerable share of the nutrients comes back, but potato is lifted out of the field tuber and all.

> **The nitrogen-fixation bonus is conditional.**
> The seed nitrogen removal of soybean (59.2 kg/t) is 4.5 times that of maize. Even if it fixes atmospheric nitrogen, if it removes more than that in the seed, the soil nitrogen balance is negative, so the bonus applies only when the stem and leaf residue is returned to the field.

### 5-2. Water Consumption (Annual Total Evapotranspiration (ET), L/㎡) `[E2]`

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 100 or less | 250 | 450 | 700 | 1,000 | 1,500 | Over 1,500 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**This is based on total requirement including rainfall.** If it were interpreted as irrigation requirement, every crop met by rainfall alone would be SS and discrimination would be lost. Ordinary field crops are distributed over 400-800mm, so the practical discriminating range is A-C; SS and S are reserved for drought-tolerant C4 crops, and D and E for aquatic and subtropical crops.

> **Correlation with the fertilization item +0.613**: crops that need a lot of fertilizer generally need a lot of water. Both are independent physical constraints, so no correction is made, but the overall score of a crop that is low on both should be read as inflated by about this correlation.

### 5-3. Sunlight (Relative Yield % Under 40% Shading) `[E1]`
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is the axis that divides crop choice in disasters where **light is cut first,** such as volcanic eruptions, nuclear winter, and prolonged overcast.

| Crop group | 20% shading | **40% shading** | 60% shading |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| Berries | 114% | **114%** | Insufficient data |
| Woody fruit trees | 114% | **113%** | Insufficient data |
| Fruit vegetables | 108% | **102%** | Insufficient data |
| Forage crops | 103% | **93%** | About 85% |
| C3 cereals | About 97% | **About 88%** | About 75% |
| Leafy vegetables | 100% or more | **86%** | About 70% |
| Tubers and root vegetables | About 95% | **About 80%** | About 60% |
| Grain legumes | About 85% | **50%** | About 30% |
| **C4 crops (maize)** | About 80% | **45%** | About 25% |
| Fungi | - | *Independent of photosynthesis* | - |

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 110% or more | 100% or more | 90% or more | 80% or more | 65% or more | 50% or more | Below 50% |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**C4 crops and legumes are the most vulnerable.** Under 40% shading, maize loses 55% of its yield and grain legumes lose 50%. Conversely, the yield of berries and woody fruit trees actually increases.

| Soot injection | Insolation decrease | Temperature decrease | Grain calorie production |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 Tg (regional conflict) | About 5% | 1.5 ℃ | -7% |
| 27 Tg | - | - | About -35% |
| 47 Tg | About 40% | - | About -50% |
| 150 Tg (full-scale war) | **About 65%** | 14.8 ℃ | **About -90%** |

> **These values must not be applied as they are to a volcanic winter or nuclear winter.**
> Shading experiments reduce only light. The relative yield of berries and fruit trees exceeds 100% because shading also relieves heat and water stress, while an actual soot scenario brings reduced insolation and low temperatures at the same time. Low temperature is handled by the cold-tolerance item, so the two items must be read together.

### 5-4. Facility Burden `[E3]`
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It is defined not as labor calories but as **the annual burden amortized over the years of use after installation.**

| Grade | Criterion |
|:--:|:---|
| SS | No facilities needed |
| S | Small materials only, used for several years |
| A | Stakes, shade nets, and the like renewed every 3-5 years |
| B | Structure installed initially and maintained for 5 years or more |
| C | Annual repair required |
| D | Large facility, renewed every 3-5 years |
| E | Large facility + entirely consumed every year |

### 5-5. Cooking and Processing Requirement `[E3]`
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In a disaster, fuel is the scarcest resource.

| Grade | Criterion |
|:--:|:---|
| SS | Eaten raw immediately after washing |
| S | Eaten raw after toolless handling such as peeling or cutting |
| A | Heated within 10 minutes |
| B | Heated within 30 minutes |
| C | Heated within 60 minutes, or heated after a short soak |
| D | Long soak (6 hours or more) + heating for 1 hour or more |
| E | **Separate equipment required** for threshing, polishing, or milling, or multistage processing essential to remove toxins |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Toxicity flags** are marked separately from the grade. They include solanine, lectins, cyanogenic glycosides, trypsin inhibitors, oxalic acid, wisterin, thujone, and favism.

> **Correlation with pest and disease pressure -0.672**: it reflects the real structure in which storable grains that need a lot of processing are stronger against pests and diseases, so no correction is made.
