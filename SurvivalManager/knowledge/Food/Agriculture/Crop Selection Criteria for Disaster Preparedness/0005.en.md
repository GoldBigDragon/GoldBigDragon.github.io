## 6. Environmental Adaptation
### 6-1. Temperature Range (Span That Still Permits Harvest, °C) `[E2]`

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 20↑ | 16↑ | 14↑ | 12↑ | 10↑ | 8↑ | below 8 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The primary value used is FAO ECOCROP's `TMAX - TMIN` (absolute temperature range). For leafy vegetables, root crops, and perennials where fruiting is not the goal, the absolute range is used; for crops such as garlic and onion that have a separate condition — bulb or clove swelling — the optimal range is used.

---
### 6-2. Cold Hardiness (Limiting Low Temperature, Unprotected Open Field, °C) `[E2]`

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| -20 or below | -10 | -5 | 0 | 2 | 5 | more than 5 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Range alone cannot tell you the **absolute position.** 5-25°C and 15-35°C both span 20°C, but the seasons and regions where each can be grown are entirely different.

> **Correlates +0.535 with the temperature-range criterion**: both come from the same ECOCROP data and overlap more than half. They are still kept separate because range and position serve different decisions in disaster response. Range answers **when** you can plant; cold hardiness answers **where** you can plant.

## 7. Cultivation Stability
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;With 8 criteria, this is the largest category, because the actual causes of crop failure in a disaster are concentrated here.

---
### 7-1. Pest & Disease Pressure (Yield Loss Without Pest Control, %) `[E1]`

```
Pest & disease pressure = total loss rate without control - loss rate from weeds
 = loss from pathogens + insect pests + viruses
```

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 5% or less | 15% | 30% | 45% | 60% | 80% | more than 80% |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Separating out weeds is the key move.** Loss without control includes 23-40 percentage points from weeds, and because weeding can be done by hand, machine, or chemicals, its control efficiency is 74%, while pathogens and insect pests reach only 32% and 39% respectively. In other words, **weeds can be dealt with by hand, but pests and diseases cannot.**

| Crop | Pests & disease only, no control | Actual loss under current control | Grade assigned |
|:---|---:|---:|:--:|
| Wheat | 26.8% | 21.5% | **A** |
| Soybean | 23.1% | 21.4% | **A** |
| Maize | 28.2% | 22.5% | **A** |
| **Potato** | **44.6%** | **17.2%** | **B** |
| Rice | 39.9% | 30.0% | **B** |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The potato is the crop with the largest swing between having control and not (conventional potato receives an average of 15 treatments a year), and this independently confirms, from separate data, the finding that potato retention alone collapsed to 17.6% in the zero-input trial.

> **"Number of known pests and diseases" must not be used as an indicator.**  
> That would measure not the crop's vulnerability but **how much agricultural research has been done on it.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For reference, in 5 years of paddy-rice pest surveillance in South Korea, the actual yield loss after control averaged 0.29% across counties and years. Against 39.9% without control, this means **the current control system suppresses 99.3% of the loss.** A disaster is the state in which that system is gone.

### 7-3. Storability (Unpowered Room-Temperature Storage Period + Measured Loss Rate) `[E2]`
**Axis 1 — Storage Period** (room temperature 15-25°C without power, ventilated indoor space, no processing, in days)

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 1,095↑ | 365↑ | 180↑ | 90↑ | 30↑ | 7↑ | below 7 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Assuming "a cold, dry environment" presupposes refrigeration infrastructure, which conflicts with the purpose here. **Unpowered room temperature is the real condition.** Burial in the ground and root-cellar-pit storage are both accepted. That said, grains must be dried down to 13-14% moisture for safe storage.

**Axis 2 — Measured Loss Rate During Storage**

| Loss-Rate Band | Representative Items (median %) |
|:---|:---|
| below 0.5 | Soybean 0.25 · cashew 0.17 · sugarcane 0.14 |
| 0.5-1.5 | Rice 1.20 · foxtail & proso millet 1.18 · barley 1.30 · mushrooms 1.62 |
| 1.5-3 | Wheat 2.65 · sorghum 2.60 · tomato 2.85 · napa cabbage 2.25 |
| 3-5 | **Maize 3.00** · cassava (fresh) 3.50 · **potato 4.00** |
| 5-10 | Welsh onion & onions 6.50 · walnut 8.00 · carrot & turnip 8.94 |
| 10 or more | Cassava (dried) 11.46 · **Chinese yam 14.00** · citrus 18.60 · green garlic 27.43 |

> **Loss rate is used only to lower the grade. It cannot be used to raise it.**  
> Loss rate is not normalized by storage duration. Lettuce stored for 3 days and wheat stored for 6 months sit in the same table. **Lettuce's low loss rate is not because it keeps well, but because it is not kept long.** Raising storability on the strength of a low loss rate produces exactly the backwards conclusion.

```
Loss rate 5% or more → lower the period grade by 1 level
Loss rate 10% or more → lower it by 2 levels (only when there is an observation for that species itself)
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Bonus:** if natural drying, salting, or fermentation alone — needing no fuel or equipment — reaches SS-level storability, the grade is raised by 1 level.

### 7-4. Continuous-Cropping Tolerance (Rotation-Break Length, in Years) `[E2]`

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| no harm from continuous cropping | 1 yr | 2 yr | 3 yr | 5 yr | 7 yr | 10+ yr |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Nightshades, cucurbits (except squash), and legumes are the crops that most need to avoid continuous cropping, and eggplant and watermelon in particular suffer greatly from it.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Medicinal crops are far worse.** About 70% of plants grown for medicinal roots suffer severe continuous-cropping injury. Ginseng, even after just one crop cycle, shows reduced yield and quality on the same land for 10+ years, and Pinellia ternata loses 80%+ of its yield within 2-4 years, requiring a 5-12 year fallow. **The cause is not only pathogens but the self-toxicity of phenolic acids the plant secretes itself,** so neither pesticides nor crop rotation alone can solve it.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Rotation raises the yield of the following crop by **20%**, and by 23% when a legume precedes it. So **"a rotation break of 0 years" means only "no break is needed," not "exempt from yield loss."** A crop that tolerates continuous cropping but demands heavy fertilizing is marked down one grade, while maize, for which continuous-cropping collapse without fertilizer has been measured, is kept at A.

> **Rice is the exception.** Flooding suppresses soil-borne disease, making it the one case where thousands of years of continuous cropping have been proven, across Asia.

### 7-5. Tillage & Digging Energy (Calorie Recovery Multiple) `[E2]`
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**The calories harvested must exceed the calories spent planting.** With no tools and no fuel, this becomes a real constraint.

| Soil type | Density (kg/m³) | Rate (m³/hr) | **kcal/m³** |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sand | 1,500 | 0.363 | **2,037** |
| Soil (ordinary earth) | 1,600 | 0.272 | **2,716** |
| Clay (hard earth) | 1,800 | 0.097 | **7,640** |
| Gravel | 1,900 | 0.076 | **9,677** |
| Rock (cobble) | 2,000 | 0.044 | **16,977** |

*An 80kg adult digging vigorously with a shovel or pickaxe uses 739 kcal per hour.*

**Calories Used per Planting Hole** (10cm diameter)

| Soil type | 10cm deep | 15cm | 20cm | 30cm |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sand | 1.6 kcal | 2.4 | 3.2 | 4.8 |
| Soil | 2.1 | 3.2 | 4.3 | 6.4 |
| Clay | 6.0 | 9.0 | 12.0 | 18.0 |
| Gravel | 7.6 | 11.4 | 15.2 | 22.8 |
| Rock | 13.3 | 20.0 | 26.7 | **40.0** |

```
Annual digging energy = volume dug (m³/m²) × kcal/m³ × tillage factor 0.4 ÷ amortization years
Recovery multiple R = calories per area (kcal/m²/yr) ÷ annual digging energy
```

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 100×↑ | 50↑ | 20↑ | 10↑ | 5↑ | 2↑ | below 2 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Sustained-work correction:** the hourly figures above are short-burst maximum intensity. Net energy for farm work sustainable over 8 hours is about 44% of that — 220 kcal/hr for moderate-intensity gardening, 280 kcal/hr for heavy digging — so this factor is applied.

### 7-6. Yield Retention Without Synthetic Inputs `[E1]`
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is the share of yield retained once synthetic fertilizer and synthetic pesticides are **both** cut off. The evidence is **the only long-term measured data that is, in effect, identical to disaster conditions.**

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 90%↑ | 58%↑ | 50%↑ | 44%↑ | 36%↑ | 25%↑ | below 25% |

| Crop | Zero-input | Conventional | **Zero-Input Retention** |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| Soybean | 1.80 | 2.84 | **63.4%** |
| Grass-clover | 7.40 | 14.48 | **51.1%** |
| Silage maize | 9.74 | 19.31 | **50.4%** |
| Wheat | 2.51 | 5.05 | **49.7%** |
| **Potato** | **1.87** | **10.61** | **17.6%** |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**The reason only the potato collapses** is that without fungicide, plants infected with late blight die early, shortening the growing period. Conventional potato receives an average of 15 treatments a year.

**Time decay:** the longer zero-input conditions persist, the further retention drops, but it does not go to zero.

```
Long-term effective retention = listed retention × δ
δ = 1.00 (0-10 yr) / 0.75 (10-50 yr) / 0.45 (50+ yr)
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The UK's unfertilized wheat plot, continuous for 170 years, holds **equilibrium** at about 1 t/ha (about 11% of conventional). **The more important conclusion is that retention converges on a floor rather than on zero.**

### 7-7. Yield Stability (Year-to-Year Yield Variation, %) `[E3]`
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This criterion looks not at average-year yield but at **the floor value in a bad year.**

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 4% or less | 6% | 9% | 13% | 18% | 25% | more than 25% |

| Crop | Variation | Grade |
|:---|---:|:--:|
| Oriental melon | 3.2% | SS |
| Tomato | 4.9% | S |
| Cucumber · squash · watermelon | 6.1-6.4% | A |
| Onion | 13.0% | B |
| Rice | 14.7% | C |
| Wheat | 19.2% | D |
| **Maize** | **24.8%** | **D** |

> **Maize is the least stable of the major cereals.** If you bet everything on maize because its average yield is high, a quarter of your food disappears in a bad year.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Greenhouse and open field differ.** For cucumber, greenhouse is 9.2 versus open field 6.1; for tomato, greenhouse is 7.2 versus open field 4.9. Since this scale assumes no power and no structures, **only the open-field value is used.**

### 7-8. Mushroom By-Product Suitability `[E3]` — Fungi Only
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is the number of by-product types that can serve as a substrate nutrient source. The 7-item checklist is as follows.

- Wood by-products (chips, firewood, logs)
- Fiber (plant matter, fruit peel)
- Slaughter by-products (protein, fat)
- Charcoal & ash
- Bone & shells
- Processed pulp (cardboard, tissue paper, paper, wallpaper)
- Metal by-products (iron filings)

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 6↑ | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Since this physically does not apply to plants, it is removed from the denominator and is not added into the overall score.
