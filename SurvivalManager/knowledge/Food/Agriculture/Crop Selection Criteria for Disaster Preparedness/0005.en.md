## 6. Environmental Adaptation
### 6-1. Temperature Range for Harvest (℃) `[E2]`

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 20 or more | 16 or more | 14 or more | 12 or more | 10 or more | 8 or more | Below 8 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;`TMAX - TMIN` (the absolute temperature range) of FAO ECOCROP is used as the primary value. For leafy vegetables, root vegetables, and perennials grown for something other than fruit set, the absolute range is used; for crops with a separate condition such as scale or bulb enlargement, like garlic and onion, the optimum range is used.

---
### 6-2. Cold Tolerance (Lowest Temperature Limit in an Uncovered Field, ℃) `[E2]`

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| -20 or lower | -10 | -5 | 0 | 2 | 5 | Over 5 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The temperature range alone does not reveal the absolute position. 5-25℃ and 15-35℃ both have a range of 20℃, but the seasons and regions in which they can be grown are entirely different.

> **Correlation with the temperature range +0.535**: the two items come from the same ECOCROP data and overlap by more than half. They are still kept separate because they serve different uses. The range answers **when** a crop can be planted, and cold tolerance answers **where** it can be planted.

## 7. Cultivation Stability
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This section has the most items, eight, because the causes of crops actually dying in a disaster are concentrated here.

---
### 7-1. Pest and Disease Pressure (Unprotected Loss Rate %) `[E1]`

```
Pest and disease pressure = total unprotected loss rate - weed loss rate
 = loss rate from pathogens + pests + viruses
```

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 5% or less | 15% | 30% | 45% | 60% | 80% | Over 80% |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Separating out weeds is the key.** The unprotected loss rate includes 23-40 percentage points from weeds. Weeding can be done by hand, machine, or chemicals, so its control efficiency is 74%, while that of pathogens and pests is only 32% and 39%, respectively. Weeds can be handled by hand, but pests and diseases cannot.

| Crop | Unprotected pests and diseases only | Actual loss under current control | Grade assigned |
|:---|---:|---:|:--:|
| Wheat | 26.8% | 21.5% | **A** |
| Soybean | 23.1% | 21.4% | **A** |
| Maize | 28.2% | 22.5% | **A** |
| **Potato** | **44.6%** | **17.2%** | **B** |
| Rice | 39.9% | 30.0% | **B** |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Potato is the crop with the largest swing between protected and unprotected cultivation. Conventional potato receives an average of 15 treatments a year, and this independently reconfirms the finding that only the potato retention rate collapsed to 17.6% in the no-input trial. The actual loss rate after control in five years of Korean paddy rice surveys averaged 0.29% per city/county-year, so compared with the unprotected 39.9%, the current control system suppresses 99.3% of the losses. A disaster is a state in which this system has disappeared.

> **The "number of known pests and diseases" must not be used as an indicator.**
> It would measure not the vulnerability of the crop but how much agricultural research has been done.

### 7-3. Storability (Unpowered Room-Temperature Storage Period + Measured Loss Rate) `[E2]`
**Axis 1 - Storable period** (unpowered room temperature of 15-25℃, ventilated indoor space, no processing, in days)

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 1,095 or more | 365 or more | 180 or more | 90 or more | 30 or more | 7 or more | Below 7 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Assuming a cool, dry environment would presuppose refrigeration infrastructure and conflict with the purpose, so unpowered room temperature is the real condition. Burial in the ground and cellar storage are accepted, and cereals must be dried to a moisture content of 13-14% for safe storage.

**Axis 2 - Measured loss rate at the storage stage**

| Loss-rate range | Representative items (median %) |
|:---|:---|
| Below 0.5 | Soybean 0.25 · Cashew 0.17 · Sugarcane 0.14 |
| 0.5-1.5 | Rice 1.20 · Foxtail millet and proso millet 1.18 · Barley 1.30 · Mushrooms 1.62 |
| 1.5-3 | Wheat 2.65 · Sorghum 2.60 · Tomato 2.85 · Chinese cabbage 2.25 |
| 3-5 | **Maize 3.00** · Cassava (fresh) 3.50 · **Potato 4.00** |
| 5-10 | Welsh onion and onion types 6.50 · Walnut 8.00 · Carrot and turnip 8.94 |
| 10 or more | Cassava (dried) 11.46 · **Yam 14.00** · Mandarin 18.60 · Green garlic 27.43 |

> **The loss rate is used only for deductions and never to raise a grade.**
> The loss rate is not normalized by storage period, so lettuce kept for 3 days and wheat kept for 6 months are in the same table. Lettuce has a low loss rate not because it withstands storage well but because it is not kept long.

```
Loss rate of 5% or more: lower the period grade by 1
Loss rate of 10% or more: lower it by 2 (only when the species itself has been observed)
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Bonus**: if a crop reaches SS using only natural drying, salting, and fermentation, which require no fuel or equipment, it is raised by one grade.

### 7-4. Continuous-Cropping Tolerance (Fallow Period, in Years) `[E2]`

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| Harmless | 1 year | 2 years | 3 years | 5 years | 7 years | 10 years or more |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Solanaceous crops such as eggplant, cucurbits (except squash), and legumes need continuous cropping avoided most, and eggplant and watermelon in particular suffer greatly from it. **Medicinal crops are far more serious.** About 70% of plants whose roots are used medicinally suffer severe continuous-cropping injury. Ginseng grown for even a single season lowers yield and quality on the same land for 10 years or more, and pinellia loses more than 80% of its yield within 2-4 years and needs a 5-12 year fallow. The cause is not only pathogens but also the autotoxicity of phenolic acids the plants secrete themselves, so it is solved neither by pesticides nor by rotation alone.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Rotation raises the yield of the following crop by 20%, and by 23% when a legume precedes it. So **a fallow period of 0 years only means no fallow is needed, not exemption from yield loss.** A heavily fertilized crop that is harmless under continuous cropping is lowered by one grade, and maize, whose collapse under continuous cropping without fertilizer has been measured, is set at A.

> **Rice is the exception.** Flooding suppresses soil-borne diseases, and it is the only case in which continuous cropping has been proven in Asia over thousands of years.

### 7-5. Land-Preparation and Digging Energy (Calorie Recovery Multiple) `[E2]`
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The calories harvested must exceed the calories spent on planting. Without tools or fuel, this becomes a real constraint.

| Soil type | Density (kg/㎥) | Work rate (㎥/hr) | **kcal/㎥** |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sand | 1,500 | 0.363 | **2,037** |
| Soil (ordinary earth and sand) | 1,600 | 0.272 | **2,716** |
| Clay (hard earth) | 1,800 | 0.097 | **7,640** |
| Gravel | 1,900 | 0.076 | **9,677** |
| Stone (boulders) | 2,000 | 0.044 | **16,977** |

*Vigorous shoveling and pickaxe work by an 80 kg adult uses 739 kcal per hour.*

**Calories used per planting hole** (diameter 10 cm)

| Soil type | Depth 10 cm | 15 cm | 20 cm | 30 cm |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sand | 1.6 kcal | 2.4 | 3.2 | 4.8 |
| Soil | 2.1 | 3.2 | 4.3 | 6.4 |
| Clay | 6.0 | 9.0 | 12.0 | 18.0 |
| Gravel | 7.6 | 11.4 | 15.2 | 22.8 |
| Stone | 13.3 | 20.0 | 26.7 | **40.0** |

```
Annual digging energy = digging volume (㎥/㎡) × kcal/㎥ × tillage coefficient 0.4 ÷ amortization years
Recovery multiple R = calories per area (kcal/㎡/year) ÷ annual digging energy
```

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 100× or more | 50 or more | 20 or more | 10 or more | 5 or more | 2 or more | Below 2 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Correction for sustained work**: the hourly values above are short-term maximum intensity. The net energy of farm work sustainable for 8 hours is 220 kcal/hr for moderate-intensity gardening and 280 kcal/hr for heavy digging, about 44%, so this factor is multiplied in.

### 7-6. Yield Retention Without Synthetic Inputs `[E1]`
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is the share of yield maintained when synthetic fertilizer and synthetic pesticides are **both** cut off. Its basis is the only long-term measurement that is practically identical to disaster conditions.

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 90% or more | 58% or more | 50% or more | 44% or more | 36% or more | 25% or more | Below 25% |

| Crop | No input | Conventional | **Retention without input** |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| Soybean | 1.80 | 2.84 | **63.4%** |
| Grass-clover | 7.40 | 14.48 | **51.1%** |
| Silage maize | 9.74 | 19.31 | **50.4%** |
| Wheat | 2.51 | 5.05 | **49.7%** |
| **Potato** | **1.87** | **10.61** | **17.6%** |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Potato alone collapses because, when fungicides cannot be used, plants hit by late blight die early and the growing period shortens.

**Time decay**: the longer the no-input state lasts, the more the retention rate falls, but it does not go to zero.

```
Long-term effective retention = stated retention × δ
δ = 1.00 (0-10 years) / 0.75 (10-50 years) / 0.45 (50 years or more)
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The unfertilized plot of continuous wheat in the United Kingdom, now 170 years old, holds an equilibrium of about 1 t/ha (about 11% of conventional). The more important conclusion is that retention does not converge to zero but reaches a lower bound.

### 7-7. Yield Stability (Year-to-Year Yield Variation %) `[E3]`
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This item looks not at the normal-year yield but at **the floor in a bad year.**

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 4% or less | 6% | 9% | 13% | 18% | 25% | Over 25% |

| Crop | Variation | Grade |
|:---|---:|:--:|
| Oriental melon | 3.2% | SS |
| Tomato | 4.9% | S |
| Cucumber · squash · watermelon | 6.1-6.4% | A |
| Onion | 13.0% | B |
| Rice | 14.7% | C |
| Wheat | 19.2% | D |
| **Maize** | **24.8%** | **D** |

> **Maize is the least stable of the major grains.**
> If everything is staked on maize because its normal-year yield is high, a quarter of the food disappears in a bad year.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Greenhouse and open-field values differ. Cucumber is 9.2 in a greenhouse and 6.1 in the open field, and tomato is 7.2 in a greenhouse and 4.9 in the open field. This scale assumes no electricity and no facilities, so **only open-field values are used.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is the number of kinds of by-products that can serve as nutrient sources for the substrate. The seven items on the checklist are as follows.

- Fibrous material (plant matter, fruit peels)
- Slaughter by-products (protein, fat)
- Charcoal and ash
- Bones and shells
- Processed pulp (corrugated cardboard, tissue, paper, wallpaper)

| SS | S | A | B | C | D | E |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 6 or more | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It does not physically apply to plants, so it is removed from the denominator and not added into the overall score.
