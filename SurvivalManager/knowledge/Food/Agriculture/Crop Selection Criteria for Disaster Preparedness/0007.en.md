## 10. The Limits of This Scale
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Before using this scale, you need to know **what you can trust and what you should not.** Looking only at the grades without knowing the limits below means treating numbers with thin evidence as if they were measured facts.

---
### 10-1. Three Things to Know First

> **First, there is no field-tested verification.**  
> None of the 222 species described in "Crop Evaluation Results for Disaster Preparedness" has actually been planted and checked against this scale. Whether what the scale rewards matches what actually survives a disaster has not been confirmed. **This entire document is a single hypothesis.**

> **Second, yield is still an estimate.**  
> The composition values for all 220 species have been replaced with measured figures from authoritative databases, but the yield multiplied against them is a conventional-farming average-year estimate. The uncertainty in the calorie criterion, which carries the largest weight, lies **entirely on the yield side.**

> **Third, the top-ranked crops tend to have the thinnest evidence.**  
> Official yield statistics do not exist at all for apios, kudzu, or Jerusalem artichoke. Their composition values are measured, but their yields are estimates, and their rankings already reflect the discount from the corroboration coefficient.

---
### 10-2. Limits in the Scale's Structure

| No. | Limit |
|:--:|:---|
| 1 | **The corroboration coefficient's 0.35 is arbitrary.** It was meant to overcorrect, but whether that degree is right has not been verified |
| 2 | **The 2.5kg cap on micronutrients is also arbitrary.** Removing the cap favors high-moisture crops; lowering it favors dry crops |
| 3 | **The P:E threshold of 10% is based on WHO adult requirements.** Growing children, pregnant women, and people doing heavy labor need 12-15% more |
| 4 | **The 8.0 g/100g sweetness threshold for taste is converted from a Brix of 10,** and the ratio used for the conversion is a general figure for fruit |
| 5 | The scenario allocation depends on the time-horizon assumption that **"the disaster is resolved within one year"** |
| 6 | **Six criteria remain at E3 (self-estimated)** - infrastructure needs, cooking & processing, non-food value, taste, mushroom by-product suitability, yield stability |
| 7 | **The 25 criteria have only about 6 effective dimensions.** There is not as much independent information as the criterion count suggests |
| 8 | **South Korea's solar irradiance is used as a single nationwide value.** The difference between the south and the mountains of Gangwon Province is not reflected |

---
### 10-3. Particularly Weak Points by Criterion

- **Yield stability**: Only 10 species have measured anchors, and 63 species use median imputation. The 15.0% figure for the 53 tree fruit and berry species is only a placement value expressing the direction "less stable than average," not a measurement.
- **Sunlight**: All 222 species are assigned to 9 crop-group values, so **differences between species within the same group are not represented.**
- **Storability**: FAO loss rates are not normalized by storage duration. Limiting the criterion to deductions only prevents the direction of error, but the bias that "crops stored longer show higher observed loss rates" remains.
- **Pest & disease pressure**: Only 16 species have observed anchors, and only 8 families have direct observations. The remaining roughly 60 families borrow from the order level or higher. **The 23 fungal species have no anchor at all.**
- **Zero-input yield retention**: The conversion factor's confidence interval is ±20 percentage points. Variation by crop group ranges from -3% for tree fruit to -33% for vegetables. **Cucurbits and mushrooms have zero organic-farming papers,** so there is no anchor.
- **Taste**: Of the three bits, only sweetness is measured; smell/aroma and texture are self-assessed.

---
### 10-4. Axes Researched but Not Yet Included
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;These are axes with evidence behind them that were held back because the data could not be filled in at the scale of 222 species. **The very fact that these axes are missing is itself something to keep in mind when reading the scale.**

| Axis | Why it matters | Why it was held back |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Soil adaptation range (pH, salinity)** | The current scale **has no soil axis at all, making this the biggest gap.** In tsunami-hit or salt-damaged areas, this axis is everything | The FAO salinity tolerance tables and ECOCROP could not be secured through machine-readable extraction |
| **Pollinator dependence** | **87 of the world's major crops depend on animal pollination.** If pollinating insects collapse, cucurbits and fruit trees are hit hardest | The loss-rate ranges by category could not be confirmed in the original sources |
| **Quantifying acute toxicity** | Bitter cassava varieties at 15-400 mg HCN/kg, α-solanine in potato sprouts at 3,500 mg/kg, and so on. **"Fixed by processing" and "fatal without knowledge" are different axes** | The composition database has no antinutrient column, so a unified database across 222 species could not be built |
| **Labor input** | Labor hours per 10a differ by up to 11.9× (rice at 9.5 hours vs. garlic at 112.9 hours) | Coverage is 23%. The formula is ready |
| **Weed pressure buildup** | In zero-input long-term trials, the weed seed bank accumulates to 11.2× that of conventional farming | There is no data on differences by crop |

## 11. Procedure for Evaluating a Crop Yourself
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is the sequence for evaluating a crop that is not on the list, or for recalculating with your own region's conditions.

---
### 11-1. The Minimum Procedure - With Just 5 Criteria
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If filling in all 25 is difficult, **these next 5 alone are enough to avoid a fatal misjudgment.**

| Order | Criterion | What to check | If it fails |
|:--:|:---|:---|:---|
| 1 | **Calories per area** | How many times yield × calories is over the self-sufficiency line of 2,616 kcal/m² | Under 0.15× means it **cannot work as a food crop** |
| 2 | **Seed saving** | Can you obtain next year's seed yourself | An F1 hybrid or triploid makes it **a one-year-only crop** |
| 3 | **Storability** | How many days it lasts at room temperature without refrigeration | Under 7 days means **stockpiling is not possible** |
| 4 | **Pest & disease pressure** | What percentage is lost if left untreated | Over 80% means **cultivation is not viable** |
| 5 | **Zero-input yield retention** | What percentage remains without fertilizer | Under 25% means **collapse from year 2** |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;These five are exactly **the 4 exclusion gates plus the criterion with the highest long-term weight.** The remaining 20 criteria are used to **rank the crops** that have passed through this gate.

---
### 11-2. The Full Procedure

```
[1] Secure composition values - look them up at the species level in the Korean Food Composition Table or USDA
     → If unavailable, do not substitute a proxy species; mark it "unconfirmed"

[2] Secure yield - a conventional-farming average-year value. The moisture basis of the yield and the composition value must match

[3] Gate check
     → Light budget: if D_e ÷ HI_max exceeds 2,705 g DM/m²/yr, reject the input
     → Lower bound: if yield is under 50 g/m², treat it as a unit error and reject

[4] Derive the 25 criteria from the threshold tables (assign descriptive criteria by hand only)

[5] Mark each cell with its evidence strength (E1/E2/E3) and evidence tier

[6] Weighted sum by scenario → multiply by K → multiply by the corroboration coefficient

[7] Exclusion-gate check → if triggered, score 0, but still record the 25-criterion evaluation as is

[8] Convert to the final grade by percentile
```

---
### 11-3. Principles for Reading the Grades

> **One. Do not look only at the overall grade.**  
> Many crops have very different short-term, long-term, and supplementary grades. Potato is short-term S and long-term B, while sorghum is short-term B and long-term S. **The right answer depends on what you need right now.**

> **Two. Do not count correlated criteria twice.**  
> Fertilizer demand and water use correlate at +0.613, and temperature range and cold hardiness at +0.535. A crop that scores well on both of a correlated pair has its score inflated by that much.

> **Three. Check the corroboration coefficient alongside the score.**  
> The same score means different things for a crop with a corroboration coefficient of 1.000 and one with 0.804. The lower one is **a score already discounted for thin evidence.**

> **Four. Do not grow only one crop.**  
> The single most important conclusion of this scale is that "under zero-input conditions, no single crop can achieve self-sufficiency for one person." **Read it as a combination of a calorie crop, a protein crop, and a supplementary crop.**
