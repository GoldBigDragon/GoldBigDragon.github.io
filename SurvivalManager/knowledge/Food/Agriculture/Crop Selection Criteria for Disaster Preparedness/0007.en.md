## 10. Limitations of This Scale
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Before using this scale, you need to know what can be trusted and what cannot. If you look only at the grades without knowing the limitations below, you will treat numbers with shallow evidence as if they were measurements.

---
### 10-1. The Three Things to Know First

> **First, there is no field-measured cultivation verification.**
> Not one of the 222 crops in the Crop Evaluation Results for Disaster Preparedness has actually been planted and compared according to this scale. It has not been confirmed that what the scale rewards is the same as what actually survives a disaster, and this whole document is a single hypothesis.

> **Second, yield is still an estimate.**
> The composition values for all 220 species have been replaced with measurements from official databases, but the yields multiplied with them are normal-year estimates under conventional cultivation. The uncertainty of the calorie item, which has the largest weight, lies entirely on the yield side.

> **Third, the higher a crop ranks, the shallower its evidence.**
> There are no official yield statistics at all for apios, kudzu, and Jerusalem artichoke. Their composition values are measured but their yields are estimated, and their ranking is the one after discounting by the corroboration coefficient.

---
### 10-2. Limitations of the Scale's Structure

| No. | Limitation |
|:--:|:---|
| 1 | **The corroboration coefficient of 0.35 is arbitrary.** Overcorrection was intended, but whether the degree is appropriate has not been verified |
| 2 | **The 2.5 kg cap on micronutrients is also arbitrary.** Removing the cap favors crops with a lot of water, and lowering it favors dry crops |
| 3 | **The P:E threshold of 10% is based on the WHO adult requirement.** Growing children, pregnant women, and people doing heavy labor need 12-15% |
| 4 | **The sweetness threshold of 8.0 g/100g in the taste item is a conversion of Brix 10,** and the ratio used in the conversion is a general value for fruit |
| 5 | The allocation among scenarios depends on the time-horizon assumption that **the disaster is resolved within a year** |
| 6 | **Six E3 (own estimate) items remain** - facility burden, cooking and processing, non-food value, taste, mushroom by-products, and yield stability |
| 7 | **The effective dimensionality of the 25 items is about 6.** There is not as much independent information as the number of items suggests |
| 8 | **Korean insolation is used as a single nationwide value.** The difference between the south and the Gangwon mountain areas is not reflected |

---
### 10-3. Items That Are Especially Weak

- **Yield stability**: only 10 species have measured anchors, and 63 species are substituted with the median. For the 53 species of woody fruit trees and berries, 15.0% is a placement value expressing only the direction "less stable than average," not a measured value.
- **Sunlight**: the 222 species are assigned to the values of 9 crop groups, so **differences between species within a group are not expressed.**
- **Storability**: the FAO loss rate is not normalized by storage period. It was limited to deductions only to prevent errors in direction, but a bias remains in that crops stored longer are observed to have higher loss rates.
- **Pest and disease pressure**: there are only 16 observed anchors and only 8 families have direct observations. The remaining 60-odd families borrow from the order level or above, and the 23 fungal species have no anchor at all.
- **Retention without inputs**: the confidence interval of the conversion factor is ±20 percentage points, and the deviation among crop groups ranges from -3% for fruit trees to -33% for vegetables. For cucurbits and mushrooms there are 0 organic-farming papers, so there is no anchor.
- **Taste**: of the three bits, only sweetness is measured; smell/aroma and texture are own judgments.

---
### 10-4. Axes Investigated but Not Yet Included
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;These are axes for which evidence exists but which were set aside because the data could not be filled in at the scale of 222 species. The fact that these axes are missing is itself a caution when reading the scale.

| Axis | Why it matters | Reason for deferral |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Soil adaptation range (pH, salinity)** | The present scale has no soil axis at all, so this is the largest gap. In areas hit by tsunami or salt damage, this axis is everything | The FAO salt-tolerance table and ECOCROP could not be obtained in machine-readable form |
| **Dependence on pollinators** | Of the world's major crops, 87 depend on animal pollination. If pollinating insects collapse, cucurbits and fruit trees are hit fatally | Yield-loss ranges by category could not be confirmed from the original sources |
| **Quantifying acute toxicity** | Bitter cassava 15-400 mg HCN/kg, potato sprouts α-solanine 3,500 mg/kg, and so on. A problem solved by processing and a problem that kills through ignorance lie on different axes | The composition database has no antinutrient column, so a batch database for 222 species could not be built |
| **Labor input** | Labor hours per 10a differ by up to 11.9 times (rice 9.5 hours ↔ garlic 112.9 hours) | Coverage is 23%. The calculation formula is ready |
| **Weed pressure accumulation** | In no-input long-term trials, the weed seed bank accumulates to 11.2 times that of conventional plots | There are no data on differences by crop |

## 11. Evaluating on Your Own
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is the procedure to follow when evaluating a crop that is not on the list or recalculating with the conditions of your own region.

---
### 11-1. Minimum Procedure
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If filling in all 25 items is difficult, **the following five alone can still avoid fatal misjudgments.**

| Order | Item | What to check | If it fails |
|:--:|:---|:---|:---|
| 1 | **Calories per area** | How many times the self-sufficiency line of 2,616 kcal/㎡ does yield × calories reach? | Below 0.15× means **it does not qualify as a food crop** |
| 2 | **Seed saving** | Can you obtain next year's seed yourself? | An F1 hybrid or triploid is **a one-year crop** |
| 3 | **Storability** | How many days does it last at room temperature without refrigeration? | Under 7 days means **it cannot be stockpiled** |
| 4 | **Pest and disease pressure** | What percentage is lost if left alone without pesticides? | Over 80% means **it cannot be grown** |
| 5 | **Retention without inputs** | What percentage remains without fertilizer? | Below 25% means **collapse from the second year** |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;These five are the **four exclusion gates and the most heavily weighted long-term item.** The remaining 20 items are used to rank crops that have passed this hurdle.

---
### 11-2. Full Procedure

```
[1] Obtain composition values: find them at the species level in the Korean Standard Food Composition Table or USDA.
     If unavailable, do not substitute a proxy species; mark the crop "unconfirmed."

[2] Obtain yield: use the normal-year value under conventional cultivation, and always match the moisture basis of yield and composition values.

[3] Gate checks
     Light budget: if D_e ÷ HI_max exceeds 2,705 g DM/㎡/year, reject the input
     Lower limit: if yield is below 50 g/㎡, regard it as a unit error and reject

[4] Derive the 25 items from the threshold tables. Only descriptive items are assigned by hand.

[5] Mark each cell with its evidence strength (E1/E2/E3) and evidence tier.

[6] Weighted sum by scenario → multiply by K → multiply by the corroboration coefficient

[7] Check the exclusion gates. If caught, the score is 0, but the 25-item evaluation is still recorded.

[8] Convert to the final grade by percentile.
```

---
### 11-3. Principles for Reading Grades

> **1. Do not look only at the overall grade.**
> Many crops have very different short-term, long-term, and supplementary grades. Potato is short-term S and long-term B, and sorghum is short-term B and long-term S. The answer depends on what is needed now.

> **2. Do not count correlated items twice.**
> Fertilization and water consumption have a correlation of +0.613, and temperature range and cold tolerance +0.535. The score of a crop that is good on both items is inflated by that much.

> **3. Look at the corroboration coefficient as well.**
> The same score has different meanings for a crop with a corroboration coefficient of 1.000 and one with 0.804. The lower one is a score already attenuated for shallow evidence.

> **4. Do not grow only one crop.**
> The most important conclusion of this scale is that one-person self-sufficiency from a single crop does not hold under no-input conditions. It must be read as a combination of a calorie crop, a protein crop, and a supplementary crop.
