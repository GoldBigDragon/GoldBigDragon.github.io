### 1-2. Meaning of the Columns

| Column | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| **Short-term** | 0-1 year. If planted now, how much food is obtained within this year? |
| **Long-term** | 1 year or more. How many years can one last while renewing seed oneself and maintaining soil fertility? |
| **Supplementary** | Usefulness in a role that adds nutrition and palatability rather than serving as the staple. **Not added into the overall score** |
| **Overall** | √(short-term × long-term) × productivity coefficient K × corroboration coefficient |
| **Corroboration coefficient** | The closer to 1.000, the sounder the evidence. A low value means the score is one already attenuated |
| **Loss-rate tier** | The evidence tier of the unprotected pest-and-disease loss rate. L1 is direct measurement; the larger the number, the more the value is borrowed from a more distant taxonomic group |
| **Light group** | The crop-group assignment in the 40% shading experiment. Crops in the same group receive the same value |

---
### 1-3. Cautions

> **1. Looking only at the overall grade**  
> Potato is **S** in the short term and **B** in the long term. Sorghum is **B** in the short term and **S** in the long term. The two look similar overall, but **the crop to plant in the first year of a disaster and the crop to plant in the third year are different.**

> **2. Mistaking a supplementary grade for a staple grade**  
> It is normal for as many as 87 crops to have supplementary SS. The supplementary scenario gives micronutrients the maximum weight (3.50) and lowers the calorie weight to 0.50, so **vegetables and wild greens occupying the top ranks in large numbers is the definition of this scenario.** A supplementary SS does not mean "one can live on this alone" but "eaten alongside, it prevents nutritional deficiency."

> **3. Reading exclusion as a low score**  
> The no-gate score of dandelion, ranked first among the excluded, is **48.2 points**, which falls within the top 20 of valid crops. Exclusion happens not because the score is low but because of **a single fatal disqualification.** Once that item is resolved, the crop becomes useful at once.

### 2-1. What to Read from the Top Ranks

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Ranks 1-4 (apios, kudzu, Jerusalem artichoke, sweet potato) are all underground tubers.** The first conclusion of this table is that tubers, not grains, are the winners under no-input conditions. Tubers have low fertilizer needs, high perenniality, and are stored in the ground, so no storage facilities are needed.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Potato, by contrast, is pushed down to 35th (B).** Its individual items are strong, with calories A, protein A, and harvest speed A, but **a yield retention without synthetic inputs of E (17.6%)** alone brings down its long-term score. Without fungicides, late blight shortens the growing period.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Maize is 44th (B).** It is the crop that yields the most under conventional cultivation, but it loses 55% of its yield under 40% shading (the worst of all crop groups), and with a year-to-year variation of 24.8% it is the least stable of the major grains.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Mushrooms rank unexpectedly high.** King stropharia is 6th, shiitake 24th, and wood ear 30th. This is because, as they do not photosynthesize, they are **the only group unaffected in a disaster that reduces insolation.** However, the substrate must be brought in from outside, so land efficiency is capped at A.
