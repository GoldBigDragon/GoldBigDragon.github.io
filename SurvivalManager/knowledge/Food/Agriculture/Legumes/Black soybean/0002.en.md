## 1. Disaster-Preparedness Evaluation

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Black soybean (a black soybean with green cotyledons) has an overall grade of A, with a score of 48.5. Broken down by scenario: short-term capability is B (45.5), long-term self-reliance capability is S (51.8), and supplementary-food value is SS (63.4). This is 1.4 points below soybean's 49.9, and the difference comes almost entirely from two criteria — maturation time and pest & disease pressure. The remaining criteria carry the same values, since they are the same species.

### 1-3. How to Read the Strengths

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;SS went to four criteria — protein density, fertilizer demand, infrastructure needs, and storability — and S went to micronutrients, continuous-cropping tolerance, zero-input yield retention, taste, prevalence, and seed saving. This is the same pattern as soybean. Since it is the same species, that is only to be expected, and what it means is that black soybean, like soybean, is a crop whose cycle keeps going even when nothing comes in from outside.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Compared with soybean, there is exactly one criterion where black soybean comes out ahead: water use. Black soybean's total evapotranspiration is about 450mm, at the lower end of soybean's 450-700mm range. In grade terms, that is the difference between A and B. The reason lies in the growing season. Black soybean's late growth stages fall in September and October, a period when temperatures drop and evapotranspiration decreases. Unlike early soybean, which fills its pods under the height of summer heat, black soybean fills its grain in cool autumn air. So even though it is the same species, its water demand is a little lower, and as long as there is no autumn drought, it can mature without irrigation.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The supplementary-food grade of SS (63.4) also carries real practical weight. In terms of calories it cannot serve as a staple food, but as a side-dish resource it ranks at the very top. A single plate of kongjaban (soybeans braised in soy sauce), or one bowl of rice mixed with beans, covers a large share of a day's protein. Combined with the SS storability grade, this means one year's harvest can keep the side-dish supply going all the way to the next year.

> **Black soybean's strength is the same underlying framework as soybean, with the added trait of using the cool of autumn. In regions with severe midsummer heat, black soybean may set its crop more reliably than soybean.**

### 1-4. How to Read the Weaknesses

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There are two criteria where black soybean scores worse than soybean. Speed to harvest drops from C to D, and pest & disease pressure drops from A to C. On top of that, calories per area stays at the same grade, C, as soybean, but the actual figure falls from 1,002 to 922kcal, and non-food value also drops from C to D.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The Speed to harvest grade of D is the most practical constraint. 160-180 days is nearly the entire frost-free period in central South Korea. Only sowing between late May and mid-June allows harvest between late October and mid-November; sow later than mid-June and the first frost arrives before grain-filling is finished. This narrow window has two consequences. First, black soybean is hard to use as a follow-on crop in double-cropping: the timing is too tight to plant it where barley or garlic has just been harvested. Second, it leaves less room than soybean for the strategy of staggering sowing dates to spread risk.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The pest & disease pressure grade of C is the price of standing in the field so long. A yield loss of 47.4% without pest control means that with no control at all you lose nearly half the crop — more than double soybean's 23%. The reason is clear. September and October are when stink-bug populations peak, and at that point black soybean is still filling its pods. Once every other bean crop has been brought in and black soybean is the only one left standing, the stink bugs from the whole area converge on it. In growing black soybean, managing stink bugs is not a side task — it is the single task that decides the yield.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The non-food value grade of D is one step below soybean's C. The criterion where soybean is recognized as a bee-forage plant does not apply to black soybean. The practical difference is not large, but it is worth remembering that, apart from nitrogen fixation, this is a crop that earns little value outside the field.

## 2. What Kind of Plant Is This?

### 2-1. The Types of Black Soybean

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Black soybean is not a single cultivar but a general term for soybean strains with a black seed coat. In South Korea, three types are commonly distinguished.

| Name | Size | Flesh color | Characteristics and uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Black soybean (sokcheong) | Medium-large | Green | Black skin, green inside. Sweet enough to mix into rice or make into kongjaban. Late-maturing |
| Heuktae (heukdaedu, literally "black soybean") | Large | Yellow | The largest of the black soybeans. Used for fermented soybean pastes (jang), tofu, and black-soybean tea |
| Seomoktae (also called jwinunikong, "rat's-eye bean", or yakkong, "medicine bean") | Small | Green or yellow | The smallest-seeded of the three. Used medicinally and for bean sprouts. Its high skin-to-seed ratio gives it a high anthocyanin density |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;From a disaster-preparedness standpoint, how to allocate effort among these three types is worth thinking through. Seomoktae has small seeds, so the same weight of seed lets you plant far more of it; it sprouts well as bean sprouts, and its high skin ratio gives it a high antioxidant-pigment content. Heuktae has large, plump seeds, good for making jang. Black soybean tastes best simply boiled and eaten as is. If you can only choose one seed, seomoktae has the advantage in area efficiency and range of uses, while black soybean is the choice for taste and yield.

### 2-2. How It Differs from Soybean

| Item | Ordinary soybean | Black soybean |
|---|---|---|
| Growing period | 120-150 days | 160-180 days |
| Optimal sowing window (central South Korea) | early May-early June | late May-mid-June |
| Harvest time (central South Korea) | early-to-mid October | late October-mid-November |
| Optimal growing temperature | 20-25°C | 20-23°C. Prefers cool conditions |
| Preferred pH | 6.0-6.5 | 6.0-7.0. Prefers closer to neutral |
| Seed coat | Yellowish-white by default | Black. Anthocyanin |
| Flesh | Yellow | Green |
| Yield | 250kg/1,000m² | 230kg/1,000m² |
| Yield loss without pest control | 23% | 47.4% |
| Total evapotranspiration | 450-700mm | about 450mm |
| Hilling | Once | 2-3 times |
| Pinching | Once (at 5-7 true leaves) | First pinching, plus a second, harder pinch if needed |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Its appearance and the structure of its growth stages are the same as soybean's: trifoliate leaves, stems and pods covered in fine hairs, small white or light-purple flowers, self-pollination, and a straight taproot with root nodules along it — all identical. What differs is the time each stage takes. Black soybean has a longer vegetative stage, running up to 8 weeks; flowering spans 8-12 weeks; and grain-filling proceeds slowly over 14-20 weeks. This slow grain-filling is what makes the seeds plumper and lets sugar accumulate.

### 2-3. Growth Stages

| Stage | Timing | Key physiology | Key nutrients |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germination and seedling stage | 0-4 weeks from sowing | Germination and early growth; nodule formation begins | Phosphorus, a little nitrogen |
| Vegetative stage | Weeks 4-8 | Stems and leaves increase; nodules become active | Nitrogen supplied by the nodules |
| Flowering stage | Weeks 8-12 | Flowers open and pollination occurs | Phosphorus, boron |
| Pod-filling stage | Weeks 10-14 | Pods and seeds enlarge | Potassium, phosphorus |
| Grain-filling and ripening stage | Weeks 14-20 | Seeds fill slowly and pigment accumulates in the seed coat | Irrigation stops |

> **Black soybean's grain-filling stage overlaps with the frost. The leaves are killed by frost, but the seeds inside pods that have already ripened are not harmed. If anything, as the frost takes the leaves, the remaining nutrients are drawn into the seeds, deepening their flavor.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The relationship between frost and black soybean is often misunderstood. Saying that black soybean is a bean that resists frost is not accurate. Its cold-hardiness grade is E, the same as soybean's, and its leaves and stems die from a single frost. It is only because the seeds are already nearly full by that point that harvest is unaffected. If frost hits while grain-filling is still incomplete, the seeds in those pods shrivel or are left empty. So the real trick to growing black soybean is not enduring frost, but finishing grain-filling before the frost arrives. It would not be an exaggeration to say that keeping to the right sowing window is everything.

| Nutrient | Amount | Notes |
|---|:---:|---|

| Phosphorus | 629mg | Relatively high |
| Iron | about 7mg | Important when meat is unavailable |

| Anthocyanin | Abundant in the seed coat | An antioxidant pigment |
| Isoflavones & saponins | Present | Antioxidant compounds |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Anthocyanin is concentrated in the seed coat, so it is lost if the skin is removed. That is why black soybean should be eaten with the skin on, and why seomoktae, with its small seeds and correspondingly higher skin ratio, has the advantage in antioxidant density. Soaking the beans in water turns the water dark red — that color is the anthocyanin — so it is better not to discard that water but to use it for cooking rice or as the boiling water. The first soaking water, however, should be discarded, since oligosaccharides and antinutrient compounds dissolve into it along with the pigment. Discarding the first water and keeping the water from the second soak onward is the practical compromise.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The sugar content of 3.28g is higher than ordinary soybean's, and it is why black soybean tastes sweet when boiled. In a situation where sweeteners have run out, this sweetness has value in its own right. It is also why kongjaban can be made without sugar. This is where the taste grade of S comes from. Whether a food is palatable is not a secondary concern in a disaster situation: food people do not want to eat ends up left uneaten, and uneaten food is waste.
