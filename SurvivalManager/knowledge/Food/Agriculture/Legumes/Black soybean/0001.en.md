## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Seoritae is the same species as soybean (Glycine max): a late-maturing black soybean type with a black seed coat and green kernel. It is harvested after the first frost, hence the name (seori means frost), and stands in the field from late October to mid November, filling its seeds last. Its overall grade is A (48.5 points, 29th of 183 valid species), with short term B (45.5), long term S (51.8), and supplementary food SS (63.4 points). Protein density is SS like soybean (37.9% of calories) and storability is also SS, and the anthocyanin in the seed coat becomes a source of antioxidants in a diet without fresh vegetables. From sowing to harvest takes 160-180 days, over a month longer than soybean's 120-150, so harvest speed is D, but the seeds grow large and sweet and the flavor deepens. Pest and disease pressure is C, worse than soybean's A, so susceptibility without control is 47.4% (soybean 23%), and yield is 230 kg per 1,000㎡, less than soybean's 250 kg. Seoritae demands time and more handwork, so if a shortage of people forces a choice between the two, soybean is the safe one.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The one item better than soybean is water consumption A, because the latter part of growth falls in September-October when evapotranspiration drops. The 160-180 days of harvest speed D is almost the whole frost-free period in the central region, so it is hard to use as a following crop after barley or garlic. Pest and disease pressure C is the price of standing in the field in September-October as well, when stink bugs peak. The grade for each item is in the Crop Evaluation Results document.

## 2. Plant Overview
| Name | Size | Kernel color | Characteristics and use |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Seoritae (green kernel) | Medium-large | Green | Late type. Sweet, used in rice and kongjaban |
| Heuktae (large black soybean) | Large | Yellow | The largest black soybean. Used for fermented pastes, tofu, and black bean tea |
| Seomoktae (rat-eye bean, medicinal bean) | Small | Green or yellow | The smallest, with high anthocyanin density. Used medicinally and for sprouts |

| Item | Ordinary soybean | Seoritae |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Days to harvest | 120-150 days | 160-180 days |
| Sowing time (central) | Early May-early June | Late May-mid June |
| Harvest (central) | Early to mid October | Late October-mid November |
| Optimum temperature, pH | 20-25℃, 6.0-6.5 | 20-23℃ (prefers cooler), 6.0-7.0 |
| Yield, susceptibility without control | 250 kg/1,000㎡, 23% | 230 kg/1,000㎡, 47.4% |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The structure of its form and growth stages is the same as soybean, with flowering 8-12 weeks after sowing, pod filling 10-14 weeks, and ripening and maturity 14-20 weeks. Ripening overlaps with frost, so even if the leaves are killed by frost, seeds in already-ripe pods are not damaged, and the remaining nutrients rush into the seeds and deepen the flavor.

> **It is not accurate to say that seoritae is a frost-hardy bean. Its cold tolerance is E, like soybean, and the knack of growing seoritae is not to endure frost but to finish ripening before frost comes.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Per 100 g of dried beans: 401-413 kcal, protein 33.6-43.1 g (standard value 38.02 g), fat 16.35 g, carbohydrate 36.40 g, calcium 224 mg, phosphorus 629 mg, iron about 7 mg, and sugars 3.28 g. Anthocyanin is concentrated in the seed coat, and sugars are higher than in ordinary soybean, which gives a sweetness when boiled.

## 3. Growing Environment and Varieties
| Condition | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Temperature | Germination 10℃ or above (optimum 20-22℃), growth 15-35℃ (optimum 20-23℃), ripening 15℃ or above (optimum 18-25℃). Accumulated temperature of 2,800-3,200℃ is needed, 300℃ or more beyond soybean. Frost kills the leaves and stems |
| Water, soil | Moisture-retentive, well-drained sandy loam or loam, pH 6.0-7.0, effective soil depth 50 cm or more. Seasonal evapotranspiration about 450 mm. It dies if submerged two days or more, and beds should be rounded and 20 cm or higher |

> **For seoritae, the price of late sowing is not a late harvest but empty pods. Planted late, it is not harvested late; there is nothing to harvest.**

| Variety | Characteristics |
|:---|:---|
| Local seoritae | Tasty and adapted to the locality, and seed saving can be continued. Yield 160-260 kg/1,000㎡ |
| Cheongja 5 | Resists disease and lodging, pods are firm and hard to burst, and stink bug damage is low. Yield about 343 kg |
| Heuktae, seomoktae | Heuktae has large seeds suited to pastes and tofu. Seomoktae has the smallest seeds, so when seed is short it can be planted over a wider area with the same weight, and used for sprouts |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Both varieties are self-pollinating fixed lines, so it is ideal to split the planting, securing yield with Cheongja 5 and local adaptation with the local type. Highlands and northern mountain areas where first frost comes in early October lack accumulated temperature, so choose an early soybean there, and set the sowing date by counting back 170 days from the average first-frost date of your own region.

## 4. Field Preparation and Sowing
| Item | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Fertilization (1,000㎡) | Nitrogen 3 kg (a minimum for the early period before nodules work), phosphorus 3 kg, potassium 3.4 kg (all as basal fertilizer), fully rotted compost 1,000 kg, and in acid soil lime 200 kg (3-4 weeks before sowing). Extra nitrogen makes only leaves lush and the plants lodge, harming it more than soybean |
| Substitute resources | Bone meal and roasted fish bone (phosphorus, calcium), leaf mold (moisture retention), rice hulls and rice straw (if poorly rotted they steal nitrogen) |
| Nodule inoculation | Coat seed lightly moistened with water with topsoil 5-15 cm deep from a field that grew soybeans, and sow in shade within 30 minutes (drying in sun kills the bacteria). Nodules from wild soybean roots also work. It fails with pH 5.5 or below, excess nitrogen fertilizer, standing water, too little phosphorus, or fungicide-treated seed |
| Nodule check | Around flowering (8-10 weeks after sowing), lift with soil using a spade, wash, and split the nodules. With 20 or more per plant and red or pink inside, it is fixing nitrogen; if white or pale green, check acidity, waterlogging, and phosphorus deficiency |

> **Legumes improve the soil not because they fix nitrogen, but because they leave part of the fixed nitrogen in the stems and leaves in the field. If those stems and leaves are carried away, the bean becomes a crop that plunders the soil.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fixation is 100-150 kg per hectare (30-60 kg in poor conditions) while 130-145 kg leaves with the seed, so the balance works only if stalks, leaves, and pod husks are cut into 20-30 cm lengths and returned to the field. Inoculation is especially important for seoritae, whose growing period exceeds five months, because if it is poor, nitrogen runs short at pod filling and the seeds fill poorly.

| Sowing | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Timing | Central: late May-mid June (limit late June; harvest late October-mid November). South: early to late June (limit early July; harvest early to late November). Following potatoes or garlic is tight, and past late June mung bean or cowpea, which need 80-90 days, are better |
| Seed preparation | Pick out by hand and discard seeds with spots, discoloration, wrinkles, or insect holes; purple stain is hard to see on a black seed coat, so check under bright light. Disinfect in 50-55℃ water for 10-15 minutes (above 60℃ germination is damaged) and inoculate only after it has cooled completely |
| Method | Depth 3-5 cm, row spacing 70 cm, plant spacing 20 cm (wider than soybean because it grows large over five months), rounded beds 20 cm or higher. Birds eat cotyledons right after sowing, so cover with netting or straw |

## 5. Water Management and Cultivation Management
| Period | Water management |
|:---|:---|
| Sowing to germination | Keep the soil from drying out completely |
| Early growth (June-July) | Leave it to natural rainfall and concentrate on drainage in the rainy season (excess water is more dangerous than dryness) |
| Flowering to pod filling (Aug-Sep) | Water plentifully so the soil does not dry (80% of its water use falls here, and drought at flowering makes flowers drop in large numbers) |
| Ripening, maturity (October) | Reduce water and stop it at maturity (if too wet, pods do not dry) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Seoritae fills its pods until late September, so autumn drought must also be watched, and in years without rain in September, irrigation directly decides yield.

| Management | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Hilling | Three times. Pile soil up to burying the cotyledons at 2-3 true leaves, to the unifoliate leaves at 4-5, and to the first trifoliate node at 6-7, finishing 10 days before flowering. Entering the field after flowering and turning soil makes flowers drop and cuts fine roots, lowering yield |
| Topping | The first is at 5-7 trifoliate leaves, before flowering: pinch off the growing tip by hand to increase side branches. The second, hard topping, is only when plants are lush from plenty of fertilizer and likely to lodge: cut leaving 20-25 cm from the base (not on weak plants in poor fields). Forbidden in the flowering period from August |
| Thinning, weeding, mulching | Leave 2 plants per hill at 1-2 true leaves. From 30-40 days after sowing scrape shallowly together with hilling. After hilling is done in July, cover 5-10 cm thick with straw, rice hulls, fallen leaves, or weeds to cut evaporation by half or more |
| Line support | Standing plants up after they lodge cuts roots, so before an August typhoon drive stakes around the field and tie a line at waist height |

## 6. Pests, Diseases, and Nutrient Problems
| Pest or disease | Symptoms and conditions | Control without chemicals |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Anthracnose, bacterial leaf spot | Black cankers on stems and pods in the pod stage after the rains / angular brown spots with yellow borders on leaves | Seed selection and disinfection, rotation, airflow, a resistant variety (Cheongja 5), Bordeaux mixture |
| Brown spot, downy mildew, purple seed stain | Brown spots on lower leaves and early leaf drop / yellow spots on the upper leaf surface and mold underneath / purple stain on the seed coat at ripening | Secure airflow, keep plant spacing, remove residue, Bordeaux mixture, sort seed under bright light |
| Black root rot, sclerotinia | Roots turn brown and rot and lower leaves yellow / in cool, damp conditions stems soften with white mycelium and black bodies | Improve drainage, raise beds, rotation (4 years or more for sclerotinia, dig out diseased plants with the soil and burn them) |
| Soybean mosaic virus, aphids | Leaves mottled in a mosaic and crinkled. Spread by aphids | Cannot be treated. Pull and burn at once and reduce aphids with soapy water (5 g of soap per 1 L of water) |
| Stink bugs | In late August-early September they pierce pods and make empty pods. If neglected, yield drops by up to 58% | See below |
| Pod borers, tobacco cutworms | Larvae eat the seeds inside pods / devour leaves and pods | Remove residue and damaged pods / catch by hand and remove egg masses with the leaf |

> **In seoritae, stink bug control is not a side task but the key work that decides yield. The three weeks from late August to mid September are the decisive stretch.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Search under the pods in the cool early morning, knock the stink bugs into a bucket by hand (every other day for three weeks noticeably reduces their numbers), and plant sesame, foxtail millet, and sorghum around the field to lure and catch them. For small plots put insect netting on after flowering (it self-pollinates, so seed set is unaffected), and cut legume weeds on field banks to remove intermediate hosts.

| Symptom | Cause and remedy |
|:---|:---|
| Whole leaf pale yellow and plant small | Nitrogen deficiency (poor nodules are the most common cause). Dig up roots and check the nodules; this season give urine diluted 10 times in small amounts |
| In September lower leaves yellow and fall quickly and seeds fill poorly | Nodules age early and nitrogen runs short late in ripening, a symptom peculiar to seoritae. Strengthen phosphorus and inoculation next year |
| Only leaves lush and dark green and plants lodge | Nitrogen excess or dense planting. Stop nitrogen, use hard topping and line support |
| The black of the seed coat is pale and blotchy | Insufficient ripening or early frost. A sign of too little time, not a nutrient deficiency, so sow earlier next year |

## 7. Intercropping and Crop Rotation
| Compatibility | Companions |
|:---|:---|
| Good | Maize, sorghum (share the nitrogen the bean fixed and divide light by height difference), marigold (suppresses nematodes), potato (on the neighboring bed), cabbage family (uses the leftover nitrogen the year after seoritae) |
| Poor | Garlic and onion types (reported to suppress nodule bacteria with their exudates), continuous cropping with other legumes (pathogens and nematodes accumulate) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Seoritae does not empty the field until November, so autumn-sown crops such as garlic, onion, and barley cannot follow it (planted in mid November they freeze before rooting); plan the field to rest until the following spring. Mixed with maize, it shrinks in maize's shade and ripens late, so it is better kept apart. The rest period is 1 year or more and the ideal cycle 3-4 years, as nematodes, black root rot, and anthracnose accumulate.

## 8. Harvest and Seed Saving
| Harvest | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Timing | When nearly all leaves have fallen (frost drops them all at once) and 80% or more of pods have turned brown. The seeds rattle when shaken, the seed coat is deep black, and seed moisture is about 15%. 7-14 days after the leaves are all down (central late October-mid November, south early to late November) |
| Method | Cut and lay in the field for a day or two for preliminary drying (bring in at once if it rains). Tie into bundles about the thickness a hand can grip, hang them upright under eaves or in an airy shed to dry (in November wind matters more than sun), and thresh when pods are fully dry and rattle when shaken |
| Threshing, winnowing | Half fill a sack with dry stalks and stamp on it or flail it, then on a windy day pour from a winnowing basket or bowl to blow off chaff and sift. When skimming empty beans off in water, anthocyanin leaches out, so soak briefly and dry at once |

> **November rain and snow are seoritae's last enemy. If dry pods get wet and dry again and again they burst and the seeds fall to the ground. When bad weather looks likely, do not wait for full ripeness; cut and bring it in.**

| Seed saving | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Selection, harvest | Mark in advance plants with no disease symptoms, many pods, no lodging, and early ripening. Thresh separately after pods turn fully brown and the seed coat is deep black |
| Sorting, drying | Discard seeds with pale or blotchy seed coats, wrinkles, or small size (a deep, glossy black and a vivid green when split mark good seed). Moisture 12-13%. Do not float seed in water (germination drops) |
| Storage | Keep in a sealed container separate from food, in a cool, dark place. 3 years sealed at room temperature, and germination is 80-90% after 1 year, 60-80% after 2, and 40-60% after 3. Every 3-4 years get seed from another region and mix some in |

## 9. Storage
| Step | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Drying | Dry on the stalk in the wind under eaves, then after threshing spread thin on a mat in the sun for 3-4 days (winter sun is weak, so one or two more days than soybean). Moisture 12-13% for food and 12% for seed, checked with the salt test |
| Bean weevil control | Full sealing matters most. Mix fine wood ash at 2-5% of the bean volume (rinse before eating), diatomaceous earth at 1-3 g per 1 kg of beans, or rub in 2-5 mL of cooking oil (not for seed). Cold treatment, putting it outside for a few days in midwinter, is favorable because the November harvest is followed soon by winter |
| Containers, place | Thick plastic lasts 1-2 years (rats gnaw through), underground storage pits 2-3 years (lose everything if water seeps in). Keep at 15 degrees or below and air humidity 65% or below. Anthocyanin breaks down in light, so shut it out completely (wrap glass jars in cloth), and check every two months by sound and smell |

> **Beans are lost not by rotting but by being eaten by insects. The art of storage is the art of keeping out insects.**

## 10. Cooking and Precautions
| Component | What it does | How to remove |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Trypsin inhibitors, lectins | Block protein digestion and cause abdominal pain, vomiting, and diarrhea, and lectins agglutinate red blood cells | Soak 8 hours, then heat at 100 degrees for 60 minutes or more |
| Saponin, oligosaccharides | Foam irritates the stomach / gas in the belly | Skim off the foam and discard the first soaking water. Fermentation greatly reduces them |

> **Undercooked beans can be more dangerous than raw ones. A light blanching breaks down cell walls so absorption is good, but the inhibitors and lectins remain. Do not stop halfway; cook all the way through.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Soak 8 hours in room-temperature water; the first two or three hours of water hold oligosaccharides and anti-nutrients and must be discarded, and the later water is used as rice water or cooking water to save the anthocyanin. Steaming (100-degree steam for 60-90 minutes) loses less pigment, and retained-heat cooking, boiling 15 minutes then 2-3 hours in an insulated box, cuts fuel by half or more. Sprouting and fermenting (doenjang, soy sauce, cheonggukjang) reduce anti-nutrients most fundamentally.

| Preparation | Procedure and value |
|:---|:---|
| Kongjaban | Boil well-soaked beans in water alone until soft, then add soy sauce and simmer (adding it at the start toughens the beans). It is sweet, so no sugar is needed, and the pigment of the cooking water is used too. 2-3 weeks in a cool place |
| Bean rice, black bean tea | Cook soaked beans with rice / roast dry beans over low heat for 20-30 minutes and boil briefly in water (roasted beans keep 2-3 months sealed, and it also disinfects drinking water) |
| Pastes, cheonggukjang | Heuktae suits better; meju is boiled, mashed, and fermented, then aged. Cheonggukjang is boiled beans wrapped in straw at about 40℃ for 2-3 days |
| Bean sprouts | Soak seomoktae or small beans 6-8 hours, put in a draining steamer, and water 3-4 times a day in a dark place at 20-25℃. Harvest in 4-7 days at 5-10 cm. In winter this gives the vitamin C that dry beans lack |

## 11. Annual Work Schedule
| Month | Work |
|:---|:---|
| Jan-Feb | Check stored beans, test seed germination, cold treatment, and grow bean sprouts. In February, plan the field, turn the compost, and make fermented pastes |
| Mar-Apr | In March, spread lime and finish the compost. In April, plow, make beds, dig drainage ditches (deep, with the rainy season in mind), and apply basal fertilizer |
| May-June | In May, select seed, disinfect it in hot water, secure inoculant soil, and begin sowing in late May. Finish sowing by mid June, then guard against birds, thin, and do the 1st hilling |
| July-Aug | In July, 2nd and 3rd hilling, weeding, topping (before flowering), and mulching. In August, flowering, nodule check, irrigation, watching for stink bugs, and line support before typhoons |
| Sep-Oct | In September, pod filling, intensive irrigation, and hand-picking stink bugs (the three weeks to late month are decisive). In October, ripening, stop irrigation, mark seed plants, and begin harvesting in late October |
| Nov-Dec | In November, harvest, hang to dry, thresh, winnow, and cut the stalks and lay them on the field (do not burn). In December, finish drying, store, make pastes, and grow sprouts |

> **Seoritae is a bean that demands time. In the central region mid June is the sowing limit, hilling and topping are finished before flowering, and if you keep to stink bug control for three weeks from late August and returning the stalks and leaves to the field, it repays you in flavor and nutrition.**
