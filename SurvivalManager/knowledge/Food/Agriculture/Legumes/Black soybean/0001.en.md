## Introduction

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Black soybean (a black soybean with green cotyledons) is the same species as soybean (Glycine max). Botanically, it is not a separate crop but a branch of the same one, referring to the late-maturing type among the black-soybean strains with a black seed coat and green flesh. Its name comes from its harvest time: it is called black soybean because it is picked after the first frost falls. It stands in the field from late October to mid-November, once every other bean crop is already in, filling out its last seeds as the frost falls on it.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This document therefore does not repeat the basic principles of soybean cultivation. The framework common to legumes — root-nodule bacteria and nitrogen fixation, nitrogen-budget accounting, the standards for antinutrient compounds and heat treatment, bean-weevil control, and tool-free methods of threshing and cleaning — applies to black soybean in exactly the same way, so it is covered here only as compactly as needed, and the rest of this document focuses on where black soybean diverges from soybean. There are three main points of divergence: maturation time, pigmentation, and the intensity of crop management.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Maturation time is the biggest difference. Black soybean takes 160 to 180 days from sowing to harvest. Ordinary soybean takes 120 to 150 days, so black soybean runs more than a month longer. This extra month cuts both ways. On the good side, the longer grain-filling period makes the seeds plumper, sweeter, and deeper in flavor. On the bad side, if the optimal sowing window is missed, the first frost cuts the grain-filling short. This is why its Speed to harvest grade is D, and why it must not be sown later than mid-June. In a disaster situation, black soybean is a bean that demands time — and if that time cannot be secured, all that is lost is the seed.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Pigmentation is a nutritional matter. The black of the seed coat is an antioxidant pigment called anthocyanin, and the green of the flesh is a chlorophyll-family pigment. This is why black soybean is also called sokcheong ("green inside"). These pigments do not contribute to calories or protein, so they are barely reflected in the evaluation score, but they have real practical value as a source of antioxidants in a diet that has lost fresh fruit and vegetables. Protein density is SS, the same as soybean, at 37.9% of calories.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Black soybean has a clear weakness. Its pest & disease pressure grade is C, distinctly worse than soybean's A. The estimated yield loss without pest control is 47.4% — in other words, without any control you lose nearly half the crop. Yield is also lower, 230kg per 1,000m² against soybean's 250kg. Because it stands in the field longer, it is exposed longer to stink bugs and the soybean pod borer, and there are reports that neglecting stink-bug control can cost up to 58% of the yield. Black soybean is a bean that takes more hands-on work than soybean. If that labor is available, it repays you in flavor and nutrition, but when labor is scarce and you must choose between soybean and black soybean, soybean is the safe choice.
