## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Soybean (Glycine max) is a legume that people refined over thousands of years from the wild beans of northeastern China and the Korean Peninsula, and it is the protein crop that best suits Korea's climate. Known as the meju bean, it is the raw material for doenjang, soy sauce, and tofu. Dry soybeans have 38 g of protein per 100 g, and the protein-to-calorie ratio of 37.9% is more than three and a half times the 10% minimum. It is rich in the lysine that grains lack, and the methionine that beans lack is found in grains, so securing grains and soybeans together completes the amino acid profile. Nodule bacteria let it draw nitrogen by itself, and in the Swiss DOK long-term trial the yield of plots given no fertilizer at all was 63.4% of conventional (potato 18%, maize and wheat in the 40s). Add to this a storability that lets dry seed keep for 3 years at room temperature and the stability of seed saving that continues through self-pollination. The overall grade is A (49.9 points, 19th of 183 valid species), with short term B (47.3), long term S (52.8), and supplementary SS (65.1 points). Its weaknesses are calories per area of C, 1,002 kcal per ㎡, only 0.38 times the self-sufficiency line; a 120-150 day growing season; death from a single frost; yield falling by half under 40% shade; and the need to soak for 8 hours and boil for over an hour.

A: pest and disease pressure, yield stability, seed longevity. B: water consumption, pest-control self-reliance |

> **The value of soybean lies not in the amount that comes from the field but in the quality of the protein in that amount and in a structure that keeps the cycle going even when nothing comes in from outside.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Calories per area C is the most practical constraint. To fill 2,100 kcal a day with soybeans alone you would have to eat 524 g, so soybean is a companion crop that builds calories with potatoes, sweet potatoes, and grains and puts protein on top, and giving it 20-30% of the field area is generally reasonable. Because of sunlight D, avoid wall shade, the area under large trees, and north-facing slopes. Cooking and processing D is a matter of safety, and making fermented pastes solves this burden in one go. The nitrogen fixation counted in non-food value C has a trap (Section 6). The grade for each item is in the Crop Evaluation Results document.

## 2. Plant Overview
| Item | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Classification | Annual legume of the genus Glycine in the family Fabaceae. Native to East Asia and self-pollinating. A short-day plant that flowers as days shorten |
| Leaves, stem | Trifoliate leaves covered with fine hairs. Upright, 60-100 cm tall, branching |
| Roots, flowers | Straight taproot and fine roots carrying bead-like nodules. Small white or pale purple flowers bloom in July and August |
| Pods, seeds | Brown pods covered with stiff hairs and holding 2-4 seeds. Seeds are round, basically yellowish white, with black, brown, and green types too |

> **Soybean takes up about 80% of all the water it uses over its life between flowering and pod filling. If water is cut off during these 4 weeks, yield collapses more than at any other stage.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Nodules begin to hand over nitrogen only 3-4 weeks after sowing, so a small amount of nitrogen in the base fertilizer is meaningful, but if the soil has plenty of nitrogen, the plant reduces nodule formation by itself. If it is too wet at ripening, the pods do not dry, and if dry pods get wet and dry again repeatedly they burst and the seeds fall (shattering). Dry soybeans per 100 g: 401 kcal, protein 38.02 g, fat 15-20 g (second only to peanut among legumes), carbohydrate 30-35 g, about 20 g dietary fiber, about 240 mg calcium, and about 7 mg iron.

## 3. Growing Environment and Varieties
| Condition | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Temperature | Germination from 10 °C (optimum 25-30 °C; at 10 °C or below the seed rots). Growth 15-35 °C (optimum 20-25 °C). Fruit set 20-30 °C, only a 10 °C range, and if 35 °C or more lasts a few days, flowers and young pods drop. Accumulated temperature 2,500 °C or more. Killed by frost. A spot lit at night flowers late |
| Soil, water | Well-drained sandy loam to loam (poorly drained ground is the worst), pH 5.5-7.5 (optimum 6.0-6.5), evapotranspiration during the crop 450-700 mm, water table 50 cm or deeper (optimum 80 cm or deeper) |

> **Soybean's pH requirement is the requirement of its nodule bacteria rather than of the plant itself. Below pH 5.5 the bean grows somehow, but the bacteria cannot live and nitrogen fixation stops.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The narrowest gate is the fruit-set temperature, and when heat and drought coincide, the flower drop rate can exceed 80%. The practical response is not to sow everything at once but to sow in two or three batches at 2-week intervals to spread out flowering.

| Type | Days to maturity | Sowing | Harvest | Features |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Early (early beans) | 100-120 days | April-May | July-August | Harvested early, low yield |
| Medium | 120-140 days | Late May to early June | Early to mid October | Well balanced |
| Medium-late (autumn beans) | 140-180 days | Late May to late June | Late October to November | Highest yield but frost risk |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Early varieties fill July and August, when old grain has run out and the autumn harvest is far off, so they are valuable even with a low yield, and because medium-late varieties may not finish ripening in a year with early frost, it is safer to sow in batches. The first criterion for choosing a variety is a local landrace whose seed can be saved, and the second is the distribution of sizes: use large-seeded medium-late types for paste and tofu, but always set aside some small-seeded type for bean sprouts. In regions with early frost choose early or medium varieties, if labor is short choose varieties resistant to lodging, and if the seed came from far away, check the latitude it was bred for.

## 4. Field Preparation and Sowing
| Item | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Field preparation | Ridge height 20 cm or more (30 cm or more in a paddy), drainage ditches 50-80 cm deep. On acidic soil, bring pH to 6.0-6.5 3-4 weeks before sowing with slaked lime or dolomitic lime at 200 kg per 1,000 ㎡ (roasted and crushed shells or eggshell powder, or wood ash, can substitute) |
| Fertilization (1,000 ㎡) | Nitrogen 3 kg, phosphorus 3.4 kg, potassium 3.4 kg (all as basal fertilizer). Saving nitrogen is the skill: more always lowers yield, and top dressing is unnecessary. Get phosphorus from bone meal and fish bones and potassium from wood ash (the upper limit is 100-200 g per ㎡), and if boron is short flowers drop, so use compost made from many materials |
| Nodule inoculation | Coat seeds lightly moistened with water with soil from 5-15 cm depth of a field where soybeans grew and formed nodules well, and sow at once (nodules from wild soybean roots also work, being the same genus). Do not combine with seed disinfection or fungicide. Fields low in phosphorus reduce effectiveness, and in fields where legumes have not been grown for long, inoculation is essential |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Check nodules around the start of flowering (6-8 weeks after sowing) by digging up a root and splitting a nodule. Red means they are fixing nitrogen, and when signs of nitrogen deficiency appear, the first thing to do is not to give fertilizer but to dig up the roots and look.

| Sowing | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Timing | Central region single crop: early May to early June (limit late June); central region double crop after rice: mid to late June; south single crop: early to mid June (limit mid-July); south double crop: late June to early July. Because shortening days are the signal for flowering, sowing after mid to late July greatly lowers the harvest |
| Seed preparation | Pick out and discard seeds with spots, discoloration, or insect holes, and kill surface pathogens by soaking in 50-55 °C water for 10-15 minutes (above that temperature germination is reduced) |
| Method | Depth 3-5 cm (seedlings cannot emerge beyond 7 cm), row spacing 60-80 cm, plant spacing 15-20 cm (10-15 cm for double crops). Seed rate: small seeds 3-4 kg, medium seeds 5-6 kg, large seeds 6-7 kg per 1,000 ㎡. The later the sowing, the smaller the plants, so plant densely: in late June use 60-70 cm × 15 cm. Birds peck the cotyledons right after sowing, so cover with straw or netting |

## 5. Water Management and Cultivation Management
| Stage | Water management |
|:---|:---|
| Sowing to germination | Keep the soil from drying out completely (the seed must absorb its own weight in water to germinate) |
| Flowering to pod filling | Give enough that the soil does not dry (80% of the water for the whole growth) |
| Ripening | Reduce water |

| Care | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Thinning, weeding | At 1-2 true leaves, leave 2 plants per hole and pull the weak ones. Do not enter the field after the last weeding before flowering |
| Hilling | 30-40 days after sowing, up to the node of the first true leaf, scrape soil up around the base of the plants. The most efficient single task in soybean cultivation, preventing lodging, promoting root development, improving drainage, and burying weeds |
| Tip pinching | At 5-7 true leaves, always before flowering, pinch off the growing tip of the stem by hand to increase side shoots and the number of pods. Pinching after flowering lowers the yield, and it is done only where the plants would grow leggy with plenty of fertilizer and rain; do not do it on poor fields to struggling beans |
| Lodging | Lodged beans multiply harvest labor several times over and their pods rot, so keep the planting distance and hill, and in typhoons tie ropes around the field to support it |

## 6. Pests, Diseases, and Nutrient Problems
| Pest or disease | Symptoms and conditions | Control without chemicals |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Soybean mosaic | Leaves mottle in a mosaic pattern and seeds get brown spots. Carried by aphids | Cannot be treated. Pull up and burn at once, and do not use spotted seed for planting |
| Bacterial blight, anthracnose, downy mildew | Angular brown spots on leaves / black cankers on stems and pods / grayish-white mold on the underside of leaves. Humid | Bordeaux mixture, seed selection and rotation, secure planting distance and drainage |
| Purple seed stain | Seed surface mottled purple. Seed-borne | Pick out stained seed thoroughly and disinfect with hot water |
| Sclerotinia stem rot, root rot, nematodes | Stems soften with white mycelium and black sclerotia / roots rot / roots deform into knobs. Cool and humid, excess moisture, continuous cropping | Rotate for 4 years or more, dig up diseased plants with the soil and burn them, drainage and raised ridges, interplant marigolds |
| Stink bugs | The biggest pest, piercing pods to suck sap and making empty pods | They move sluggishly in the early morning when dew is on, so shake the area under the pods and catch them in a bucket of water, catch them on trap crops of sesame, foxtail millet, and sorghum around the field, and finish ripening before late summer with early varieties |
| Aphids, pod borers, tobacco cutworms | Suck sap and carry virus / larvae enter pods and eat the seeds / devour leaves and pods | Soapy water (5 g of soap per 1 L of water), early sowing and early harvest, catch by hand and remove egg masses |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Bordeaux mixture is made by dissolving 400 g of copper sulfate and 400 g of quicklime separately in 100 L of water, mixing them, and using it immediately.

| Symptom | Cause and action |
|:---|:---|
| Pale yellow from the lower leaves, small plant | Nitrogen deficiency (poor nodules are the most common cause). Dig up the roots and check the nodules; if there are none, give a small amount of urine diluted 10 times that year and make sure to inoculate next year |
| Flowers bloom but no pods set | Boron deficiency or high temperature and drought at flowering. Compost made from many materials, and lower the temperature with irrigation and mulching |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The saying "planting beans improves the soil" is a truth with conditions attached. 250 kg of seed contains about 15 kg of nitrogen (150 kg per hectare), 4.5 times more than the same weight of maize seed, while nodules fix 100-150 kg per hectare under good conditions and 30-60 kg under poor ones, and 130-160 kg leaves with the seed. 25-40 kg remains in stems and leaves and 10-20 kg in roots and nodules, so if only the seed is harvested and the stems and leaves are returned the balance is about even, but burning the stalks or carrying them off as feed is a definite loss. So return all the stalks, leaves, and pod hulls to the field, do not dig up the roots, cut the stalks to 20-30 cm and spread them, and if livestock were fed, put their manure on that field. Burning drives off the nitrogen, so always return the ash to that field.

## 7. Intercropping and Crop Rotation
| Compatibility | Companions |
|:---|:---|
| Good | Maize and sorghum (receive some of the nitrogen the beans fix and share light through height difference), potato (diseases do not overlap and nutrient demands differ), Chinese cabbage and cabbage group (planted after beans, they use the leftover nitrogen) |
| Poor | Tomato, chili pepper, and eggplant (share pests and diseases in the same field), onion-family plants such as garlic and onion (sulfur compounds are reported to suppress nodule bacteria, so keep them apart) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When mixing with maize, alternate two rows of maize with two rows of beans and orient the maize rows north-south to avoid shade. Soybean's continuous-cropping tolerance is S, a 1-year rest group, but nematodes and root rot accumulate, so do not plant legumes in succession. A 4-year rotation example is soybean → leafy vegetables or onion family (use the nitrogen the beans left) → grains or tubers → root vegetables or green manure. If the field is small, divide it into four plots and move one plot each year, or change the position of the bean rows every year.

## 8. Harvest and Seed Saving
| Harvest | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Timing | When the leaves have turned yellow and nearly all fallen, and the seeds rattle inside the pods when shaken in the field. Too early and the seeds are wrinkled; too late and shattering loses nearly 30%. If rain is forecast, harvest without waiting for full maturity |
| Technique | On a clear, dry day after the dew has dried, from 10 a.m., cut the stem base with a sickle and leave the roots. Hang to dry, then thresh |
| Threshing, cleaning | Thresh by treading dry stalks in a sack, flailing them thinly spread on a mat, beating them with a club, or striking them against the inside wall of a tub (drying is the key). Winnow in the early morning or at dusk when the wind is steady, pouring in a thin stream two or three times. Float them in water to skim off empty and insect-eaten seeds and always dry them again |

| Seed saving | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Selection, separation | Mark in the field beforehand plants with no disease symptoms, many pods, and no lodging (tie a thread on them), and thresh them separately so they do not mix with eating beans. It is self-pollinating, so isolation is unnecessary |
| Storage | Dry to 12-13% moisture, put in a sealed container, and keep in a cool, dark place. Germination is 90% or more in the harvest year, 80-90% after 1 year, 60-80% after 2, 40-60% after 3, and drops sharply from year 4, so use freshly saved seed every year and keep old seed as a reserve. Test old seed for germination; if only half germinate, double the seed rate, and below 30%, eat it |
| Renewal | Purple seed stain, anthracnose, and mosaic accumulate through seed, so discard spotted seed, and every 3-4 years obtain seed from elsewhere and mix some in |

## 9. Storage
| Step | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Drying | Pre-dry in the stalks in the field or under the eaves for a day or two. Put dry salt and beans in a jar and shake: if the salt sticks to the glass, the beans are still damp. They are right when a bitten bean splits cleanly without the tooth sinking in |
| Weevil control | Eggs are already laid on the pods in the field, so harvest on time. Complete sealing is the surest: fill containers completely and use several small ones, and eggs and larvae die in 3-4 weeks. Mix in sifted wood ash at 2-5% of the volume (rinse before eating). Rub in 2-5 mL of cooking oil per 1 kg of beans (only for what will be eaten within half a year; not for seed) |
| Environment, inspection | 15 °C or below, air humidity 65% or below. Put an ear to the container and check for rustling, a musty smell, and small round holes |

> **Beans are not lost to rot but eaten by insects. The art of storage is the art of keeping insects out.**

## 10. Cooking and Precautions
| Compound | Effect | Removal |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Trypsin inhibitors | Block protein-digesting enzymes, causing abdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhea, and lower protein utilization | Soak 8 hours, then heat at 100 °C for 30-60 minutes or more |
| Lectins | Stick to the intestinal lining and block absorption, agglutinating red blood cells and causing severe abdominal pain, vomiting, and diarrhea | After soaking, heat at 100 °C for 10-30 minutes or more |
| Phytic acid | Blocks absorption of iron, zinc, calcium, and magnesium | Reduced 60-80% by soaking, sprouting, and fermenting |

> **Beans that are underdone can be more dangerous than raw ones. A brief blanch breaks down cell walls so absorption is better, but the inhibitors and lectins remain. When cooking, do not stop halfway; cook all the way.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Dry beans have a hard surface, so boiling without soaking leaves the inside underdone, which is the most dangerous, and soybean lectin is weaker than that of kidney beans, but cooking thoroughly in fully boiling water is the only safe method.

| Cooking | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Basic | Soak in room-temperature water for 8 hours (6 hours in summer, changing the water). Steam at 100 °C for 60-90 minutes, or pressure-cook at 120 °C for 15-20 minutes (uses the least fuel). Pressed between thumb and forefinger it mashes easily when done |
| Saving fuel | Boil for 15 minutes or more, turn off the fire, and wrap in a straw- or rice-hull box or a quilt for 2-3 hours (boil again if a hard center remains). Dry beans roasted over low heat for 20-30 minutes lose much of their inhibitors and can be eaten as they are. Roasted and ground bean flour keeps 2-3 months sealed |
| Bean sprouts | Dry beans contain no vitamin C, so soak small-seeded beans for 6-8 hours and grow them in the dark at 20-25 °C; 100 g becomes 400-500 g and is a winter source of vitamin C |
| Making pastes | Meju is made by boiling and mashing beans, shaping blocks, wrapping them in straw, and fermenting them for a month or more; doenjang and soy sauce are aged in brine for several months and keep for years. Cheonggukjang is boiled beans wrapped in straw and kept at about 40 °C for 2-3 days, the fastest fermented bean. Tofu is made by grinding soaked beans, boiling and straining, and curdling with bittern, the most easily digested form |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fermentation is the most fundamental method, in which microorganisms break inhibitors and lectins into amino acids and break down phytic acid, removing the antinutrients, and making a paste pays the fuel for the hour of boiling just once for a year's supply and keeps for years with salt.

## 11. Annual Work Schedule
| Month | Work |
|:---|:---|
| Jan-Feb | Check stored beans, test seed germination, mend tools. In February, plan the field, turn the compost, and prepare for making pastes |
| Mar-Apr | In March, spread lime (3-4 weeks before sowing), finish compost, and ferment meju |
| May-June | In May, select seed, disinfect with hot water, sow early varieties, and secure soil for inoculation. In June, main sowing (early month in the central region, mid to late month in the south), bird protection, first weeding, and sowing in batches at 2-week intervals to spread flowering |
| July-Aug | In July, thinning, weeding, hilling, tip pinching, and checking nodules. In August, flowering and pod filling, concentrated irrigation, and catching stink bugs in the early morning |
| Sep-Oct | In September, ripening management, stop irrigation, and mark seed plants. In October, harvest, pre-drying, hanging to dry, and threshing (do not wait for full maturity if rain is forecast) |
| Nov-Dec | In November, cleaning, drying, storage, returning stems and leaves (cut the stalks and spread them on the field; do not burn them), and boiling meju. In December, making pastes, checking storage, and growing bean sprouts |

> **Soybean earns its keep when you give no more than 3 kg of nitrogen per 1,000 ㎡, dig up roots at flowering to check the red of the nodules, give water in the 4 weeks from flowering to pod filling, and return the stalks to the field after harvest.**
