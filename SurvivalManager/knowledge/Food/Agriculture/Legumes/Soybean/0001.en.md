## Introduction

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Soybean (Glycine max) comes from East Asia. It is a crop that people refined over thousands of years from the wild beans of northeastern China and the Korean Peninsula, which is why it is the protein crop best suited to South Korea's climate. Known by such names as mejukong (beans for making meju, fermented soybean blocks), baektae (white soybean) and hwangtae (yellow soybean), this bean is the raw material for doenjang (Korean soybean paste), soy sauce and tofu, and it is very nearly the only field crop that can step in to carry the protein load on a table where meat has disappeared. When crops are ranked from a disaster-preparedness perspective, soybean always lands near the top, not because it is rich in calories but because it has overwhelmingly more protein relative to its calories.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The numbers look like this. 100g of dried soybeans contains 38g of protein. Converted to the protein share of calories, that is 37.9%, which, given that the minimum a person needs is 10%, is more than three and a half times the requirement. Rice is at about 7%, potato 12% and sorghum 12.5%, so soybean is a crop in a completely different tier. On top of that, soybean is rich in lysine, which grains lack, while methionine, which beans lack, is found in grains. The old custom of mixing beans into cooked rice is the result of people discovering this complementarity through experience. Secure grains and soybeans together, and that alone completes the amino acid profile.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Soybean's second strength shows when chemical fertilizer is cut off. Because it lives in symbiosis with root-nodule bacteria (rhizobia) and draws nitrogen from the air by itself, it does not collapse as badly as other crops when nitrogen fertilizer disappears. In the Swiss DOK long-term trial, the soybean yield in plots that received no fertilizer at all held at 63.4% of the conventional level. In the same trial, potato fell to 18%, and maize and wheat fell into the 40% range. This is the highest figure among legumes, and this single line is what makes soybean a pillar of long-term self-reliance. Add to this its storability, since dry seed keeps for 3 years at room temperature; its stable seed saving, since self-pollination keeps the pure line intact; and its prevalence, since seed can be found anywhere across South Korea's eight provinces.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;However, Calories per area comes out at grade C. The calories produced per 1m² amount to only 38% of the self-sufficiency line, so soybean alone cannot fill the stomach. Soybean becomes a proper diet only when it is paired with grains or tuber crops (tubers/storage roots). It takes 120-150 days from sowing to harvest, and it is harvested only once a year. At 40% shade the yield falls by half, so it handles shade poorly, and a single frost kills it. It must be soaked for 8 hours and boiled for more than an hour before it can be eaten, so it takes a lot of both fuel and water.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;And there is one point that is misunderstood more than any other. The saying that planting beans improves the soil is a truth that comes with conditions. Each 1kg of soybean seed carries off about 4.5 times as much nitrogen as 1kg of maize seed. If you harvest the beans and haul them out of the field, more nitrogen can leave the field than the nodules fixed. Whether a soybean field revives the soil or ruins it depends on what is done with the stems and leaves. This document covers that fork in detail in Section 5.
