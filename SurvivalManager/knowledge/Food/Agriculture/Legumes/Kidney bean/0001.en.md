## Introduction

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The kidney bean (Phaseolus vulgaris) originated in Central and South America. It is a domesticated form of wild beans from Mexico and the Andes, and together with maize and squash it formed one of the three pillars of Native American agriculture. It reached South Korea in the late Joseon period and is now grown throughout South Korea. White kidney beans, black kidney beans, speckled kidney beans, and tiger kidney beans are all names for the same species, and so are the kidney, pinto, and navy beans of Western naming.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The advantages of the kidney bean for disaster preparedness are clear. Early bush varieties give a dry-bean harvest in 70-80 days from sowing. Green kidney beans can be eaten after 60 days. Water use is lower than soybean, with total evapotranspiration of 400mm, seed can be obtained anywhere in South Korea, and self-pollination keeps the line pure. Pole varieties, given only a support to climb, have a long harvest period and a high yield per area. Above all, the Three Sisters planting with maize and squash is a combination proven over a long span of time, making it possible to get calories, protein, and vegetables from a single field at once.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;But before anything else in this document, Section 14 must be read first. Kidney beans contain a toxic substance called Phaseolus lectin, also known as phytohaemagglutinin (PHA). Its level of danger is on an entirely different scale from the lectins in other beans.

> **Just four or five raw or undercooked kidney beans can bring on severe vomiting and diarrhea within a few hours. Even more dangerous, heating them at a low temperature around 80°C can raise the toxicity to as much as five times higher than in the raw bean. You must soak them in water for at least five hours, discard the soaking water, and then boil them at a full boil, 100°C, for at least 10 minutes, for this to be safe.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Without knowing this fact, the reasonable-seeming choice to save fuel, cooking for a long time over a low flame, becomes the most dangerous method of all. The scarcer fuel is in a disaster, the greater this danger becomes. If kidney beans are part of your food plan, everyone who eats them must know this one fact.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Returning to the evaluation, the kidney bean's overall grade is C, a score of 40.7. Calories per area D, cold hardiness D, and cooking & processing D pulled the score down. It is not a crop that fills the stomach, it is sensitive to frost, and it takes a great deal of hand labor and fuel to cook. Its supplementary-value grade, however, is a high SS at 61.4. The kidney bean's place is not as a staple but as a side dish, and as a companion planted alongside a cornfield.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Finally, there is one misunderstanding common to legume crops as a whole: the idea that planting beans improves the soil. That is only half true. Kidney beans, too, draw in nitrogen through a partnership with root-nodule bacteria, but if the seed is harvested and carried off the field, more nitrogen can leave the field than the amount that was fixed. Section 5 works through that fork in the road with numbers.
