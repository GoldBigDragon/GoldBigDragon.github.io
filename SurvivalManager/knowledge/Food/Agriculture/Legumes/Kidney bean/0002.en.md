### 1-3. How to Read the Strengths

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The criteria graded SS are three: fertilizer demand, infrastructure needs, and storability. S went to protein density, micronutrients, taste, prevalence, and seed saving. A went to water use, yield retention without synthetic inputs, yield stability, and seed longevity.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In practical terms, the S for prevalence matters most. The kidney bean is grown in all eight provinces of South Korea and seed is easy to find. Unlike chickpea or lentil, getting hold of seed at all is not a constraint here. Add the S for seed saving and the A for seed longevity, and the loop closes: once you have obtained seed, you can carry it forward yourself, and the dry bean lasts 3 years at room temperature.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The S for protein density and the S for micronutrients are what earn the SS supplementary grade. A P:E of 24.1% is more than double the requirement line, and 16 nutrients reach 10% of their recommended amount. Dietary fiber runs over 24g per 100g, and calcium, iron, and magnesium are all well represented. When meat and dairy have disappeared, one bowl of kidney beans changes the character of the diet.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There is one more strength that the grading table does not capture: the Three Sisters planting. Maize is planted and its stalk used as a support; the pole-type kidney bean climbs it while sharing nitrogen with the maize; and squash is laid underneath to cover the ground, suppress weeds, and hold in moisture. It is an arrangement Native Americans refined over thousands of years, and it gets calories (maize), protein (kidney bean), and vegetables (squash) from a single field at once. Where the field is small and materials are scarce, this combination is worth more than the sum of the individual crops' grades.

> **The kidney bean's place is not in a monoculture but in a combination. Grown upward using a maize stalk as its support, it yields more protein from the same patch of ground without spending anything on stakes or extra area.**

### 1-4. How to Read the Weaknesses

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The criteria graded D are seven: calories per area, perenniality, sunlight response, cooking & processing, cold hardiness, tillage energy, and non-food value. C went to temperature range and pest & disease pressure.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For the kidney bean, D for cooking & processing is not mere inconvenience but a safety issue. The 8-hour soak and the hour or more of heating are not a matter of taste but a required procedure for destroying the lectin. Skip this procedure, or do it halfway, and poisoning follows. What makes this crop especially demanding is that heating it for a long time at a low temperature to save fuel actually raises its toxicity. All of Section 14 is devoted to this problem.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;D for calories per area means 696kcal per m², 0.27× the self-sufficiency line. Kidney beans alone cannot fill the stomach. Cereals or tuber crops (tubers/storage roots) need to supply the calories, with kidney beans added on top for protein, and the Three Sisters planting realizes exactly that structure in a single field.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;D for cold hardiness and C for temperature range together narrow the growing window. Growth stops at 5°C or below and frost kills the plant, and the temperature range for setting seed is only 18-28°C, a span of just 10°C. Planting must wait until the last frost is completely over, and if days above 30°C continue, the flowers drop. Where the flowering period falls between South Korea's summer heat waves and the monsoon season (jangma, late June to July in South Korea) decides the yield.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Continuous-cropping tolerance B means a 3-year break group. That is longer than soybean's 1 year but shorter than pea's 5-7 years. Anthracnose and nematodes build up in the soil, so replanting in the same spot causes yields to collapse within a few years.

## 2. What Kind of Plant Is This?

### 2-1. Taxonomy and Origin

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The kidney bean is an annual herb of the legume family (Fabaceae), genus Phaseolus. Besides the kidney bean, this genus includes cultivated species such as the lima bean and the runner bean, all native to Central and South America. One feature shared across this genus runs through this entire document: the seeds of Phaseolus species contain a far stronger lectin than other legumes. Where the lectins in soybean, pea, and lentil cause no more than digestive discomfort, the lectin in Phaseolus species causes acute poisoning in small amounts.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Its native range is Central and South America, from Mexico to the Andes. It is known to have been domesticated separately in two places, which is why the Central American and Andean lineages differ slightly in character. In general, the Andean lineage has larger seeds and suits cooler climates, while the Central American lineage has smaller seeds and tolerates heat better. What reached South Korea was mainly the large-seeded lineage.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The root-nodule bacteria that partner with the kidney bean belong to the Rhizobium group, a different lineage from those of soybean or pea. In practice, soil from a field that has previously grown kidney beans is the most reliable source of inoculum. Because the kidney bean is grown throughout South Korea, an inoculum source is not hard to find.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The kidney bean self-pollinates. Fertilization is completed inside the flower before it opens, with the petals enclosing the male and female parts. It is not as perfect as in pea, however, so cross-pollination by bees occurs rarely. Planting several varieties side by side can mix them at a very low rate, so if you want to keep a pure line strictly, plant them several meters apart and choose your seed stock from plants toward the interior of the field.

### 2-2. Appearance

| Organ | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| Leaves | Trifoliate, with three leaflets to a set. Heart-shaped, with fine hairs on the surface |
| Stem | Pole types climb up to 2-3m. Bush types stop at 40-60cm |
| Roots | A straight taproot with fine lateral roots and root nodules. Spreads relatively shallowly |
| Flowers | Butterfly-shaped, in white, pale pink, or purple |
| Pods | Cylindrical or flat, 10-20cm long. Holds 4-10 seeds |
| Seeds | Kidney-shaped and large. Comes in white, black, mottled, red, and other colors |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The difference between pole and bush types is the first fork in the road for growing kidney beans. Pole types have a stem tip that winds around other objects as it climbs, reaching 2-3m. The direction of winding is fixed, and if someone forces it to wind the other way, it comes loose. The best support for the vine to wind around is about 2cm in diameter; it cannot wind around a thick post. A maize stalk fits this condition well.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Bush types have short internodes, stop at 40-60cm, and need no support. Their growing period is also short, at 50-60 days, so they are harvested quickly. In exchange, each plant yields less and the harvest period is short. The rule is: bush type when stakes or labor are lacking, pole type when both are available.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;That the seed is kidney-shaped and large matters for cooking. Because the seed is large, heat takes a long time to reach its core, and this is the structural reason it is so easy to end up with undercooked beans. A bean that has softened on the outside but is still hard at the core is the most dangerous state of all.

### 2-3. Growth Stages

| Stage | Timing | Key physiology | Key nutrients |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germination & seedling | Sowing to 2-3 true leaves | Grows on stored nutrients from the cotyledons | Phosphorus, a small amount of nitrogen |
| Vegetative growth | True leaves unfolding to branching | The vine extends or the plant branches | Nitrogen supplied by the root nodules |
| Flowering | First flower to full bloom | Flowers open and pollination occurs | Boron, phosphorus |
| Pod filling | Pod formation and swelling | Seeds enlarge and draw in nutrients | Potassium, phosphorus |
| Ripening & maturity | Seed fill to yellowing | Pods dry and turn yellow | Irrigation stopped |

> **The kidney bean is a crop whose cotyledons come up above the ground. Because the early nutrients are stored in those cotyledons, a plant whose cotyledons are pecked off by birds usually dies. The ten days right after germination are the most dangerous stretch.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;An old saying goes that of three seeds sown, one is for the birds, one is for the insects, and one is the farmer's share. This is because the kidney bean's cotyledons are large and tender, making them a target for birds and soil-dwelling insects. The practice of sowing two or three seeds per hole comes from this, and covering the ground with netting or straw until germination is especially worth the effort.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There is also a point worth noting about early nitrogen. The root nodules do not work right from sowing. It takes three to four weeks for the bacteria to infect the roots, for nodules to form, and for nitrogen to be handed over. In the meantime, the young kidney bean survives on the nutrients stored in its cotyledons and on nitrogen from the soil. This is why putting a small amount of nitrogen in the base fertilizer makes sense. It must be a small amount, though: if the soil has plenty of nitrogen, the plant reduces nodule formation on its own, growing lush leaves while producing fewer pods.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The amino acid profile deserves a separate look. Kidney bean protein is generous in lysine and a little short on methionine. Maize is exactly the opposite, particularly short on lysine. There is a history of the deficiency disease pellagra appearing in diets based on maize alone, and eating kidney beans alongside it resolves this problem to a considerable degree. The long-standing habit in Central and South America of serving maize and kidney beans on the same plate, and the Three Sisters planting arrangement, both rest on this nutritional complementarity.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;At 24.4g, dietary fiber is high even among legumes. Suddenly feeding a large amount to someone who has been starving for a long time, or to a child, brings on bloating and diarrhea. This can be mistaken for lectin poisoning, so care is needed. Start with a small amount and increase it over several days, and always discard both the soaking water and the cooking water.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A fat content as low as 0.5-1.5% is an advantage for storage. There is almost no rancidity, so it keeps well even when ground into flour. In exchange for a lower calorie density, it is on the easier side to store.
