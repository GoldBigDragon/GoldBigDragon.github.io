## 1. Disaster-Preparedness Evaluation

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The lentil's Overall grade is C, 41.9 points. Broken down by scenario, its Short-term ability to get through the early stage of a disaster is D (40.5), its Long-term ability to sustain itself over several years is C (43.2), and its Supplementary value as a side-dish resource beyond the staple diet is SS (63.4). The fact that only the Supplementary grade stands out this high sums up the lentil's character. It is not a crop that fills the stomach, but one that changes both the quality of the table and the burden of cooking.

### 1-3. How to Read the Strengths

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Three criteria carry an SS: Fertilizer demand, Infrastructure needs, and Storability. An S goes to Protein density, Micronutrients, Water use, Taste, and Seed saving. An A goes to Cold hardiness, Yield retention without synthetic inputs, Yield stability, and Seed longevity.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The value most worth noting in this table is actually the C. A Cooking & processing grade of C is the highest among the legumes. Soybean, fava bean, kidney bean, mung bean, cowpea, and chickpea all received a D, and pea received a C, but the lentil's C carries a lighter actual burden than even that pea. The reason is simple: no soaking is needed, and it cooks in 20-30 minutes. Split, hulled red lentils take only 10-15 minutes.

> **In a disaster, fuel runs out just as fast as food. The difference between a bean that needs an hour of fire every meal and one that needs only 10 minutes adds up, over a year, to a whole stack of firewood.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This difference comes from the lentil's structure. The seed is flat and thin, only 2-3mm thick, meaning heat has only a short distance to travel to the center. The time a bean takes to cook is set by its thickness, not its volume, and the lentil is the thinnest of all edible legumes. Its seed coat is also thin, so water penetrates immediately.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The Water use grade of S deserves equal weight. Total evapotranspiration of 250mm is less than half that of soybeans, because the growing season is short, the leaves are small, and the roots go deep. Where water has to be carried by hand, this difference translates directly into a difference in labor.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The Micronutrients grade of S is the basis for the Supplementary grade of SS. As many as 17 nutrients reach 10% of the recommended intake, with iron and folate especially abundant. When meat and dairy disappear, anemia is a threat that creeps in quietly over months, and lentils are high in iron while being easy enough to cook that people actually eat them often. Where how often you actually eat something matters more than the numbers on a nutrition table, this point is decisive.

### 1-4. How to Read the Weaknesses

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Five criteria carry a D: Calories per area, Perenniality, Sunlight response, Tillage energy, and Non-food value. A C goes to Pest & disease pressure and Prevalence in South Korea.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The D in Calories per area weighs heaviest. At 548kcal per m², it is only 0.21× the self-sufficiency line - the second-lowest among the legumes in this crop table, after mung bean. The yield itself, 150kg per 1,000m², is about half that of soybean's 250kg or fava bean's 300kg, because the seeds are small, a pod holds only one or two of them, and the plant itself is small. The D in Tillage & digging energy follows from this. The energy recovered relative to the energy spent tilling the field is only 3.2×, one of the lowest among the legumes. Filling a field with lentils is a loss by the calorie accounting.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The C in Prevalence in South Korea is also a real-world wall. Lentils are almost never grown commercially in South Korea. There is one more point that needs special caution here. Lentils sold in stores for eating, especially red lentils, are hulled and split and will never germinate. Even brown lentils will not sprout if they went through heat treatment during distribution. Plantable seed must be sourced separately, and once obtained, a small-scale germination test should be run first.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There is one more practical difficulty that the grade table does not capture: the high heat and monsoon of South Korean summer. Above 25°C, flowering and fruit-set become difficult for lentils, and continuous rain during ripening rots the pods and spreads disease. Sown in spring, the flowering period runs into the heat of June; sown in late summer to dodge the heat, ripening runs into winter cold. It should be understood in advance that lentils have a narrower window in South Korea's climate than one might expect. If a high-altitude, cool upland site is available, that is the best fit for lentils.

## 2. What Kind of Plant Is This?

### 2-1. Taxonomy and Origin

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lentil is an annual herb in the family Fabaceae, genus Lens. Phylogenetically, it is close to pea, fava bean, and vetch. This fact matters in practice. The root-nodule bacterium that pairs with lentil is Rhizobium leguminosarum bv. viciae, the same species used by pea, fava bean, and wild vetch. That means soil from a field that grew any of those can be used as an inoculant, a far more favorable condition than chickpea, which requires its own dedicated strain.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Its native range is Southwest Asia, in what is now Turkey and Syria. Its wild ancestor, Lens orientalis, still grows there. As a plant adapted to a Mediterranean climate of wet winters and bone-dry summers, the lentil grows while it is cool and sets seed before the heat arrives. Its short 100-110 day growing season is likewise the result of a strategy to finish its work before summer comes.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lentil is self-pollinating. Its flowers are very small and the petals enclose the reproductive organs, making it hard for outside pollen to get in. Even planting several lines side by side does not mix their traits, and seed saved and replanted as-is produces the same as the parent. The Seed saving grade of S comes from this. The harder a crop is to source seed for, the more this trait is worth.

### 2-2. Appearance

| Organ | Feature |
|---|---|
| Leaves | Pinnately compound. 4-7 pairs of leaflets, ending in a short tendril |
| Stem | Thin and upright, branching heavily near the base. 30-45cm tall |
| Roots | A taproot goes in deep, with small nodules on the fine roots |
| Flowers | White or pale purple, butterfly-shaped. Very small, around 5mm across |
| Pods | Flat and short. Hold only 1-2 seeds |
| Seed | A convex disc shape. At 2-3mm thick, the thinnest among legumes |

> **Every practical advantage of the lentil comes from the thinness of its seed. Heat has only a short distance to travel to the center, so it cooks quickly, and water penetrates immediately, so it needs no soaking.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It also helps to know that the whole plant is small and delicate. At around 40cm tall with thin stems, it gets smothered if weeds grow even a little. Pods also sit low, often within 10cm of the ground, which means the plant must be cut close to the ground with a sickle at harvest, and rain causes the lower pods to touch the soil and rot. This is why building up a raised bed is especially valuable for lentils.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It does have tendrils, but they are too short and weak to climb anything. Their role is only to hook onto neighboring plants for mutual support. This is why planting lentils densely, so plants lean on each other, actually makes them more resistant to lodging. There is no need to set up stakes the way you would for peas.

### 2-3. Growth Stages

| Stage | Timing | Key Physiology | Key Nutrients |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germination and seedling stage | 0-2 weeks after sowing | Germinates quickly. Germinates slowly even at 4°C | Phosphorus, a little nitrogen |
| Vegetative growth | 2-6 weeks | Stems stand up and branch | Nitrogen supplied by root nodules |
| Flowering | 6-8 weeks | Small flowers open in sequence from the lower nodes upward | Phosphorus, boron |
| Pod filling | 8-10 weeks | Pods enlarge while staying flat, and seeds thicken | Potassium, phosphorus |
| Ripening and maturity | 10-14 weeks | Lower pods dry to brown first | Stop irrigation |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Because the lentil's growing season is short, there is no time to recover from mistakes. If it gets smothered by weeds or runs short on nutrients early on, it simply flowers as a stunted plant with no chance to catch up. This is why early management, especially weed control in the first month, largely determines the yield.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It should also be understood in advance that ripening is uneven. By the time the pods at the lower nodes turn brown and dry, green pods are still hanging above. Harvesting by the lower pods loses the upper ones; waiting for the upper ones lets the lower ones split and shatter. The known compromise point is when the third pod from the bottom rattles with the sound of seeds knocking inside when shaken.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There is also a point worth noting about nitrogen in the early stage. Root nodules do not start working right after sowing. It takes 3-4 weeks from seed germination through root growth, bacterial infection, and nodule formation before nitrogen is actually handed over. For a lentil with a 100-day growing season, these 3 weeks amount to 20% of the whole cycle, a larger share than in other beans. That is why putting a small amount of nitrogen in the base fertilizer is worthwhile. It must be a small amount, though. If the soil already has plenty of nitrogen, the plant feels no need to bargain with the bacteria and reduces nodule formation on its own.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lentil protein is rich in lysine and a little short on methionine. Grains are exactly the opposite. Eating them together with rice or bread fills in each other's gaps and noticeably raises protein utilization. The Indian habit of eating dal (an Indian lentil dish) over rice, and the Middle Eastern habit of pairing bread with lentil soup, both rest on this principle.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The 7mg of iron and the abundant folate should be read together. Both nutrients are used together in making red blood cells, and it is uncommon for a single food to contain plenty of both. However, phytic acid interferes with iron absorption, so soaking and discarding the water, or sprouting, is nutritionally meaningful. Iron absorption also rises sharply when eaten together with vitamin C. Pairing lentils with something sour, such as vinegar, kimchi, or dried fruit, is a real practical help.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fat content as low as 1-2% is an advantage for storage. Even ground into flour, rancidity develops slowly, so it keeps longer than soybean or peanut. In exchange for lower calorie density, it is on the easy-to-store side.
