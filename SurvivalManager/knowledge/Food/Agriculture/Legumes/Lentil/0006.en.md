## 13. Seed Saving

| Item | Method |
|---|---|
| Selecting plants for seed | Mark plants in the field in advance that show no rust symptoms, survived to the end in a field where wilt disease was going around, and carry many pods |
| Harvest time | Harvest after the pods have turned completely brown, but before they burst |
| Separation | Thresh separately. Rubbing by hand does not damage the seed |
| Sorting | Discard all cracked, spotted, discolored, or small seeds |
| Drying | 12% moisture or below. Dry slowly in the shade |
| Storage | Put in a sealed container and keep in a cool, dark place. A separate container from the food supply |
| Storage period | 3-4 years sealed at room temperature |
| Renewal | If obtainable, mix in some seed from elsewhere every 3-4 years |

> **Lentil is a crop for which plantable seed is very difficult to obtain again in South Korea. Most lentil sold in stores for eating will not germinate. Seed saving is not a choice but a precondition for continuing to grow this crop at all.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most important trait for seed saving is tolerance of rust and resistance to wilt disease. The most valuable seed comes from a plant that stayed clean while the plants around it were covered in rust color, or one that survived to the end and set seed in a field where wilt disease was going around. Because resistance is inherited, selecting and carrying forward such plants builds, within a few years, a line that holds up on that particular field.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The second trait is adaptation to South Korea's climate. Select out plants that set pods even in weather above 25°C, or that ripened before the monsoon arrived. Since lentil is a difficult crop in South Korea's climate, this selection over several years makes a bigger difference than any other management practice.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Because lentil is a self-pollinating crop, there is no need to worry about isolation distance. Planting several lines side by side does not mix their traits. However, because the seed is small, it is hard to sort out by eye once mixed, so harvest and store each line separately, and label the container with which field and which year it came from.

| Seed Storage Duration and Germination Rate |
|---|
| Year of harvest: 90% or above |
| After 1 year: 80-90% |
| After 2 years: 70-80% |
| After 3 years: 50-70% |
| After 4 years: drops sharply |

## 14. Toxicity and Antinutrient Compounds

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lentil is among the legumes with the lowest toxicity risk. It has neither the strong lectin that causes poisoning from just a few raw seeds, as in kidney bean, nor the compound that triggers acute hemolysis in certain people, as in fava bean. The antinutrient content itself is on the low side among legumes, and this is another reason lentil's cooking time is short.

| Compound | What It Does | Symptoms | How to Remove It |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trypsin inhibitor | Blocks the action of protein-digesting enzymes | Abdominal discomfort, reduced protein utilization | 20-30 minutes in boiling water is enough. Contains less than a third of soybean's level |
| Lectin | Binds to the intestinal lining and interferes with absorption | Harmless in small amounts | Easily destroyed by heat. Unlike kidney beans, its toxicity is very weak |
| Phytic acid | Binds with iron, zinc, and calcium, blocking their absorption | Long-term intake can cause anemia and zinc deficiency | Soaking, sprouting, or fermenting reduces it by 60-80% |
| Oligosaccharides | Not digestible by humans; gut bacteria ferment them | Causes bloating | On the low side among legumes. Discarding the cooking water reduces it further |
| Tannin | The astringent compound in the seed coat | Interferes with iron absorption | Almost disappears once the hull is removed |
| Mold toxins | Produced by mold during storage | Dangerous | Washing or cooking moldy seed does not make it safe. Discard it |

> **What actually harms people with lentil is not the bean itself but a bean that has molded from improper storage. If it smells musty or shows a bluish powder on the surface, do not hesitate to throw it out.**

| Treatment | Time/Temperature | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Soaking | Not needed | At 2-3mm thick, water penetrates immediately |
| Boiling (unhulled) | 20-30 minutes in boiling water | Softens while holding its shape |
| Boiling (hulled and split) | 10-15 minutes in boiling water | Breaks down into a porridge |
| Washing | Rinse in water before cooking and skim off what floats | Removes dust and empty seeds |
| Checking doneness | Presses easily flat between the fingers | If a hard core remains, it is undercooked |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It is also worth noting that lentil digests more easily than other beans. Low oligosaccharide content means less bloating, and hulled red lentil is especially gentle. This is why lentil porridge is the safest choice when beans must be fed to someone long-starved, sick, elderly, or a child. People who get upset stomachs from other beans often tolerate lentil just fine.

## 15. Storage Without Refrigeration

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lentil's Storability grade is SS. Dried seed lasts 3 years at room temperature, and with fat as low as 1 to 2%, rancidity is almost nonexistent. However, because the seed coat is thin, it is relatively easy for insects to bore into.

> **Beans are not lost to rotting but to being eaten by insects. The art of storage is, in essence, the art of keeping insects out.**

### 15-1. Drying Comes First

| Stage | Method | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Preliminary drying | Dry with the stems on, in the sun for 4-6 days, or hung under the eaves | Pods become fully dry |
| Drying after threshing | Spread thin on a mat in the sun for 1-2 days | The seed is thin, so it dries quickly |
| Target moisture | 12% or below, for both eating and seed | Biting it, teeth do not sink in; it snaps and splits |
| Salt test | Put dry salt in a jar with the beans and shake | If salt sticks to the jar wall, it is still damp |
| Cooling | Bring in from the sun and let cool completely before storing | Sealing while warm causes condensation |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lentil is thin, so it dries much faster than other beans. Drying that takes soybean two or three days can finish in a single day for lentil. On the other hand, drying it too much makes it prone to cracking, so do not leave it in strong sun for long. A rhythm of laying it out in the morning and bringing it in by afternoon is suitable, and drying in a breezy shade is enough.

### 15-2. Controlling Bean Weevils During Storage

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Bean weevils are the biggest threat to stored legumes. The frightening part is that it already begins in the field. Adult weevils lay eggs on pods still hanging in the field, and those eggs hatch inside storage and bore into the seed. The eggs are already riding along when the harvest comes in, and since one generation takes less than a month, half a year is enough for a whole sack to fill up with holed-out shells. Because lentil's seed coat is thin, it is easier to bore through than other beans.

| Method | Principle | Application | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full sealing | Cuts off oxygen to suffocate them | Fill a glass jar or metal can completely and seal it | Most reliable. In 3-4 weeks even the eggs and larvae die |
| Mixing in wood ash | Fine particles scratch the waxy layer of the exoskeleton, causing dehydration | Mix in 2-5% of the bean volume | The seed is small, so the ash coats it evenly. Rinse with water before eating |
| Diatomaceous earth | Silica shells damage the exoskeleton | 1-3g per 1kg of beans | More effective than ash. Only works when dry |
| Oil coating | An oil film prevents eggs from sticking and hatching | Add 3-5mL cooking oil per 1kg of beans and rub together | The seed is small with more surface area, so it takes a bit more oil. Do not use for seed |
| Cold treatment | Eggs and larvae die below freezing | Set outside for a few days in the depth of winter, then bring back in | Watch for condensation when bringing it back in |
| Heat treatment | Eggs and larvae die at 60 degrees | Spread thin in the sun for half a day | Do not use for seed |
| Dried chili, Sichuan pepper, or mugwort | Essential oils suppress egg-laying | Layer them in | A supplementary measure |
| Charcoal or roasted salt | Absorbs moisture | Wrap in cloth and place inside | Also helps prevent mold |
| Burying in ash | Combines sealing and physical control | Layer ash and beans in a crock | Widely used traditionally |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most important thing is complete sealing. Insects breathe too. Inside a sealed container, as beans and insects both use up oxygen, oxygen falls and carbon dioxide rises, killing the insects. The beans, being dormant, tolerate this condition. The fuller the container and the less empty space, the faster the effect. Packing several small containers tightly is better than one large one, which also has the advantage that if one container spoils, you do not lose everything.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mixing in wood ash suits lentil especially well. The seed is small, giving a large surface area for the same volume, and ash works evenly into the gaps between seeds, leaving insects no room to move. Sift out the coarse charcoal chunks and mix in only the fine part, at 2-5% of the bean volume. Rinsing three or four times in water before eating makes it clean, and since beans get washed before cooking anyway, this adds almost no extra bother. Insects cannot develop resistance to it, and it is harmless to people.

### 15-3. Storage Containers and Locations

| Container | Sealing | Duration | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|:---:|---|---|---|
| Glass jar | Very good | 3 years or more | Condition can be checked by eye | Small capacity, does not block light |
| Metal can | Very good | 3 years or more | Fully blocks rats and insects | Spoils at any spot where condensation forms |
| Earthenware crock | Good | 2-3 years | Slow temperature change, rats cannot get through | Heavy and breakable |
| Thick plastic | Fair | 1-2 years | Light and cheap | Rats chew through, and the small seed leaks out |
| Underground storage pit | Good | 2-3 years | Temperature stays constant and low | If water seeps in, the whole store is lost |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most reliable senses for checking are hearing and smell. If you put your ear to the container and hear a rustling sound, insects are active, and if there is a musty smell, mold has started. Small round holes on the surface mean weevils have already gone through a generation. Even at this point, taking everything out, drying it in the sun, floating it in water to skim off empty seeds, mixing in ash, and sealing it again can save what remains. Checking once every two months is the standard.

## 16. Cooking and Processing - The Bean That Needs the Least Fuel

### 16-1. Hulling and Splitting

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The core of lentil processing is hulling it and splitting it in half. This is the form sold as red lentil in stores; once the brown lentil's hull is removed, the inside is orange, which looks red. Made into this form, cooking time drops from 20 minutes to 10, and it breaks down into a porridge in water.

| Step | Details |
|---|---|
| 1 | Briefly dip dried lentils in water to lightly dampen the surface |
| 2 | Leave it in the shade for half a day so only the hull absorbs moisture |
| 3 | Put it in a mortar and pound very lightly - just enough to loosen the hull, not to crush the seed |
| 4 | Toss it in a winnowing basket or fan it to blow away the loosened hulls |
| 5 | Pound the remaining seed lightly again, and it splits into two pieces |
| 6 | Dry completely and store sealed |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Hulled lentil stores less well. Without the protective barrier of the seed coat, insects get in easily and it also crumbles easily. So the rule is not to process it all at once, but to make only what will be eaten within a month or two. Store unhulled, process a little at a time - that is the basic principle. Do not throw away the removed hulls; collect them for livestock feed or compost.

### 16-2. Fuel-Saving Cooking

| Method | Fuel | Procedure |
|---|:---:|---|
| Hulled lentil porridge | Least | Breaks down in water in 10-15 minutes |
| Boiling unhulled | Little | 20-30 minutes. Holds its shape, used for namul or dressed salads |
| Residual-heat cooking | Very little | Boil for 5 minutes, turn off the heat, wrap in a blanket or straw box, and leave for 1 hour |
| Roasted lentil flour | Almost none | Roast, grind, and stir into water to eat |
| Sprouting | None | Once sprouted, it is ready to eat as is |
| Cooking together with grain | No additional fuel | Adding it to rice or porridge needs no separate fire |

> **Lentil's value lies in this: add it to rice while cooking, and protein reaches the table without lighting a separate fire. No other bean can do this.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This one thing decides lentil's place in a fuel-scarce situation. Mixing soybean or chickpea into rice requires soaking ahead of time and boiling separately, but lentil cooks in the same time as rice. If hulled, it actually cooks faster than rice, to the point of needing to be added later. Not lighting a separate fire every meal adds up, over a year, to saving a considerable amount of firewood.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Residual-heat cooking also works almost perfectly for lentil. Boil for just 5 minutes and put it into an insulated box, and it will be fully cooked within an hour. A wooden box packed with straw or rice husks, or a thick blanket wrapped entirely around the pot, works as that insulated box. Because lentil has no strong toxic compound that requires complete destruction, it can be safely cooked this way with residual heat.

### 16-3. Sprouting and Storage Processing

| Method | Procedure | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Sprouting | Soak 8 hours, drain, cover with a damp cloth for 2-3 days | New vitamin C forms, phytic acid decreases, and digestion becomes easier |
| Lentil flour | Roast and grind | Sealed, 3-6 months. Low fat means it keeps a long time |
| Lentil pancake | Grind soaked lentil into a batter and fry it thin | The same principle as India's dosa |
| Fermented batter | Grind and leave overnight; it ferments sour | Rises and becomes easier to digest |
| Blanching then drying | Boil, dry, and store; rehydrate in water when needed | Cuts cooking time further |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lentil is one of the easiest beans to sprout. Soak for 8 hours, drain, cover with a damp cloth, and leave for two or three days, and about 1cm of sprout appears. In this process, vitamin C forms that was not present in the dry bean, phytic acid and inhibitors decrease, and digestion becomes much easier. When there are no fresh vegetables in winter, this sprouting becomes a cheap way to prevent vitamin C deficiency. Unlike other bean sprouts, lentil sprouts need only the lightest blanching, and because the seed is small, an edible amount is ready within just a few days.

## 17. Annual Work Calendar

| Month | Task | Key Point |
|---|---|---|
| January | Check storage, germination test for seed | Much of the lentil sold in stores does not germinate. Always check |
| February | Plan the field, apply lime, turn the compost | Prepare for highland spring sowing |
| March | Highland spring sowing (from mid-month), fine leveling of the soil | The seed is small, and large soil clods lead to uneven germination |
| April | First weeding, thinning | Weed management in the first month determines the yield |
| May | Second weeding, flowering, checking nodules | Dig up a root, split a small nodule open, and check for red color |
| June | Pod filling, watching for rust, irrigation | Ensure airflow and stay out of the field after rain |
| July | Highland harvest, hang-drying, threshing | When the third pod from the bottom rattles |
| August | Cleaning, storage, late-summer sowing (end of month) | This is sowing time in the lowlands |
| September | Weeding the late-summer sowing, field cleanup | Early management is everything |
| October | Flowering and pod filling (late-summer sowing), overwintering sowing in southern South Korea | Splitting into three planting schedules spreads the risk |
| November | Harvest the late-summer sowing, drying, storage | If frost gets hard, harvest even if underripe |
| December | Processing (hulling and splitting), sprouted greens, planning next year | Process only as much as will be eaten within a month or two |

### 17-1. Five Things Not to Forget

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Seed check | Store-bought red lentil is split and does not germinate. Always run a germination test before sowing |
| Planting schedule | The window is narrow in South Korea's climate. Try splitting sowing across highland spring, late summer, and southern overwintering |
| Weeds | The legume most vulnerable to weeds. The first month is everything |
| Fuel | Add it to rice and no separate fire is needed. This is lentil's greatest value |
| Harvest timing | When the third pod from the bottom rattles. Waiting for the top loses the bottom |
