## Introduction

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lentil (Lens culinaris) is a bean named, quite literally, for its resemblance to a lens. The seed's convex disc shape came first, and the optical lens later took its name from that shape. Lentils originated in the Fertile Crescent of Southwest Asia and are among the oldest crops ever domesticated, alongside wheat, barley, and peas. They remain a daily staple protein source in India, Turkey, the Middle East, and Ethiopia.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;From a disaster-preparedness standpoint, lentils have one advantage no other bean can match: cooking them takes almost no fuel. Dried lentils need no soaking, and split, hulled red lentils turn into a porridge-like mash after just 10-15 minutes in water. That is an entirely different order of magnitude from soybeans, which need 8 hours of soaking and an hour of boiling. A Cooking & processing grade of C is the highest value among legumes, and this single line decides where lentils stand.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Added to this is a Water use grade of S. Total evapotranspiration of 250mm is among the lowest of the legumes, less than half that of soybeans. A bean that needs little fuel and little water - that is the lentil. It also carries a Protein density of S, Micronutrients of S, Storability of SS, and Cold hardiness of A. Dried lentils contain 22.64g of protein per 100g, 24.8% of calories.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Yet the lentil's Overall grade is C, 41.9 points. There are two reasons. First, Calories per area is D. At 150kg per 1,000m², or 548kcal per m², it comes to only 0.21× the self-sufficiency line, one of the lowest in this table. This is because the seeds are small and a pod holds only one or two of them. Second, Prevalence in South Korea is C. It is only trial-grown in one or two provinces, and seed is practically hard to obtain anywhere else.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This second problem should not be brushed aside. When distribution breaks down, seed you do not already have is as good as nonexistent. Imported lentils sold for eating are common in stores, but most have been hulled or heat-treated and will not germinate. Setting aside plantable seed separately and building up its stock over several years of seed saving is a precondition for including this crop in a plan at all.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There is also a misconception common to legume crops as a whole: that planting beans improves the soil. That is only half true. Lentils, too, live in symbiosis with root-nodule bacteria and draw nitrogen from the air, but if the seed is harvested and hauled off the field, more nitrogen can leave the field than was fixed. Section 5 works through that fork in the road with numbers.
