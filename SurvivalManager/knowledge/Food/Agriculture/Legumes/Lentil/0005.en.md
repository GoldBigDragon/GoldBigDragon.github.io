## 8. Crop Management

### 8-1. Weed Management Is Almost Everything

| Timing | Task | Key Point |
|---|---|---|
| 2 weeks after emergence | First weeding | Pull weeds while they are still thread-thin |
| 4-5 weeks after emergence | Second weeding | This is the last one |
| After flowering | Stay out of the field | The stems are thin and break if stepped on |

> **At 40cm tall with thin stems, lentil is the legume most vulnerable to weeds. Its growing season is short, so once it is smothered, there is no time to recover. Weeding in the first month largely determines the yield.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The trick to weeding is shallow and early. Lentil's fine roots spread out shallowly near the surface, so digging a hoe in deep cuts the roots. Lay the hoe flat and scrape only the top 1-2cm of soil to lift weeds out, and pull weeds close to the plant by hand. Also, pulling weeds after they have grown noticeably is already too late, because pulling them then drags the lentil's roots out along with them.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Planting densely to cover the ground quickly is also a countermeasure against weeds. Once the lentil plants touch each other and shade the ground, weeds do not increase much after that. This is one reason for setting the sowing density somewhat higher. Straw mulch also helps, but because the seed is small and sprouts have trouble pushing through straw, apply it only after germination is finished.

### 8-2. Lodging Management

| Cause | Response |
|---|---|
| Excess nitrogen | Stick to the fertilizer rate. This is the fundamental fix |
| Too sparse a planting | Plant densely so plants lean on each other |
| Wind and rain | Tie a string around the field's perimeter at knee height |
| Weight of the pods | If ripening is near, there is little to be done. Consider an early harvest |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lentil does not need stakes. It is short, and plants hook tendrils onto each other for mutual support. However, if it lodges, the lower pods touch the soil and rot, making harvest difficult, so in a spot with frequent wind and rain, drive stakes around the field's perimeter and run a single strand of string at knee height. If it has already lodged, do not force it back up; it is better to prop it up slightly off the ground with tree branches.

## 9. Pests and Diseases

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lentil's Pest & disease pressure grade is C. Actual damage is concentrated in two fungal diseases, rust and wilt, with the rest relatively minor. When lentil is in humid conditions, these two diseases come together, and under dry conditions, things are generally quiet.

### 9-1. Major Diseases

| Disease | Symptoms | Conditions for Onset | Response Without Chemicals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rust | Reddish-brown powdery pustules appear on leaves and stems; once it spreads, leaves dry up and drop | When humid and airflow is poor. Lentil's biggest disease | Ensure airflow, remove diseased leaves, select resistant plants. Burn the residue |
| Wilt | During flowering, the lower leaves wilt first and the whole plant dries up and dies | Soil-borne. Survives in the soil for several years | A rotation of 3 years or more is the only defense. Save seed from surviving plants |
| Root rot | The base of the stem and roots turn brown and soft | Excess moisture | Drainage, raising the bed |
| Gray mold | Pods and stems are covered with gray mold | When rain continues during ripening | Raise the bed, use straw mulch to keep soil from splashing, harvest early |
| Anthracnose | Black spots form on leaves and stems | High humidity | Seed selection, rotation, airflow |

> **Rust is a disease found wherever lentils are grown, and resistance is inherited. Selecting and carrying forward the plants that hold out to the end in a field where the disease is going around is the most reliable defense without chemicals.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Rust progresses fast. From the stage where a little reddish-brown powder first shows on the leaves, the whole field can turn a rusty color within days. Once it spreads there is no way to reverse it, so prevention is everything. Leave generous row spacing to ensure airflow, go easy on nitrogen so growth does not get too lush, and stay out of the field after rain. If it shows on only a few plants early on, pull those plants out whole and burn them to slow the spread.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Wilt disease has a different character. It survives in the soil as dormant spores for years and wakes up when lentil is planted again. There is no chemical for it and no way to wash it out. Rotation of 3 years or more is the only defense, and the second is selecting and carrying forward plants that survived to the end and set seed in that field.

### 9-2. Major Pests

| Pest | Damage | Control Without Chemicals |
|---|---|---|
| Aphids | Attach to new shoots and flower stalks, suck sap, and spread viruses | Spray them off with a strong jet of water. Soapy water (5g soap per 1L water). Protect ladybugs |
| Lentil weevil | Lays eggs in pods in the field and bores into the seed during storage | Fully drying and sealed storage is key. See Section 15 |
| Cutworm | Cuts young stems off at the base at night | Digging the soil around a cut plant in the morning finds the larva |
| Ants | Carry off sown seed | Coat the seed with wood ash |
| Birds | Eat young sprouts and ripening seed | Cover with straw or netting until germination |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Because the lentil plant is small with thin leaves, the proportion of damage is large once a pest takes hold. Losing a few leaves on a soybean plant and losing the same area on a lentil plant mean very different things. However, the growing season is short, so harvest often finishes before pest populations can grow very large, and this is lentil's real practical defense.

### 9-3. Control Materials Without Chemicals

| Material | How to Make | Target | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bordeaux mixture | Dissolve 400g copper sulfate and 400g quicklime separately in 100L water, then combine | Rust, fungal diseases | Requires access to copper. Use immediately after mixing |
| Soapy water | 5g unscented soap per 1L water | Aphids, mites | The leaves are thin and get damaged if used too concentrated. Keep it dilute |
| Spraying with a strong jet of water | As described | Aphids | The easiest method for lentil |
| Dusting with dry ash | Dust fine ash onto the leaves on a dewy morning | Suppresses mold, repels ants | Washes off in the rain |
| Wood ash water | Soak 1 doe (about 1.8 L) of ash in 10L water overnight and use the top liquid | Suppresses mold | Alkaline, so use it diluted |
| Wood vinegar | Dilute the liquid produced when making charcoal 300-500 fold | Repellent, growth promotion | The concentrate is strongly acidic, so always dilute it |

### 9-4. Cultural Control

| Method | Principle |
|---|---|
| Ensure airflow | Rust worsens the longer leaves stay wet. Leave generous row spacing |
| Limit nitrogen | Lush growth traps humidity inside and triggers rust outbreaks |
| Rotation of 3 years or more | Waits for wilt spores in the soil to decline |
| Straw mulch | Keeps raindrops from splashing soil onto lower pods and starting mold |
| Drainage | Most lentil diseases are related to excess moisture |
| Seed selection | Anthracnose and other diseases spread through seed |
| No entering the field after rain | A person brushing against wet leaves carries spores along |
| Residue management | Burn only diseased residue and return healthy residue to the field |
| Save seed from surviving plants | Resistance to rust and wilt is inherited |

## 10. Nutrient Deficiency and Excess

| Symptom | Cause | Natural-Material Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Leaves overall pale yellow, plant small | Nitrogen deficiency. Poor nodulation is the most common cause in lentil | First dig up a root and check the nodules. If there are none and it is still early, apply a very small amount of urine diluted tenfold |
| Leaf edges dry brown | Potassium deficiency | Scatter wood ash between plants and lightly rake it in |
| Growth is slow and leaves take on a purplish tint | Phosphorus deficiency. Lentil has a high phosphorus demand | Bone meal, ground burnt fish bones |
| Flowers open but pods fail to set | Boron deficiency, or heat during flowering | Compost mixed from several materials. If heat is the cause, adjust the sowing time next year |
| Yellowing between leaf veins | Iron or manganese deficiency. Common in soil leaning alkaline | Stop using ash. Increase organic matter with leaf mold and well-rotted compost |
| Only stems and leaves grow lush, few pods | Nitrogen excess | Stop the nitrogen supply immediately |
| Growth is poor overall | Strongly acidic soil | Lime, or ground burnt shells |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For lentil, when a deficiency is noticed matters a great deal. With only a 100-day growing season, noticing it after flowering has begun means nothing you do will help that year. A practical habit is to examine the field closely once, between 3 and 4 weeks after sowing, checking leaf color and size. If growth is poor at that point, a small amount of nitrogen gets it through the early stage; if it is normal, leave it alone after that.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Micronutrient deficiencies are hard to address one at a time. Without chemical fertilizer, there is practically no way to obtain molybdenum or cobalt. Instead, compost rotted from a mix of vegetable scraps, fruit peels, eggshells, fish bones, fallen leaves, weeds, and ash carries a broad spread of micronutrients. The principle of putting everything into the compost heap is the practical strategy for managing micronutrients.

## 11. Intercropping and Crop Rotation

### 11-1. Compatibility

| Compatibility | Crop | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Good | Barley, wheat, oat | Planted between grain rows, they support each other and share nitrogen |
| Good | Potato | Diseases do not overlap, and lentil is harvested first |
| Good | Napa cabbage and radish family | Uses the nitrogen lentil leaves behind afterward |
| Good | Marigold | Root exudates suppress nematodes |
| Fair | Leafy vegetables | They grow together early on, but lentil is weak against weeds and loses out in competition |
| Bad | Garlic and onion family | There are reports that their exudates suppress root-nodule bacteria |
| Bad | Other legumes | Pathogens and nematodes accumulate |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mixed planting of lentil and grain is a method long used in its native range. Planting lentil between rows of barley or wheat gives the lentil the grain's upright stems to lean on, and part of the nitrogen lentil fixes flows to the grain. On top of that, the two crops' harvest times are similar, so they can be cut together, threshed together, and then separated with a sieve and wind. Lentil is much smaller and heavier than grain, making sorting easy. However, because lentil's yield drops by half at 40% shade, rows must be spaced generously so it does not sit deep in the grain's shadow.

### 11-2. Crop Rotation

| Item | Standard |
|---|---|
| Break period | 3 years or more. Wilt disease remains in the soil |
| Ideal cycle | 4 years |
| Continuous-cropping damage | Wilt and root rot accumulate |

| Example of a 4-Year Rotation |
|---|
| Year 1: Lentil |
| Year 2: Grain (barley, wheat, oat) |
| Year 3: Potato or sweet potato |
| Year 4: Napa cabbage and radish family, or green manure |
| Back to lentil |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Here is a compromise for when a field is too small to allow rotation. Because lentil is a small plant, the area it needs is also small. Simply moving a narrow spot in the corner of the field from year to year already counts as rotation. If the main goal is keeping the seed line going, a few m² is enough, and an area that size can be shifted to a new spot every year in any field.

## 12. Harvest and Threshing

### 12-1. Judging Harvest Time

| Criterion | Details |
|---|---|
| Pods | When the third pod from the bottom rattles with the sound of seeds knocking when shaken |
| Leaves | The lower leaves turn yellow and begin to drop |
| Overall color | Two-thirds of the plant has turned brown |
| Seed | Biting it, teeth do not sink in; it snaps and splits |
| Timing | 100-110 days after sowing |

> **Lentil ripens from the bottom up, and ripe pods shatter easily. Waiting for the top to fully ripen loses the bottom. The point when the third pod from the bottom rattles is the compromise.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lentil shatters faster than other legumes. Its pods are flat and thin, so they twist open easily as they dry, and especially if a dry pod gets rained on and dries out again repeatedly, it bursts open in no time. Check the field daily during ripening, and if rain is forecast, it is better to harvest even if slightly underripe. Once cut and finished drying in the shade, the seed will ripen somewhat further.

### 12-2. Harvest Technique

| Step | Details |
|---|---|
| 1 | Do it on a clear, dry day, after 10am once the dew has dried |
| 2 | Cut the stems right above the ground. The pods hang low |
| 3 | As you cut, lay the plants straight onto cloth or a mat to catch falling seeds |
| 4 | Tie into bundles and dry in the sun for 4-6 days, or hang under the eaves |
| 5 | Thresh once the pods are fully dry and rattle when shaken |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Catching seeds as they fall during cutting is the trick to harvesting lentil. The pods are thin and burst easily, so even the vibration of a sickle brushing past pops seeds out. Laying cloth or a mat over the field and moving cut bundles onto it noticeably reduces loss. Cutting early in the morning while some dew remains bursts fewer pods, but if left piled up wet, mold develops, so spread it out to dry right after cutting.

### 12-3. Threshing - Methods Without Tools

| Method | Tool | Procedure | Efficiency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treading in a sack | A thick burlap sack or bag | Put the dry stalks in the sack, tie the opening, and tread on it with your feet | Most convenient for small amounts. Small seeds do not fly off |
| Flail | A tool with a swinging stick attached to a long handle | Spread a mat, lay the stalks out thin, and strike down from above | Suited to a medium scale |
| Rubbing with the feet | None | Spread on a mat and grind with shod feet in a twisting motion | Lentil's pods are weak, so this alone releases the seed well |
| Beating with a stick | A wooden stick | Put it in a sack or spread on a mat and beat it | A substitute when there is no flail |
| Rubbing by hand | None | Rub dry bundles between both hands | Suited to very small amounts, for seed |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lentil is the easiest of the legumes to thresh. Its pods are thin and weak, so if well dried, almost all the seed comes loose just by putting it in a sack and treading on it. However, because the seed is small, it easily slips out through gaps in a loosely woven sack or over the edge of a mat, so use a tightly woven sack and wall off the edges of the mat with cloth. Small seed is an advantage for threshing but a disadvantage for recovery.

### 12-4. Cleaning - Sorting with Wind

| Method | Principle | Procedure |
|---|---|---|
| Winnowing in the wind | The seed is heavy while hulls and debris are light | On a windy day, hold a winnowing basket or wide bowl high and pour it out slowly |
| Winnowing basket toss | Shake up and down while tossing forward | Heavy seeds stay at the back while light material gets pushed forward |
| Fanning | One person pours while another fans air with a fan | Used on a windless day |
| Sieving | Separates by hole size | The seed is small, so a fine sieve is needed. Separates out soil and fine debris |
| Floating in water | Empty husks and insect-damaged seed are light | Pour into a bucket of water and skim off what floats. Dry it again the same day |
| Rolling down an inclined board | Being a convex disc, it rolls well | Set a board at an angle and roll the seed down it |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There are three tricks to winnowing in the wind. Choose early morning or dusk when the wind blows steadily, pour it out in a thin stream rather than all at once, and repeat two or three times. Lentil seed is small and light, so if the wind is strong, even the seed blows away. It should be done in a lighter wind than other beans need, and if fanning, fanning gently is safer. Test with just a little at first to judge the wind's strength before doing it in earnest.
