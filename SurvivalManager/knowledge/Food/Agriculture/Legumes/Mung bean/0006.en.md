## 13. Seed Saving

| Item | Method |
|---|---|
| Selecting plants for seed | Mark in the field beforehand the plants whose pods ripen all at once, do not burst readily even after turning black, and have not lodged |
| Harvest timing | Pick right after the pods have turned completely black |
| Separation | Thresh separately. Rubbing by hand does not injure the seeds |
| Sorting | Discard every seed with insect holes, discoloration, wrinkles, or small size |
| Drying | Moisture 12% or less. Spread thinly in the sun to dry |
| Storage | Put in an airtight container and keep in a cool, dark place. Use a container separate from the one for food |
| Storage period | 3-4 years airtight at room temperature. With good care, germination ability lasts up to 5-6 years |

> **The most valuable trait in saving mung bean seed is not yield but uniform ripening. When there is nobody to go in three or four times to pick, a single line that ripens all at once can double the harvest.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Next in importance is a pod that does not burst readily. If you see pods that stay hanging without bursting even several days after turning black, mark that plant and harvest it separately. This trait is heritable, so repeating the selection for just a few years produces a line that loses little even if the harvest time is missed by a little. Traditional mung beans keep their tendency to burst easily because that is how they scatter seed in the wild, but when people keep selecting in the opposite direction, that changes.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mung bean is a self-pollinating crop, so there is no need to worry about isolation distance. Even when several lines are planted side by side, their traits do not mix. However, if adzuki bean and cowpea are nearby, keep harvesting and storage clearly separate so the seeds do not get mixed.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Long seed life is also a big advantage. Airtight at room temperature, seed keeps its germination ability for 3-4 years, and with good care for 5-6 years. There is no need to save seed every year, and even if one year's crop is ruined, you can start the next year with old seed. However, always run a germination test on old seed first, and if the germination rate is half, compensate by doubling the sowing rate.

| Seed storage period and germination rate |
|---|
| Year of harvest: 95% or more |
| After 1 year: 90% or more |
| After 2 years: 80-90% |
| After 3 years: 70-80% |
| After 4-5 years: 50-70% |
| After 6 years: drops sharply |

## 14. Toxicity and Antinutritional Compounds

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mung bean is among the legumes with the lowest toxicity risk. It has neither the strong lectin of kidney bean, which can cause poisoning from just a few raw beans, nor the compound of fava bean that causes acute hemolysis in certain constitutions. The fact that mung bean sprouts (sukju namul) can be eaten raw without causing illness is the proof.

| Compound | What it does | Symptoms | How to remove it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trypsin inhibitor | Blocks the action of protein-digesting enzymes | Abdominal discomfort, lower protein utilization | Soak for 4 hours, then boil for 30 minutes to 1 hour. Content is far lower than in soybean |
| Lectin | Binds to the intestinal lining and interferes with absorption | Harmless in small amounts | Easily destroyed by heating. Unlike kidney beans, its toxicity is very weak |
| Phytic acid | Binds iron, zinc, and calcium and blocks their absorption | Anemia and zinc deficiency with long-term consumption | Reduced by 60-80% by soaking and sprouting |
| Oligosaccharides | Humans cannot digest them, and gut bacteria ferment them | Gas in the belly | Content is on the low side among legumes. Discarding the soaking water reduces it further |
| Mold toxins | Produced by mold during storage | Dangerous | Moldy beans do not become safe by washing or cooking. Throw them away |

> **What actually harms people in mung bean is not the bean itself but beans that were stored badly and have gone moldy. If there is a musty smell or a bluish powder on the surface, throw them away without regret.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mung bean is known as an easily digested bean that is gentle on the stomach. This is because it has few oligosaccharides, a weak lectin, and soft tissue. It is among the safest legumes to feed to the sick, the elderly, and children, and the traditional use of mung bean porridge as food for the sick, on the belief that it brings down fever and detoxifies the body, is not unrelated to this property.

| Treatment | Time / temperature | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Soaking | 4 hours in room-temperature water | Softens the skin and shortens cooking time |
| Discarding the soaking water | Always | Throws away the oligosaccharides and antinutrients that leached into the water |
| Boiling | 30 minutes to 1 hour in boiling water | Split, skinned beans need only 20 minutes |
| Skinning and splitting | Soak, remove the skins, and split in half | Cooking time drops to half or less |
| Sprouting (mung bean sprouts) | 3-5 days | Antinutrients decrease and vitamin C is produced |
| Checking doneness | Easily mashed when pressed with a finger | If a hard core remains, it is underdone |

## 15. Storage Without Refrigeration

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mung bean's Storability is SS. Dry seeds last 3 years at room temperature, and as seed up to 5-6 years. At 1% fat it has the least of any legume, so rancidity is almost nonexistent. However, the adzuki bean weevil is the greatest threat to stored mung bean, and because the seeds are small, once a seed is bored through, its inside is quickly emptied.

> **Beans are lost not to rot but to being eaten by insects. The art of storage is the art of keeping insects out.**

### 15-1. Drying Comes First

| Step | Method | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Preliminary drying | Put the picked pods in a net and dry in the sun for 2-3 days | About half of them burst open by themselves |
| Drying after threshing | Spread thinly on a straw mat (meongseok) or a woven mat and dry in the sun for 1-2 days | The seeds are small and dry quickly |
| Target moisture | 12% or less for both food and seed | Bitten with the teeth, the bean splits with a crack instead of letting the teeth sink in |
| Salt test | Put dry salt in a jar with the mung beans and shake | If the salt sticks to the wall of the jar, the beans are still damp |
| Cooling | After bringing them in from the sun, let them cool completely before packing | Sealing them while warm causes condensation |

### 15-2. Controlling Bean Weevils in Storage

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The adzuki bean weevil (Callosobruchus chinensis) is the greatest threat to stored mung bean. What makes it frightening is that it starts in the field. Adults glue their eggs onto pods still hanging in the field, and those eggs hatch inside the storeroom and bore into the beans. Eggs come in along with the beans, and since one generation takes less than a month, within half a year one sack is filled with holed husks and powder.

| Method | Principle | Application | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complete sealing | Cuts off oxygen and suffocates them | Fill glass jars or metal tins to the brim and seal | Most reliable. Within 3-4 weeks even eggs and larvae die |
| Hot-water treatment | Kills eggs and larvae | Dip briefly in boiling water, cool in cold water, and dry again in the sun | A traditional method. Not used for seed |
| Mixing with wood ash | Fine particles scratch the wax layer of the exoskeleton and dehydrate the insect | Mix in 2-5% of the volume of the mung beans | Seeds are small, so the ash coats them evenly. Rinse with water before eating |
| Diatomaceous earth | Silica shells damage the exoskeleton | 1-3g per 1kg of mung beans | More effective than ash. Works only when dry |
| Oil coating | The oil film prevents eggs from attaching and hatching | Add 3-5mL of cooking oil per 1kg of mung beans and rub it in | Seeds are small and have a large surface area, so slightly more oil is needed. Not used for seed |
| Cold treatment | Eggs and larvae die below freezing | Put outside for a few days in midwinter, then bring back in | Beware of condensation when bringing back in |
| Heat treatment | Eggs and larvae die at 60°C | Spread thinly in the sun for half a day | Not used for seed |
| Dried chili pepper, sancho (Zanthoxylum pepper), mugwort | Essential oils inhibit egg laying | Layer them in | A supplementary measure |
| Charcoal, roasted salt | Absorb moisture | Wrap in cloth and put in | Doubles as mold prevention |
| Burying in ash | Gains sealing and physical control together | Layer ash and mung beans in a jar | Widely used traditionally |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most important is complete sealing. Insects breathe too. When beans and insects use up the oxygen in a sealed container, oxygen drops and carbon dioxide rises, and the insects die. Beans are dormant and tolerate these conditions. The fuller the container and the less empty space, the faster the effect. It is better to pack tightly into several small containers than into one big one, and this also has the advantage of not losing everything if one container goes bad.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Hot-water treatment is a method traditionally used for mung bean and adzuki bean. Dip briefly in boiling water, cool immediately in cold water, and dry thoroughly in the sun again. Eggs and young larvae on the surface and in shallow spots die. However, this treatment destroys germination ability, so use it only on the portion to be eaten. Seed is handled with cold treatment or sealing. Sift the wood ash through a sieve, pick out only the fine part, and mix in 2 to 5% of the volume. Because mung bean seeds are small, ash works its way evenly in between them and does better than on other beans, and since mung beans are washed before cooking anyway, the extra trouble is small.

### 15-3. Storage Containers and Places

| Container | Airtightness | Duration | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|:---:|---|---|---|
| Glass jar | Very good | 3 years or more | The condition can be checked by eye | Small capacity and does not block light |
| Metal tin | Very good | 3 years or more | Completely keeps out rats and insects | Where condensation forms, spoilage sets in |
| Earthenware jar | Good | 2-3 years | Temperature changes slowly and rats cannot gnaw through | Heavy and breakable |
| Thick plastic bag | Fair | 1-2 years | Light and cheap | Rats gnaw through and the small seeds leak out |
| Underground storage pit | Good | 2-3 years | Temperature is constant and low | If water seeps in, the whole lot is lost |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most reliable senses for inspection are hearing and smell. If you put your ear to the container and hear a rustling sound, insects are active, and a musty smell means mold has begun. Small round holes on the surface mean the weevils have already gone through a generation. Even then, taking all of the beans out, drying them in the sun, floating them in water to skim off the empty seeds, mixing in ash, and sealing them again can save what is left. The standard is an inspection once every two months.

## 16. Cooking and Processing - Mung Bean Sprouts

### 16-1. Why Mung Bean Sprouts Matter

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Dry mung beans contain practically no vitamin C. But once they sprout, vitamin C that was not there is produced. The reason is that the physiological shift of germination takes place and the plant synthesizes it anew. In winter, when there are no fresh vegetables at all, a handful of dry mung beans, water, and a dark spot are enough to get fresh vegetables in three to five days. No field, no sunlight, and no soil are needed.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Vitamin C deficiency advances quietly over months and then shows as bleeding gums, wounds that do not heal, and loss of energy. It is the disease that sailors suffered in the Age of Discovery, and it recurred in societies that got through long winters on stored food alone. Mung bean sprouts are a cheap and reliable answer to this problem. The principle is the same as kongnamul (soybean sprouts), which are grown from soybeans, but mung bean sprouts grow faster and have a milder taste.

> **100g of dry mung beans becomes 500-700g of sprouts. Most of the added weight is water, so calories do not increase, but vitamin C that was not there appears, phytic acid and inhibitors decrease, and part of the protein is broken down into a more digestible form.**

### 16-2. How to Grow Mung Bean Sprouts

| Step | Details | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Choosing mung beans | Small-seeded types are best. Discard every broken or insect-eaten seed | Broken seeds rot and spoil the whole batch |
| 2. Washing | Rinse several times and skim off what floats | Floating seeds are usually dead seeds |
| 3. Soaking | Soak in lukewarm water for 6-8 hours | The mung beans swell to twice their size and the seed coats begin to split |
| 4. Filling the container | Put them in a container with drainage or in a siru (a perforated earthenware sprouting pot) and cover with a wet cloth | Standing water causes rot |
| 5. Keeping dark | Block out light completely. 20-25°C is best | If light gets in, they turn green and become tough and bitter |
| 6. Watering | Pour water generously from above 3-4 times a day and let it run through | Serves both temperature control and removal of waste |
| 7. Pressing down | Put a light board or stone on top | The stems become thick and plump |
| 8. Harvest | 3-5 days, length 4-7cm | Harvest before the roots turn brown |

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Light gets in | The heads turn green, the stems get tough, and a bitter taste develops |
| Too little watering | Many fine roots grow and the sprouts get tough |
| Standing water | Rot starts from the bottom and a sour smell appears |
| Temperature above 30°C | They grow fast but are soft and spoil quickly |
| Temperature below 15°C | They grow very slowly |
| A heavy weight on top | The stems become thick and plump. This is why commercial mung bean sprouts are thick |
| Skins not removed | The shed green skins rot and smell |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Watering is nearly everything in growing sprouts. Water supplies moisture, cools the heat produced during germination, and washes away accumulated waste. Pour generously from above three to four times a day and let it drain completely; increase the frequency in summer and use lukewarm water in winter. Many fine roots are a sign that water is short.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;As mung bean sprouts grow, the green seed coats come loose and float up. These have to be skimmed off at every watering. Left alone, they rot, smell, and spoil the whole batch. When pouring the water, just scoop out the husks floating to the top by hand or with a sieve.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Without a siru, use a jar or tub with holes punched in the bottom, a tightly woven tray, or a basin with holes. With a water catcher underneath and a black cloth over the top, that is a siru. Only two things matter: a structure that drains water and a way to block light. In winter, find a spot that stays around 20°C, such as beside the kitchen hearth (agungi) or the cooler far end of an ondol (floor-heated) room.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mung bean sprouts are safe to eat raw, but blanching or stir-frying them is better. The trace of remaining inhibitors decreases, the volume shrinks, and you can eat more at one time. To blanch, put them briefly in boiling water, lift them out, and rinse in cold water. Blanching for long makes them soft and also washes out the vitamin C.

### 16-3. Other Cooking and Processing

| Processing | Procedure summary | Keeping | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mung bean porridge | Boil soaked mung beans, mash them, and cook together with rice | Same day | Easy to digest, so used as food for the sick |
| Mung bean starch (for cheongpomuk, a starch jelly) | Soak, grind, let settle, and dry the sediment | Months as starch powder | Low fat, so the starch sets well |
| Bindaetteok (mung bean pancake) | Grind soaked mung beans and pan-fry | Same day | Can be ground with the skins on |
| Skinned, split mung beans | Soak, remove the skins, and split | Airtight 1-2 months | Cooking time is halved |
| Roasted mung bean flour | Roast and pound | Airtight 3-6 months | No cooking fuel needed |
| Mung bean skins | The skins left after extracting the starch | Dried | Used as livestock feed and compost |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mung bean starch has unexpected value in disaster conditions. When soaked mung beans are ground and stirred into water, the heavy starch settles and protein-bearing water floats on top. Drying this sediment gives pure starch powder, and mixed with water and boiled it becomes cheongpomuk. Starch keeps longer than grain flour, and the protein left in the floating water is collected separately and pan-fried. In effect, one ingredient yields storable starch and an instant protein food at the same time. This is possible because mung bean has an extremely low fat content of 1%.

## 17. Annual Work Calendar

| Month | Work | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| January | Check stored beans, germination test of seed, growing mung bean sprouts | Winter vitamin C comes from the sprouts |
| February | Plan the field, turn the compost, decide the layout with the preceding crop | The basic approach is to place mung bean as a following crop |
| March | Spread lime, tend the preceding crop | Apply lime 3-4 weeks before sowing |
| April | Sow the first crop of double cropping in southern South Korea (late April), prepare mulching materials | The first crop risks having its grain filling coincide with the monsoon season (jangma, late June to July in South Korea) |
| May | Prepare to harvest the preceding crop, put the raised beds and drainage channels in order | Beds of at least 20cm are a prerequisite for growing mung bean |
| June | Main sowing (early June in central South Korea, mid-to-late June in southern South Korea), mulching, bird protection | Plant right away in the spot where the preceding crop was harvested |
| July | Thinning, weeding, staking, last window for late sowing | Finish weeding before flowering. If another crop has failed, replant by this time |
| August | Flowering and pod setting, aphid control, check drainage channels, check nodules | If the field is flooded by the monsoon rains, the nodules die first |
| September | Start staged harvesting, dry in nets | Pick black pods the same day or the next day |
| October | Finish harvest, thresh, clean, return stalks and leaves | Lay the remaining stalks down on the spot or dig them in |
| November | Drying, storage, processing, tend autumn crops | The napa cabbage planted in the mung bean spot uses the leftover nitrogen |
| December | Check storage, grow mung bean sprouts, plan next year | Put your ear to the container and listen for insects |

### 17-1. Five Things Not to Forget

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Drainage | The weakest of the legumes against waterlogging, and its growing season overlaps with the monsoon. Beds of 20cm are a prerequisite |
| Staged harvesting | Black pods burst within days. Pick three or four times at intervals of 5-7 days |
| Late sowing | Almost the only grain crop that can still be planted up to mid-to-late July in South Korea. Keep seed in reserve for recovery from failures |
| Mung bean sprouts | The answer to winter vitamin C. Always set aside some small-seeded beans separately |
| Stalks and leaves | If the stalks left after picking the pods are not returned to the field, mung bean becomes a crop that robs the soil |
