## 2. What Kind of Plant Is This?

### 2-1. Two Plants, One Name

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The two plants grouped under the name kkae are rather distant from each other in classification.

| Item | Sesame | Perilla |
|---|---|---|
| Scientific name | Sesamum indicum | Perilla frutescens |
| Family | Sesame family (Pedaliaceae) | Mint family (Lamiaceae) |
| Origin | Dry regions from Africa to India | East Asia |
| Main use | Sesame oil from the seeds | Perilla oil from the seeds; the leaves are kkaennip (perilla leaves) |
| Properties of the oil | Goes rancid slowly. A semi-drying oil | Goes rancid quickly. A drying oil |
| Leaves | Narrow and lance-shaped, with hairs. They can be eaten, but this is not the main use | Broad and heart-shaped, with serrated edges. Used mainly as a vegetable |
| Flowering (in South Korea) | July-August | August-September |
| Waterlogging | Extremely weak | Fairly tolerant |
| Drought | Very tolerant | Moderate |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Both belong to the same order, Lamiales, and share square stems and leaves that grow in opposite pairs. In cultivation, however, they have to be treated as different crops. Sesame is given a dry, sunny spot, and perilla a spot that can be a little damp. Sesame needs a break of 3-4 years, whereas perilla is not as demanding.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;One more distinction has to be made here. Perilla divides into oil perilla, grown for seed, and leaf perilla, grown for leaves. The varieties differ, and so do the cultivation methods. The leaves of oil perilla are tough and hard to use as ssam (food wrapped in leaves), while leaf perilla gives a small seed yield. You have to decide on the purpose first and choose a variety to match.

### 2-2. What Sesame Looks Like

| Organ | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| Leaves | Narrow and lance-shaped. Broad at the base and narrowing toward the top. The surface has fine hairs, and the leaves are thick |
| Stem | Square and upright. Height 60-150cm. There are lines that grow as a single stem and lines that branch |
| Roots | A straight taproot goes down deep. Strong at finding water in dry ground |
| Flowers | White or pale pink, tube-shaped. They open in sequence from bottom to top, at each leaf axil |
| Fruit | A capsule. Cylindrical, 2-3cm long, borne at each leaf axil. When ripe, the suture at the top splits open |
| Seeds | Flat, teardrop-shaped, around 3mm long. There are white, yellow, brown and black lines |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The fine hairs and the thickness of sesame leaves are among the structures that give this crop its drought tolerance. The hairs trap a layer of air on the leaf surface and keep wind from touching it directly, and the thick leaves store water. This is a build commonly seen in plants of deserts and dry lands.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most important structure is the capsule. When a sesame fruit ripens, its upper end splits lengthwise and opens. If the stem is touched in that state, the seeds spill out. This is called dehiscence, and it is the normal way for wild plants to disperse their seeds. Cultivated varieties have kept this trait almost unchanged. The harvesting method in Section 12 comes entirely from this one fact.

> **It is normal for sesame capsules to split open when ripe. Harvesting means getting one step ahead of this splitting, and if you are late, everything spills onto the field.**

### 2-3. The Trait of Indeterminate Inflorescence

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Another important trait of sesame is that its flowers do not open all at once. Flowers open first at the lower leaf axils of the stem and then in sequence as one moves up. As long as the growing point at the tip of the stem keeps growing, flowers keep opening. This is called an indeterminate inflorescence.

| Consequence | What Happens |
|---|---|
| The flowering period is long | Flowers open over 30-40 days |
| Ripening is uneven | When the lower capsules are fully ripe, the upper capsules are still green |
| The harvest moment is ambiguous | If you wait for the upper ones, the lower ones spill; if you time it to the lower ones, the upper ones are not fully filled |
| Resources are dispersed | The plant shares its resources even with late-opening flowers, so all the seeds end up smaller |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The way to solve this problem is pinching. When the tip of the stem is cut off, no more flowers open above that point, and the plant's resources are channeled to the capsules already set. As a result the seeds grow larger and the ripening time is concentrated, which makes harvesting easier. The moment for this is 35-40 days after the first flower opens, and it is covered in detail in Section 8.

### 2-4. What Perilla Looks Like

| Organ | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| Leaves | Broad and heart-shaped with serrated edges. Fine hairs on both sides, and a distinctive aroma |
| Stem | Square and upright. Height 60-150cm |
| Roots | A straight root with many fine rootlets |
| Flowers | Small white flowers gather in a spike. They bloom in August-September in South Korea |
| Fruit | A small capsule holding four seeds each |
| Seeds | Round grains 1.5-2mm in diameter. Grayish brown to black |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The aroma of perilla leaves comes from a compound called perillaldehyde. People find this aroma pleasant or unpleasant depending on the person, and the caveat "off-putting smell and aroma" attached to the D for Taste in the evaluation table refers to this. The same compound also serves to drive off insects and animals, and it forms the basis for the C in Non-food value. Perilla also contains an antioxidant compound called rosmarinic acid.

### 2-5. Growth Stages

| Stage | Sesame | Perilla (for leaves) | Key Nutrients |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germination and seedling stage | 3 weeks from sowing | 3 weeks from sowing | Phosphorus, a small amount of nitrogen |
| Vegetative growth stage | Weeks 3 to 8 | Weeks 3 to 8 | Nitrogen |
| Flowering and fruit-setting stage | Weeks 8 to 12 | Leaf harvest period (late June to late September in South Korea) | Phosphorus and potassium for sesame; nitrogen and potassium for perilla |
| Ripening and harvest stage | Weeks 12 to 16 | Regrowth and repeated harvests continue | Potassium |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sesame is finished after one seed harvest a year, but leaf perilla is different. When the large lower leaves are picked off, new leaves keep coming from above, so it is harvested all summer long. The B for Perenniality in the evaluation table refers to this fact. If one field is divided between sesame and leaf perilla, leaves come in summer and oil comes in autumn.

### 2-6. Nutrition per 100g

| Nutrient | Sesame (roasted) | Perilla leaves (raw) |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Calories | 539kcal | 38-47kcal |
| Protein | 21.3g | 3.69g |
| Fat | 45-55g | Negligible |
| Carbohydrate | 27.4g | Mostly dietary fiber |
| Dietary fiber | 10-12g | 4.5g |
| Calcium | Abundant | 296mg |
| Iron | Abundant | Abundant |
| Magnesium | Abundant | Moderate |
| Vitamin E | Very abundant | Abundant |
| Beta-carotene | Low | 7,565 μg |
| Characteristic compounds | Sesamin, sesamolin | Rosmarinic acid, perillaldehyde |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It is no exaggeration to say that sesame's fat content of 45-55% is the whole point of this crop. The composition of this fat is around 40% oleic acid and around 45% linoleic acid, with little saturated fat. Linoleic acid is an essential fatty acid that the body cannot make on its own, so it must be obtained from food.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sesamin and sesamolin are compounds found only in sesame, and they carry special value in a disaster. These two have a strong antioxidant action and slow the rate at which oil spoils when it meets air. This is why sesame oil lasts longer than other vegetable oils, and it is the basis on which oil can be kept and used for months at a time where there is no refrigerator. Perilla oil lacks these compounds and instead contains more than 60% alpha-linolenic acid, which oxidizes easily, so its smell changes within a few weeks at room temperature.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The 7,565μg of beta-carotene in perilla leaves is 1.4 times the value for carrots. It is a compound that the body converts into vitamin A, and a shortage of this vitamin causes night blindness, in which one cannot see well in the dark. However, beta-carotene is absorbed only when dissolved in oil, so if perilla leaves are eaten without oil, you get little compared with what they contain. The fact that sesame oil and perilla leaves come from the same field has meaning here.

## 3. Growing Conditions

### 3-1. Temperature

| Item | Sesame | Perilla |
|---|---|---|
| Germination temperature | 20-25°C, minimum 15°C | 15-25°C |
| Optimal growth temperature | 20-30°C | 20-25°C |
| Optimal fruiting temperature | 20-28°C | 20-25°C |
| High-temperature limit | Tolerates even above 35°C as long as water is available | Leaves turn tough above 30°C |
| Low-temperature limit | Growth stops at 10°C | The same |
| Lethal temperature | Killed by a single frost | The same |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The C for Temperature range in the evaluation table comes from a fruiting range of 20-28°C, a width of 10°C. This means the range is wide for mere growth but narrow for filling the seeds. Outside this range, even if flowers open, seed does not set or the grains do not fill. The reason sesame is sown as late as mid-May to early June in South Korea is so that flowering and ripening fall within this summer temperature range.

### 3-2. Light

| Item | Standard | Remarks |
|---|---|---|
| Sunshine required by sesame | At least 6 hours a day | Cultivation is difficult in highlands where sunshine is short |
| Sunshine required by perilla | At least 6 hours a day | Grows in partial shade too |
| At 40% shade | Relative yield 86% | A common value for leafy vegetables and the basis of the B grade in the evaluation table |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The shade value in the evaluation table was set on the basis of leaves, so it fits leaf perilla well but fits sesame seed poorly. Filling sesame seeds takes a great deal of energy, so when sunlight is short, both the number of capsules and the size of the grains decrease. A layout that gives shaded spots to leaf perilla and sunny spots to sesame is reasonable.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There is one more trait of perilla that you need to know. Perilla is a short-day plant, which flowers when days become short. From late August in South Korea, as the days shorten, flower stalks rise, and from then on the leaves become smaller and tougher. To harvest leaves for longer, a spot that receives light at night is actually advantageous, but this is not a method that can be used when there is no electricity. For that reason, the open-field leaf harvest period is fixed as ending in late September.

### 3-3. Water and Soil

| Item | Sesame | Perilla |
|---|---|---|
| Total evapotranspiration | About 400mm per growing season | Similar |
| Drought | Very tolerant | Moderate. Leaves wilt when it dries out |
| Excess moisture | Extremely weak. Even a day or two of flooding causes major damage | Fairly tolerant |
| Soil type | Well-drained sandy loam to loam | Soil rich in organic matter and well drained |
| Soil depth | 30cm or more | 20cm or more |
| pH | 6.0-7.0 | 6.0-7.0 |
| Ridge height | 20-30cm or more | 15-20cm |

> **The most common cause of failure in growing sesame is not drought but water. On land where water does not drain, sesame is not planted, however good the other conditions are.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Why it is so weak comes from the nature of the roots. Sesame roots are built to go down deep and thick in search of water in dry ground, and the price for that is a high oxygen demand. When water fills the gaps in the soil and pushes the air out, the roots are suffocated at once. On top of this, the late blight pathogen spreads with the water and burrows into the damaged roots. This is the mechanism by which a day or two of flooding brings down a whole field. This problem is dealt with in Section 7.

## 4. Varieties

### 4-1. Sesame Varieties

| Variety | Characteristics | Yield | Suitable Situations |
|---|---|:---:|---|
| Oedae-kkae (single-stem sesame) | Grows as a single stem without branching | Average | When planted densely in a narrow spot. Management is simple |
| Gaji-kkae (branching sesame) | Produces side branches. Each plant is large | High | When there is plenty of space. A small number of plants is enough |
| Daheuk-kkae (Miryang No. 28) | A black sesame line | Average | Used as a medicinal material, and also pressed for oil |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The choice between Oedae-kkae and Gaji-kkae depends on the circumstances of the field. Oedae-kkae is small per plant and can be planted densely, and because it does not branch, the capsules are concentrated on a single stem, which makes harvesting simple. Gaji-kkae yields a lot per plant, but the ripening time differs from branch to branch, which makes the harvest decision harder. When seed is plentiful, planting Oedae-kkae densely is advantageous; when seed is scarce, planting Gaji-kkae sparsely is advantageous.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Black and white sesame do not differ greatly in oil content. The hulls of black sesame contain more oxalic acid along with pigment, which interferes slightly with calcium absorption; this issue is covered in Section 14.

### 4-2. Perilla Varieties

| Group | Varieties | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| Leaf perilla | Ipdeulkkae No. 1, Donggeul No. 2, Pungnyeon-deulkkae, Namcheon-deulkkae | Broad, soft leaves. Withstands repeated harvesting well |
| Oil perilla | Many local landraces | Sets plenty of seeds. The leaves are tough and hard to use as ssam |

> **To obtain perilla leaves, you must plant a leaf perilla variety. The leaves of oil perilla are tough and too strongly scented to use as ssam, and leaf perilla sets too few seeds to be worth pressing for oil.**

### 4-3. What to Choose in Each Situation

| Situation | Sesame | Perilla | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| When oil is the goal | Oedae-kkae, Gaji-kkae | Oil perilla | Sesame oil keeps longer. Perilla oil spoils quickly |
| When lamp oil is the goal | Sesame | - | Sesame oil gives little soot and climbs the wick well |
| When a summer leaf vegetable is the goal | - | Ipdeulkkae No. 1, Donggeul No. 2 | Harvested from late June to late September in South Korea |
| When space is tight | Oedae-kkae | Leaf perilla | Can be planted densely |
| A field that drains poorly | Not planted | Perilla | Sesame collapses under waterlogging |
| When you want to drive off animals | Sesame | Perilla | The strong aroma makes Korean water deer and wild boar shy away |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In any province of South Korea, sesame and perilla seed can be obtained, and each region retains landraces that have long been grown there to suit the local climate. That these are seeds you can obtain from a neighbor even when logistics have broken down is a great advantage of this crop.
