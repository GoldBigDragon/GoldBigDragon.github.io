## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In Korean, one name (kkae) covers two different plants. Sesame (Sesamum indicum) is an annual of the sesame family whose seeds yield sesame oil, and perilla (Perilla frutescens) is an annual of the mint family whose seeds yield perilla oil and whose leaves are eaten as perilla leaves. In the evaluation table this crop was excluded as a food crop because its calories per area are graded E (below 15% of the self-sufficiency line), and that verdict is accurate. But the value of this crop lies in oil, not calories. Oil makes up 45-55% of the seed, so pressing 1 kg yields about 400 g, and the oil cake left behind still holds 35-40% protein and becomes feed and fertilizer. A few rows supply a year's oil. Its weak point is moisture. Sesame is extremely drought-hardy, but if the field is flooded for even a day or two the roots are damaged and blight spreads, so in Korea, with its rainy season, how high the ridges are raised and how well the drainage is laid out decide success or failure. Harvest has to begin when the lower capsules start to split; if that moment is missed, the seed spills onto the field.

> **The fact that this crop's calorie grade is the lowest and the judgment that it is useless in a disaster are entirely different matters. What this crop yields is not calories but oil.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Oil is used for cooking by frying and stir-frying, for absorbing fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K), for essential fatty acids, for lamplight on nights without electricity, for soap, for waterproofing and preserving wood, leather and paper, and for lubricating metal. Grains, vegetables and root crops cannot replace any of these. The grade for each item is in the Crop Evaluation Results document.

## 2. Plant Overview
| Category | Sesame | Perilla |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Origin | Dry regions from Africa to India | East Asia |
| Main use | Sesame oil from the seed | Perilla oil from the seed, leaves as perilla leaves |
| Oil character | Goes rancid slowly | Goes rancid quickly |
| Leaf | Narrow lance shape, hairy | Wide heart shape, toothed edge |
| Drought and waterlogging | Extremely drought-hardy, extremely weak to waterlogging | Average in drought, fairly tolerant of waterlogging |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Both have square stems 60-150 cm tall and are mainly self-pollinating. Sesame has a straight taproot that goes deep and finds water in dry ground, and its fruit is a capsule 2-3 cm long whose upper seam splits open when ripe. A sesame seed is about 3 mm long and a perilla seed is 1.5-2 mm across. Sesame flowers open one after another from bottom to top over 30-40 days, so when the lower capsules are ripe the upper ones are still green, and because the plant also shares resources with the late flowers, the seeds end up evenly small. Topping solves this problem.

| Nutrition per 100 g | Sesame (roasted) | Perilla leaf (raw) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Calories | 539 kcal | 38-47 kcal |
| Protein / fat | 21.3 g / 45-55 g | 3.69 g / negligible |
| Carbohydrates / dietary fiber | 27.4 g / 10-12 g | Mostly dietary fiber / 4.5 g |
| Others | Rich in calcium, iron, magnesium and vitamin E. Sesamin and sesamolin (slow rancidity) | Calcium 296 mg, beta-carotene 7,565 μg |

## 3. Growing Environment and Varieties
| Condition | Sesame | Perilla |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Temperature | Germination 20-25℃ (minimum 15℃), growth 20-30℃, fruiting 20-28℃. Growth stops at 10℃, killed by frost | Germination 15-25℃, growth and fruiting 20-25℃. Leaves toughen above 30℃ |
| Light | 6 hours or more a day | 6 hours or more, partial shade is possible |
| Water | About 400 mm total evapotranspiration | Similar |
| Soil | Well-drained sandy loam to loam, pH 6.0-7.0, soil depth 30 cm or more | Soil rich in organic matter and well drained, pH 6.0-7.0, soil depth 20 cm or more |
| Ridge height | 20-30 cm or more | 15-20 cm |

> **The most common cause of failure in sesame is not drought but water. Do not plant sesame on ground that does not drain, however good the other conditions.**

| Variety | Characteristics |
|:---|:---|
| Single-stem sesame | Single stem. For dense planting in a narrow space, simple to manage |
| Branching sesame | Produces side branches. Each plant is large and the yield is high. When there is room |
| Dahuk (Miryang 28) | A black sesame line. Used both as medicine and for oil |
| Leaf perilla (Leaf Perilla 1, Donggeul 2, Pungnyeon, Namcheon) | Wide, soft leaves that withstand repeated picking. Few seeds set |
| Oil perilla (local landraces) | Many seeds set. Leaves are tough and unsuited to wraps |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sesame is for oil and lamp oil (it keeps longer and burns with little soot), leaf perilla is for summer leaf vegetables, and perilla is for fields that drain poorly.

## 4. Field Preparation and Sowing
| Item | Sesame | Leaf perilla |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Ridges | Height 20-30 cm or more, width 55-60 cm. Deep, wide furrows, a drainage ditch around the field, and on sloping fields a diversion ditch above that turns uphill water aside | Height 15-20 cm |
| Compost and compound fertilizer (per 1,000㎡) | 1,000-2,000 kg and 30-35 kg. All as basal fertilizer 2 weeks before sowing, no top dressing | 1,500 kg and 20-30 kg. A little top dressing every 10-15 days until the harvest period |
| Sowing | Mid May to early June (air temperature 15℃ or higher). 0.3-0.6 kg per 1,000㎡ | Late April to early May (after the frost risk has passed). Oil perilla in May-June |
| Spacing and depth | Ridges 55-60 cm, plants 15-20 cm apart, depth 2-3 cm, 5-6 seeds per hole. Press lightly after sowing | 10 × 5 cm or 7 × 7 cm, depth 0.5-1 cm. Cover with straw, cloth or newspaper and remove it as soon as the seedlings emerge |
| Thinning | At 2-4 true leaves, leave the strongest one per hole | At 1-2 true leaves and again at 4-6 true leaves |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Without chemical fertilizer, use well-rotted compost (unrotted compost invites blight), wood ash (a handful per ㎡), fermented oil cake (raw cake heats up in the soil), urine diluted 10-20 times (for sesame only a little, early on), bone meal, and liquid manure made by soaking grass (top dressing for leaf perilla).

| Seed treatment | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Use only the seed that sinks | Soak for 3-4 hours and skim off the floating empty seeds |
| Hot water treatment | 50-55℃ water for 10-15 minutes. Prevents damping-off |
| Coating with wood ash | Also disinfects and keeps the seeds from sticking together |

> **Seed disinfection is not optional for sesame. Damping-off is carried on the seed, and once it spreads in the seedling stage the whole spot has to be replanted.**

## 5. Water Management and Cultivation Management
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If flooding lasts from a few hours to a day, the roots run out of oxygen and the leaves begin to wilt; after two days the root tips begin to rot; after three days or more the blight pathogen spreads to the base of the stem. When the sun comes out after the water drains, the remaining roots cannot cope and the plant wilts sharply instead.

| Period | Sesame | Leaf perilla |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Germination to seedling | Keep the topsoil from drying out, but afterwards save water so the roots go down | Keep from drying out |
| Vegetative growth | Leave it to natural rainfall. Keeping water from pooling is all there is | Water thoroughly when the topsoil turns pale |
| Flowering and fruiting | Supplement only in severe drought (flowering and early ripening are the most valuable times) | Keep the leaves from wilting |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Before the rainy season, clear soil and weeds from the furrows; in June, pile soil around the base to raise the ridges again; and right after rain, set fallen plants upright and tread them in. Plants with blight are taken out of the field at once, and no water is given at ripening.

| Management | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Topping (sesame) | 35-40 days after the first flower, on a clear morning, cut just below the uppermost flower bud (also the tips of side branches in branching sesame). Flowering stops, the seeds grow larger, ripening is concentrated, and the plant is shorter and falls over less |
| Topping (leaf perilla) | When the plant exceeds 1 m, cut off the top shoot to increase side shoots |
| Preventing lodging | Hill up at 30-40 cm plant height, reduce nitrogen from the start, do not plant densely, and use string lines when the plant exceeds 80 cm |
| Weeds | Plow before sowing to make the weed seeds sprout and then scrape them off shallowly. In the seedling stage scrape only between the rows, shallowly. Hardly needed once the plants close over |

## 6. Pests, Diseases, and Nutrient Problems
| Disease | Symptoms and conditions | Response |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Phytophthora blight | Roots and stem base rot black and the plant wilts. Rainy season, when water pools | Raise the ridges and ensure drainage. Lift diseased plants with the soil and take them out of the field |
| Fusarium wilt | Leaves wilt on one side first and the inside of the stem turns brown. Hot period, fields with continuous cropping | No cure. A 3-4 year rotation is the only remedy |
| Damping-off | The base of young shoots narrows and they topple | Seed disinfection. Do not sow densely and avoid unrotted manure |

> **Disease control for sesame comes down to two things: raising the ridges to drain the water, and resting the field for 3-4 years.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For brown spots on the leaves (leaf blight, rust), ensure ventilation and remove diseased leaves. Wash aphids off with a strong jet of water or soapy water; pick stink bugs and moth caterpillars (oriental tobacco budworm, tobacco cutworm) by hand in the early morning or at dusk and remove egg masses together with the leaf. Control materials are soapy water (20 g of unscented soap in 1 L of water), wood ash, garlic infusion (100 g of garlic in 1 L of water), wood vinegar diluted 500-1,000 times, and Bordeaux mixture (to prevent leaf blight).

| Symptom | Cause and response |
|:---|:---|
| Whole leaf yellow and small plants | Nitrogen deficiency. Diluted urine, fermented oil cake, well-rotted compost |
| Flowers open and then drop | Phosphorus or boron deficiency. Compost mixed from many materials, bone meal |
| New leaves yellow with only the veins green | Iron deficiency. Use less wood ash and add organic matter |
| Capsules set but the seeds are empty | Potassium deficiency or stink bugs. Wood ash; check for stink bugs |
| Tall plants that fall over, only leaves lush | Excess nitrogen. Cut off nitrogen, hill up, and top earlier |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Most nutrient problems in sesame are not deficiency but excess nitrogen, so finish with a single basal application. For leaf perilla the leaf is the harvest, so nitrogen is supplied continuously.

## 7. Intercropping and Crop Rotation
| Compatibility | Crop |
|:---|:---|
| Good | Legumes (cowpea, mung bean, soybean), sweet potato and potato (different rooting depth), border planting around the field (the strong scent keeps away water deer and wild boar) |
| Fair | Maize and sorghum (they cast shade, so place them to the north) |
| Poor | Continuous sesame, next to crops that are irrigated, directly after nightshades (pepper, tomato, potato) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Rotate sesame in year 1, a legume in year 2, a cabbage-family or gourd-family crop in year 3 and a grain (sorghum, foxtail millet, maize) in year 4, and return to sesame in year 5. If the field is small, move the planting spot every year. Perilla needs only 2 years, but avoid alternating it with sesame on the same ground.

## 8. Harvest, Threshing, and Seed Saving
| Step | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Timing | Late September to early October. When two or three capsules at the bottom of the stem start to split and the lower leaves turn yellow and fall. If you wait for the top to ripen, you lose all of the bottom |
| Cutting | On a cloudy day, in the early morning or evening, cut close to the ground. Gather the plants standing upright without laying them down or shaking them, and put cloth underneath when carrying them |
| Drying | Tie five or six plants at the bottom and stand three or four bundles together in a cone shape. Spread a straw mat underneath and leave 7-15 days. Cover or bring them in if rain is forecast |
| Threshing and cleaning | Hold the bundle upside down over the mat and beat it with a stick, dry for two or three more days and repeat. Separate with sieving, winnowing, wind or water washing (if washed, dry completely the same day; if left wet in a pile, the seed molds and becomes unusable) |
| Leaf perilla | From about four true leaves, when the leaf is fully open and 7-10 cm. Always leave the 2-4 young leaves at the top and pick only the large lower leaves. In summer every 3-5 days, from late June to late September |

| Seed saving | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Plants | Mark plants that are disease-free, carry many capsules and have not fallen over at flowering. Do not use plants that had wilt |
| Where to take seed | Capsules in the middle of the stem. Leave until fully brown and dry, and put a cloth bag over them to catch what spills |
| Storage | Dry in shade to 12% moisture or less, put in an airtight container and keep in a cool, dark place. Twice the amount required. Never roast seed meant for planting |
| Lifespan | 1-2 years airtight, cool and dark; less than 1 year at plain room temperature; a few months where sun or heat reaches. Collect every year and plant every year |

## 9. Storage
| Form | Storage at room temperature | Reason |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Unroasted seed | About 1 year | The oil is locked inside the cells and touches the air less |
| Roasted seed | 3-6 months | The cells are broken and the oil is exposed to air |
| Ground sesame | Within 1 month | The surface area increases and it spoils rapidly |
| Pressed sesame oil / perilla oil | 6 months to 1 year / 2-3 months | Sesame oil lasts thanks to sesamin; perilla oil is rich in alpha-linolenic acid and spoils quickly |

> **Keep the seed stored as seed and roast and press only what you will use at the time. If you press everything at once, it becomes inedible within half a year.**

| Item | Tips |
|:---|:---|
| Seed | Dry to 12% moisture or less, cool completely in the shade, then fill jars, glass bottles or metal tins to the top and seal them (air in empty space spoils the oil). Keep them in a cool, dark place with little temperature change, divided among several containers |
| Oil | Divide it into small bottles filled to the top and stopped tightly, in dark bottles or jars in a cool place. Open only a bottle holding as much as you will use |
| Perilla leaf | As is 2-3 days; dried and sealed, pickled in soy sauce, or salted for several months; kimchi 2-3 months |

## 10. Pressing Oil and Processing
| Category | Oil pressed from roasted seed | Oil pressed from unroasted seed |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Aroma and color | Nutty aroma, dark brown | Almost no aroma, clear yellow |
| Yield | More comes out | Less comes out |
| Smoke point | Low. Unsuited to stir-frying | High. Can be used for frying |
| Use | Seasoned dishes, oil drizzled at the end | Stir-frying, frying, lamps, soap |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When roasting, stir without stopping over low to medium heat. When the seeds pop, turn light brown and smell nutty, take them off the heat at once and spread them out to cool. Grind the seed finely, steam it in a steamer or warm it in a pan, put it in a hemp or cotton bag and press, then let it settle for a day or two and pour off only the clear upper part.

| Pressing method | Yield relative to seed weight |
|:---|:---|
| Screw press (needs iron) | 40-50% |
| Wedge press (put the bag in a log frame and drive in a wooden wedge with a mallet) | 35-45% |
| Lever press (hang one end of a long log on a post and tie a heavy stone to the other) | 30-40% |
| Twisting by hand (clamp the bag between two sticks and twist) | 20-30% |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Without a press, grind roasted sesame finely, add water a little at a time and grind it into a thick paste, boil it in a wide pot over low heat, skim the oil that rises with a ladle, and heat the skimmed oil gently again to drive off the water. If any water remains, the oil spoils.

| By-product and use | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Oil cake | People pound it and mix it into porridge or rice cake (it is coarse and hard to eat in quantity), and it is a protein source for chickens, pigs and cattle (too much at once causes diarrhea). As fertilizer it holds about 5-6% nitrogen, 2% phosphate and 1% potassium and must be fermented before use |
| Lamp oil | Insert a wick twisted from cotton thread, hemp thread or cloth into oil in a shallow dish. Sesame oil gives little soot, while perilla oil crusts the wick. Use oil that has become hard to eat first |

| Soap making | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Lye and causticizing | Stir hardwood ash into water, leave it for a day and take the water from the top (softwood ash is weak); add quicklime (made by burning shells or limestone), stir and let it settle |
| Strength | Enough when a fresh feather starts to dissolve or a potato floats up to the size of a coin |
| Mixing and boiling | Warm the oil and pour in the lye a little at a time while stirring without stopping, then boil over low heat for several hours |
| Setting | Adding salt makes the soap clump and float. Skim it into a mold to set and let it cure for several weeks |
| Cautions | If lye gets in the eyes or on the skin, wash with water for a long time and rinse with diluted vinegar. Aluminum pots corrode, so use cast iron or earthenware. Unfinished soap still contains alkali |

> **For soap, use oil that has become inedible, animal fat and oil from the dregs of the oil cake first, and sound sesame oil last.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The seed is roasted and sprinkled over food or eaten as sesame salt, sesame porridge or sesame brittle, and without a press, eating it ground is the most efficient. Perilla leaves are eaten as wraps, pickles, pancakes and steamed dishes, and added to soups at the end.

## 11. Toxicity and Precautions
| Precaution | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Toxicity | Sesame and perilla contain no toxic components harmful to people |
| Allergy | Sesame is a major food allergen. Start with a small amount for someone eating it for the first time, and if there is a reaction, stop everything including sesame oil and sesame salt. Perilla does not necessarily cause a reaction |
| Oxalic acid and phytic acid | The hull (especially in black sesame) hinders absorption of calcium, iron and zinc. Roasting, soaking and fermenting reduce it. Because the hull holds much of the calcium, eating it roasted with the hull is better than removing it |
| Rancid oil | A sour or paint-like smell, bitter taste, cloudy color and stickiness appear. Do not eat it; use it for lamp oil or soap |
| Others | The high fat content causes diarrhea if too much is eaten at once. Perilla leaves accumulate nitrates when heavily fertilized, so do not feed much to infants |

## 12. Annual Work Schedule
| Month | Sesame | Perilla |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Jan-Mar | Check the seed and stored oil, disinfect seed, prepare compost, decide where to plant (rotation order) | Prepare seed |
| April | Plow, make ridges, apply basal fertilizer | Sow leaf perilla in late April |
| May | Sow from mid May, raise the ridges high | Finish sowing by early May, thin |
| June | Thin, weed, hill up, tend furrows and drainage ditches | Begin leaf harvest from late June |
| July | Flowering begins, drainage management in the rainy season and blight inspection | Leaf harvest, top dressing |
| August | Top 35-40 days after the first flower, check for stink bugs and moths | Leaf harvest |
| September | From late month check the lower capsules, put bags over the stems for seed saving | Finish leaf harvest in late month |
| Late Sep-Oct | Harvest, dry standing, thresh, clean | Harvest and thresh oil perilla |
| Nov-Dec | Store, press oil, ferment the oil cake into fertilizer | Store |

> **Sesame farming means raising the ridges in May and judging the harvest by eye in September. How high the ridges were raised is half of it, and noticing the day the lower capsules split is the other half.**
