## 9. Pests and Diseases - How to Prevent Them Without Pesticides

### 9-1. Sesame Is a Crop Protected by Prevention

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Once disease breaks out in sesame, treatment is practically impossible and it spreads fast. There are reports that an outbreak cuts the yield by about 25%, and in severe cases the whole field is lost. Even with chemicals it is a hard problem, so where there are no chemicals, there is no path other than prevention.

| Disease | Symptoms | When It Occurs | Response Without Chemicals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Late blight | The roots and the base of the stem rot black and the plant wilts | In the monsoon season (jangma, late June to July in South Korea), when water stands | Raise the ridges and secure drainage. Remove diseased plants together with the soil and take them out of the field |
| Fusarium wilt | Wilting begins with the leaves on one side, and when the stem is split open, the inside is brown | In hot periods, in fields under continuous cropping | There is no cure. A 3-4 year rotation is the only countermeasure |
| Leaf blight | Brown spots form on the leaves, spread and dry up | When humid | Secure ventilation and remove diseased leaves. Do not plant densely |
| Damping-off | The base of young sprouts constricts and the sprouts fall over | At the seedling stage | Seed disinfection is the key. Do not sow densely, and avoid under-rotted manure |
| Powdery mildew | A white powder settles on the leaves | Late summer | Secure ventilation. If the damage is not serious, it can be left alone |

> **Disease control for sesame comes down, in practice, to two things: raising the ridges to drain water, and resting the land for 3-4 years. If these two are observed, the remaining diseases do not become a big problem.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fusarium wilt deserves separate mention. It is a disease in which a fungus living in the soil enters through the roots and blocks the water-conducting vessels, and once this fungus has established itself it stays in the soil for many years. The basis attached to the C for Continuous-cropping tolerance in the evaluation table, "Sesame needs a 3-4 year break because Fusarium wilt persists in the soil," refers to this disease. It cannot be cured with chemicals, and resistant varieties are not complete either. There is no way out other than moving the field.

### 9-2. Major Insect Pests

| Pest | Damage | Response Without Chemicals |
|---|---|---|
| Aphids | Suck sap from new shoots and the undersides of leaves. They increase in dry weather | Wash them off with a strong stream of water. Spray soapy water. Protect ladybugs and lacewings |
| Stink bugs | Stick their mouthparts into ripening capsules and suck out the contents | Pick them off by hand in the early morning. Clear the weeds around the edge of the field |
| Cotton bollworm (Helicoverpa armigera) larvae | Gnaw leaves and stems and bore holes in the capsules | Pick them off by hand at dusk. Remove egg masses together with the leaf |
| Tobacco cutworm (Spodoptera litura) larvae | Gnaw the leaves. They also attach to perilla | The same methods |
| Perilla rust | Brown spots form on the leaves | Secure ventilation and remove diseased leaves |

### 9-3. Pest-Control Materials You Can Make Without Chemicals

| Material | How to Make It | What It Is Used For | Principle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soapy water | Dissolve 20g of unscented soap in 1L of water | Aphids, spider mites | The soap dissolves the insects' oily coating and blocks their breathing pores |
| Wood ash | Sift it and scatter it on the leaves and the ground | Crawling pests | The ash draws moisture out of the insects' bodies |
| Garlic infusion | Grind 100g of garlic, steep it in 1L of water, and strain | Repelling pests | The smell of sulfur compounds drives insects away |
| Wood vinegar | Dilute 500 to 1,000 times | Repelling pests, suppressing mold | Its acidity and phenolic compounds do the work |
| Bordeaux mixture | Made by dissolving copper sulfate and quicklime in water | Preventing leaf blight | Copper blocks spore germination |
| Perilla leaf (kkaennip) infusion | Crush perilla leaves and steep them in water | Repelling pests | The strong aroma of perillaldehyde drives insects away |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The last row is an example of a cycle closing within this crop. The tough perilla leaves left after picking, or the tips cut off in pinching, become a pest-control material if they are steeped and used instead of thrown away. The effect is not to kill pests but only to keep them from settling, but where there is nothing else, it has value.

### 9-4. Prevention Before Planting

| Method | Details |
|---|---|
| Seed disinfection | Hot-water treatment and wood ash. Damping-off comes on seed |
| Crop rotation | Sesame rests for 3-4 years. This is the whole of the countermeasure for Fusarium wilt and late blight |
| Drainage | Raise the ridges and cut the furrows deep. This is the whole of the countermeasure against late blight |
| Not planting densely | Ventilation holds off fungal diseases |
| Mulching to keep soil from splashing | Laying straw or dry grass keeps raindrops from splashing soil that carries disease onto the leaves |
| Diseased plants out of the field | If diseased plants are discarded in the field, the pathogens stay there |

## 10. Nutrient Deficiency and Excess

### 10-1. Reading Deficiency Symptoms

| Symptom | Where It Appears First | Cause | Why | Remedy with Natural Materials |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leaves turn yellow all over and the plant is small | Starts with the older lower leaves | Nitrogen deficiency | Nitrogen moves around within the plant, so it is pulled out of older leaves and sent to new leaves | Diluted urine, fermented oil cake (kkaenmuk), well-rotted compost |
| Leaf edges scorch brown | Starts with the lower leaves | Potassium deficiency | Potassium is also highly mobile, so older leaves are sacrificed first | Scatter a small amount of wood ash |
| Leaves dark and purplish, growth slow | The whole plant | Phosphorus deficiency | It can also appear when phosphorus uptake falls in cold temperatures | Bone meal, wood ash |
| Flowers open and then drop right away | Flowers | Phosphorus or boron deficiency | Pollen is not formed properly or fertilization does not occur | Compost made from a mix of materials, bone meal |
| New leaves yellow with only the veins green | Starts with the new upper leaves | Iron deficiency | Iron is not readily translocated, so the new leaves starve first | Reduce wood ash and add organic matter |
| New shoot tips dry up and twist | Growing point | Calcium or boron deficiency | They travel with the flow of water and do not reach the new shoots well | Powder of burned shells and eggshells |
| Capsules form but the seeds are empty | Capsules | Potassium deficiency or stink bugs | The nutrients used in ripening are lacking or have been sucked out | Wood ash. Check for stink bugs first |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most useful clue in reading symptoms is where they appear first. If the older leaves are affected first, nutrients that move readily within the plant (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, magnesium) are lacking; if the new leaves are affected first, nutrients that do not move readily (iron, calcium, boron) are lacking.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;With sesame, however, there is one trap. Many of the symptoms above cannot be told apart from the early symptoms of late blight or Fusarium wilt. If the whole plant wilts, suspect disease before nutrients, and when wilting starts with the leaves on one side in particular, it is almost always Fusarium wilt. If you split the stem lengthwise and the inside is brown, it is disease. If you cannot make this distinction and apply fertilizer, you only feed the disease.

### 10-2. Excess Symptoms

| Symptom | Cause | Why | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| The plant grows tall but the stems are soft and lie down | Excess nitrogen | The cell walls stretch thin and the tissue weakens | Stop nitrogen. Prop the plants up by hilling |
| The leaves are lush but there are few capsules | Excess nitrogen | Resources go to the stems and leaves and do not reach fruit set | Bring pinching forward |
| The leaves are dark blue-green and aphids gather | Excess nitrogen | The sap becomes rich in amino acids, which aphids prefer | The same remedy |
| Flowering is late and ripening drags on | Excess nitrogen | Vegetative growth continues and the plant fails to move on to reproductive growth | Stop nitrogen and pinch |
| Leaf edges dry out and the roots turn brown | Excessive fertilizer concentration | The salt concentration of the soil rises and water is drawn out of the roots | Water thoroughly to flush the salts out |

> **Most nutrient problems that appear in sesame are not shortages but excess nitrogen. Finishing with a single base application and giving no top-dressing is the basic rule for this crop.**

### 10-3. Leaf Perilla Is the Opposite

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Because the leaves themselves are the harvest in leaf perilla, nitrogen has to be supplied continuously. Every time leaves are picked, the plant loses part of its body and has to make it anew, and the material for that is nitrogen. The standard is small amounts at 10-15 day intervals, and diluted urine or liquid fertilizer from steeped grass is suitable. However, too much nitrogen causes nitrate to accumulate in the leaves, so it is not applied just before harvest.

## 11. Intercropping and Crop Rotation

### 11-1. Compatibility

| Compatibility | Crop | What Happens |
|---|---|---|
| Good | Legume family (cowpea, mung bean, soybean) | Part of the nitrogen fixed in the root nodules is released into the soil. Sesame takes up little nitrogen, so there is little competition |
| Good | Planting around the field edge | The strong aroma of sesame and perilla makes Korean water deer and wild boar shy away |
| Good | Sweet potato, potato | The roots reach different depths, so their layers do not overlap |
| Average | Maize (corn), sorghum | They are tall and shade the sesame. Placing them on the north side works fine |
| Poor | Planting sesame in succession | Fusarium wilt and late blight build up in the soil |
| Poor | Next to crops that are irrigated | Water seeping in from the side soaks even the sesame ridge |
| Poor | Right after the nightshade family (chili pepper, tomato, potato) | Some soil-borne diseases overlap |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Border planting brings a practical advantage in a disaster. The basis attached to the C for Non-food value in the evaluation table, "the strong aroma has a repellent effect," is this very matter. Where there is neither fence nor net, there are not many ways to protect a field. If a row of sesame or perilla is run along the edge of the field, the entry of Korean water deer and wild boar can be reduced to some extent. It cannot block them completely, but it is better than doing nothing, and you get oil as well.

### 11-2. Crop Rotation

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The evaluation table gives Continuous-cropping tolerance a C, with the basis "Sesame needs a 3-4 year break because Fusarium wilt persists in the soil. Continuous-cropping injury is relatively severe among oilseed crops." It is among those with the longest rotation breaks of the crops covered in this document. Leafy vegetables need 1-2 years and amaranth 2 years, while sesame needs 3-4 years.

| Year | What Is Planted | What You Gain |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | Sesame | Oil. The deep roots drill through the soil |
| Year 2 | Legume family (soybean, cowpea, mung bean) | Returns the nitrogen sesame used |
| Year 3 | Cabbage family (Brassicaceae) or gourd family (Cucurbitaceae) | Being a different lineage, they do not carry on the pathogens |
| Year 4 | Cereals (sorghum, foxtail millet, maize) | As members of the grass family, their soil-borne diseases are different |
| Year 5 | Return to sesame | |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If the field is too small to run a four-year rotation, there is a way to move the sesame planting spot every year. If the field is divided into four sections and sesame is planted in one section each year, each section receives sesame only once every 4 years. Only 25% of the total area becomes sesame, but if the aim is to obtain oil, that is often enough.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Perilla is not as demanding as sesame, and about 2 years is enough. This makes one want to alternate sesame and perilla in the same spot, but it is better to avoid that. They belong to different families, but there are soil-borne diseases they share, and above all, perilla does not help with the 3-year rest that sesame needs.

## 12. Harvest - When the Lower Capsules Split Open

### 12-1. When to Cut

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The hardest judgment in all of sesame cultivation is the timing of harvest. This is because the capsules ripen in sequence from bottom to top, and ripe capsules split open by themselves and spill their seeds.

| Criterion | Details |
|---|---|
| Capsules | Two or three capsules at the bottom of the stem begin to split open |
| Leaves | The lower leaves turn yellow and begin to fall |
| Stem color | The green fades and the stem turns yellow starting from the bottom |
| Timing | Usually late September to early October in South Korea |
| Seeds | Split a capsule open; if the seeds have taken on their proper color and are firm, they are ripe |

> **The criterion for harvest is not whether the upper capsules have all ripened but whether the lower capsules have begun to split open. If you wait for the top, you lose all of the bottom.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If cut at this point, the upper capsules are still not fully ripe. However, while the cut plants stand to dry, the nutrients remaining in the stem keep the upper capsules ripening. This is called after-ripening, and it is why the method of pulling sesame up by the roots or cutting it with a sickle and standing it in bundles has lasted for so long.

### 12-2. How to Cut

| Item | Details | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Weather | Choose an overcast day | In strong sun the capsules dry out completely and burst at a mere touch |
| Time of day | Cut in the early morning or evening | Dew dampens the capsules, so they burst less |
| Where to cut | Cut close to the ground | Long stems are best for tying into bundles and standing them up |
| Handling | Gather the plants upright without laying them down | If laid down or shaken, they spill on the spot |
| Catching | Hold a cloth or sack underneath when moving them | It catches what falls during transport |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The advice to choose an overcast day, early morning or evening is the heart of harvesting this crop. Sesame capsules split open when they dry and close up again when they absorb moisture. On a morning with dew, the capsules are closed and easy to handle, whereas at midday, dried out under the sun, they spill at a mere brush. Even if the same person cuts the same field, the losses differ by several-fold depending on the time of day.

### 12-3. Standing to Dry and Shaking Out

| Step | Method | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tying bundles | Tie the lower part of five or six plants together with straw or string | Keeps them from scattering and makes them easier to stand up |
| Standing them up | Lean three or four bundles against each other and stand them in a cone shape | Even in rain, water runs off, and wind passes through the inside |
| Location | Stand them in a yard or at the field's head where sun and wind reach well | In shade they do not dry and mold easily |
| Catching | Spread a straw mat (meongseok) or cloth underneath | It catches what falls by itself while they dry |
| Duration | 7 to 15 days | Leave them until most of the capsules have split open |
| Rain precautions | If rain is forecast, cover them or bring them indoors | If they get wet, the capsules close up again and the seeds spoil |

| Shaking Stage | Method |
|---|---|
| 1st | Over a straw mat, hold a bundle upside down and tap the stems lightly with a stick |
| 2nd | Stand them up again, dry them two or three more days, then repeat |
| 3rd | Once more when the upper capsules have split open |
| Finishing | Strip the remaining capsules by hand, or rub the stems to get the seeds out |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It is normal for the seeds not to all come out in one go. The lower capsules empty on the first round, while the upper ones need several more days of drying before they split open. If you find repeating it two or three times a nuisance and strike hard once, bits of stem and leaf fall along with the seeds and only make the cleaning harder. The knack is lightly, many times.

### 12-4. Cleaning and Sorting

| Method | Details |
|---|---|
| Sieving | Use a coarse sieve to remove pieces of stem and capsule |
| Winnowing | Put the seeds in a winnowing basket (ki) and toss them, and the light husks blow away. Sesame seeds are heavier than amaranth, so winnowing works well |
| Using the wind | On a windy day, pour the seeds slowly from a height and the husks blow off to the side |
| Washing in water | Stir the seeds in water, and husks and dust float while the seeds sink |
| Drying | Spread out seeds that have been washed in water immediately and dry them completely |

> **If sesame has been washed in water, it must be dried within the same day. When oil-rich seeds are piled up still holding moisture, they heat up and grow mold, and once that happens, the oil takes on an off smell and becomes unusable.**

### 12-5. Harvesting Leaf Perilla

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Start | From about the time there are four true leaves |
| Best time | When the leaf is fully unfolded, the surface of the leaf is bright green and the veins are purplish; size 7-10cm |
| Method | Always leave two to four of the young, not-yet-unfolded leaves at the top, and pick only the large leaves below |
| Interval | 3 to 5 days in summer |
| Period | Late June to late September in South Korea |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Leaving two to four young leaves at the top is the key to repeated harvesting. Those leaves keep photosynthesizing and produce the next leaves. If you get greedy and pick everything including the top, the plant cannot recover and simply stops.

## 13. Seed Saving and Seed Storage

### 13-1. Seed Saving

| Step | Method | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing plants | Choose plants that are disease-free, carry many capsules, and have not fallen over | Seeds resemble their parents. Never use a plant that had Fusarium wilt |
| Marking | Mark them in advance during flowering | They are hard to tell apart at harvest |
| Where to take seed from | Take seed from the capsules in the middle of the stem | The lower ones spill first and the upper ones are less filled. The middle is the soundest |
| Ripening fully | Leave the capsules until they are completely dry and brown | Under-ripe seeds have low germination ability |
| Bagging | Cover the stems to be harvested for seed with a cloth bag and tie it | Catches what splits open and spills by itself |
| Drying | Dry in the shade to a moisture content of 12% or less | Leaving them long in the sun spoils the oil |
| Storage | Put in an airtight container and keep in a cool, dark place | Light, heat and air spoil the oil |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The S for Seed saving in the evaluation table rests on the fact that both sesame and perilla are mainly self-pollinating. Bees do sometimes carry pollen, but fertilization is mostly completed within the plant's own flower, so varieties do not mix much. Even with several varieties in one field, seed can be collected separately.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;However, when choosing plants for seed saving, one thing in particular must be checked. Never take seed from a plant that shows any trace of Fusarium wilt. This disease is also transmitted through seed, so if seed taken from a diseased plant is sown the following year in a new field, even that field is contaminated. Even if you keep to a 3- or 4-year rotation, this one slip undoes it.

### 13-2. Storage

| Condition | Period Germination Ability Is Retained |
|---|:---:|
| Fully dried, sealed, in a cool and dark place | 1-2 years |
| Simply left at room temperature | Less than 1 year |
| A place exposed to sun or heat | A few months |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The C for Seed longevity in the evaluation table, "Short-lived as an oil-rich seed, 1-2 years at room temperature," is one of the heaviest constraints of this crop. It is very different from amaranth seed, which lasts 3-5 years, and kale, which lasts 4-5 years. The reason is oil. When the oil inside the seed meets air and oxidizes, peroxides form, and these damage the embryo inside the seed. That seeds with more oil have shorter lives is a rule that applies to oilseed crops as a whole.

> **Sesame and perilla are crops whose seed cannot be stored for long. You have to collect it every year and plant it every year, and if you skip a year, nothing sprouts the following year.**

| Ways to Extend Longevity | Principle |
|---|---|
| Dry completely before storing | Remaining moisture speeds up oxidation |
| Seal airtight to keep air out | Oil oxidizes only where oxygen is present |
| Keep in a cool place | Every 10°C rise in temperature doubles the oxidation rate |
| Keep in the dark | Light promotes oxidation |
| Use unroasted seed | Roasting breaks the cells and exposes the oil to air. Never roast seed meant for planting |
| Collect plenty | Allowing for a drop in germination rate, collect twice the amount needed |
