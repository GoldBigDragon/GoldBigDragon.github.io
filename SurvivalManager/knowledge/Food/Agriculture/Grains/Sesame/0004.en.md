## 5. Field Preparation and Fertilizer

### 5-1. Choosing the Site

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In choosing a site for sesame, the first and nearly the only condition is whether water drains well. Sunlight and fertilizer come next. Sesame is not planted in a paddy plot where water stands, in low-lying ground, or in a clay-soil field, however good the other conditions are. The upper edge of a sloping field, a field mixed with sand, and a spot with a slight slope are prime ground for sesame.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Perilla fares somewhat better. Because it tolerates moisture to a degree, it is possible to place perilla in spots unsuitable for sesame. However, perilla roots are also damaged when submerged, so this means only that it is relatively better off, not that it may be planted anywhere.

### 5-2. Raising the Ridges

| Item | Sesame | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tilling depth | 20-30cm | The taproot has to go deep for the plant to withstand drought |
| Ridge height | 20-30cm or more | Keeps the layer where the roots sit from being submerged |
| Ridge width | 55-60cm | One or two rows are planted on each ridge |
| Furrows | Make them deep and wide | They are the path by which rainwater drains away instead of pooling |
| Drainage ditch | Dug around the edge of the field | Blocks water coming in from outside and lets water inside drain out |
| Water channel outside the field | Diverts water coming down from above | Especially important on sloping fields |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It deserves notice that the standard of 20-30cm for ridge height is higher than for other crops. Napa cabbage is 15cm and chili pepper around 20cm, whereas sesame is higher than that. The reason is simple. Even when the furrows fill with water in a heavy rain, the root layer on top of the ridge has to stay above the water.

> **The most important task in a sesame field is neither sowing nor applying fertilizer, but raising the ridges and cutting the furrows deep. This one thing decides whether the field is kept or lost in the monsoon season (jangma, late June to July in South Korea).**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If plastic film is available, covering the ridges with it helps. It raises the soil temperature so that early growth is faster, keeps rainwater from soaking directly into the ridge, and suppresses weeds. Where there is no film, straw or dry grass can take its place, but it does not raise the soil temperature and gives only weed suppression and protection against soil splashing up. Keeping soil from splashing onto the leaves in raindrops is more important than it seems, because the late blight pathogen lives in the soil and moves onto the leaves together with the splashed soil.

### 5-3. Fertilizer Rates

| Fertilizer | Sesame (1,000m²) | Leaf perilla (1,000m²) |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Compost | 1,000-2,000kg | 1,500kg |
| Compound fertilizer | 30-35kg | 20-30kg |
| Base fertilizer | The whole amount 2 weeks before sowing | The whole amount |
| Top-dressing | Mostly omitted | Small amounts at 10-15 day intervals until the end of harvest |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;What stands out is that the fertilizing of sesame and leaf perilla is exactly opposite. Sesame is finished with one application of base fertilizer, with almost no top-dressing. Leaf perilla gets a little more every time its leaves are picked. This is because the purposes differ. In sesame, too much nitrogen makes the stems and leaves lush, the capsules poor, and the plants prone to falling over. In leaf perilla, lush leaves are the harvest itself, so nitrogen is needed continuously.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The A for Fertilizer demand in the evaluation table, 18g per m², is on the low side. Read together with the A for Yield retention without synthetic inputs, 56%, it makes clear that this crop does not depend much on fertilizer. The heritage of having originally grown in barren dry land remains as it was.

### 5-4. When There Is No Chemical Fertilizer

| Alternative | Nutrients Supplied | How to Use | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Well-rotted compost | Balanced | Apply generously as base fertilizer | Under-rotted compost damages the roots and invites late blight |
| Wood ash | Potassium, phosphorus, calcium | Mix a handful per m² into the base fertilizer | It is alkaline, so too much raises the pH |
| Sesame and perilla oil cake (kkaenmuk) | Nitrogen, phosphorus | Ferment what is left after pressing oil and use it as base fertilizer | Unfermented cake generates heat in the soil and damages the roots |
| Diluted urine | Nitrogen, potassium | Dilute to 10-20 times its volume and apply | For sesame, use only a small amount early in growth |
| Bone meal | Phosphorus, calcium | Burn, grind and mix into the base fertilizer | The effect appears slowly |
| Liquid fertilizer from steeped grass | Nitrogen | Ferment for 2 weeks, then dilute and use | Suited as top-dressing for leaf perilla |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The use of oil cake as fertilizer deserves separate mention. Oil cake left after pressing oil contains 5-6% nitrogen, which is better than most organic fertilizers. However, if it is put into the field as is, it rots rapidly in the soil, giving off heat and gas and damaging the roots. It is used after being soaked with water and left to ferment for a month or more, or mixed into a compost pile and rotted together. Putting oil cake back into the sesame field closes the cycle, but since continuous cropping has to be avoided, in practice it is better to direct it to other crops.

## 6. Sowing

### 6-1. Planting Time

| Crop | Sowing Time in South Korea | Harvest Time in South Korea | Criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sesame | Mid-May to early June | Late September to early October | Air temperature of 15°C or higher, with soil temperature close to 20°C |
| Leaf perilla | Late April to early May | Late June to late September | After the risk of frost has passed |
| Oil perilla | May to June | October | Can be planted late |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There are two reasons why sesame sowing is late, after mid-May. One is soil temperature. If the soil has not warmed enough, germination is late and uneven, and damping-off sets in. The other is matching the timing of flowering and ripening. Sesame flowers in July-August and needs 20-28°C for ripening. If it is planted too early, flowering overlaps with the monsoon and the flowers drop, and if planted too late, ripening runs into the cool temperatures of autumn.

### 6-2. Sowing Sesame

| Item | Standard | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ridge spacing | 55-60cm | Secures ventilation and reduces humidity |
| Plant spacing | 15-20cm | Set narrower for Oedae-kkae and wider for Gaji-kkae |
| Sowing depth | 2-3cm | The seeds are small, but not as small as amaranth, so they can handle this depth |
| Per hole | 5-6 seeds | Only the strongest one is kept later |
| Seeding rate | 0.3-0.6kg per 1,000m² | The seeds are small, so a small amount sows a large area |
| Firming | Press lightly after sowing | The seed has to be in close contact with the soil to take up water |

### 6-3. Sowing Leaf Perilla

| Item | Standard | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Planting spacing | 10 × 5cm or 7 × 7cm | Leaves are picked, so it is planted densely |
| Sowing depth | 0.5-1cm | Sown shallower than sesame |
| Per hole | 5-6 seeds | Thin and keep one |
| Germination care | Cover with straw, cloth or newspaper | The aim is to keep the topsoil from drying out. Remove it as soon as the sprouts emerge |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The timing for removing the cover matters. If it is left on after the sprouts have come up, the young stems reach for light, grow thin and long, and fall over. Peek under the soil surface, and as soon as sprouts begin to show, remove the cover that same day.

### 6-4. Seed Treatment

| Method | Procedure | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Soaking and sorting in water | Soak in water for 3-4 hours and use only the seeds that sink | Hollow, empty seeds float. The germination rate rises |
| Hot-water treatment | Soak in 50-55°C water for 10-15 minutes | Reduces pathogens on the seed coat. Especially important for preventing damping-off |
| Coating with wood ash | Coat washed, drained seeds with fine ash | Doubles as disinfection and keeps seeds from sticking together |

> **For sesame, seed disinfection is not optional. Damping-off is a disease carried on seed, and once it spreads at the seedling stage, the whole spot has to be replanted. Where no chemicals are available, hot-water treatment and wood ash take their place.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In hot-water treatment, temperature is decisive. Above 55°C the embryos of the seeds die, and below 45°C the pathogens survive. If there is no thermometer, mix boiled water and cold water and use as the guide the point at which the water feels hot when you dip your hand in but does not scald, in other words, hot enough that it is hard to keep your hand in for long. Spread the treated seeds out to dry right away, then sow them.

### 6-5. Thinning

| Crop | Round | Timing | What to Leave |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sesame | 1st | 2-4 true leaves | The strongest one per hole |
| Leaf perilla | 1st | 1-2 true leaves | Roughly even out the spacing |
| Leaf perilla | 2nd | 4-6 true leaves | One per hole |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sesame is thinned just once and that is all. If thinning is late, the plants compete for light, grow leggy and thin, and stems grown that way later cannot bear the weight of the capsules and lie down. The moment is between two and four true leaves, and if this is missed, pulling a seedling also lifts the roots of its neighbor.

## 7. Water Management - Waterlogging Is More Dangerous Than Drought

### 7-1. Why Waterlogging Is Fatal

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sesame is the archetype of a crop that is strong against drought and weak against water. These two traits come from the same root structure. To find water in dry ground, the roots have to go down deep and thick, and such roots, being so active, use a great deal of oxygen. When the gaps in the soil are filled with water and the air is pushed out, the root cells are suffocated at once.

| Time | What Happens |
|---|---|
| A few hours of flooding | The oxygen in the roots runs out and nutrient uptake stops |
| One day | The leaves begin to wilt. They appear to wilt even though water is overflowing |
| Two days | The root tips turn brown and begin to rot |
| Three days or more | The late blight pathogen burrows into the damaged roots and spreads to the base of the stem |
| After the water drains | When the sun comes out, the remaining roots cannot cope, and the plants instead wilt sharply |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The last row is the most common scene of failure. It is frequent for the field to collapse all at once as the monsoon ends and the sun comes out. This is not because things improved once the water drained, but because the damaged roots cannot cope with the suddenly increased transpiration. There is not much that can be done at that point. Raising the ridges in the first place so that water does not reach the root layer is the only countermeasure.

### 7-2. Water Management by Stage

| Period | Sesame | Leaf perilla |
|---|---|---|
| Sowing to germination | Keep the topsoil from drying out | Moisten thoroughly |
| Seedling stage | Hold back on water. Let the roots go down in search of water | Keep from drying out |
| Vegetative growth stage | Leave it to natural rainfall. Keeping water from pooling is all there is to do | Water thoroughly when the topsoil turns pale |
| Flowering and fruit-setting stage | Supplement only in severe drought | Keep the leaves from wilting |
| Monsoon season | Maintain the furrows and drainage ditches | The same |
| Ripening stage | Do not water | Not applicable |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The advice to hold back on water at the seedling stage differs from other crops. If young sesame is watered often, the roots stay in the topsoil and do not go down. Plants grown that way collapse immediately when drought comes later. After germination is finished, it is better to let the soil dry out a little so that the roots reach down in search of water.

### 7-3. Preparing for the Monsoon

| Task | Timing | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Clearing the furrows | Before the monsoon | Remove the soil and weeds that have built up in the furrows to open the water path |
| Digging drainage ditches | Before the monsoon | Dig around the edge of the field to make a path for water to drain |
| Cutting an upslope diversion ditch | Before the monsoon | On a sloping field, divert water coming down from above to the side |
| Hilling (earthing up) | June | Gather soil around the base to raise the ridge again. Restores what the rain has worn away |
| Righting fallen plants | Right after rain | Stand them up while the soil is soft and firm the soil with your feet |
| Removing diseased plants | As soon as found | Pull plants infected with late blight and take them out of the field |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The last row is especially important. Late blight spreads fast and there is practically no way to treat it. When it is found on a single plant, remove that plant together with the soil around it, carry it out of the field, and burn it or bury it deep. If it is left in the field, rain washes it down to the plants below.

### 7-4. When Water Is Short

| Symptom | What Is Happening |
|---|---|
| Leaves droop at midday and recover in the evening | A temporary shortage, not yet damage |
| Lower leaves turn yellow and drop first | A chronic shortage. The plant is shedding leaves to reduce transpiration |
| Flowers open and then drop right away | Drought at flowering. The number of capsules decreases |
| Capsules form but the seeds do not fill | Drought during ripening. The oil content drops as well |
| Short stature with short internodes | A shortage that has continued since early growth |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For sesame, water is most valuable at flowering and early ripening. Drought at this time directly cuts the number of capsules and the size of the grains, and even lowers the oil content. If there is room to supply water, giving it once or twice at this time is more effective than at any other time. However, avoid irrigating by filling the furrows; pouring water around the roots is safer. If the furrows are filled, the water soaks into the ridge and creates a risk of waterlogging.

## 8. Crop Management - How Pinching Synchronizes Ripening

### 8-1. Why Pinch the Tips?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The single most important task in managing a sesame crop is pinching. The reason lies in the trait of indeterminate inflorescence that we saw in Section 2. Sesame keeps producing new flowers as long as the tip of the stem is growing. They open in sequence from bottom to top, and while the lower capsules are filling, new flowers are still opening higher up.

| Without pinching | With pinching |
|---|---|
| Flowering continues for more than 30-40 days | Flowering stops at the point where the tip was cut |
| Resources are shared with late-opening flowers | Resources are channeled to the capsules already set |
| All seeds end up smaller | The seeds grow larger |
| The ripening times spread far apart | The ripening times are concentrated |
| Harvest decisions are difficult and losses are large | Harvest is finished in one go |
| The plants grow tall and fall over easily | The plants are shorter and fall over less |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This principle can be summarized in one line. The plant tries to open more flowers than it can fill, and the grower cuts it off so that only those up to a certain point are filled with seed. If the cut comes late, the effect is small, and if it comes early, you lose capsules that could have set.

| Item | Standard |
|---|---|
| Timing | 35-40 days after the first flower opens |
| Method | Pinch off the young tip at the end of the stem by hand or cut it with scissors |
| Where to cut | Cut just below the topmost flower bud |
| For Gaji-kkae | Cut the tips of the side branches as well |
| Weather | Do it on a clear morning. The cut surface heals over quickly |

> **Pinching sesame means cutting off the plant's greed. If it tries to fill even the flowers that opened late, all the seeds end up small, and only when it is cut off at some line do the ones that set earlier fill properly.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There is another way to decide the timing of pinching. If you do not remember the day the first flower opened, count the capsules from the bottom of the stem and take the point when about 20 to 25 have formed as your guide. Any more than that will not fill completely anyway.

### 8-2. Pinching Leaf Perilla

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In leaf perilla, pinching has a different purpose. It is meant not to make the seeds larger but to increase the side shoots so that there are more places for leaves to come out, and to lower the height so that the plants do not fall over.

| Item | Standard |
|---|---|
| Timing | When the plant exceeds 1m in height |
| Method | Cut off the top shoot |
| Effect | Side shoots increase, so the leaf harvest grows and lodging decreases |

### 8-3. Preventing Lodging

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sesame grows taller than 1m and has thin stems, so it easily lies down in wind and rain. Once it is down, the capsules touch the ground so the seeds rot or get buried in the soil, and disease enters at the places where the stems have snapped.

| Method | Timing | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Hilling (earthing up) | At 30-40cm in height | Gather soil up around the base. It also serves to raise the ridge again |
| Reducing nitrogen | From the start | With too much nitrogen the stems are soft and lie down easily |
| Not planting densely | At sowing | If crowded, plants compete for light and grow thin and long |
| Pinching | 35-40 days after the first flower | Lowers the height |
| Staking with string | At 80cm in height or more | Drive stakes at both ends of the row and run string across to support the plants |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Where there are no tools, the cheapest countermeasures are hilling and reducing nitrogen. Neither needs any materials, and hilling in particular is done in a single motion with raising the ridges again, so it doubles as monsoon preparation.

### 8-4. Weed Management

| Timing | Method | Remarks |
|---|---|---|
| Before sowing | Till to make weed seedlings sprout, then scrape them off shallowly | Greatly lightens the early burden |
| Seedling stage | Scrape shallowly between the rows by hand or with a hoe | The sesame roots are shallow at this stage, so do not scrape deeply |
| After 5 true leaves | Weed while hilling at the same time | Two jobs in one motion |
| After the plants close the canopy | Almost unnecessary | The sesame casts shade and suppresses the weeds |
