## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Maize (Zea mays) is a crop that people improved over thousands of years from teosinte, a wild grass of Mexico. Its seeds are wrapped in husks and cannot scatter by themselves, so it exists only when people plant it. Its greatest strength under disaster conditions is calories per area. It yields 600 kg per 1,000㎡ (2,148 kcal per ㎡), 0.82 times the self-sufficiency line, the highest of any grain. The price is heavy fertilizer needs, depletion of soil fertility, the problem of F1 seed, and the risk of pellagra. This document focuses on how to meet those conditions.

## 2. Plant Overview
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Maize is an annual grass of the genus Zea in the grass family, native to the tropical highlands of Central and South America. It uses C4 photosynthesis, so sunlight directly determines yield, and it is vulnerable to frost, flooding, and shade. The stalk is 1.5-3 m tall and is held up by brace roots at the base. The male flowers are borne at the top of the stalk and the female flowers in the leaf axils, and each silk (the thread of the female flower) connects to one kernel. A place on the cob where the silk received no pollen becomes an empty kernel.

| Stage | Timing | Key points |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Germination and seedling stage | 3 weeks after sowing | Phosphorus and nitrogen are important |
| Vegetative growth stage | Weeks 3-6 | Stalk and leaves increase rapidly |
| Silking and flowering stage | Weeks 6-8 | The pollination period. Drought, heat above 35℃, or a nutrient shortage leads to empty ears |
| Grain-filling stage | Weeks 8-12 | Starch fills the kernels |
| Maturity | After week 12 | Kernels harden and a black layer forms at the base |

> **The two weeks before and after silking decide the entire harvest. If water can be given only once, give it then.**

| Nutrition per 100 g | Content |
|:---|:---|
| Calories | 358 kcal (dry kernels). Steamed green corn is 132 kcal |
| Carbohydrate / fat | About 70 g / about 4 g (the fat is concentrated in the germ) |
| Protein | 9.94 g (P:E 11.1%) |
| Abundant | Dietary fiber, potassium, phosphorus |
| Lacking | Lysine, tryptophan, absorbable niacin |

## 3. Growing Environment and Varieties
| Condition | Optimum | Limits and effects |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Growing temperature | 20-30℃ | Growth stops below 12℃ and frost kills the plant |
| Germination | 32-34℃ | Emergence takes 18-20 days at a soil temperature of 13℃ and 8-10 days at 15-18℃ |
| Silking stage | 25-28℃ | Above 35℃ the pollen dies and the ears are empty |
| Light | 8-10 hours of direct sunlight a day | At least 6 hours needed. Yield is 45% under 40% shading |
| Water | 550 mm in total | 400-700 mm. Drought at silking is the most damaging |
| Soil | Well-drained sandy loam to loam, pH 6.0-6.8 | Phosphorus becomes bound below pH 5.5 |
| Flooding | None | Up to 3 days. Four days or more and yield collapses |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Assign maize the most fertile, sunniest flat land, and fields close to the compost heap or livestock shed, and give poor scraps of land to sorghum or foxtail millet. Avoid shaded spots and low ground where water collects.

| Variety | Maturity | Use and disaster perspective |
|:---|:---:|:---|
| Waxy corn | 80-90 days | For green corn. A food for one summer season |
| Super-sweet corn | 70-80 days | Can be eaten raw, hard to store |
| Godangok | About 80 days | Two crops a year are possible |
| Grain and feed types | 90-100 days | Dried for storage and milling. Only this type can serve as a long-term stockpile |
| Popcorn type | - | Popping uses almost no fuel |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Much of the commercial seed is F1 hybrid, and if seed taken from it is planted, inbreeding depression cuts the harvest to less than half. If the disaster is expected to last long, secure landraces or open-pollinated varieties. Keep waxy corn at least 200 m from other varieties, or offset the sowing by at least 3 weeks.

## 4. Field Preparation and Sowing
| Item | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Preparing the soil | Till to at least 18 cm (25-30 cm if possible), dig drainage channels around the field and in the furrows, and make ridges 15-20 cm high |
| Acidity | pH 6.0-6.8. Correct acid soil with wood ash |
| Nitrogen (N) | 10-15 kg per 1,000㎡. 50% as basal fertilizer and 50% as top dressing at the 5-7 leaf stage |
| Phosphorus (P) / potassium (K) | 8-10 kg each. All the phosphorus as basal fertilizer and the potassium split |
| Compost | 1,000-1,500 kg. Fully matured compost, all as basal fertilizer |

| Substitute fertilizers | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Legume green manure | Plowing under hairy vetch or Chinese milk vetch as the preceding crop gives 8-12 kg of nitrogen. The most effective |
| Interplanted beans | Plant beans between the maize plants |
| Urine | Dilute 10 times with water and apply along the furrows at the 5-7 leaf stage. The main top dressing |
| Wood ash | Spread 40-60 kg. Supplies potassium and phosphorus and corrects acidity |
| Rotted compost | Let leaves, grass, and manure rot for 3-6 months. Start piling it the autumn before |
| Night-soil compost | Mix with sawdust or rice hulls and let it rot completely for 6 months or more. If not fully rotted, pathogens remain |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;10-15 kg of nitrogen equals 2-3 tons of matured compost, so planting maize without a fertilizer plan is only going through the motions.

| Sowing | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Timing | Early April in the south and mid-April in the center. Soil of 12℃ or more is the criterion. A later crop goes in during early July, and the limit is late June. Sow in batches 1-2 weeks apart |
| Seed selection | Large, even kernels from the middle of the ear. Discard kernels that float in water |
| Seed disinfection | Dusting with wood ash (also keeps birds and ants away), 50-55℃ water for 10 minutes, wood vinegar diluted 500 times for 6-12 hours |
| Planting | Depth 3-5 cm, 70 cm between rows, 25 cm between plants, 2 kernels per hole |
| Layout | Group at least four rows into a block. In a single row the pollen blows out of the field |
| Thinning | Leave 1 plant when 15 cm tall. Cut at the base rather than pulling |

## 5. Water, Cultivation Management, and Pests and Diseases
| Management | Content |
|:---|:---|
| Water | Keep the soil from drying at germination, give plenty during vegetative growth and grain filling, **always give plenty at silking and flowering,** cut off at maturity, and in the rainy season drainage is the management |
| First weeding | 2 weeks after emergence |
| Second weeding | At the 5-7 leaf stage. Do hilling (soil 20 cm high at the base) and top dressing at the same time |
| Hand pollination | If seed set is poor, shake the male flowers after the dew has dried so the pollen falls. Repeat over several days |
| Other | Do not remove side shoots or lower leaves. Stand up lodged plants and hill them. Cover ears with paper bags as they ripen to keep birds off |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Of the unprotected potential loss of 68.5% in maize, 40.3 percentage points come from weeds. Weeds are solved by hand, not by pesticides, so concentrate the weeding in the 30-45 days after sowing.

| Pest or disease | Symptoms and timing | Response without pesticides |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Ear rot | Rain during grain filling. Risk of mycotoxins | Harvest early, strip the husks and dry, discard diseased ears |
| Smut | White galls form on ears and stalks and burst into black powder | Cut off and burn before they burst, rotation |
| Stalk and root rot | Contaminated water, excess moisture | Improve drainage, furrow irrigation |
| Leaf spot, sheath blight | Hot humid weather, dense planting | Ensure airflow, burn diseased leaves |
| Asian corn borer | 3 generations a year (late May to early October). Larvae bore into the stalk | Burning the stalks or chopping and burying them after harvest is the most effective. Remove egg masses, protect parasitic wasps |
| Aphids | Suck sap and cause sooty mold | Spray soapy water (5-10 g of pure soap per 1 L of water) |
| Birds, rats | Grain filling and storage | Paper bags, nets, metal containers |

| Deficiency symptom | Cause and remedy |
|:---|:---|
| Lower leaves yellow in a V shape along the midrib | Nitrogen deficiency. Diluted urine, rotted compost |
| Leaves dark purple | Phosphorus deficiency or cold. Bone meal, wood ash |
| Leaf edges scorched | Potassium deficiency. Scatter wood ash between plants |
| No kernels at the ear tip | Lack of nutrients or water at silking. Bring top dressing forward and irrigate |

## 6. Companion Planting and Rotation
| Crop | Role |
|:---|:---|
| Maize | The support. Climbing beans climb it |
| Beans | Rhizobia fix nitrogen from the air |
| Squash | Broad leaves cover the ground and suppress weeds and evaporation |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is the Three Sisters method of Native Americans. Plant the maize first, the beans when the maize is knee-high, and the squash after that. If the beans are planted too early they wind around the maize and pull it down. The beans supply the lysine and tryptophan that maize lacks, so the nutrition is complementary too. Planting with chili pepper or paprika reduced viral disease by 72%. Avoid sorghum and foxtail millet, which are also grasses and share pests and diseases.

| Rotation | Crop |
|:---|:---|
| Year 1 | Maize (interplanted with beans) |
| Year 2 | Legumes alone (soybean, adzuki bean, cowpea) |
| Year 3 | Leafy or root vegetables |
| Year 4 | Grains (sorghum, foxtail millet, oat) |
| Year 5 | Back to maize |

> **Continuous cropping of maize collapses in the third year. Even where land is scarce, at least alternate maize and beans every year.**

## 7. Harvest, Drying, and Seed Saving
| Type | Timing and criterion |
|:---|:---|
| Green corn | 22-25 days after silking. Pressed, the kernel gives white juice and a cheese-like consistency. Pick in the early morning. The best time lasts only a few days, so sow in batches |
| Dry kernels | 50-60 days after silking. A black layer forms at the base and a fingernail does not sink in |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Snap the ear downward and twist it off, leave two or three husks, and braid the ears together to hang under the eaves. Pick out ears showing mold separately and discard them, and burn the stalks left in the field after harvest or chop and bury them.

| Drying | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Target | Bring moisture from 25-35% down to 13-14%. Mold grows above 15% |
| Sequence | Hang to dry for 3-6 weeks → sun-dry on the cob for 3-5 days → shell → sun-dry the kernels for 2-4 days → rest in the shade for a day to even out |
| Judging | Twisting makes the kernels fall off in a rush, a fingernail leaves no mark, and a kernel cracks crisply when bitten |
| Heat drying | 50℃ or lower. Above that the seed dies and the taste worsens |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most practical way to shell is to remove the first row with the thumb to make an empty groove and then push the rest sideways. You can also rub two dry ears together, put them in a sack and trample them, or rub them on a board studded with bottle caps or nails. The cob is a high-heat fuel and its ash is a potassium fertilizer. Separate the chaff by winnowing or sieving, and pick out moldy and insect-eaten kernels by hand.

| Seed saving | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| F1 | Seed saving is impossible. Only open-pollinated varieties and landraces work |
| Isolation | At least 200 m from other varieties, or offset sowing by at least 3 weeks, or cover the ear with a paper bag before the silks emerge and shake in pollen of the same variety |
| Selection | From 20-30 plants that are disease-free and not lodged, evenly. Use only kernels from the middle of the ear |
| Storage | Moisture 12-13%, sealed, in a cool dark place. 2-3 years at room temperature |
| Germination test | Wrap 20 kernels in a damp cloth for 5-7 days. 18 or more is normal, 14-17 means 3 per hole, 12 or fewer means replace |

## 8. Storage
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The storability of SS applies only to completely dry kernels. Green corn lasts only three days at room temperature. The enemies of storage are mold (moisture of 15% or more, aflatoxin), insects (20℃ or more, moisture of 12% or more), and rats.

| Item | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Conditions | Moisture 13-14% (12-13% for seed), temperature 15℃ or less, surrounding humidity 65% or less, light blocked, checked every two months |
| Storing on the cob | Good airflow means less mold, but the volume is three times or more and rats reach it easily. For what will be eaten by spring |
| Storing as kernels | Small volume and easy to seal. Only when complete drying is confirmed. For what will last into the next year |
| Containers | Metal drum or glass jar 3 years or more / earthenware jar 2-3 years / thick plastic 1 year / sack 6 months |

| Insect control | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Complete sealing | Fill the container to the brim, shake it to settle, and fill again. Oxygen is used up and the insects die |
| Wood ash | Mix fine ash into 2-5% of the grain volume. Wash it off before eating |
| Diatomaceous earth | 1-3 g per 1 kg of grain. Works only when dry |
| Supplements | Dried chili pepper, charcoal, roasted salt, mugwort leaves |
| Heat and cold | Spread thinly in the sun at 60℃ for a few hours (not for seed), or leave outdoors for a few days in midwinter |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Every two months check for condensation on the container surface and a moldy smell, small holes in the kernels and powder at the bottom (weevils), cobweb-like threads on the surface (Indian meal moth), warmth when held in the hand (too much moisture), and signs of rats.

## 9. Cooking and Cautions
> **If maize is the staple, without beans and without alkali treatment, pellagra (niacin deficiency) comes within a few months.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The niacin in maize is in a bound form that cannot be absorbed, and tryptophan is also lacking. Symptoms are inflammation of sun-exposed skin, diarrhea, and mental disturbance, and it can end in death. It can be prevented by eating maize with beans or by alkali treatment.

| Step | Alkali treatment (nixtamalization) |
|:---:|:---|
| 1 | For 1 part of dry kernels, use 3 parts of water, and add wood ash at 5% of the kernel volume (1% for quicklime) |
| 2 | Bring to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer for 30-50 minutes |
| 3 | Turn off the heat and leave overnight |
| 4 | Pour off the water and rub by hand to remove the skins |
| 5 | Rinse three or four times until the water runs clear |
| 6 | Cook as it is, grind it, or dry it |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This treatment makes niacin absorbable, removes the skins, and softens the kernels so that milling is easier. Dry maize is very hard and difficult to grind as it is, so go through alkali treatment or roasting first, and grind only a month's worth of flour at a time. Always soak and cook dry kernels.

> **Moldy maize does not become safe by washing, boiling, or roasting. Do not feed it to people or livestock; discard it.**

| Cooking | Fuel and tips |
|:---|:---|
| Flour porridge | Stir flour into water and cook for 10 minutes. Uses the least fuel |
| Green corn | Steam for 20 minutes with a little water |
| Residual-heat cooking | Boil for 10 minutes, wrap in straw or a quilt, and leave for an hour |
| Popcorn type | Put in a hot pot, cover, and shake for a few minutes |
| Boiling dry kernels as they are | Soak overnight and boil for an hour or more. Uses the most fuel |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Processing that also serves storage includes drying after alkali treatment (sealed, 1 year), roasted flour (3-6 months), starch (several years, insects do not enter), dried green corn (1 year), and maize syrup. The husks are used in place of string and the silks are dried for tea.

## 10. Annual Work Schedule
| Month | Work |
|:---|:---|
| January-February | Check storage and seed, plan and turn the compost, germination test |
| March | Deep tilling, drainage channels, basal fertilizer, spreading wood ash |
| April | Sowing (soil 12℃ or more, 1-2 weeks apart) |
| May | Thinning, first weeding, watch for the first generation of corn borer |
| June | Top dressing, second weeding, hilling, sowing beans |
| July | Intensive irrigation at silking, hand pollination |
| August | Harvest green corn, keep birds away |
| September-October | Harvest dry kernels (check the black layer), strip husks and hang to dry, deal with stalk residue |
| November-December | Shelling, final drying, storage, keep seed separate, alkali treatment and milling |

> **Five things not to forget: a fertilizer plan, no continuous cropping, planting several rows in a block, securing open-pollinated seed, and alkali treatment.**
