## 5. Field Preparation and Fertilizer

### 5-1. Choosing a Site

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The priorities for choosing a site for amaranth are clear. Sun comes first, drainage second. As the E grade for sunlight indicates, the plant loses half its yield in shade, and its roots rot if water stands. How much fertilizer the soil holds is a secondary question. Since this crop grows even on poor land, a sunny, well-drained dry slope beats a rich, damp lowland.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Growing up to 2m tall also affects placement. Put it at the north end of the field and it will not shade shorter crops to the south. On the other hand, avoid a spot with strong wind: even with a thick stem, the seed heads grow heavy and the plant lodges.

### 5-2. Preparing the Field

| Item | Method | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tilling | Till and break up the soil to a depth of 20-30cm | The taproot must go down deep to withstand drought. Break up any hardpan layer underneath |
| Raised bed | Build it at least 20cm high | Waterlogging is more dangerous than drought |
| Drainage ditch | Cut a furrow along the edge of the field | Gives water somewhere to drain during the monsoon season |
| Leveling the soil | Break the surface up fine and level it | Since the seed is small, large clods bury it or leave it with too little contact |
| Firming | Press the surface down before and after sowing | The tiny seed must be in close contact with the soil to take up water |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Breaking the surface fine and firming it down are the two most important tasks in preparing an amaranth field. Work that can be done carelessly for other crops decides the germination rate for amaranth. Since the seed is only 1mm across, one that falls into a gap between clods stays buried and never comes up, and if it does not touch enough soil it cannot take up water and simply dries out.

### 5-3. Fertilizer Amounts

| Fertilizer | Per 1,000m² | Per m² | Notes |
|---|:---:|:---:|---|
| Nitrogen | 8kg | 2.4g | Too much makes it grow leggy and lodge |
| Phosphorus | 12kg | 3.6g | All applied as base fertilizer |
| Potassium | 12kg | 3.6g | All applied as base fertilizer |
| Compost | 1,000kg | 300g | Use fully matured compost |
| Dolomitic lime | 120kg | 36g | Add only when the soil is strongly acidic |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;What stands out in this table is how little nitrogen there is — roughly 2/3 the level of phosphorus or potassium. That is the exact opposite of most crops, where nitrogen is applied in the largest amount. There are two reasons. One is that, being a C4 plant, it needs less nitrogen to carry out the same amount of photosynthesis. The other is that too much nitrogen only makes the stems and leaves grow lush, causing the plant to lodge and the seed to fill poorly.

> **Deciding to give amaranth plenty of nitrogen is almost always a mistake. The plant grows taller and the leaves grow lush, but the seed heads grow light, and a single storm lays the whole field flat.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When the goal is leaves, however, the picture changes. When growing it as a leafy vegetable, it is better to give a bit more nitrogen to make the leaves larger and more tender. Keep in mind that the fertilizing strategy splits depending on whether seed or leaf is the goal.

| Type | Timing | What | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base fertilizer | 2 weeks before sowing | All the compost, all the phosphorus and potassium, and all or half the nitrogen | Phosphorus barely moves through soil, so it must be mixed in beforehand |
| Top-dressing | Mid-July, optional | A small amount of nitrogen | Give it only if leaf harvest is continuing. Stop once the seed heads begin to form |

### 5-4. When There Is No Chemical Fertilizer

| Substitute | Nutrients supplied | How to use | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wood ash | Potassium, phosphorus, calcium | Mix a handful per m² in as base fertilizer | Alkaline, so use less if soil pH is already high |
| Fully matured compost | All-round | About 300g per m² as base fertilizer | Under-composted material damages young roots |
| Diluted urine | Nitrogen, potassium | Dilute 10 to 20-fold and pour around the roots | Too much nitrogen causes lodging. Do not use once seed heads have formed |
| Bone meal | Phosphorus, calcium | Char, grind, and mix into base fertilizer | Acts slowly, which suits base fertilizer |
| Shell and eggshell | Calcium | Char, grind, and scatter | Used in place of dolomitic lime to correct acidic soil |
| Fermented plant liquid fertilizer | Nitrogen | Ferment 2 weeks, then dilute 5 to 10-fold | Use only during the early growth stage |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Amaranth is a light feeder, so these substitute resources alone cover most of its needs. The same logic explains why its B grade, 45.1%, for yield retention without synthetic inputs on the evaluation table beats leafy vegetables' 40.8%. It loses less than other crops when chemical fertilizer is cut off. Working in wood ash and compost as base fertilizer while giving almost no nitrogen actually suits this crop well.

## 6. Sowing — Handling Seeds Smaller Than a Millet Grain

### 6-1. Why Sowing Is Difficult

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sowing is the stage where amaranth cultivation fails most often. There is one cause: the seed is simply too small. With a seed where 1,000 grains weigh less than 1g, all the usual rules of thumb for sowing break down.

| Problem | What happens |
|---|---|
| Buried too deep, it cannot emerge | With so little food reserve in the seed, its pushing power does not exceed 1cm |
| Hard to judge how much to sow | A handful in your hand is tens of thousands of seeds. Sow it as is and there is no room to set a foot |
| Blows away in the wind | The seed is so light it blows away even while you are sowing |
| Dries out without soil contact | Suspended between soil particles, it cannot take up water |
| Weak sprout | The sprout that emerges is thread-thin and dies if the soil covers it even slightly |

> **The two rules of sowing amaranth are to bury it very shallowly and to always press it down firmly. Keep these two, and the rest largely takes care of itself.**

### 6-2. Timing

| Method | Sowing time | Harvest time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct sowing in the field | Late April to early May | Early August to mid-September | Soil temperature above 15 degrees, after frost has fully ended |
| Transplanting after raising seedlings | Raise seedlings in early March | From late July | Needs an indoor space or a hotbed |
| Late sowing for leaf purposes | Through June | From August | A method that gives up on seed and harvests only leaves |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Direct sowing is the standard method. Because amaranth's taproot goes down deep, transplanting cuts that root, and drought tolerance drops sharply. Even when seedlings are raised, they must be moved while still young, lifted with the soil intact so the roots are not damaged.

### 6-3. Sowing Method

| Item | Standard | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Sowing depth | 0.5-1cm | Any deeper and the sprout cannot push through the soil. Sometimes no soil is added at all — just pressing down is enough |
| Row spacing | 20-30cm | Wider when the goal is seed, narrower when the goal is leaf |
| Plant spacing | 40-50cm | Secures airflow and sunlight. Achieved by thinning |
| Sowing method | Row sowing or spot sowing | The seed is too small for broadcast sowing to be manageable |
| Seed amount | 200-400g per 1,000m² | Germination rate is high, so even a small amount is enough |
| Firming | Press down with a board or your feet after sowing | Brings soil and seed into close contact so water can transfer |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There is a technique for when the seed is too small to sow evenly. Mix it with dry sand, fine soil, or roasted bran at ten to twenty times the seed's own volume, then sow the mixture together. The added bulk makes it easier to control by hand, and because the sown spots become visible, overlapping or missing patches happen less. Mixing in wood ash makes the spots even easier to see and adds a disinfecting effect at the same time.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The importance of firming cannot be overstated. Only when the seed touches the soil does the water clinging to soil particles transfer into it. A large seed can hold enough water of its own to survive even sitting slightly off the ground, but an amaranth seed has no such margin. This one action — pressing with a board or stepping on the ground after sowing — makes a large difference to the germination rate.

### 6-4. Seed Treatment

| Method | Procedure | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-water treatment | Soak in 50-55°C water for 10-15 minutes | Reduces surface pathogens. Above 55°C the seed dies |
| Coating with wood ash | Coat the seed with fine ash | Doubles as disinfection and makes the seed more visible when sowing |
| Sorting | Pick out damaged or discolored seed | Since the seed is too small to sort by eye, float it in water and discard what rises |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Amaranth seed does not need soaking. Its coat is thin and takes up water quickly, and soaked seed actually sticks together, making it harder to sow.

### 6-5. Thinning

| Round | Timing | Spacing left |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | 2-3 true leaves | 20cm between plants |
| 2nd | 4-5 true leaves | 40-50cm between plants |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The thinned young plants are eaten right away as namul (a seasoned vegetable side dish). This thinning namul is the first thing you get to eat from growing amaranth. When pulling them, though, be careful not to lift the roots of neighboring plants along with them, since roots tangle together where the seed was sown thickly. If pulling is difficult, cut the plant off at ground level with scissors instead.

## 7. Water Management

### 7-1. Two Distinct Periods

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Water management for amaranth is completely different in the early stage than afterward. From sowing until it has 2-3 true leaves, it is more sensitive to water than almost any other crop; once past that stage, it is more drought-tolerant than almost any other crop.

| Stage | Water management | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Sowing to germination | Keep the topsoil from drying out. Moisten it once or twice a day | The seed is small and holds little water, and it dies immediately if the topsoil dries out |
| Seedling stage (2-3 true leaves) | Keep the soil continuously moist | The roots are still shallow and depend only on the topsoil |
| Vegetative growth stage | Leave it to natural rainfall | The taproot has gone deep enough to find its own water |
| Flowering and ripening stage | Supplement only during severe drought | Water shortage at this stage directly cuts the seed yield |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;How you water matters in the early stage. Pour a heavy stream and it simply washes the tiny seed and young sprouts away. Sprinkle finely with a watering can, or, lacking one, pour water over a covering of straw or cloth so it seeps in slowly.

### 7-2. What Mulching Does

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Without plastic sheeting, the surest way to keep early moisture in is a thin covering of straw or dry grass. With amaranth, though, how you apply it is different.

| Timing | How to mulch | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Right after sowing | Very thinly, thin enough that the soil still shows through | If thick, the thread-thin sprout cannot push through |
| After sprouting | Add a little more between the sprouts | Blocks evaporation and suppresses weeds |
| After the plants are grown | A thick layer is fine now | Suppressing weeds is now the main purpose |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;That mulching suppresses weeds is especially valuable for amaranth. Because this crop's early growth is slow and it is easily overtaken by weeds, mulching relieves a large part of that problem.

### 7-3. Too Little or Too Much Water

| Symptom | Cause | What is happening |
|---|---|---|
| Leaves wilt by day, recover by evening | Temporary water shortage | Not yet damage. Being a C4 crop, it recovers quickly |
| Leaves curl inward | Severe drought | A defensive response that reduces the evaporating surface |
| Seed heads small, grains fill poorly | Drought during flowering and ripening | Yield is directly cut. Water matters most at this stage |
| Lower leaves yellow, roots turn brown | Waterlogging | The roots cannot breathe. Drainage must be fixed |
| The whole plant wilts and the base turns mushy | Root rot from waterlogging | Hard to recover. Pull it out and fix the drainage |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For amaranth, the real losses from water problems come from waterlogging more than from drought. In drought, the plant curls its leaves and holds on, reviving once rain falls, but once the roots sit submerged, it does not revive. In a year with a long monsoon season, raising the beds and digging the furrows deep matters far more than irrigating.

## 8. Crop Management

### 8-1. Getting Through the First 3 Weeks

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The stretch where amaranth cultivation fails most is the 3 weeks after sowing. Because the seed is small, the sprout is small, and a small sprout grows slowly. Over that same stretch, weeds grow much faster. It is common, looking at the field in week 3, to find the amaranth sprouts hidden entirely under weeds.

| Cause | What happens | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| Slow early growth | Weeds steal the light, and the plant grows leggy and dies | Till the field once before sowing to sprout weed seeds, then till again to clear them |
| Weak sprout | Cannot emerge if even a little soil covers it | Sow shallow and rake the surface fine beforehand |
| Hard to tell apart from weeds | You may end up pulling amaranth while leaving weedy pigweed | Sow in rows and pull everything outside the row |
| Topsoil dries out | Shallow young roots wilt immediately | Thin mulching and frequent light watering |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sprouting weeds once before sowing and then clearing them is the single most effective technique where no herbicide is available. Till the field 2 weeks before sowing and water it to sprout the weed seeds, then scrape them out shallowly while still young. This uses up the weed seed that was sitting near the surface in one pass, so competition drops sharply while the amaranth is coming up. Tilling deep at this point would bring buried weed seed back up, so scraping shallow is the key.

> **The fight between amaranth and weeds is decided within 3 weeks. Once past those 3 weeks, amaranth grows up to 2m and shades itself, and weeds cannot win after that.**

### 8-2. Staking and Lodging

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Growing up to 2m tall with heavy seed heads, the plant lodges easily. If a typhoon or heavy rain hits in late August or September, when the seed heads are filling, the whole field can go down. Once down, the seed heads touch the ground and the seed rots, and even if you stand the plants back up, disease sets in wherever the stem bent.

| Method | Timing | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Hilling | At 30-50cm tall | Mound soil up around the base. More roots grow from the buried part of the stem, increasing support |
| Staking | At 50cm tall | Drive in a stake for each plant, or one for every few plants |
| Stringing | At 1m tall | Drive stakes at both ends of the row and run a string across to support the plants |
| Pinching | At 60-80cm tall | Cut the tip to lower the height and encourage side branches |
| Cutting back nitrogen | From the start | Too much nitrogen softens the stem and makes it lodge easily |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When materials are scarce, the cheapest methods are hilling and pinching. Hilling needs nothing but soil and a hoe; once the lower stem is buried, adventitious roots emerge from that part and the plant's own strength to stay upright increases. Pinching needs no tools at all. There is a cost to pinching, though.

| When you pinch | When you do not |
|---|---|
| Height drops, and it lodges less | One large seed head stands and concentrates the yield |
| More side branches form multiple seed heads | Management stays simple |
| Each seed head ripens at a different time | Everything ripens together, and harvest finishes in one pass |
| Better for leaf harvest | Better for seed harvest |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;So it comes down to your goal. If seed is the main purpose and you want the harvest finished in a single pass, skip the pinching and support the plant with stakes instead. If you also want to harvest leaves, or have no material for stakes, use pinching to lower the height.

### 8-3. Combining Leaf Harvest and Seed Harvest

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There is an order to getting both leaves and seed from the same plant. Pick leaves only until flowering, and stop once the seed heads begin to form. Keep picking leaves after flowering and the plant runs short on resources to fill the seed, leaving the seed heads weak.

| Timing | What to do |
|---|---|
| From day 40 to before flowering | Pick the lower leaves first. Never take more than a third of the plant at once |
| Once seed heads begin to form | Stop harvesting leaves |
| Flowering to ripening | Leave it alone |
| After seed harvest | Gather the remaining leaves and stems. Eat the leaves; use the stems as compost or fuel |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The rule of never taking more than a third of the plant at once exists so the plant keeps enough leaves to sustain itself through photosynthesis. Cross that line and recovery slows, ultimately cutting the seed yield. If you want to harvest a lot of leaf, it is better to set aside dedicated leaf plants and give up on seed from those particular plants.

### 8-4. Weed Management

| Timing | Method | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Before sowing | Till to sprout weeds, then scrape them out shallowly | The most effective step |
| Seedling stage | Scrape shallowly between rows by hand or with a hoe | Amaranth's roots are shallow and get damaged by deep scraping |
| After 5 true leaves | Weed while hilling at the same time | Does two jobs in one motion |
| Once the canopy closes | Barely needed | The leaves cover the ground and weeds cannot come up |
