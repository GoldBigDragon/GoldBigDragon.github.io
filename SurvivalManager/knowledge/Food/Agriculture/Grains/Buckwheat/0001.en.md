## Introduction

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Buckwheat (Fagopyrum esculentum) is not a grain. It isn't a member of the grass family but a broadleaf plant in the knotweed family (Polygonaceae), the same family as dock and smartweed. What we eat like a grain is its seed, not a caryopsis. This isn't a taxonomic curiosity — it's the starting point for explaining nearly everything about buckwheat. The traits that grass grains gained over thousands of years of human selection — high yield, uniform ripening, self-pollination — are mostly traits buckwheat lacks. Instead, it has what grasses don't: the speed to set seed in two months, and the ability to grow in almost any poor soil.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Buckwheat's overall grade is C, a score of 41.6. That's the lowest among the grains covered in this document. There's one reason: yield. 150kg per 1,000m², 561kcal/m² — 0.21× the self-sufficiency line. That's 25% of maize's yield. Covering one person's annual calories with buckwheat alone would take more than 470m², more than 140 pyeong. Buckwheat cannot serve as the backbone of a staple-food plan.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Even so, buckwheat remains the byword for a famine-relief crop, for other reasons. First, it takes 60-75 days from sowing to harvest — faster than any other crop in this document. In July, when summer crops have been wiped out by drought or flood; in May, when a late frost has ruined the spring planting; or on a field just plowed under after disease — sowing buckwheat there is almost the only option left for harvesting something that same year.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Second, it grows in poor land and acidic soil. Its fertilizer demand, 12g/m², is among the lowest of any crop, and it needs only 250mm of water.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Third, it needs no pest control. Its pest-control self-reliance grade is SS — a crop with nothing to do even under conditions with no pesticides at all. Fourth, its protein quality outclasses that of grains. At 13.9% of calories, with an amino-acid score above 90, it doesn't have the lysine shortfall common in grains.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The weaknesses need to be weighed too. It's frost-tender, and growth stops below 5°C. It's cross-pollinating and self-incompatible, so it cannot set seed alone — it needs bees or wind. Seed longevity is short, only one to two years, so it's hard to keep in storage. The hull is hard and must be removed, and after that the grain must be milled before it can be eaten. And in some people it triggers serious allergies. With all this in mind, this document covers in detail how to hull and grind buckwheat without tools, how to dry it down to 13-14% moisture, how to keep storage pests away without pesticides, and the issues of rutin and allergies.
