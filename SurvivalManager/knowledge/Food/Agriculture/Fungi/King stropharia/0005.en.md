## 9. Contamination, Pests, and Diseases
### 9-1. Green Mold Is the Number One Cause
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most common reason mushroom cultivation fails in a disaster is not pests or diseases but **losing the substrate to competing molds**. By far the most frequent of these is Trichoderma, known as green mold.

| Feature | Wine cap (king stropharia) mycelium | Green mold |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Color | White, with a slight yellowish tinge | White at first, soon **blue-green** |
| Texture | Thick, thread-like strands, somewhat coarse | Fine like powder, cottony |
| Spreading speed | 3-8mm a day | 10-30mm a day, **3-5 times faster** |
| Smell | Forest-soil smell, mushroom scent | Musty mold smell, sometimes a coconut-like scent |
| Margin | Even along the substrate | Radiating outward from round spots |

> **Green mold has already spread before any color shows.**
> Blue-green is the stage at which spores have formed, that is, **after it has begun to reproduce**. By that point you must assume a large part of the substrate is already lost. Early identification is made not by color but by **spreading speed and texture**.

---
### 9-2. When You Find Contamination

| Situation | Response |
|:---|:---|
| **The spot is smaller than the palm of your hand** | **Scoop it out generously**, taking the surrounding soil as well. Sprinkle wood ash on the spot to make it alkaline |
| **It has spread to several places** | Give up that section. Keep only the parts where the mycelium has won and move them elsewhere |
| **The whole substrate is blue-green** | **Discard all of it.** Burn it, or bury it far from the growing site |

> **Do not send contaminated substrate to the compost pile.**
> Green mold spores survive in the compost pile and return to the substrate the following year. Always **burn it or bury it deep in a place separate from the growing site.**

---
### 9-3. Five Ways to Prevent Contamination
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Wine cap is a mushroom grown without fully sterilizing the substrate, so the strategy against contamination is **not "sterilization" but "making sure the wine cap wins the competition."**

| Method | Principle |
|:---|:---|
| **Use plenty of spawn** | 5-10% of the substrate weight. Whoever occupies the ground first wins |
| **Make the substrate alkaline** | Raise the pH to 7.5-8.5 with slaked lime or wood ash. Green mold prefers weakly acidic conditions |
| **Do not overdo nitrogen** | When nutrients are abundant, competing molds explode first |
| **Do not pack the substrate down too hard** | The mycelium spreads properly only when air can pass through |
| **Wash your hands and tools** | Wash your hands with soap, and heat knives and shovels over a fire or dip them in boiling water |

---
### 9-4. Pests

| Pest | Damage | Response |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Fungus gnats (sciarids)** | They eat into the mycelium, and maggots infest the fruiting bodies. They carry green mold spores | Sprinkle a thin layer of wood ash on the substrate surface. If they appear, use yellow sticky traps or water-bowl traps |
| **Mites** | The mycelium turns brown and stops growing | Keep conditions dry and isolate the affected section |
| **Slugs** | They eat young fruiting bodies | Block them with a band of ash or sawdust around the bed. Pick them off by hand at night |
| **Centipedes and stink bugs** | Little direct damage, but they disturb the mycelial layer | If it is not a big problem, leave them alone |
| **Rats and birds** | They carry off fruiting bodies | Cover with netting or thorny branches |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Because this is outdoor cultivation, pests cannot be kept out completely.** Wine cap fruits in large quantities, so a harvest remains even if some is lost. Where no pesticides are available, **accepting the damage and expanding the area** is more efficient than putting effort into control.

---
## 10. Diagnosing Growth Problems

| Symptom | Cause | Action |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Mycelium does not spread | Substrate too dry, low temperature, dead spawn | Water to bring moisture to 65-70%. Check the soil temperature |
| Mycelium spreads and then stops | Substrate nutrients exhausted; oxygen shortage from excess moisture | Lay fresh substrate over the top |
| Mycelium is healthy but no mushrooms appear | Insufficient stimulus (temperature difference, moisture); substrate not yet mature | Water with cold water and remove the covering to create a temperature difference |
| Fruiting bodies small, stems only getting long | Too little light, too much carbon dioxide | Remove the covering to provide light and ventilation |
| Caps crack and dry out | Air too dry | Sprinkle water around the bed to raise the humidity |
| Stems soften and fall over | Excess moisture, bacterial soft rot | Stop watering and ventilate. Remove the diseased ones |
| Color pale and mushrooms weak | High temperature. 25°C or above | Add shade and water to lower the soil temperature |
| Fruits once and never again | Substrate nutrients used up | Return the spent substrate to the field and make new substrate |

---
## 11. Relationship with the Field, Crop Rotation, and the Spent-Substrate Cycle
### 11-1. Growing Mushrooms Among Crops
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The greatest advantage of wine cap is that it **needs no separate field**. The shade between tall crops such as maize and sunflowers, the ground under fruit trees, and the shade along fences, that is, **partial shade where crops struggle to grow**, are ideal for this mushroom.

| Combination | Effect |
|:---|:---|
| **Between maize and sorghum** | The bed gets shade and humidity, and after harvest the stalks become the next substrate |
| **Under fruit trees** | Partial shade all year round. Spent substrate feeds the fruit tree roots |
| **Under squash and cucumber vines** | The leaves provide shade and keep moisture in |
| **North side of fences and walls** | Makes use of spots where crops cannot grow |

> **This is half the reason this mushroom ranks near the top in the evaluation table.**
> The grade for Calories per area is middling, but **because the same ground can be shared with other crops, the real land cost is almost nil.**

---
### 11-2. Spent Substrate Is Not Waste
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The substrate left after the mushrooms have finished fruiting is **high-quality organic matter whose woody material the mycelium has already broken down**. It is far more decomposed than fresh rice straw, so it can go straight onto the field.

| Use | Method |
|:---|:---|
| **Base fertilizer** | Spread on the field in autumn and plow it in. It supplies more organic matter and potassium than nitrogen |
| **Mulch** | Lay it 5cm thick between crops to suppress weeds and moisture evaporation |
| **Inoculum for new substrate** | Lumps with living mycelium mixed into new substrate act as spawn |
| **Compost accelerator** | Mixed into a compost pile, it speeds up decomposition |
| **Earthworm bedding** | Earthworms eat it readily. You can obtain worm castings |

```
Rice straw, wood by-products → substrate → mushroom harvest → spent substrate → field organic matter
                                                              └ part goes to new substrate as inoculum
```

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Turning inedible rice straw and wood scraps into food, then returning what is left to the soil as fertility, **this cycle is the strength of the method in a long-term disaster.** 

---
### 11-3. How Many Years on the Same Spot
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If you keep laying substrate on top of the same spot, the density of green mold and pests rises from the second or third year.

| Year | Management |
|:--:|:---|
| Years 1-2 | It is fine to lay new substrate over the same spot |
| Year 3 | It is safer to move the bed. Remove all the old substrate and take it to the field |
| After that | After moving, rest the original spot for 1-2 years |

---
## 12. Mistaking Poisonous Mushrooms and Safety Checks
> **This section is directly tied to life and death. Confirming that a mushroom is safe to eat matters more than growing it.**

---
### 12-1. Identifying Characteristics of Wine Cap
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Even in a cultivated bed, wild mushrooms can come up mixed into the same substrate. **Never assume a mushroom is safe just because it grew on the substrate.** Check the characteristics below one by one.

| Part | Wine cap |
|:---|:---|
| Cap | Hemispherical at first, then flattening. Diameter 5-15cm, up to 30cm for large ones. **Reddish brown to wine-colored**, paler when dry |
| Cap surface | Smooth or with a fine fibrous pattern. When young, fragments of white membrane may be attached |
| **Gills** | Grayish white at first → **darkening to purple-brown and purple-black as it matures** |
| **Spore color** | **Purple-brown to purple-black.** Easily confirmed by placing the cap on white paper and collecting a spore print |
| Stem | Thick and firm. White to pale yellow. Length 8-15cm |
| **Ring on the stem** | **Present.** The membrane that covered the cap when young tears and remains on the stem |
| **Base of the stem** | **No sac (volva).** Root-like bundles of white mycelium are attached |
| Flesh | White. **Does not turn blue** when cut or bruised |
| Smell | Mushroom scent. No unpleasant odor |

---
### 12-2. What You Must Never Confuse It With - Spore Color Is the Dividing Line

| Feature | Wine cap | **Amanita species (deadly)** |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Spore color** | **Purple-brown to purple-black** | **White** |
| **Gill color** | Deepens to dark purple-brown as it matures | **White to the end** |
| **Stem base** | No sac | **Has a membranous sac (volva)** |
| Cap color | Reddish brown to wine-colored | White, yellow, green, gray, and so on |
| Where it grows | Rice straw or wood by-product substrate | **Ground near the roots of living trees** |

> **Place the cap face down on white paper, cover it with a cup, and leave it for a few hours. If the powder that falls is white, never eat it.**
> Deadly poisonous mushrooms such as the destroying angel (Amanita virosa) and the fool's mushroom (Amanita verna) have **white spores, gills that stay white to the end, and a sac at the stem base.** If even one of these three applies, do not eat it.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**You must dig to check for a volva.** If you look only at the part above ground, the sac is buried in the soil and cannot be seen. Make it **a habit to dig up the base together with the soil and check it**.

---
### 12-3. Other Look-Alikes

| Mushroom | How to tell apart |
|:---|:---|
| **Black scalycaps** | The cap has many coarse scales and they grow in clusters. Toxicity is mild, but the species are hard to tell apart |
| **Some hallucinogenic Stropharia species** | There are species in the same genus that contain hallucinogenic compounds. Do not eat any whose **flesh turns blue when cut** |
| **Ink caps** | The cap melts into black liquid as time passes. Eating them together with alcohol causes poisoning |
| **Yellow coral mushroom (Ramaria flava) and wood pinkgill (Entoloma rhodopolium)** | Umbrella-shaped cap, white spores. Strong gastrointestinal toxicity |

---
### 12-4. Rules for the First Time You Eat It
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Where no medical support is available, **the verification procedure itself is the means of defense**.

| Step | Details |
|:--:|:---|
| 1 | **Take a spore print and check its color.** If it is white, throw the mushroom away |
| 2 | **Dig up the stem base and confirm there is no sac** |
| 3 | **Always cook it thoroughly.** Do not eat it raw |
| 4 | The first time, **only one person eats a small amount (one piece)** and then waits 24 hours |
| 5 | If nothing goes wrong, increase the amount. Several people do not eat it at the same time |
| 6 | **Set aside** one specimen of the mushroom that was eaten. It can be used for identification in case of poisoning |

> **Poisoning whose symptoms appear after 6 hours or more is the most dangerous.**
> Stomach pain within 30 minutes to 2 hours of eating is usually just gastrointestinal irritation, but **vomiting and diarrhea that begin 6-24 hours later are the typical course of amatoxin poisoning, which destroys the liver**. The later the onset, the more serious it is. In this case there is a period when the symptoms subside for a while, so the person seems to have recovered, but in reality the liver is being destroyed.

---
## 13. Storage Without Refrigeration
### 13-1. Fresh, They Will Not Last Three Days
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In the evaluation table, this mushroom's Storability is **E on a fresh basis**. With a water content of around 90%, it starts to soften in two days at room temperature.

> **But once it is dried, the grade changes completely.**
> The exclusion list and the Storability grade are based on **"raw, unprocessed" mushrooms**. Drying uses almost no fuel or equipment, so in a disaster the storage problem of mushrooms is **effectively solved by drying alone.**

---
### 13-2. Drying

| Method | Conditions | Time | Result |
|:---|:---|:--:|:---|
| **Sun drying** | Slice the caps to 4-6mm and spread them out on a drying screen | 2-3 sunny days | The easiest, and vitamin D increases |
| **Shade and air drying** | Under well-ventilated eaves | 4-7 days | Color and aroma are well preserved |
| **Stringing and hanging** | Thread whole mushrooms on string and hang them under the eaves | 5-10 days | Takes up less space |
| **Drying beside a fire** | A shelf above the hearth, 40-50°C | One day | Usable in the monsoon season (jangma, late June to July in South Korea). Take care not to burn them |

| Stage | Judgment |
|:---|:---|
| Fully dried | **When you break it by hand, it snaps with a crisp sound.** If it bends, it is not yet dry enough |
| Storage | After cooling completely, **in a sealed container together with charcoal or roasted salt** |
| Period | **1-2 years** in a cool, dark place. Longer if ground into powder |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Slicing before drying is better than drying whole.** Thick stems do not dry through to the core and develop mold. Because this mushroom in particular has thick stems, **always split them** before drying.

---
### 13-3. Other Storage Methods

| Method | How to make it | Period |
|:---|:---|:--:|
| **Salt curing** | Blanch, drain, and layer with salt at 20% of the weight | 6 months-1 year |
| **Jangajji (soy-sauce pickle)** | Blanch and soak in soy sauce and vinegar | 3-6 months |
| **Mushroom powder** | Grind to powder after complete drying | 2 years or more |
| **Fermented storage** | Soak in brine for lactic acid fermentation | 3-6 months |

> **Mushroom powder is especially valuable in a disaster.**
> It is compact, adds a savory (umami) depth to broth, and can be mixed into grain flour to add protein and micronutrients. **If dried mushrooms start to crumble, it is better to turn them into powder than to throw them away.**

---
## 14. Cooking and Processing
### 14-1. Always Cook Before Eating
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Wine cap is not eaten raw. The raw mushroom contains compounds that interfere with digestion and irritate the stomach, and above all, **unless it is cooked, the nutrients inside the cell walls cannot be absorbed.**

| Cooking | Fuel | Time | Notes |
|:---|:--:|:--:|:---|
| Grilling | Low | 5-10 min | You can also put it on embers. The thick stems suit grilling |
| Stir-frying | Low | 5-8 min | A little oil greatly improves the flavor |
| Soup or stew | Medium | 10-15 min | Gives the broth a savory depth |
| Boiling | Medium | 10 min | Blanch and season as namul (seasoned vegetable side dish) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**The stems are thick and firm, giving a meat-like texture.** The fact that it is **an ingredient that gives the satisfaction of chewing** when protein is scarce is reflected in the Taste criterion of the evaluation table.

---
### 14-2. How to Use Dried Mushrooms

| Step | Method |
|:--:|:---|
| 1 | Soak in lukewarm water for 20-30 minutes to rehydrate |
| 2 | **Do not throw away the soaking water.** Savory flavor and water-soluble nutrients have dissolved into it |
| 3 | Use only the upper water as broth, avoiding the sand that has settled on the bottom |
| 4 | Slice the rehydrated mushrooms and stir-fry them or add them to soup |

---
### 14-3. Combinations That Make Up for a Grain Shortage

| Combination | Effect |
|:---|:---|
| **Mushrooms + grain porridge** | Mushroom protein adds the lysine that grains lack |
| **Mushroom powder + wheat or buckwheat flour** | Mixed into the dough for sujebi (hand-torn dough soup) or kalguksu (knife-cut noodles) to raise the protein |
| **Mushrooms + namul** | Their micronutrient profiles differ from each other |
| **Mushrooms + oil** | Absorption of fat-soluble compounds improves |

> **Mushrooms should be used not as a source of calories but as a supplementary source of protein and micronutrients.**
> This is what the SS grade for the Supplementary scenario in the evaluation table means. Fill the calories with grains or tuber crops (tubers/storage roots), and use mushrooms to add **the amino acids that grains cannot supply, plus vitamin D, selenium, and copper**.

## 15. Annual Work Calendar

| Month (South Korea) | Work |
|:--:|:---|
| Jan-Feb | Gather logs and rice straw. Check the spawn. Check the condition of dried mushrooms |
| **Mar** | Start making the substrate. Soak the rice straw and begin pre-fermentation |

| Jul | Hot season. Protect the mycelium with shade and watering. Harvest continues |
| Aug | Add a second round of substrate. Use part of the spent substrate as inoculum |
| **Sep-Oct** | **Second flush.** The period with the largest harvest. Dry in bulk |
| Nov | Collect the spent substrate and work it into the field. Cover with straw and fallen leaves to prepare for winter |
| Dec | Check the reserved spawn. Check how the spore prints are being stored |
