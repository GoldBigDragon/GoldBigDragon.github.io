## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The lion's mane mushroom (Hericium erinaceus) was judged excluded in the evaluation table. Storability E (raw basis) and perenniality B combined, and its no-gate score is 42.5 (rank 83 of 183 valid species), the highest of the three excluded mushrooms. Perenniality B means 3-4 harvests can be taken from the same substrate. However, if simply dried this mushroom turns bitter and tough, so the key is to harvest on time, blanch before drying, and turn it into powder. Its strengths are micronutrients A (14 nutrients meeting 10% of the RDA, including vitamin D), protein density SS (79.1% of calories), water consumption A, and sunlight SS, since it does not photosynthesize and yield holds even when sunlight falls. Its greatest advantage is safety. With no cap, stem or gills but only a mane of hanging white spines, no poisonous mushroom of this shape is known; it does not grow from soil but on wood; and every other species of the same genus is edible. The weak point is the sterilization equipment and the consumable bags (facility burden D), and switching to log cultivation lasts several years without sterilization.

## 2. Mushroom Overview
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It belongs to the genus Hericium of the family Hericiaceae, order Russulales, phylum Basidiomycota, and is a white-rot fungus that decomposes the dead stems and wounds of broadleaf trees. The Korean name comes from its resemblance to the white rump of a roe deer.

| Feature | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Shape | Forms no cap and grows as a mass 5-25 cm across. Late in growth, white needle-like spines hang 1-5 cm long. No stem, gills, ring or volva |
| Color | Pure white when young, turning yellow to yellow-brown as it grows |
| Flesh and spores | Soft, spongy and white. The spore print is white |
| Mycelium | Turns from white to yellow to pink, which is normal maturation |
| Habitat | Dead broadleaf trees and wounds. Does not grow from the ground |

| Stage | Period and temperature | Key point |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Spawn run | 20-30 days, 22-25℃ | The substrate is covered white and matures yellow |
| Inducing pinning | 3-7 days, 15-20℃, humidity 85-95% | Wounding the surface makes white lumps form |
| Growth | 15-20 days, 15-24℃, humidity 80-85% | The spines hang down. 50-60 days from spawning to harvest |
| Re-fruiting | At 10-15 day intervals | 3-4 flushes |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Substrate pH is mildly acidic at 4.0-5.5, and rice bran lowers it naturally. Treating with wood ash or lime to make it alkaline, as for the button mushroom, stops the mycelium growing, so contamination control rests on sterilization and spawn amount. Per 100 g it has 13 kcal, 2.57 g of protein (79.1% of calories), 0.54 g of sugars, abundant dietary fiber, vitamin D and B vitamins, and hericenones and erinacines (reported to promote nerve growth factor synthesis).

## 3. Substrate and Sterilization
| Method | Yield | Period | Sterilization |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Bottle cultivation | 356 g per 850 ml bottle | 40-45 days | Needed |
| Bag cultivation | 810 g per 2,500 ml bag | 50-60 days | Needed (bags are consumed) |
| Log cultivation | 1,195.5 g per 0.1 m³ of logs (biological efficiency 17.3%) | 69 days or more, then several years | Not needed |

> **Where there is no electricity or fuel, log cultivation is the answer. What produced the facility burden D was the sterilization equipment and consumable bags, and logs skip both.**

| Item | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Logs | Oak is best; alder, poplar and acacia are also possible. Conifers are forbidden because resin and terpenes inhibit the mycelium. Cut after the leaves fall and before buds open (November-March) and leave in shade for 1-2 months to lower the moisture to 40-45%. Diameter 10-20 cm, 15-20 cm blocks (stacked upright) or 1 m logs |
| Sawdust substrate | Hardwood sawdust 80% + rice bran 20% (rice bran 20-30% for bottles). Rice bran, wheat bran and beet pulp together do not exceed 30%. Moisture 65-67% for bottles and 68-75% for bags. 580-620 g in an 850 ml bottle, 2 kg per bag |
| Substitutes | Crush rice straw, bean pods and maize stalks finely to replace 20-30% of the sawdust. 20% maize stalks actually raises yield |
| Sterilization | Atmospheric 100℃ for 4 hours or 98℃ for 6 hours. The no-electricity standard is a 200 L drum (20-30 cm of water at the bottom, containers on a support, steam at 100℃ for 4-6 hours, 30-50 kg of firewood), filled full and processed all at once. Boiling sacks for 2-3 hours is not full sterilization |
| Containers | Glass jars (break if cooled quickly), bamboo tubes, heat-resistant plastic bags (mouth plugged with cotton or cloth) |

| Growing space | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Temperature | Spawn run 22-25℃, fruiting 15-24℃ (mycelium lower limit 6℃, upper limit 35℃). Spring and autumn indoors, caves, cellars, north-facing sheds |
| Light | Indirect light enough to read a newspaper. In total darkness the spines clump into a lump, and direct sun dries it and turns it yellow |
| Humidity and ventilation | Put a water dish in a large box or jar. Ventilate well (when carbon dioxide builds up it deforms like coral). Do not spray water directly on the mushroom |

## 4. Obtaining Spawn and Spawning
| Method | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Spore print | Place a mature specimen with hanging spines spines-down on aluminum foil and cover it with a bowl, and a white spore powder remains. Dry it in shade, fold it into a zip bag with charcoal, seal with beeswax and keep it in a cave, where it lasts 5-10 years or more (not as liquid). Make 20-30 sheets in the first year and put them away |
| Tissue culture | From a young, pure-white specimen with short spines, take rice-grain-sized pieces of white flesh from inside the base, set them into boiled and cooled substrate or rice straw, and culture in the dark at 22-25℃. Spreads in 3-5 days. The surest |
| Transplanting | Split a log whose mycelium has spread to the cut face and has no contaminants, set pieces of the inner wood into holes of a new log and plug them with wooden plugs (spring or autumn). Well-grown substrate mixed into new substrate at 5-10% also works. After four or five generations vigor falls |

| Spawning | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Sawdust substrate | Spawn at a substrate temperature of 18-20℃ after cooling 13-14 hours following sterilization. The standard is 2-3% of the substrate weight, and 5-10% without a sterile room. Half in the center hole and half on the surface. Plug the mouth with cotton or cloth and tie it. Right after rain, with the dust settled, without breathing out over it, and quickly |
| Logs | Not sterilized. In March-May or September-October drill holes 12 mm across and 25-30 mm deep in a spiral at 15-20 cm spacing (20-30 holes for one log 15 cm across and 1 m long), fill them with spawn, and seal with beeswax, pine resin, wet clay or wooden plugs. Stack them lying in shade and cover with straw or leaves so they do not dry. In 2-3 months white mycelium shows around the holes |

> **When there is no sterile room, the key is not perfect sterility but speed and the amount of spawn. A few contaminants getting in cannot be prevented, so make the mushroom mycelium overwhelmingly numerous so that it takes the space first.**

## 5. Spawn Run, Inducing Fruiting, and Harvest
| Stage | Management |
|:---|:---|
| Spawn run (20-30 days) | 22-25℃, dark. 35℃ is the absolute limit, and because the mycelium produces metabolic heat the inside of the substrate can exceed it even when the surroundings are 30℃, so do not stack containers densely. The mycelium turning from white to pale yellow to pale pink is normal; contamination is blue-green, black or deep orange, drifts like powder, spreads radially from round spots, and advances 10-30 mm a day (normal is 3-5 mm). After it reaches the bottom, leave it 5-7 more days (ripening) |
| Inducing pinning | Stab or cross-cut the surface 5-10 mm deep with a knife tip, nail or skewer disinfected in boiling water, in 2-3 places per bottle or 3-5 places on one face of a bag, to tear the mycelium layer (highest re-fruiting rate and quality). To grow one large fruiting body, make only one or two wounds |
| Growth | 15-24℃ (best 15-20℃; the lower, the longer and firmer the spines and the less bitter; above 22℃ it is soft and quickly turns yellow), humidity 85-95% at pinning and 80-85% during growth. For the last 3-4 days before harvest spray no water at all and lower the humidity slightly, which keeps the color white and raises storability |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Without electricity it is a spring and autumn crop (spring April to early June, autumn September to early November). In midsummer the mycelium dies at 35℃, and in midwinter pinning stops. For outdoor log cultivation, stand or lay logs in partial shade and leave it to the seasons.

| Harvest | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Timing | When the whole fruiting body is milky white and the spines have grown 1-5 cm but their tips have not dried. Once a yellowish tinge starts it is already late (bitterness strengthens and the tissue toughens). The bitterness comes mainly from over-ripeness |
| Method | Wrap the base in your hand and twist off gently. Do not wash (the spongy tissue soaks up water and turns yellow and soft within a day) |
| Re-fruiting | Remove the remaining stubs on the surface, keep humidity at 22-25℃, and wound the surface again. A total of 3-4 flushes |

## 6. Contamination, Pests, and Problem Diagnosis
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most common cause of failure is losing the substrate to a competing mold (green mold). Green mold has already spread before its color shows, so judge by texture, smell and speed rather than color; and because this mushroom's own mycelium turns yellow and pink, do not discard on color alone. Alkaline treatment cannot be used, so sterilize thoroughly (atmospheric 100℃ for 4-6 hours), keep nutrient sources (rice bran, wheat bran) to 30% or less, stack containers with gaps, and most fundamentally switch to log cultivation, where the bark and wood themselves are the defensive wall.

| Situation | Response |
|:---|:---|
| A small spot on one container's surface | Take out that container at once without opening it |
| Spread to several containers | Move the whole culture area, empty the old site, wash and dry it |
| Pests | For mushroom flies, cover the entrance with fine cloth and set water-dish and sticky traps. For mites, keep dry and isolate the section. For slugs (the soft spines make it especially vulnerable), lay a band of ash or sawdust and catch them by hand at night. On logs, squirrels, rats and birds carry the mushrooms off, so cover with a net |

> **Do not turn contaminated substrate into compost. Green mold spores survive in a compost pile and return to the substrate the next year. Burn it or bury it deep away from the growing site.**

| Symptom | Cause and action |
|:---|:---|
| Mycelium spreads and stops | Substrate above 35℃ or short of water. Space the containers to let heat out |
| Good mycelium but no mushrooms | Surface not wounded, temperature not lowered, or no light at all |
| Splits like coral or cauliflower | Excess carbon dioxide. Greatly increase ventilation |
| Spines do not hang but clump in a lump | Too little light. Give indirect light |
| Turns yellow | Over-ripe, above 22℃, or direct sun. Harvest on time and move to cooler shade |
| Soft with brown liquid | Water touched the mushroom directly (bacterial soft rot). Ventilate and remove the diseased ones |
| Small and split into several | Too many wounds. Make only one or two next cycle |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Spent substrate is used as field organic matter and as bedding for earthworms (contaminated substrate is never returned); logs that have finished fruiting are put into the field or used as mulch once they crumble, split for inoculating new logs if the mycelium is alive, and used as seedbed soil once rotted. Empty and wash the culture site each cycle and dry it, and move it after 2-3 years.

## 7. Safety Checks
> **This section bears directly on life. The lion's mane is the mushroom with the lowest risk of confusion, but you have to know why it is low to make proper use of that advantage.**

| Reason | Details |
|:---|:---|
| A unique form | No deadly poisonous mushroom is known with no cap, stem or gills but a mane of hanging white spines |
| Does not grow from soil | It grows on dead broadleaf trees or wounds, and all Amanitas grow from the ground |
| The whole genus is edible | No poisonous species has been reported in the genus Hericium (coral tooth, bear's head tooth and others) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Even so, there are things to check. Climacodon grows in tiers like shelves on trees, with short spines and leathery tissue, often bitter, and cannot be eaten. White mold lumps have no spines and are cottony or powdery contaminants. Young puffballs are round without spines and cottony white inside when split. Crust fungi spread thin on wood and are tough.

| Check | Lion's mane standard |
|:---|:---|
| Shape | Spines hang down from a mass. Not stuck on in tiers like shelves |
| Spine length | 1-5 cm. Must be clearly long to the eye |
| Tissue | Soft and spongy, yields to a finger press and tears by hand. If tough as leather it is something else (the surest) |
| Substrate and taste | Dead broadleaf trees or wounds (one from the ground is not this). Touch it to the tip of the tongue, and if strongly bitter, spit it out |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For the first meal, go through every check, cook it thoroughly (raw it is hard to digest and irritates the stomach), and have one person eat a small amount and wait 24 hours. If you handle other mushrooms too, remember that vomiting and diarrhea beginning 6-24 hours after eating is the typical course of amatoxin poisoning, which destroys the liver. The safest mushroom food in a disaster is what grew on a substrate I spawned myself.

## 8. Storage, Cooking, and Annual Schedule
> **Storability E is solved by drying, but this mushroom turns bitter and tough if simply dried. When the spongy tissue shrinks as it dries and the fibers tangle, soaking in water does not undo it, and the bitter compounds of over-ripe specimens become concentrated. The biggest cause is not drying technique but harvest timing.**

| Storage | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Fresh | 3-5 days. Spoils if washed, sealed in plastic or piled up. Wrapped in dry cloth or paper in a 12-15℃ cave for 1-2 weeks |
| Drying (recommended) | Harvest when white and tear by hand along the grain into pieces 1-2 cm thick (do not cut with a knife). Blanch 3-5 minutes in salted boiling water (the key), soak in cold water once more to leach it and squeeze out the water, dry slowly in shade by wind, and finish with half a day in the sun (vitamin D rises). Hot, rapid drying fixes the bitterness and hardens the tissue |
| Powder (the most practical) | Grind thoroughly dried, blanched pieces in a mortar or millstone, sieve, and seal with charcoal in a cool, dark place. The toughness problem disappears and the bitterness disperses |
| Others | Salting (blanched, salt at 20%, 6 months to 1 year; the texture survives better than drying), soy sauce pickles (3-6 months), lactic fermentation in 3% brine (3-6 months), oil preserving (1-3 months). Use half of what is stored as pickle and half as powder |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The lion's mane is not eaten raw. Blanching takes 3-5 minutes, stir-frying 5-8 minutes (fried in oil it takes on a crab- or chicken-like grain), grilling 5-8 minutes, soup or stew within 10 minutes, pancakes 5-8 minutes, and before cooking the water is squeezed out hard. If it tastes bitter, blanch and leach it several times in cold water, cook it with soybean paste, chili paste, oil or vinegar, or turn it into powder and use a spoonful per pot of broth. Soak dried ones in lukewarm water for 30 minutes or more, taste the soaking water, and discard it if bitter or use it as broth if not. This mushroom is a supplement of protein, vitamin D and B vitamins, not a source of calories.

| Month | Work |
|:---|:---|
| Jan-Mar | Cut logs (November-March), gather sawdust, in March dry the logs in shade and prepare and sterilize substrate for the spring crop (all at once in the drum) |
| Apr-June | In April inoculate logs and spawn and culture sawdust substrate, in May induce pinning (wound the surface) and first harvest, in June harvest peak and mass drying and powder making |
| July | Too hot to grow (mycelium dies above 35℃). Keep logs in shade and only keep them from drying |
| Aug-Sep | In August prepare and sterilize substrate for the autumn crop (secure sawdust and maize stalks), in September spawn and culture |
| Oct-Dec | October induce pinning and harvest (cool, so spines are long and less bitter, the best time), November harvest peak and mass drying, December put spent substrate in the field, check spore prints, cover logs with straw for winter |

> **Never forget: harvest while white, blanch before drying, store as powder, never spray water directly on the mushroom, induce pinning by wounding the surface, give faint light, put no wood ash or lime in the substrate, the mycelium turning yellow and pink is normal, never exceed 35℃, and to save fuel switch to log cultivation.**
