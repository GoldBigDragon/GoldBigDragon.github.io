## 12. Confusion with Poisonous Mushrooms and Safety Checks

> **This section is a matter of life and death. Confirming that it is safe to eat matters more than growing it.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Of the six species covered in this document, lion's mane mushroom is **the one with the lowest risk of misidentification.** That is this section's conclusion, but you need to know why the risk is low to make proper use of that advantage.

### 12-1. Three Reasons the Misidentification Risk Is Low

| Reason | Details |
|:---|:---|
| **1. The shape is one of a kind** | No cap, no stem, no gills - just a hanging bundle of white needle-like spines. No deadly poisonous mushroom with this shape is known |
| **2. It does not grow from soil** | It always grows attached to dead hardwood or a wound on a hardwood tree. Amanita mushrooms all grow from the ground, so there is no room for overlap |
| **3. Every species in the same genus is edible** | No toxic species has been reported in the genus Hericium |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Enoki mushroom and button mushroom are risky for good reason. Both have the classic mushroom shape of cap, stem, and gills, several deadly poisonous mushrooms share that shape, and even their season and substrate overlap. Button mushroom in particular grows from soil and is picked at the white button stage, which overlaps exactly with young Amanita specimens.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lion's mane mushroom has none of those conditions. Every amatoxin-containing Amanita has a cap, a stem, gills, and a volva, and grows from the ground. There is no Amanita that attaches a hanging bundle of needle-like spines to a tree.

> **This is an advantage that is hard to put a price on in a disaster situation.** Misidentification is the greatest risk of eating mushrooms when there is no medical support, and this mushroom is structurally free of that risk. It is a species that even someone with no experience identifying mushrooms can handle with relative confidence.

### 12-2. Other Species in the Same Genus - All Edible

| Species | Characteristics | Edibility |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Lion's mane mushroom (H. erinaceus)** | Spines hang long, 1-5cm. Clump-shaped | Edible |
| Coral tooth fungus (H. coralloides) | Branches like coral, with short spines | Edible |
| H. laciniatum | Branches, with medium-length spines | Edible |
| H. caput-medusae | Clumps into a head shape | Edible |
| H. cirrhatum | Grows in overlapping tiers | Edible |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Even mistaking one species in this genus for another will not poison you. Depending on the species, though, shorter spines can mean a smaller yield and a different texture. Lion's mane mushroom has the highest cultivation value of them all.

### 12-3. Still Worth Checking

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A low misidentification risk does not mean you can skip checking altogether. There are a few white, clump-shaped fungi that are not edible.

| Look-alike | Distinguishing Point | Risk |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Climacodon species** | Attaches to trees **in tiered, shelf-like layers.** The spines are short and only on the underside, and **the flesh is tough like leather.** Often bitter-tasting | Mildly toxic and not edible |
| **White mold clumps** | No spines; cottony or powdery | These are contaminant fungi |
| **Young puffballs** | Ball-shaped, with no spines. Cut open, the inside is evenly cottony white | Young ones are edible, but cut them open to check |
| **Jelly fungi and Stereum species** | Spreads thinly over wood, with spines that are very short or absent. Tough | Not edible |

| What to Check | Lion's Mane Mushroom's Standard |
|:---|:---|
| **Shape** | **Spines hang downward from a clump.** It does not attach in tiered, shelf-like layers |
| **Spine length** | **1-5cm.** Must be visibly long |
| **Flesh** | **Soft like a sponge; it gives when pressed with a finger.** If it is tough like leather, it is something else |
| **Color** | White to milky white. Yellow to tawny brown as it grows |
| **Substrate** | Dead hardwood or a wound on a hardwood tree. **One growing from the ground is not this mushroom** |
| **Taste** | A young specimen is almost never bitter. **If it tastes strongly bitter on the tip of your tongue, spit it out** |

> **The softness of the flesh is the most reliable distinguishing point.** Press lion's mane mushroom with your hand and it gives like a sponge, and it tears apart by hand. Other fungi that could be confused with it are usually leathery or woody and will not tear by hand.

### 12-4. Rules for the First Time You Eat It

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Even with a low misidentification risk, follow the same procedure. When there is no medical support, the verification procedure itself is your defense.

| Step | Details |
|:--:|:---|
| 1 | **Confirm it grew from wood.** Discard anything that grew from the ground |
| 2 | **Check that the spines hang down 1cm or more.** Discard anything short and shelf-shaped |
| 3 | **Press it by hand to confirm it is soft like a sponge.** Discard it if it is tough |
| 4 | **Touch a little to the tip of your tongue.** If it is strongly bitter, spit it out and do not eat it |
| 5 | **Always cook it thoroughly.** Raw, it is hard to digest and irritates the stomach |
| 6 | The first time, have **only one person eat a small amount (one piece)** and wait 24 hours |
| 7 | If nothing is wrong, increase the amount |

> **The most dangerous poisonings are the ones whose symptoms take more than 6 hours to appear.**
> Stomach pain within 30 minutes to 2 hours of eating is usually just stomach irritation, but vomiting and diarrhea starting 6 to 24 hours later is the classic course of liver-destroying amatoxin poisoning. There is almost no room for this to happen with lion's mane mushroom, but if you handle other mushrooms too, keep this course in mind.

> **The safest mushroom food in a disaster situation is one grown from a substrate you inoculated yourself. Lion's mane mushroom is a rare species where you can also forage wild specimens with relative safety on top of that.**

## 13. Storage Without Refrigeration

> **This section covers the trickiest part of lion's mane mushroom. A storability grade of E is normally solved by drying, but this mushroom turns bitter and tough if you just dry it plain.**

### 13-1. Three to Five Days Fresh

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;On the evaluation table, this mushroom's storability is graded E for the fresh product. That is 3-5 days at room temperature with no electricity. Its sponge-like flesh holds so much moisture that it softens wherever it is pressed first, and as the white turns yellow the bitterness rises.

| What Not to Do | Why |
|:---|:---|
| **Store it washed** | The sponge-like flesh soaks up the water and turns yellow within a day |
| Seal it completely in plastic | Condensation forms and it rots. Wrap it in paper or dry cloth so it can breathe |
| Stack it up | It spoils wherever it is pressed first. Spread it out in a single layer |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Wrapped in dry cloth or paper and kept in an earthen cellar at 12-15°C, it can last 1-2 weeks. That is the best you can get without electricity; beyond that, you move on to processing.

### 13-2. Drying Makes It Bitter and Tough

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Drying this mushroom brings two problems together.

| Problem | Cause |
|:---|:---|
| **Bitterness** | An over-mature specimen has more bitter compounds, and as moisture leaves during drying, that taste concentrates. Fast drying at high heat locks the bitterness into the flesh |
| **Toughening** | The sponge-like flesh shrinks drastically as it dries, and its fibers tangle together. Once tangled, the fibers do not come loose even when rehydrated, and it stays leathery |

> **The single biggest cause of both problems is not drying technique but harvest timing.** One picked while white stays only mildly bitter even dried, while one that has turned yellow is bitter no matter how you dry it. The harvest-timing judgment covered in Section 8 determines more than half of your storage quality.

### 13-3. A Drying Method That Reduces Bitterness and Toughness

| Step | Method | Effect |
|:--:|:---|:---|
| 1 | **Harvest while white** | Removes the root cause of the bitterness |
| 2 | **Stop misting 3-4 days before harvest** | It is harvested with less moisture, making it easier to dry |
| 3 | **Tear it by hand along its grain into 1-2cm-thick pieces** | A thick clump will not dry through and grows mold. Tearing rather than cutting with a knife damages the flesh less |
| 4 | **Blanch it in salted boiling water for 3-5 minutes** | **This step is the key.** The bitter compounds leach into the water, and the heat sets the flesh so it tangles less once dry |
| 5 | **Soak it in cold water to leach it once more, then squeeze out the water** | This removes the remaining bitterness |
| 6 | **Dry it slowly in the shade with airflow** | Fast, high-heat drying locks in the bitterness and stiffens the flesh |
| 7 | Finish with half a day in the sun | This increases its vitamin D |

| Method | Conditions | Duration | Result |
|:---|:---|:--:|:---|
| **Blanch, then shade-and-air dry** | After going through step 4, under a well-ventilated eave | 3-5 days | **Most recommended. Less bitter and rehydrates better** |
| Dry raw in the shade | Tear and hang immediately | 3-5 days | Stays bitter and toughens |
| Dry raw in the sun | Tear and hang in the sun | 1-2 days | Fast, but bitterness and toughness are worst |
| Dry by the fire | On a rack over the hearth, 40-50°C | Half a day | Usable during the monsoon, but locks in the bitterness |

| Stage | Assessment |
|:---|:---|
| Fully dried | **It crumbles when rubbed by hand.** If it bends, it is still not dry enough |
| Storage | Once fully cooled, **in an airtight container with charcoal or roasted salt** |
| Duration | **Over 1 year** in a cool, dark place. Over 2 years if ground into powder |

### 13-4. Powder Is the Most Practical Form

> **In a disaster situation, the best storage form for lion's mane mushroom is not dried whole, but powdered.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There are three reasons. First, the toughening problem disappears - powder has no texture to speak of, so whether the fibers are tangled no longer matters. Second, the bitterness gets diluted - used a spoonful at a time in porridge or soup, the bitterness is barely noticeable. Third, it takes up little space and keeps for a long time.

| Step | Method |
|:--:|:---|
| 1 | Blanch it and dry it completely |
| 2 | Grind it in a mortar or millstone |
| 3 | Sift it, keeping only the fine powder, and re-grind anything coarse |
| 4 | Put it in an airtight container with charcoal and keep it somewhere cool and dark |
| 5 | Mix it into porridge, soup, dough for hand-torn noodles, or grain flour |

### 13-5. Other Storage Methods

| Method | How to Make It | Duration | Notes |
|:---|:---|:--:|:---|
| **Salt-packing** | Blanch, drain, and layer with salt at 20% of its weight | 6 months-1 year | **Keeps the texture better than drying** |
| **Jangajji (pickled vegetables in soy sauce or paste)** | Blanch and steep in soy sauce and vinegar | 3-6 months | The soy sauce masks the bitterness |
| **Fermented storage** | Steep in 3% brine for lactic fermentation | 3-6 months | The tartness covers the bitterness |
| **Oil-packing** | Blanch, squeeze dry, and submerge in oil | 1-3 months | Only when oil is plentiful |
| **Short-term earthen-cellar storage** | Unwashed, wrapped in paper, at 12-15°C | 1-2 weeks | Spread out in a single layer |

> **For this mushroom, salt-packing can be a better choice than drying.** Since the flesh does not shrink, its original texture stays intact, and the bitterness has already leached out during blanching. If salt is plentiful, a good split is to put half of what you store into pickling and half into powder.

## 14. Cooking and Processing

### 14-1. Always Cook It Before Eating

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lion's mane mushroom is not eaten raw. Raw, it is hard to digest, and some of its compounds irritate the stomach. And without cooking, you cannot absorb the nutrients locked inside its cell walls.

| Cooking Method | Fuel | Time | Notes |
|:---|:--:|:--:|:---|
| Blanching | Low | 3-5 min | Also the basic step for removing bitterness |
| Stir-frying | Low | 5-8 min | **Fried in oil, it takes on a texture like crab meat or chicken** |
| Grilling | Low | 5-8 min | You can also tear it into thick pieces and lay it over embers |
| Soup, stew | Medium | Under 10 min | Gives a clear broth with a subtle sweetness |
| Pancakes (jeon) | Medium | 5-8 min | Tearing it into the batter stretches the quantity further |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The trick is to **squeeze the water out firmly** before cooking. Its sponge-like flesh holds so much water that stir-frying it right away just releases water and steams it instead. Tear it by hand, press it dry with both hands, and stir-fry it, and the texture comes through with a meat-like bite. The fact that it gives a satisfying chew when protein is scarce is reflected in the evaluation table's taste grade of S.

### 14-2. If It Tastes Bitter

| Method | Explanation |
|:---|:---|
| **Blanch and leach it** | Blanch it 3-5 minutes in salted boiling water, then soak it in cold water, changing the water several times |
| **Add it to doenjang (Korean soybean paste) or gochujang (Korean chili paste)** | The strong flavor covers the bitterness |
| **Cook it with oil** | The fat-soluble bitter compounds dissolve into the oil and disperse |
| **Turn it to powder, used a little at a time** | The most reliable fix. One spoonful in a whole pot of soup and the bitterness is not noticeable |
| **Add acidity** | Cooking it with vinegar or sour kimchi masks the bitterness |

### 14-3. Using the Dried Form

| Step | Method |
|:--:|:---|
| 1 | Soak it in lukewarm water for at least 30 minutes to rehydrate. This takes longer than for other mushrooms |
| 2 | **Taste the soaking water.** Discard it if it is bitter; use it as broth if it is not |
| 3 | Squeeze the rehydrated mushroom firmly by hand to remove the water |
| 4 | If bitterness remains, blanch it once more in boiling water |
| 5 | Slice it and stir-fry it or add it to soup |

> **For other mushrooms, you are always told to use the soaking water, but lion's mane mushroom is an exception.** Because its bitter compounds leach into the water, taste it first and decide from there. If it was picked while white and dried after blanching, the soaking water will not be bitter, so use it as broth.

### 14-4. Combinations That Fill the Gap Left by Grain Shortages

| Combination | Effect |
|:---|:---|
| **Mushroom powder + grain porridge** | Adds the lysine grain lacks, plus vitamins B and D |
| **Mushroom powder + wheat or buckwheat flour** | Mixed into dough for hand-torn noodles or knife-cut noodles. The bitterness gets diluted |
| **Mushroom + oil** | Vitamin D is fat-soluble, so eating it with oil improves absorption |
| **Mushroom + potato or sweet potato** | Tuber crops (tubers/storage roots) fill the calories, the mushroom supplies the protein |
| **Mushroom + doenjang (Korean soybean paste) soup** | Doenjang's strong flavor covers the bitterness and adds umami |

> **The mushroom should be used not as a calorie source but as a supplement for protein and micronutrients.**
> For lion's mane mushroom, that point is especially clear: 13kcal per 100g, 2.57g of protein, meeting 14 micronutrients including vitamin D. Filling your calories with grain or tuber crops (tubers/storage roots) and adding this mushroom for protein and vitamins D and B is exactly the right way to use it.

## 15. Annual Work Calendar

| Month | Work |
|:--:|:---|
| **Jan-Feb** | **Fell logs**, after the leaves have dropped but before new buds appear. Collect sawdust. Check on dried mushrooms |
| **Mar** | Dry logs in the shade (to 40-45% moisture). **Prepare and sterilize the spring-cycle substrate,** all at once in a drum |
| **Apr** | **Inoculate logs** (drill holes and insert spawn). Inoculate and incubate the sawdust substrate |
| **May** | **Induce pinning and take the first harvest.** Wound the surface. Hold at 15-20°C. Blanch and dry immediately |
| **Jun** | **Peak of the spring-cycle harvest.** 2nd-3rd flush. Bulk drying and making powder |
| Jul | Too hot to grow. **Above 35°C, the mycelium dies.** Just keep logs in the shade and prevent them from drying out |
| **Aug** | **Prepare and sterilize the autumn-cycle substrate.** Secure sawdust and corn stalks |
| **Sep** | **Inoculate and incubate.** 22-25°C |
| **Oct** | **Induce pinning and harvest.** The best-quality season - the cool weather makes for long spines and little bitterness |
| **Nov** | **Peak of the autumn-cycle harvest.** Bulk drying. Logs fruit too |
| Dec | Work spent substrate into the field. Check spore prints. Cover logs with straw to prepare for winter |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The key to this calendar is that it **runs two cycles, spring and autumn, and rests in the height of summer and winter.** Midsummer is a temperature at which the mycelium dies, and midwinter is a temperature at which pinning stops. Forcing year-round cultivation leads to failure, and that gap is filled by wine cap in summer and enoki mushroom in winter.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The autumn harvest is better quality than the spring one. October and November temperatures of 15-20°C fall exactly within this mushroom's optimal range. So it works well to make your storage stock from the autumn harvest and eat the spring harvest as you go.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;And a good long-term strategy is to inoculate a few more logs every year. Logs need no sterilization, and one inoculation keeps fruiting for several years, so the fuel and labor spent on the sawdust substrate shrink year after year.

### Never Forget

| Item | Details |
|:--:|:---|
| 1 | **Pick it while white.** Once yellow appears it is already too late, and that is when the bitterness begins |
| 2 | **When drying, blanch it first.** Dried plain, it turns bitter and tough |
| 3 | **The most practical storage form is powder.** The toughening problem disappears and the bitterness gets diluted |
| 4 | **Do not mist the mushroom directly.** Water pooling between the spines spoils it starting from that spot |
| 5 | **Wounding the surface works best for inducing pinning** |
| 6 | **It needs weak light.** In total darkness, the spines will not elongate |
| 7 | **Do not add wood ash or lime to the substrate.** This mushroom requires a slightly acidic pH of 4.0-5.5 |
| 8 | **It is normal for the mycelium to turn yellow and pink.** Do not discard it based on color alone |
| 9 | **Above 35°C, the mycelium dies.** Because of metabolic heat, the inside of the substrate can exceed that even when the surroundings are 30°C |
| 10 | **This is the mushroom with the lowest misidentification risk.** It does not grow from soil, no toxic species share its genus, and no poisonous mushroom has this shape |
| 11 | **To save fuel, switch to log cultivation.** It needs no sterilization and keeps fruiting for years |
| 12 | **Because it does not photosynthesize, it is a food source that does not waver in a disaster where sunlight is reduced.** An SS grade for sunlight does not mean it uses light well - it means it is outside that axis altogether |
