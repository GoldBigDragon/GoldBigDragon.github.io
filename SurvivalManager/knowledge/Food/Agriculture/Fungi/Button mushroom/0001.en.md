## Introduction

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The button mushroom (Agaricus bisporus) was excluded in the disaster-preparedness evaluation. The reason is that Storability E and Perenniality C overlap, so stockpiling does not work. The hypothetical score without the gate applied is 40.8, which would rank 103rd among the 183 valid species.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Of the two reasons behind the exclusion, Storability E is scored on raw mushrooms alone. Button mushrooms are 90% water, and the cap browns quickly, so they lose market quality within three to five days at room temperature. Sliced thin and dried, though, they keep for a year in a sealed container. Drying uses almost no fuel or equipment, so a knife and a drying rack alone resolve half of what caused the exclusion. Perenniality C means the compost must be remade after two or three flushes from the same bed, and that part is a real weakness.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;But the button mushroom has one advantage: **it can sterilize its substrate without electricity**. Shiitake, enoki, and lion's mane mushroom all need their sawdust substrate boiled sterile under high or ordinary pressure, and the firewood and time that takes are the biggest obstacle in a disaster. The button mushroom is different. Stack rice straw, manure, and gypsum together, and the thermophilic microbes inside the pile generate their own heat, reaching 60 to 70 degrees. No one lights a fire; people only turn the pile. That heat kills pathogens, weed seeds, and pest eggs, while also burning off the sugars competing fungi would otherwise eat, leaving a selective substrate that only button mushroom mycelium can digest. Composting is sterilization and substrate preparation at the same time.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Simply being a fungus also means it does not photosynthesize, so its yield does not drop even if volcanic ash or soot darkens the sky. An SS in the Sunlight item on the evaluation table does not mean it uses light well; it means light is not even on its axis. In a scenario where rice, wheat, and potatoes all collapse together, mushrooms are effectively the only food source whose output holds up, and the button mushroom is the one species among them that needs no firewood. On top of that, its Mushroom By-Product Suitability is S, the highest of the six species covered here. Rice straw, horse manure, chicken manure, cattle manure, rice hulls, waste paper, shells, eggshells, and wood ash can all serve as substrate material.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;But there are two costs. One is labor. Turning a compost pile five or six times over two to three weeks takes a great deal of effort; it trades electricity for labor. The other is danger. The button mushroom grows out of soil, the ideal harvest stage is the closed white button, and its stem has a ring, and these three traits overlap with the young stage of the poisonous fool's mushroom and destroying angel. Cases of people mistaking these for button mushrooms, eating them, and dying are repeatedly reported both in South Korea and abroad. Section 12 is written at length just to draw that distinction.
