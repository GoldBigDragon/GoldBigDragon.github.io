## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The button mushroom (Agaricus bisporus) was judged excluded in the evaluation table. The reason is that storability E (raw basis) and perenniality C combine so that stockpiling does not hold, and its no-gate score is 40.8 (rank 103 of 183 valid species). The storability E is resolved with just a knife and a drying tray, because thinly sliced and dried mushrooms keep a year in an airtight container. The greatest advantage of this mushroom is that **the substrate can be sterilized without electricity or firewood**. When rice straw, manure and gypsum are piled up, the microbes inside the pile heat it by themselves to 55-70℃, killing pathogens and insect eggs and burning off the sugars that contaminant fungi would feed on, which leaves a substrate only button mushroom mycelium can eat. People light no fire; they only turn the pile. Because it does not photosynthesize, yield does not fall even when ash or soot darkens the sky (sunlight SS), nearly any by-product serves as substrate material, including rice straw, horse manure, chicken manure, rice hulls, waste paper and shells (mushroom by-product adaptability S), and even if it fails the compost remains. The costs are the labor of turning the compost five or six times over 2-3 weeks, and the danger of confusing it with deadly poisonous mushrooms (the fool's mushroom Amanita verna and the destroying angel Amanita virosa). Section 7 is set apart at length for telling them apart.

## 2. Mushroom Overview
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It belongs to the genus Agaricus of the family Agaricaceae, phylum Basidiomycota, and the Korean name yangsongi means only "western matsutake" and has no relation to the matsutake mushroom. It is a saprophyte living in heaps of organic matter that have decomposed a good way, so it is not given a sterilized substrate but eats a substrate that microbes have already cooked. That is why compost fermentation is 70% of cultivation.

| Feature | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Cap | 3-10 cm across, hemispherical opening to flat. White, cream or brown. Smooth, not slimy |
| Gills | Pale pink at first, then brown, then dark brown. Spores brown to chocolate brown |
| Stem | Short and thick (3-7 cm long). Has a ring; no volva at the base |
| Flesh | White; when cut it slowly changes from pale pink to brown. Mushroom aroma |

| Stage | Period and temperature | Key point |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Compost phase I (outdoors) | 2-3 weeks, 55-70℃ inside the pile | Turn every 2-3 days |
| Compost phase II (indoors) | 5-7 days, conditioning at 60℃ then 45-55℃ | The ammonia smell disappears |
| Spawning and spawn run | 2-3 weeks, 23-25℃ | The whole compost turns white |
| Casing | 2-3 cm | Without casing no mushrooms form |
| Pinning and growth | 10-15 days after casing (18-22℃), then 5-7 days (16-18℃) | White pins swell into buttons |
| Harvest | 40-60 days after filling the beds | Up to 3 flushes at 1-2 week intervals |

| Nutrition per 100 g | Content |
|:---|:---|
| Calories / protein | 15 kcal / 3.12 g (83.2% of calories, the highest in this group) |
| Others | Water about 90 g, potassium 212-384 mg, B vitamins. Vitamin D rises greatly when sun-dried |

## 3. Substrate (Compost) and Growing Room
| Category | Standard mix | Disaster substitute |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Carbon source | Rice, wheat or barley straw 80% | Dry grass, maize stalks, reeds, rice hulls, scrap paper |
| Nitrogen source | Chicken manure, urea, rice bran | Horse manure (the traditional nitrogen source), cow manure, pig manure, aged urine, bean pods, oil cake |
| Minerals | Gypsum (calcium sulfate) | Burned and ground shells, eggshell powder, wood ash |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Per 100 kg of rice straw, mix 10-15 kg of chicken manure (30-50 kg if horse manure), 5 kg of rice bran, 400-500 g of urea (10-15 L if urine) and 3-5 kg of gypsum to bring the C/N ratio to 28-32 to 1 at the start (straw alone is over 70 to 1, so nitrogen is added).

| Fermentation | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Phase I | In pre-wetting give the straw 70% or more of the total water to soften it; in the main stack layer the nitrogen sources and gypsum with the straw into a turtle-back shape (1.5-1.8 m high, 1.5-2 m wide). Turn it 5-8 times at 2-3 day intervals, swapping outside and inside. Inside the pile 55-70℃, with enough moisture that a squeeze gives a few drops. An ammonia smell is normal at this stage, a sewer smell is anaerobic rot, and white actinomycete powder rising when turned is a good sign |
| Phase II | Fill the beds with 50-60 kg per ㎡ and hold conditioning at 60℃ for 6 hours (55℃ for 12 hours or 50℃ for 24 hours can substitute), then 45-55℃ for 5-7 days. Without electricity, fill hot compost 25-30 cm thick into a small room or cave and cover with straw sacks or mats, cover with plastic on a clear day, or warm an ondol floor |
| Done when | No ammonia smell at all, the compost surface and interior covered with white actinomycetes, dark brown to chocolate color, moisture 68-72%, pH 6.8-7.2 |

> **Never spawn while an ammonia smell remains. The spawn dies on the spot. Increase ventilation and ferment a few more days.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The growing room is a closed space that can hold 16-25℃; a cave, cellar, north-facing storehouse, semi-underground pit or earthen-walled shed is good. Beds are 1-1.2 m wide with compost 20-30 cm deep, built in 2-4 tiers from logs, boards and bamboo (3㎡ of bed from 1㎡ of floor), with vents at the bottom and top. Light is not needed.

| Casing | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Material | Leaf mold + sand + lime, or paddy soil + rice hulls + shell powder. pH 7.5 or higher (green mold likes mild acidity), kept crumbly |
| Sterilization | Steam for several hours at 60℃ or higher, or roast on a griddle (dry bubble disease and mushroom flies enter with the soil) |

## 4. Obtaining Spawn and Spawning
| Method | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Commercial spawn | The easiest in ordinary times, so secure it in advance |
| Tissue culture | Split a fresh button just before the cap opens lengthwise by hand (a knife carries contaminants on the surface inside), take rice-grain-sized pieces of white flesh from where the cap meets the stem, set them into boiled and cooled rice straw or compost, and culture them in the dark at 23-25℃. White mycelium spreads in 3-5 days. The surest method |
| Bed transplant | Right after the mycelium has spread through all the compost and before casing, scoop out fist-sized pieces where the white mycelium is dense and free of contaminants, and bury them at 20-30 cm spacing at 3-5% of the new compost's weight. After four or five generations vigor falls, so return to tissue culture |
| Spore print | Place the cap of a large, fully opened mushroom gills down on aluminum foil, cover it with a bowl, and after 24 hours a brown to chocolate-brown spore powder remains. The button mushroom carries only two spores per basidium and can form a mushroom from a single spore, but germination is low, so sow plenty |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Dry the spores in shade, fold the foil into a zip bag together with thoroughly roasted salt or charcoal, and keep it in a cave or cellar with constant temperature through the seasons and no light, where it lasts 5-10 years or more (3-6 months if stored as liquid). In the first year make 20-30 sheets from the largest, healthiest specimens and put them away.

| Spawning | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Conditions | Compost cooled to 25℃ or below, no ammonia smell, moisture 68-72%. Bare hands may be used to mix it in, and without a sterile room use the upper end of the recommended amount generously |
| Method | Break 1.5-3.6 kg of spawn per 3.3㎡ (125 kg of compost) into rice-grain-sized pieces and mix evenly into the upper 10-15 cm layer of compost, level the surface, and cover with wet cloth or straw sacks |

## 5. Spawn Run, Inducing Fruiting, and Harvest
| Stage | Management |
|:---|:---|
| Spawn run (2-3 weeks) | 25-27℃ for days 1-5 after spawning, then lower to 20℃ from day 6-7. The mycelium's metabolic heat makes the inside of the compost hotter, so keep it from exceeding 30℃ (mycelium dies at 34℃). Do not water the compost directly; sprinkle only the cover when it dries. Ventilate briefly once or twice a day |
| Casing | After the mycelium reaches the bottom of the compost (2-3 weeks after spawning), apply 2-3 cm and soak thoroughly |
| Inducing fruiting | Lower the temperature to 16-18℃ and open the vents kept closed until then to let the carbon dioxide out, and mushrooms form (mycelium likes carbon dioxide, fruiting bodies dislike it). Air humidity 85-95%; below 12℃ pins do not form |
| Water management | Reduce or stop water at the pinning stage, then a little at a time once pins are pea-sized, just enough that the casing does not dry. None for the last 2-3 days before harvest |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To make 16-18℃ without electricity, use a cave, cellar or semi-underground pit, and do not grow in midsummer or midwinter; run two crops, in spring (late March to May) and autumn (September to November).

| Harvest | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Timing | Cap 2-4 cm across, the button stage when the cap is curled round and the white veil between cap and stem has not yet torn, in the early morning. Later ones can also be used |
| Method | Hold the cap, twist gently and pull out the whole mushroom to the base, and fill the hole. Do not wash |
| Cycle | Each flush comes in clusters over 3-5 days at 7-14 day intervals. The second flush is 50-70% of the first and the third 20-30%. A total of 10-20 kg from 50-60 kg of compost per ㎡ |

> **The white button stage that is the right time to harvest is the time when it looks most like the young stage of the deadly Amanitas. Be sure to read section 7 and pick only what came from the bed you made.**

## 6. Contamination, Pests, and Problem Diagnosis
| Problem | Symptoms and cause | Response |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Green mold | White at first, soon blue-green. 10-30 mm a day, 3-6 times faster than button mushroom mycelium (3-5 mm). By the time color shows, a large part of the substrate is already lost, so judge early by speed and texture | Remove the affected section. Do phase II properly and use plenty of spawn |
| Dry bubble disease | Mushrooms deformed into crushed white lumps. When the casing soil was not sterilized | Remove diseased mushrooms and the soil around them and sprinkle salt or ash. Always steam the casing |
| Brown blotch, bacterial soft rot, cobweb disease | Brown sunken spots on the cap, softening with a stench, white cobweb-like mold. Excess water and poor ventilation | Reduce water, increase ventilation and remove the affected ones |
| Mushroom flies | They eat the mycelium and maggots get in. They carry mold spores and dry bubble disease | Two layers of fine cloth over the vents and a wet-cloth curtain at the door. They surge from May. Water-dish, sticky and lamp traps |
| Mites, nematodes, slugs, rats | Mycelium turns brown, compost turns soaked black, young mushrooms are chewed, beds are disturbed | Steam the casing, make sure of 60℃ conditioning, a band of ash or sawdust, a net over the beds |

| Symptom | Cause and action |
|:---|:---|
| No mycelium visible after a week of spawning | Ammonia remained in the compost, or spawned while hot. Ferment longer |
| Mycelium spreads and stops | Compost above 30℃ or short of water. Lower the room to 20℃ |
| Good mycelium but no mushrooms | No casing, poor ventilation, or temperature too high |
| Long stems and small caps | Excess carbon dioxide. Ventilate |
| Caps open at once and thin | Temperature 22℃ or higher. Move somewhere cooler |
| Pins form and then dry up or go soft and die | Too little humidity, or watering too hard |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The spent compost left after harvesting mushrooms is used as basal fertilizer for the field, mulch, seedling soil, bedding for earthworms and to improve acid soil. Compost contaminated by green mold or dry bubble disease is not put on the field but burned or buried deep far away (the spores return the next year). Because piling heat and making substrate are the same process, even if you get no mushrooms, the compost remains. Each cycle, empty the bed frames, wash them and dry them in the sun, and once every 2-3 years coat the walls and floor with lime-sulfur or strong wood-ash lye.

## 7. Mistaking Poisonous Mushrooms and Safety Checks
> **This section bears directly on life. The button mushroom is the mushroom with the most reported deaths from confusion with deadly poisonous mushrooms. Checking whether it is safe to eat is more important than growing it.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Three conditions overlap with the young stage of the fool's mushroom and the destroying angel (amatoxin, deadly): they grow from the soil, are picked at the white button stage, and have a ring on the stem. Even in a bed you grew, wild spores can mix into the casing soil and other mushrooms can appear alongside.

| Category | Button mushroom | Fool's mushroom and destroying angel (deadly) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Stem base | No sac | A membranous sac (volva) surrounds the base |
| Gill color | Pale pink to brown to dark brown | Pure white from start to end |
| Spore color | Brown to chocolate brown | White |
| Stem | Short and thick (3-7 cm) | Long and slender (9-20 cm). The destroying angel has white fibrous scales |
| Flesh color change | Pinkish then brown when cut | No change, stays white |
| Habitat | Fermented compost and casing, in clusters on the bed | The ground near the roots of living trees, singly in the forest |
| Ring | Present | Present (cannot be told apart) |

> **What separates the button mushroom from the fool's mushroom is the volva at the base. Looking only at the part above ground, the sac is buried in the soil and cannot be seen, so dig it out with the soil to check. If you split one button lengthwise and the gills are pale pink, it is a button mushroom; if they are pure white to the end, do not eat it. If you put a cap face down on white paper under a cup for a few hours and the fallen powder is white, never eat it.**

| Look-alike | How to tell |
|:---|:---|
| Yellow stainers (stomach poison) | Turn bright yellow when cut or rubbed, with a foul ink- or chemical-like smell (stronger when cooked). Grow on grass and roadsides. Not fatal, but severe vomiting and diarrhea |
| Young Amanita (egg stage) | Looks most like a button in a white egg shape. Split lengthwise, if a folded mushroom shape is inside and there is a slimy jelly layer under the outer skin, it is an Amanita |
| Puffballs | Split open, the inside is evenly white and cottony with no gills |
| Inky caps | The cap melts into black over time. Poisonous if eaten with alcohol |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Vomiting and diarrhea beginning 6-24 hours after eating is the typical course of amatoxin poisoning, and the later it starts, the more serious. For the first meal, go through every check, cook it thoroughly, have one person eat a single piece and wait 24 hours, and set one specimen of what was eaten aside. The safest way is not to find it in the wild but to grow it on a bed you made yourself.

## 8. Storage, Cooking, and Annual Schedule
| Storage | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Fresh | Spoils if washed, sealed in plastic or piled up. Wrapped unwashed in paper in a 12-15℃ cave for 5-7 days |
| Drying | Slice 4-6 mm thick and spread gills up on mats to sun-dry for 1-2 days on a clear day (ergosterol in the gills takes UV and vitamin D rises greatly). On rainy days, half a day beside a fire at 40-50℃. Done when it snaps cleanly when broken by hand; after cooling, seal with charcoal or roasted salt in a cool, dark place for a year or more (2 years or more as powder) |
| Others | Salting (salt at 20%, 6 months to 1 year), pickles, lactic fermentation in 3% brine, oil preserving (1-3 months). Mushroom powder adds umami to broth and adds protein and vitamin D to grain flour |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The button mushroom is the only one in this group that can be eaten raw, and only if home-grown, but it is usually cooked. Grilling and stir-frying take 5-8 minutes, soup within 10 minutes and blanching 2-3 minutes. Soak dried ones in lukewarm water for 20-30 minutes, and use the soaking water, which holds umami and water-soluble nutrients, as broth, avoiding the sand. A mushroom is a supplement of protein and vitamin D, not a source of calories, so the correct use is to fill calories with grain or root crops and eat it with oil.

| Month | Work |
|:---|:---|
| Jan-Feb | Gather rice straw and manure, burn and grind shells and eggshells, repair bed frames, start phase I for the spring crop in February |
| Mar-Apr | Phase II and spawning (after checking ammonia), spawn run, casing and fruiting (16-18℃), first harvest at the end of the month |
| May-June | Harvest peak and immediate sun-drying, check insect screens, put spent compost on the field and wash beds and growing room |
| Jul-Aug | Too hot to grow (continue if you have a cave), start phase I for the autumn crop in August |
| Sep-Nov | Phase II and spawning, casing and fruiting, harvest peak in November (best quality) and mass drying |
| December | Tidy spent compost, check spore prints and dried mushrooms |

> **Never forget: dig out the stem base to check for a sac, split a button to see the gill color, do not eat if the spores are white, do not eat if it turns yellow and smells of chemicals, do not spawn compost that still has ammonia, steam the casing soil, and dry with the gills facing up.**
