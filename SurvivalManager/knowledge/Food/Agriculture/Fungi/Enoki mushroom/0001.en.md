## Introduction

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Enoki mushroom (Flammulina velutipes) received an Excluded verdict in the disaster-preparedness evaluation. The reason is two factors combined: storability is E, and perenniality is D. This means stockpiling doesn't work, and the hypothetical score without applying the gate is 39.6 points, ranking 117th among the 183 valid species. It is the lowest score among the six mushroom species this document covers.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;But the word "Excluded" should not be read to mean useless. Storability E is strictly a raw-product rating. Enoki mushroom is 90% water, so at room temperature it starts browning from the base after three days and turns mushy after four. However, if torn into thin strips and sun-dried, it keeps for a year in a sealed container, and grinding it into powder makes it last even longer. Drying uses almost no fuel or equipment. So the disqualifying factor behind enoki's exclusion is the kind of shortcoming a person can fix the same day with nothing more than a knife and a drying tray. Section 13 covers this issue separately.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Perenniality D is the weak point. Enoki mushroom is grown on a sawdust substrate packed into a bottle or bag that gets used once and discarded, and a single substrate is finished after two or three harvests. It cannot be stretched across four or five years from one log the way shiitake can, and its bed does not come back to life year after year the way a wine cap bed laid in the ground does. Because the sawdust and rice bran must be freshly prepared every cycle, more than 60kg of organic matter per m² goes in each year, and this burden grows heavier moving into the second and third years of a disaster.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Even so, there are three reasons enoki mushroom stays on the list. First, its Sunlight criterion is SS. This does not mean it uses light well — it means light is not an axis that applies to it at all. Fungi have no chlorophyll and do not photosynthesize, and the carbon enoki mushroom consumes is already fixed in the sawdust. When a volcanic winter or nuclear winter darkens the sky and rice, wheat, and potatoes collapse all at once, mushrooms are the only food source whose yield stays almost unchanged. Second is its cold-loving habit. This mushroom grows fruiting bodies between 4-9°C. Midwinter, when every other mushroom stops, is enoki's season, so it can fill the stretch when fresh food disappears completely in winter. Third is protein density SS. At 21kcal it has 2.48g of protein, 47.2% of calories. It is not a calorie source, but it has high value as a supplementary food.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Finally, the longest and most important section in this document is Section 12. Of the six species this document covers, enoki mushroom carries the highest risk of being confused with a poisonous mushroom in the wild. Wine cap and button mushroom have a simple safeguard — don't eat it if the spores are white — but enoki mushroom's own spores run from white to pale yellow, so this rule does not apply. Instead, you have to check the growing substrate, the sliminess of the cap, and the dark brown hairs at the base of the stem. If you don't know how to tell the difference, it is better not to grow this mushroom.
