## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The enoki mushroom (Flammulina velutipes) was judged excluded in the evaluation table. It is the result of storability E (raw basis) and perenniality D together, and its no-gate score is 39.6 (rank 117 of 183 valid species). Storability is solved with just a knife and a drying tray, because torn into thin strips and dried, it keeps a year in an airtight container. Perenniality D means that the sawdust substrate in bottles or bags is used once and discarded, and two or three harvests from one substrate is the end of it. There are still three reasons it is not struck off. First, it does not photosynthesize, so yield holds in a disaster that cuts sunlight (sunlight SS). Second, it is cold-loving and grows its fruiting bodies at 4-9℃, so midwinter, when all other mushrooms stop, is its season, and it fills the stretch when fresh food runs out. Third, with 21 kcal and 2.48 g of protein it has a protein share of 47.2% of calories (protein density SS) and is rich in vitamins B1 and B2. Without electricity it is a winter crop and is not grown in summer (34℃ is a danger). The most important caution is confusion with poisonous mushrooms. Enoki spores are white to pale yellow, so the simple defense of rejecting white-spored mushrooms cannot be used, and if you do not know the identification in section 7, do not collect it in the wild.

## 2. Mushroom Overview
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It belongs to the genus Flammulina of the family Physalacriaceae, phylum Basidiomycota, and is a white-rot fungus that decomposes the dead stems and stumps of broadleaf trees. The wild form (the velvet shank) and the cultivated form look completely different, and without knowing this you will fail to recognize it in the wild or believe a wrong mushroom to be enoki.

| Organ | Wild form | Cultivated form |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Cap | 2-8 cm across. Yellow-brown to orange-red-brown. Clearly slimy when wet | 1-3 cm across. Pure white, pale yellow |
| Gills | White to pale cream. Do not turn brown even when mature | White |
| Stem | 2-10 cm long. Base covered with short velvety hairs, dark brown to blackish brown | 8-15 cm long, slender |
| Ring, cortina, volva | All absent | All absent |
| Spores | White to pale yellow | Same |
| Growth | Dense clusters on rotting broadleaf stumps. Late autumn to early spring | Clusters inside bottles. Artificial cold |

| Stage | Period | Temperature | Humidity |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Spawn run | About 22 days | 23-25℃ | 65-75% |
| Scratching | 1 day | - | - |
| Pinning | 8-10 days | 14-15℃ | 95-98% |
| Growth suppression | 5-15 days | 5-7℃ | 75-96% |
| Growth | Until harvest | 4-9℃ | 75-85% |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The total from spawning to harvest is 50-70 days. A single spore does not make a mushroom (the mycelia of two different mating types must join), so sow spores generously. Per 100 g it has 21 kcal, 2.48 g of protein and 0.37 g of sugars, the highest dietary fiber among mushrooms, vitamins B1 and B2 at about 25% of the daily requirement, and lysine and arginine that supply the amino acids grain lacks.

## 3. Substrate and Sterilization
| Item | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Wood species | Being able to use conifers (Douglas fir, pine, Korean pine) is this mushroom's own advantage (age it outdoors in the rain for six months to a year). Poplar, willow, hackberry, mulberry and persimmon are high, oak and elm medium. Walnut and butternut (allelopathic compounds) and Japanese cypress, arborvitae and juniper (antibacterial compounds) are forbidden |
| Mix | Sawdust 60% + spent substrate 20% + rice bran 20% (without spent substrate, sawdust 80% + rice bran 20%). Do not exceed 20% bran. Eggshell broken to about 5 mm at 10% of the sawdust amount gives 121% yield |
| Moisture | 65-70% (squeezed hard, moisture shows between the fingers but no drops fall). pH 4.5-8.6 |
| Containers | Glass jars (mouth covered with cloth and tied; break if cooled quickly), bamboo tubes, earthenware, heat-resistant plastic bags (mouth plugged with cotton or cloth). 600-750 g of substrate in a 1,000 ml bottle |

| Sterilization | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Atmospheric steam in a drum (the no-electricity standard) | 20-30 cm of water in a 200 L drum, containers standing on a support, lid on with the gaps sealed with wet cloth or clay. 4-6 hours at 100℃ after the steam rises, then put the fire out and let it cool overnight. 30-50 kg of firewood |
| Boiling | Put the substrate in sacks and boil 2-3 hours, then squeeze and adjust the moisture. Heat-resistant bacterial spores remain, so spawn quickly after cooling and use plenty of spawn |
| Rice straw heated substrate | Boil rice straw or bean pods thoroughly for 1 hour or more, squeeze and cool, sprinkle plenty of spore powder, cover with clean cloth and keep at 20-25℃ in the dark for 2-4 weeks. Used to raise spawn from spores; the poor nutrition makes it less attractive to green mold too |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Cold comes from winter itself (a north-facing shed or under eaves from November to March), caves and tunnels (10-15℃), cellars (12-18℃), pits on a north slope (8-14℃), an ice pit (0-5℃), and hanging wet cloth (3-5℃ below the surroundings). In summer 4℃ cannot be made in a cave, so without electricity it is a winter crop.

## 4. Obtaining Spawn and Spawning
| Method | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Spore print | Place a large, healthy specimen with a fully open cap gills down on aluminum foil, cover it with a bowl and leave it for 24 hours. Dry it another day in shade, fold it in half into a zip bag, gather the bags in a glass jar sealed with beeswax, and keep it in a cave or cellar with constant temperature through the seasons, where it lasts 5-10 years or more (not as liquid). Make 20-30 sheets in the first year and put them away |
| Tissue culture | Split a fresh specimen lengthwise by hand, take rice-grain-sized pieces of white flesh from where the cap meets the stem, and set them into boiled and cooled sawdust substrate or rice straw. Spreads in 3-5 days at 20-25℃ in the dark. The surest |
| Substrate splitting | Mix the substrate from a contamination-free jar whose culture has just finished into new substrate at 5-10% of its weight (one jar becomes 5-10). After four or five generations vigor falls, so return to tissue culture or spores |

| Spawning | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Conditions | Right after the substrate has cooled to 25℃ or below (left longer, airborne spores settle), in the early morning or right after rain |
| Without a sterile room | In the smallest room with no draft, sprinkle the floor and walls with water to settle the dust, wipe hands with soap and strong liquor or vinegar, heat tools in fire and cool them, cover the mouth with cloth, and finish opening the container, adding the spawn and covering it within 10 seconds. Spawn at 5-10% of the substrate weight, two or three times the standard 2-3%. Plug the mouth with cotton or cloth but do not seal it fully with plastic |

> **When there is no sterile room, the key is not perfect sterility but speed and the amount of spawn. A few contaminants getting in cannot be prevented, so make the mushroom mycelium overwhelmingly numerous so that it takes the space first.**

## 5. Spawn Run, Inducing Fruiting, and Harvest
| Stage | Management |
|:---|:---|
| Spawn run (about 22 days) | 23-25℃, dark. The absolute upper limit is 34℃, and because the mycelium produces metabolic heat the inside of the substrate can exceed it even when the surroundings are 30℃, so do not stack bottles densely. White to pale cream spreads down 2-4 mm a day; once it reaches the bottom, leave it 3-5 more days (ripening). Blue-green, black, orange or pink is contamination |
| Scratching | When culture ends, scrape off only 2-5 mm of the old mycelium layer evenly with a spoon or a hook disinfected in boiling water, to trigger pinning all at once |
| Pinning | Move to 14-15℃ at 95% humidity or higher (cover with wet cloth or water the floor) with faint light. In 8-10 days, dense millet-like pins form |
| Growth suppression | Keep 5-15 days at 5-7℃ to firm the stems thick and short. Without it the stems come out thin and long, weak, and leave only threads when dried, so do not skip it. A midwinter night is the easiest way |
| Growth | 4-9℃, humidity 75-85%. Restricting ventilation raises carbon dioxide, giving long stems and small caps (market shape); ample ventilation gives thick stems and large caps (wild shape). The latter is better in a disaster, and judge by feel |

> **Even if the temperature steps cannot be set precisely, mushrooms will still come. The direction is what matters. After the spawn run it must be moved somewhere colder. Left at the same temperature, the mycelium only ages and no mushrooms form.**

| Harvest | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Timing | When the caps are still curled and the stems have grown long enough. If the caps open and white powder (spores) drifts from between the gills, it is late |
| Method | Hold the cluster at the base and twist off gently. Do not wash |
| Re-fruiting | After a 10-15 day rest (scrape off the remaining stubs and keep at 13-15℃ with humidity), harvest again. Up to 2-3 times; the third flush is 20-30% of the first |
| Yield | From one jar (600-750 g of substrate), 150-250 g in the first flush and 250-350 g including the second. Biological efficiency is 30-50% of the substrate's dry weight |

## 6. Contamination, Pests, and Problem Diagnosis
| Problem | Symptoms and cause | Response |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Green mold | White at first, soon blue-green. 10-30 mm a day, 5-10 times faster than enoki mycelium (2-4 mm). By the time color shows, a large part of the substrate is already lost, so judge early by speed and texture | One jar: take it out at once without opening it. Several jars: move the whole culture area and wash and dry the old site. Whole batch contaminated: discard |
| Black mold, pink mold, hairy mold, bacteria | Black powder, pink cotton, tall white hairs, a sticky sour smell. Poor sterilization and excess water | Discard and lengthen sterilization next cycle |
| Mushroom flies, mites, slugs, rats | Eat the mycelium and young fruiting bodies | Cover the entrance with cloth and set water-dish and sticky traps, keep dry, a band of ash or sawdust, put the bottles on a shelf with a net |

> **Do not turn contaminated substrate into compost. Green mold spores survive in a compost pile and return to the substrate the next year. Burn it or bury it deep away from the growing site.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Prevention is sterilizing thoroughly, using plenty of spawn, using no more than 20% bran (with rich nutrition the competing molds explode first), stacking bottles with gaps, spawning right after rain, and changing the culture site every year. Because it is grown in winter, pests are few.

| Symptom | Cause and action |
|:---|:---|
| No mycelium visible after 5 days of spawning | Spawned while the substrate was hot. Cool it to 25℃ or below and respawn |
| Mycelium spreads and stops | Substrate above 34℃ or short of water. Space the bottles to let heat out |
| Covered with mycelium but no mushrooms | Temperature not lowered, or scratching not done. Move to 14-15℃ and scratch |
| Pins form and then dry up or go soft and die | Too little humidity, or drops of water falling. Raise it with air humidity only |
| Only the stems come out thin and long | Growth suppression skipped, excess carbon dioxide. Keep at 5-7℃ and ventilate |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Spent substrate is mixed into the next cycle's substrate up to 20%, and used as basal fertilizer for the field, a compost accelerator, bedding for earthworms, and substrate for other mushrooms; dried, it can also be firewood. After 2-3 years spores of contaminants accumulate at the culture site, so empty it, wash and dry it, and coat the walls and floor with lime-sulfur or strong ash lye.

## 7. Safety Checks - Mistaking Poisonous Mushrooms
> **This section bears directly on life. Enoki carries the greatest risk in the wild of being confused with poisonous mushrooms. Checking whether it is safe to eat is more important than growing it.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For enoki, spore color is not a means of screening out Amanitas. The spores of the destroying angel and the fool's mushroom are also white and cannot be told apart; conversely, if the spores are rust-brown or purple-brown, it is used to rule out enoki. Check all five traits, and if even one does not match, do not eat it.

| No. | Trait | Enoki standard |
|:---|:---|:---|
| 1 | Substrate | Rotting broadleaf stumps and dead trees. One that grew from the ground is not enoki |
| 2 | Cap slime | Sticky and slippery when wet (check after rain) |
| 3 | Stem base | Short velvety hairs, dark brown to blackish brown |
| 4 | Habit | Dense clusters joined at the base. Solitary ones are other mushrooms |
| 5 | Ring and volva | Both absent. Dig out the base to check |

| Look-alike | How to tell |
|:---|:---|
| Spectacular rustgill, Gymnopilus junonius (poisonous) | Rust-brown spores; gills yellow turning rust-brown; a ring on the stem; cap dry and fibrous, not sticky; base swollen like a club and hairless; cap 6-15 cm; very bitter. Poisoning begins within 30 minutes to 3 hours |
| Destroying angel and fool's mushroom (deadly) | Spores and gills both white, so cannot be told apart. They grow singly on ground near the roots of living trees, with a volva and ring, the cap white to pale green-yellow and smooth, no hairs at the base, and far larger (cap 6-15 cm and stem 9-20 cm) |
| Webcaps, Cortinarius (orellanine, kidney destruction) | Rust-brown spores; a cobweb-like cortina between the cap edge and stem when young (a rust-brown band on the stem when mature); scattered on the ground. Symptoms appear after days to 3 weeks |
| Sulfur tuft (poisonous) | Same substrate and same clusters. Spores purple-brown to blackish brown; gills sulfur-yellow then olive then purple-brown; very bitter; no hairs at the stem base and the whole stem yellow to yellow-brown |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There are also pinkgills (pink spores, grow on the ground, gastrointestinal poison) and dung mushrooms, and the brick tuft (edible) is hard to tell from the sulfur tuft, so do not eat it unless sure. When tasting, touch a little to the tip of the tongue, and if it is bitter, spit it out and do not eat it.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For the first meal, go through every check, cook it thoroughly, have one person eat a single piece and wait 24 hours, and set one specimen of what was eaten aside. Vomiting and diarrhea beginning 6-24 hours after eating is the typical course of amatoxin poisoning, and even if the symptoms subside for a while and it looks like recovery, the liver is being destroyed. The safest enoki is the one that came from a bottle I spawned myself.

## 8. Storage, Cooking, and Annual Schedule
| Storage | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Fresh | At room temperature the base browns in three days and goes soft in four. In midwinter, hung under a north-facing eave or in a cave for 2-3 weeks |
| Drying | Tear the cluster by hand and spread on mats or trays to sun-dry for 1-2 days on a clear day (vitamin D rises), shade and wind drying 2-4 days, strung on thread under the eaves 3-5 days, beside a fire at 40-50℃ for half a day, or in winter freeze it outdoors and dry in the cold wind for 5-10 days (best suited to winter harvests). Done when it crumbles when rubbed by hand. Only thickly grown mushrooms keep their form after drying |
| Powder | Turn pieces that start to crumble into powder. It is compact, adds umami to broth, and adds protein and B vitamins to grain flour |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Enoki is always cooked. Blanching takes 2-3 minutes (the least fuel), soup or stew 5-10 minutes, grilling 5 minutes and pancakes 5-8 minutes. Soak dried ones in lukewarm water for 10-15 minutes and use the soaking water as broth, avoiding the crumbs at the bottom. If a grain-heavy diet is dragged on all winter, thiamine shortage brings numb legs, swelling and severe fatigue, and a handful of enoki makes up that lack. From November to March it is the only fresh protein source and source of B vitamins.

| Month | Work |
|:---|:---|
| Jan-Mar | Midwinter harvest (north-facing shed, cave) and immediate drying, manage the rest for the second flush, in March return spent substrate to the field and wash and boil the containers |
| Apr-June | Gather sawdust (age conifer sawdust outdoors), dry and grind eggshells, secure rice bran |
| Jul-Aug | Do not grow (34℃ danger zone). Hand over to other mushrooms |
| September | Prepare and sterilize substrate (gather firewood and process it all at once in the drum) |
| October | Spawn and spawn run (indoors at 23-25℃, choose right after rain to spawn) |
| November | Scratching and pinning (move to 14-15℃ in a cave or north-facing shed) |
| December | Growth suppression (5-7℃) and growth (4-9℃), first harvest |

> **Never forget: one that grew from the ground is not enoki, and if the gills turn rust-brown or yellowish it is not enoki. Above 34℃ the mycelium dies, after the spawn run move it somewhere colder, do not skip growth suppression, and without electricity it is a winter crop.**
