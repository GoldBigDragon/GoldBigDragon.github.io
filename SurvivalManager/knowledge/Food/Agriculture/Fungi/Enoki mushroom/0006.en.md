## 13. Storage Without Refrigeration

### 13-1. Fresh, It Will Not Last Three Days

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In the evaluation table, enoki's storability as a fresh mushroom is E. Without power, that means 3-4 days at room temperature and 2 weeks at low temperature. Moisture runs close to 90%, and the thin stem gives it a large surface area, so it loses water fast and spoils fast at the same time. And this mushroom **browns starting from the base.** Once the bottom of a cluster starts to brown, it spreads through the whole thing within a day or two.

> **But drying changes the grade completely.**
> The exclusion verdict and the storability grade are based on the raw, unprocessed mushroom. Drying uses almost no fuel and almost no equipment, so in a disaster situation, enoki's storability problem is effectively solved by drying alone. One of the two disqualifications behind its exclusion is solved with nothing more than a knife and a drying rack.

### 13-2. Drying

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Enoki is an easy mushroom to dry. Its thin stem lets the inside dry quickly, so it rarely develops mold from an undried core the way a thick-capped wine cap mushroom does.

| Method | Condition | Duration | Result |
|:---|:---|:--:|:---|
| **Sun drying** | Tear the clusters apart by hand and spread them on a mat or drying rack | 1-2 days, clear weather | Easiest, and it raises vitamin D |
| **Shade air-drying** | Under well-ventilated eaves | 2-4 days | Keeps color and aroma well |
| **String-hanging** | Thread whole clusters on a string under the eaves | 3-5 days | Takes up less space |
| **Fireside drying** | On a rack over a hearth, 40-50°C | Half a day | Usable during the monsoon. Watch that it doesn't scorch |
| **Winter freeze-drying** | Set out below freezing, then dry in the cold wind | 5-10 days | Suits a winter harvest best |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The last method suits enoki especially well. Since this mushroom is harvested in the depth of winter, weak sunlight and high humidity can make sun drying difficult. Set it out in a cold, freezing wind instead, and the ice sublimates away directly as it dries. The color stays white and the tissue does not break down, giving the best dried quality. It is the same principle as drying pollock.

| Stage | Verdict |
|:---|:---|
| Fully dry | **Crumbles when rubbed between the fingers.** If it still bends, it is not dry enough yet |
| Storage | Once fully cooled, **in an airtight container with charcoal or roasted salt** |
| Duration | **A year or more** in a cool, dark place. Ground into powder, two years or more |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;One caution. Enoki shrinks drastically once dried and turns thread-like. Drying 1kg leaves about 80g. And being thin, it crumbles at the slightest touch. So rather than keeping dried enoki whole for a long time, **it is more practical to grind it into powder.**

> **Thin, drawn-out enoki leaves almost nothing once dried. Only a mushroom properly thickened through the growth-restraint stage in Section 7 keeps its shape after drying. The restraint stage is directly tied to how well it stores.**

### 13-3. Other Storage Methods

| Method | How to make it | Duration |
|:---|:---|:--:|
| **Mushroom powder** | Fully dry, then grind. Sieve and keep only the fine part | 2 years or more |
| **Salted** | Blanch, drain, and layer with salt at 20% of the weight | 6 months - 1 year |
| **Jangajji (pickled)** | Blanch, then steep in soy sauce and vinegar | 3-6 months |
| **Fermented storage** | Submerge in 3% brine for lactic fermentation | 3-6 months |
| **Oil-preserved** | Blanch, drain, and submerge fully in oil | 1-3 months |
| **Winter natural refrigeration** | Hang out under a north-facing eave or in a root cellar | 2-3 weeks. Winter only |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Winter natural refrigeration is a method especially suited to enoki alone. Since the harvest season is the depth of winter, the outside air is already at 0-5°C. Hang it in a net under a north-facing eave, and it lasts 2-3 weeks with no power at all. Repeated freezing and thawing turns it soft, so choose a spot that stays frozen, or one that stays consistently above 0°C.

> **Mushroom powder is especially valuable in a disaster situation.**
> It takes up little space, adds savory depth to broths, and can be mixed into grain flour to add protein and B vitamins. Once a dried batch starts to crumble, grinding it into powder is better than throwing it away.

## 14. Cooking and Processing

### 14-1. Always Cook It Thoroughly

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Do not eat enoki raw. The raw mushroom contains compounds that hinder digestion and irritate the stomach, and without cooking, the nutrients inside the cell walls cannot be absorbed. And even a wild specimen that has passed the checks in Section 12 must still always be cooked.

| Cooking | Fuel | Time | Note |
|:---|:--:|:--:|:---|
| Blanching | Little | 2-3 min | Uses the least fuel. Dress it as a namul side |
| Stir-frying | Little | 5-8 min | A little oil greatly improves the flavor |
| Soup or stew | Medium | 5-10 min | Adds savory depth to the broth |
| Grilling | Little | 5 min | Can even be laid whole over embers, cluster and all |
| Pan-fried batter | Medium | 5-8 min | Mixed into batter, it stretches a meal further |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;From a fuel-saving standpoint, enoki is an advantageous mushroom. It is thin, so it cooks in 2-3 minutes. Unlike shiitake or wine cap mushrooms, which need 10 minutes or more of boiling, enoki is enough just added at the very end of a finished soup right before the heat is turned off.

### 14-2. Using Dried Enoki

| Order | Method |
|:--:|:---|
| 1 | Soak in lukewarm water for 10-15 minutes. Being thin, it plumps up quickly |
| 2 | **Do not discard the soaking water.** It holds savory flavor and water-soluble nutrients |
| 3 | Use only the top of the water as broth, avoiding the debris settled at the bottom |
| 4 | Slice the softened mushroom and stir-fry it or add it to soup |
| 5 | Powder needs no soaking — add it straight into broth or dough |

### 14-3. Combinations That Make Up for a Grain Shortage

| Combination | Effect |
|:---|:---|
| **Mushroom + grain porridge** | Mushroom protein makes up for the lysine grain lacks |
| **Mushroom powder + wheat or buckwheat flour** | Mixed into dough for hand-pulled noodles or knife-cut noodles, it adds protein and B vitamins |
| **Mushroom + namul greens** | Their micronutrient makeup differs from each other |
| **Mushroom + oil** | Improves absorption of fat-soluble compounds |
| **Mushroom + beans** | Overlapping protein boost, but a different amino acid profile |

> **Mushrooms are right to treat not as a calorie source but as a supplement for protein and micronutrients.**
> For enoki, that means vitamin B1 and B2 above all. Running a grain-heavy diet all winter brings on thiamine deficiency — numb legs, swelling, severe fatigue. A handful of enoki fills that gap, and when winter leaves nothing else fresh, there is nothing to replace it with.

### 14-4. Its Place in the Winter Diet

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Enoki's real value lies in its season. From November to March there are no green leafy vegetables, stored root vegetables have lost much of their nutrition, and dried foods have lost a good share of their vitamin C and B vitamins. Almost no crop comes in fresh during that stretch. Enoki is what grows then.

| Season | Fresh food | Enoki's role |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Spring-autumn | Namul greens, leafy vegetables, fruiting vegetables | Supplementary. Little need to grow it deliberately |
| **November-March** | **Almost none** | **The only source of fresh protein and B vitamins** |

## 15. Annual Work Calendar

| Month | Task |
|:--:|:---|
| January | **Deep-winter harvest.** Harvested from north-facing sheds and root cellars. Freeze-dry or sun-dry it right away |
| February | Continue harvesting. Rest and manage the 2nd flush. Check spore prints |
| March | Final harvest. Clear spent substrate back to the field. Wash and boil the containers |
| April-May | Gather sawdust. Stack softwood sawdust outdoors and let it weather under the rain |
| June | Collect and dry eggshells, then grind them. Secure rice bran |
| July-August | **Do not grow it.** The 34°C danger zone. Switch to other mushrooms (wine cap, wood ear) |
| **September** | **Prepare and sterilize the substrate.** Gather firewood and process it all at once in the drum |
| **October** | **Inoculate and incubate.** Indoors at 23-25°C. Time the inoculation for right after rain |
| **November** | **Scratch and pin.** Move to a root cellar or north-facing shed at 14-15°C |
| **December** | **Growth restraint, then growth.** Through 5-7°C to 4-9°C. The first harvest comes in |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The core of this schedule is resting in summer. Commercial growers use refrigeration to grow it year-round, but without electricity you cannot grow it in summer. Past 34°C the mycelium dies, and there is no way to create 4-9°C. Rather than forcing a summer crop, hand that season to other mushrooms and fix enoki as a winter-only crop.

### Never Forget

| Item | Details |
|:--:|:---|
| 1 | **The fact that its spores are white is what makes enoki especially dangerous.** Spore color cannot be used to rule out Amanita. Check together whether it grew from wood, whether it is clustered, whether the base has no sac, and whether the stem base is dark brown and velvety |
| 2 | **One growing from the ground is not enoki.** Enoki grows only on rotting hardwood stumps and dead standing trees |
| 3 | **If the gills turn rusty brown or take on a yellow tint, it is not enoki.** That means Gymnopilus junonius or Hypholoma fasciculare |
| 4 | **Past 34 degrees, the mycelium dies.** Because of metabolic heat, the inside of the substrate can exceed this even when the surroundings are at 30°C |
| 5 | **After incubation, always move it somewhere colder.** If the temperature is not lowered, no mushrooms will form |
| 6 | **Do not skip the growth-restraint stage.** Only a mushroom grown thick keeps anything worth eating once dried |
| 7 | **Drying makes the storability problem disappear.** Do not be fooled by the E grade given to the raw mushroom |
| 8 | **Without electricity, it is a winter crop.** Trying to grow it in summer will fail |
| 9 | **Because it does not photosynthesize, it is a food source unshaken by a disaster that cuts sunlight.** An SS for sunlight does not mean it uses light well — it means it stands outside light's reach entirely |
| 10 | **The safest enoki is the one that grew from a bottle you inoculated yourself** |
