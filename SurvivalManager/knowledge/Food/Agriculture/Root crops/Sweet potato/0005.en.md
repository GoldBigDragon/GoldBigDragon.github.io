## 12. Harvest
### 12-1. Judging When to Harvest
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Unlike the potato, the sweet potato is not left until the leaves have dried up completely. The roots keep thickening as long as the leaves are alive, so the rule is to delay the harvest as long as possible, right up until frost comes.

| Criterion | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Days of growth | **120-150 days** after planting (110 days for early varieties, 160 days for late varieties) |
| Soil temperature | Be sure to finish **before it falls to 15°C or below** |
| Air temperature | Before frost falls. If the leaves are caught by frost, the cold damage travels down into the roots |
| Trial digging | Dig one or two plants in advance to check the size |

> **The harvest time for sweet potatoes is decided not by "Is it fully grown?" but by "Is it before the cold sets in?"**
> Sweet potatoes that were in ground where the soil temperature dropped below 10°C will certainly rot in storage after being dug. Even if they look sound on the outside, they have already suffered cold damage inside. **Even if they are not yet fully thick, digging before frost is always the right choice.**

---
### 12-2. How to Dig

| Step | Method |
|:--:|:---|
| 1 | **Clear the vines away 5-7 days before harvest.** This gives the skin time to harden |
| 2 | Dig in the morning on a **clear, dry day**. If the soil is wet, the skin peels off |
| 3 | Dig down with a spade or hoe 30cm outside the plant and lift the soil |
| 4 | Do not pull on the roots; **lift them up with the soil, then separate them by hand** |
| 5 | Shake off the soil gently, but **never wash them with water** |
| 6 | Spread them in the shade in the field for 2-3 hours to dry the surface moisture |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sweet potato skin is far thinner and weaker than potato skin. A single scratch from a fingernail becomes the starting point of rot in storage. **Damaged ones are set aside and eaten first.**

---
### 12-3. Leaves and Stems Are Food Too
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sweet potato shoots have long been eaten as namul (seasoned vegetable side dish) in South Korea, and they are nutritious enough to be scored as a separate item in the evaluation table. Because shoots can be picked and eaten all summer until the roots are dug, **a single sweet potato crop yields both summer greens and autumn calories.**

| Part | Use | Caution |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Young shoots | Blanched for namul, or for soup | Picking too many delays root bulking |
| Leaf stalks (stems) | Peeled for namul, or dried as mugnamul (stored dried namul) | Always peel off the tough outer skin |
| Leaves | Blanched for ssam (leaf wraps), or dried into powder | They are astringent, so discard the blanching water |
| Whole vine | Livestock feed, compost | Returning it to the field after harvest brings the nutrients back |

---
## 13. Maintaining Seed Sweet Potatoes and Securing Slips
### 13-1. Sweet Potatoes Are Not Perpetuated by Seed
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sweet potatoes do flower and set seed, but flowering is rare in temperate regions, and plants grown from seed vary in their traits. **In practice, all propagation is vegetative, that is, by sprouting shoots from the roots, cutting the shoots, and planting them.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For this reason, the sweet potato receives two opposite grades at the same time in the evaluation table: **S for Seed saving and E for Seed longevity.** The genotype is preserved 100%, so the variety does not drift; the trade-off is that **seeds cannot be dried and stored, so the plant must be carried through the winter as living sweet potatoes.**

> **The most dangerous period in growing sweet potatoes is not in the field but in the winter storehouse.**
> If the seed sweet potatoes freeze or rot, there is nothing to plant the following year. Unlike other crops, a fresh start by simply "taking out a packet of seeds" is not possible.

---
### 13-2. Choosing Seed Sweet Potatoes

| Criterion | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Size | **150-250g**. If too small, the sprouts are weak; if too large, it takes up too much space |
| Shape | Evenly spindle-shaped, with no constrictions |
| Condition | Completely free of wounds, lesions, and black spots. One infected root passes it on to all the others |
| Selection | Choose those **from the best-growing plants**. Repeating this selection every year produces a strain suited to that field |
| Quantity | **60-80kg per 1,000m²** of the area to be planted. This assumes cutting the shoots 3-4 times |

---
### 13-3. Getting Through the Winter - Temperature Is Everything

| Stage | Conditions | Duration |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **① Curing** | **30-33°C, humidity 90-95%** | 3-4 days |
| **② Cooling** | Lower gradually to 13°C | 4-5 days |
| **③ Storage** | **12-15°C, humidity 85-90%** | All winter |

> **Sweet potatoes suffer cold damage below 9°C. This is the exact opposite of the potato.**
> If sweet potatoes are put in the same cool root-cellar pit where potatoes are kept, only the sweet potatoes are wiped out. **The two crops must always be stored separately.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**How to make 30°C without electricity** - the curing stage lasts only 3-4 days, so it can be set up as a temporary arrangement.

- Put them in a thick cardboard box or wooden chest and place it in the **araenmok (the warmest spot of an ondol, i.e. floor heating) of a sunny room**.
- Wrap them thickly in straw or blankets, and during the day cover with black cloth to absorb solar heat.
- Put in a container of water as well to maintain humidity.
- Once curing is finished, a cork layer forms on the skin and the wounds heal, and **storage losses drop to half or less.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Winter storage place** - 12-15°C is about the temperature of a corner of a room where people live or the back of a kitchen. An underground root-cellar pit suits potatoes but is too cold for sweet potatoes. **Keeping them inside the living space is the safest.**

---
### 13-4. Raising Slips (Spring)

| Step | Method |
|:--:|:---|
| 1 | Start **50-60 days before** the planned planting date |
| 2 | Lay the seed sweet potatoes down in rows and cover them with **sand or fine soil to a depth of 3-4cm** |
| 3 | Keep the temperature at **30-33°C** for 5 days to induce sprouting |
| 4 | When sprouts emerge, lower it to **23-25°C** and give plenty of sunlight |
| 5 | When the shoots grow to **25-30cm**, cut them off, leaving 3-5cm at the base |
| 6 | New shoots grow again from the cut spot. **3-4 harvests of shoots from one root** are possible |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**When there is no electrically heated hotbed, use fermentation heat.** Layer fresh straw, rice bran, and livestock manure in a pit, sprinkle water, and tread it down; fermentation starts, and after a few days the inside rises to 40-50°C. Cover it with 10cm of soil and bury the seed sweet potatoes in it, and it becomes a hotbed. **When judging without a thermometer, put a hand in; the right level is "warm but not hot."**

---
### 13-5. Managing Degeneration
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Repeated vegetative propagation of sweet potatoes also causes viruses to accumulate and yields to fall. It is not as sharp as in the potato, but within 5-6 years the decline becomes noticeable.

| Response | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Selection | Keep seed sweet potatoes every year only from the **most vigorous plants** |
| Shoot choice | When cutting slips, use the **shoots at the end farthest from the mother plant**. Virus concentration is lower |
| Isolation | Plants with yellow-mottled or crinkled leaves are eaten rather than used as seed sweet potatoes |
| Exchange | If possible, **swap seed sweet potatoes with those from another region every 4-5 years** |

## 14. Toxicity and Precautions
### 14-1. Sweet Potatoes Themselves Are Not Toxic
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Unlike the potato, the sweet potato contains no constantly present toxic substance such as solanine. It can be eaten with the skin, and if it sprouts, just remove that part. It can even be eaten raw.

---
### 14-2. But Three Things Call for Caution

> **First - never eat a sweet potato infected with black rot (black spot disease).**
> Sunken black spots appear on the skin, and when cut open, the flesh is brown all the way through and **extremely bitter.** The ipomeamarone that forms at this point is **a liver- and lung-toxic substance that is not destroyed by heating.** Livestock that eat it die of pulmonary edema.  
> **The way to identify it is by taste. If it tastes bitter, spit it out at once and throw all of it away.**

> **Second - do not cut away only the rotten part and eat the rest.**
> Mold toxins spread far more widely than the visibly discolored area. Sweet potatoes are high in moisture, so the spread is fast. **If part of it has gone soft, discard the whole thing.**

> **Third - eating a lot of raw sweet potato causes stomach pain.**
> Raw starch is not digested and ferments in the large intestine, causing gas and abdominal pain. A small amount is fine, but **when sweet potato is eaten as a staple it must always be cooked** so that its calories are also absorbed properly.

---
### 14-3. Handling Sweet Potato Shoots

| Item | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Oxalic acid | Found in the leaves and stems. **Always discard the blanching water** |
| Skin | The outer skin of the leaf stalks is tough and astringent. It must be peeled off |
| Large intake | Eating large amounts every day carries a risk of kidney stones, so alternate with other namul |

## 15. Storage Without Refrigeration
### 15-1. Curing Is Everything
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The success or failure of sweet potato storage is decided in the **3-4 days right after harvest**. How this period is handled determines the following 6 months.

```
[Stage 1] Curing        30-33°C · humidity 90-95% · 3-4 days
         → A cork layer forms on the wound surface. Blocks pathogen entry
   ↓
[Stage 2] Cooling       Gradually to 13°C over 4-5 days
         → Rapid chilling causes condensation and mold growth
   ↓
[Stage 3] Main storage  12-15°C · humidity 85-90% · darkness · gentle ventilation
         → 5-6 months
   ↓
[Stage 4] Inspection    Once every 2 weeks. Remove soft ones at once
```

| Condition | Reason |
|:---|:---|
| **Never 9°C or below** | Cold damage. The inside turns hard, loses flavor, and soon rots |
| **Never 18°C or above** | They sprout and lose weight |
| Humidity 85-90% | If low, they shrivel; if high, mold grows |
| Complete darkness | They sprout when exposed to light |

---
### 15-2. Comparison of Storage Methods

| Method | How | Duration | Features |
|:---|:---|:--:|:---|
| **Indoor box storage** | After curing, layer them in a cardboard box lined with newspaper. In a corner of a room where people live | 4-6 months | **The most reliable** |
| **Rice-hull or sand layering** | Fill a box with sweet potatoes alternating with rice hulls or dry sand | 3-5 months | Prevents rot from spreading |
| **Insulated straw pile** | Spread straw thickly in an indoor corner, pile the sweet potatoes on it, then cover with straw and straw mats | 3-4 months | When straw is the only material |
| **Underground pit** | At least 1m deep, straw on the bottom | 2-3 months | **Not recommended.** It is hard to hold 12°C |
| **Under the araenmok of an ondol** | The space beside the gudeul (the heating flues under the floor) | 5-6 months | Best if the temperature is right. If too hot, they sprout |

> **Do not put sweet potatoes in a pit meant for potatoes.**
> Potatoes suit 3-5°C and sweet potatoes suit 12-15°C. **Each crop's optimal storage temperature falls in the other's cold-damage or sprouting range.**

---
### 15-3. Very Long-Term Storage - Dried, It Lasts Years

| Processing method | How | Duration |
|:---|:---|:--:|
| **Jeolgan goguma (sun-dried sweet potato slices, also called ppaettaegi)** | Slice raw sweet potatoes 5mm thick and dry completely in the sun for 3-5 days | **1-2 years** |
| **Dried steamed sweet potato strips (mallaengi)** | Steam, peel, slice lengthwise, and dry | 6 months-1 year |
| **Starch extraction** | Grate, stir into water, and dry the sediment that settles | **Several years** |
| **Sweet potato flour** | Pound jeolgan goguma into powder | 1-2 years |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Jeolgan goguma is a traditional famine food of the island areas of Gyeongsang Province in southeastern South Korea.** All it takes is slicing thin and drying; the volume shrinks to 20%, and as long as insects are kept out, it keeps beyond the year. It is soaked in water and cooked into porridge, or pounded into flour to make tteok (rice cake).

> **The best combination for disaster preparedness is "indoor box storage + jeolgan goguma."**
> Get through the winter on the boxed stock, eating fresh ones, and immediately slice and dry any that start to soften in storage, turning them into a long-term reserve. **The decision to dry before rotting protects the harvest.**

## 16. Cooking and Processing
### 16-1. Fuel-Saving Cooking Methods

| Method | Fuel | Time | Taste |
|:---|:--:|:--:|:---|
| **Roasted in ash embers (gun-goguma)** | Almost none | 40-60 minutes | The sweetest |
| Steaming | Medium | 25-35 minutes | Decent |
| Boiling | A lot | 20-30 minutes | Sugars leach into the water |
| Residual-heat cooking | **Little** | 30 minutes of heating, then 2 hours of keeping warm | Same as steaming |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**The reason roasted sweet potatoes are the sweetest** is that they spend a long time in the 60-75°C range. At these temperatures, beta-amylase converts starch into maltose. If cooked quickly over high heat, this enzyme is denatured before it can do its work, and the sweetness is lower.

> **Burying sweet potatoes in the embers left after a fire has been used up is the best option for fuel efficiency.**
> Put into the ash of an agungi (traditional firewood hearth) or a campfire site after cooking is done and covered, it cooks without any extra fuel.

---
### 16-2. Processed Foods

| Processed food | How to make | Use |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Jocheong and yeot (grain syrup and hard taffy)** | Pour malt (yeotgireum) water over steamed sweet potato, saccharify at 60°C for 6-8 hours, then boil down | Sweetener. For when there is no sugar |
| **Starch** | Grate, stir into water, collect the sediment, and dry | Muk (starch jelly), noodles, paste |
| **Sweet potato makgeolli (Korean cloudy wine)** | Steamed sweet potato + nuruk (Korean fermentation starter) + water, 5-7 days at 20-25°C | Preserves calories, disinfects water |
| **Mugnamul (dried namul)** | Blanch and dry sweet potato shoots | Winter greens |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Sweet potato jocheong is especially valuable in a disaster.** When sugar cannot be obtained, sweetness decides whether meals remain sustainable, and jocheong keeps for a long time at room temperature. It can be made with nothing more than malt (yeotgireum; barley sprouted and dried).

## 17. Annual Work Calendar

| Month (in South Korea) | Task |
|:--:|:---|
| Jan-Feb | Check stored sweet potatoes every 2 weeks. Remove soft ones |
| **March** | Sort the seed sweet potatoes, **set up the hotbed and bury them** (50-60 days before planting) |
| April | Raise slips. Till the field, add compost and wood ash, form the raised beds |
| **May** | **First cutting of slips and planting** (after confirming soil temperature of 15°C or above) |
| June | Check establishment, replant missing hills, weed, use the second cutting of slips |
| July | Lift the vines, start harvesting sweet potato shoots |
| August | Vine management, keep harvesting shoots. Root bulking stage |
| September | Check size by trial digging. Confirm the timing of frost |
| **October** | Clear the vines → **harvest** 5-7 days later, cure immediately |
| November | Curing complete, cooling, start main storage. Dry jeolgan goguma |
| December | Check storage. Process spoiled ones at once |

| Item | Details |
|:--:|:---|
