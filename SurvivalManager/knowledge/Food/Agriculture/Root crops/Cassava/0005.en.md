## 14. Cyanogenic Glycosides and Detoxification

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This section is not a recipe. Eating cassava without knowing what is in this section can kill a person or leave them unable to walk for the rest of their life. Anyone who intends to treat cassava as food must learn this section before the growing methods.

### 14-1. What Is In It

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Every part of cassava contains cyanogenic glycosides. Most of it is linamarin, and about 5% is lotaustralin. These compounds are not themselves poisonous. The poison is released when they break down.

| Step | What Happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | Linamarin sits inside the cell's vacuole |
| 2 | An enzyme called linamarase sits separately, near the cell wall |
| 3 | When the tissue is broken, the two meet. This happens when peeling, slicing, or chewing |
| 4 | The enzyme cleaves linamarin into glucose and acetone cyanohydrin |
| 5 | Acetone cyanohydrin breaks down spontaneously at pH 5 or above, or at 35°C or above |
| 6 | This produces acetone and hydrogen cyanide (HCN) — prussic acid gas |
| 7 | Hydrogen cyanide has a boiling point of 26°C and is highly volatile |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;These seven steps contain the entire principle behind processing cassava. Detoxification ultimately means turning linamarin into hydrogen cyanide and then either releasing it into the air or washing it away with water. To do that, the cell must first be broken so the enzyme and its substrate can meet, and then time, temperature, and air must be provided so the gas can escape.

### 14-2. How Dangerous Is It

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Sweet-variety fresh root | About 50mg or less of cyanide equivalents per 1kg fresh weight |
| Bitter-variety fresh root | From 100mg up to 1,000mg or more |
| Skin (including the inner white layer) | Several to several dozen times higher than the flesh |
| Leaves | Commonly higher than the root |
| International standard (cassava flour) | 10mg or less of cyanide equivalents per 1kg dry weight |
| Adult acute lethal dose | About 0.5-3.5mg of hydrogen cyanide per 1kg body weight |
| Converted for a 60kg adult | Roughly 30-200mg |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Multiplying out this table makes the situation clear. A 200g portion — one meal's worth — of a bitter-variety root containing 1,000mg of cyanide equivalents per kg contains 200mg of cyanide equivalents. That falls at the upper end of the adult lethal-dose range. Saying that a single meal of unprocessed bitter cassava can kill a person is not an exaggeration. For a child, a far smaller amount is already dangerous.

### 14-3. Why Boiling Alone Is Not Enough

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is the most important part, and the most widely misunderstood. Common sense suggests that boiling it a long time should drive off the poison, but boiling it whole or in large pieces actually leaves the poison behind. The reason lies in the enzyme.

| Situation | What Happens |
|---|---|
| Boiling a large piece whole | By the time heat reaches the inside, the enzyme is destroyed first |
| The enzyme is killed | Linamarase loses activity at about 72°C |
| Linamarin remains intact | Linamarin itself is heat-stable and barely breaks down from boiling |
| The person eats it | Bacteria in the gut break the linamarin down |
| Cyanide is released inside the body | Poisoning symptoms appear a few hours later |

> **Boiling drives off hydrogen cyanide, but it leaves behind any linamarin that has not yet turned into hydrogen cyanide. And that linamarin turns into poison inside the human gut.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;That is why the order of processing is decisive. First break the cells to let the enzyme go to work, then allow enough time for linamarin to pass through acetone cyanohydrin all the way to hydrogen cyanide, and only heat it at the end. Grinding, soaking, fermenting, and drying — every one of these steps exists to build this sequence. Sweet varieties start with such a low content that peeling and boiling alone bring them below the safety threshold, but if a bitter variety skips this sequence, danger remains no matter how long it is boiled.

### 14-4. Telling Bitter and Sweet Varieties Apart

| Method | Reliability | Explanation |
|---|:---:|---|
| Variety information | Highest | If you know the line you planted, go by that |
| Taste | Moderate | Touch a very small amount of the raw root to your tongue — a bitter variety tastes strongly bitter. Do not swallow it |
| Skin color and thickness | Low | Use only as a reference |
| Growing conditions | Reference | Even the same variety has higher cyanide content when grown in drought or poor soil |

> **Bitterness and cyanide content are broadly proportional, but not exactly proportional. Treat and process any cassava of unknown variety as a bitter variety. This one rule alone prevents most accidents.**

### 14-5. Removal Procedure - Step by Step

#### 14-5-1. Peeling

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Principle | Cyanogenic glycosides are concentrated in the skin |
| Method | Make a lengthwise cut, then peel off the outer brown layer and the inner white layer together |
| Thickness | The full skin is 2-3mm thick. Scraping off only a thin layer accomplishes nothing |
| How to judge | The peeled surface must be smooth and white. If a pink or pale layer remains, it has not been peeled enough |
| Removal rate | A substantial share of the total is removed at this step |
| Handling the peel | Not eaten by people or livestock. Let it rot thoroughly and use it as compost |
| Central core | Remove the tough fibrous core at the center as well |

#### 14-5-2. Soaking in Water

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Principle | Cyanogenic glycosides and their breakdown products dissolve into the water and leach out |
| Sweet variety | Cut into pieces; 6-12 hours is enough |
| Bitter variety (whole root) | 3-6 days. Peel it and keep it fully submerged in water |
| Bitter variety (cut into pieces) | 24-72 hours. The smaller the pieces, the faster it goes |
| Changing the water | Change it at least once a day. Without changing it, the concentration reaches equilibrium and nothing more leaches out |
| Temperature | The warmer, the faster. Around 30° is efficient |
| The soaking water | Poison is dissolved in it. Do not let people or livestock drink it, and do not dump it on the field |
| Caution | Do not soak in a sealed container. Gas builds up |

#### 14-5-3. Grinding and Fermenting

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Principle | Break the cells as thoroughly as possible so the enzyme can act throughout. This is the most effective method |
| Method | Grate or pound the peeled root completely with a grater or a stone mortar |
| Fermentation | Put the mash in a sack or container and leave it in the shade for 3-5 days |
| Pressing | Put it in a sack and press it under a stone to squeeze out the juice. Poison is dissolved in the juice |
| Smell | A sour, fermented smell develops. This is normal |
| Removal rate | Done properly, this removes 95% or more |
| Afterward | Roast or steam the pressed mass and eat it |
| Caution | Discard the squeezed-out juice. To recover starch, let the juice settle and rinse only the sediment several times before using it |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is the method behind West African gari and fufu. Four stages — grinding, packing into a sack to ferment for several days, pressing, and then roasting or steaming again — form a detoxification process refined over generations. If bitter varieties must be processed in bulk in a disaster, this method is the most reliable.

#### 14-5-4. Complete Drying

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Principle | Slicing it thin lets the enzyme act, and hydrogen cyanide escapes into the air while it dries |
| Thickness | Slice thin, 5mm or less. If it is thick, residue remains inside |
| Method | Spread it out to dry in the sun. 4-7 days |
| Shade drying | Slower than sun drying, but there are reports that the enzyme works longer, giving an even higher removal rate |
| How to judge | Dry until it snaps with a crack and crumbles by hand |
| Removal rate | 60-90% depending on conditions |
| Caution | Bagging it before it is fully dry causes mold to grow and leaves poison behind as well |

#### 14-5-5. Thorough Heating

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Principle | Drives off remaining hydrogen cyanide and cooks the starch |
| Sweet variety | Cut into 2-3cm pieces, boil for 20-30 minutes. Do not cover with a lid |
| Bitter variety | Heat only after going through the previous steps. Heating alone is not enough |
| The boiling water | Always discard it. Cyanide is dissolved in it |
| Lid | Leave it off. The gas must be able to escape |
| Ventilation | Open a window when boiling large quantities indoors |
| How to judge | The bitter and astringent taste must be completely gone |

### 14-6. Minimum Procedure by Variety

| Category | Procedure | Time Required |
|---|---|---|
| Sweet variety | Peel thickly, cut into pieces, soak in water for 6-12 hours, discard the water and boil for 20-30 minutes, discard the boiling water | One day |
| Bitter variety (home consumption) | Peel, cut into pieces, soak in water for 3-5 days changing the water daily, slice to 5mm and dry completely for 4-7 days, grind into flour, heat thoroughly when cooking | 1-2 weeks |
| Bitter variety (bulk processing) | Peel, grind completely, pack in a sack and ferment for 3-5 days, press, roast or steam | 1 week |
| Unknown variety | Follow the bitter-variety procedure | - |

### 14-7. Re-Moistening the Flour

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Dried, ground cassava flour often still contains residual linamarin. This is especially true of flour that was dried insufficiently or in a hurry. There is a simple way to reduce this residue further.

| Step | Details |
|---|---|
| 1 | Mix the flour with an equal volume of water to make a damp, dough-like mixture |
| 2 | Spread it thinly, 1cm thick or less, on a wide bowl or board |
| 3 | Leave it undisturbed in the shade for 5 hours or more |
| 4 | During this time, the enzyme still present in the flour breaks down the linamarin, and the hydrogen cyanide escapes |
| 5 | After that, boil or steam it as usual and eat it |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The principle is simple. Linamarase cannot work while dry, but it does not die either. It regains activity as soon as it meets water. Spreading it thin lets the gas that forms escape quickly, and keeping it in the shade is because direct sunlight destroys the enzyme. Adding just this one step can cut residual cyanide to a fraction of what it was. It requires no tools and no fuel, which makes it especially valuable in a disaster.

### 14-8. Processing the Leaves

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Nutrition | 5-10g of protein per 100g fresh weight. Rich in lysine |
| Risk | Cyanide content is commonly higher than in the root |
| Step 1 | Pick young leaves and remove the thick leaf stalks |
| Step 2 | Put them in a mortar and pound completely. Breaking the cells is the key |
| Step 3 | Boil for 30 minutes or more, changing the water |
| Step 4 | Discard the boiling water |
| How to judge | If bitterness remains, boil again |
| Caution | Lightly blanching and eating them is dangerous. They must not be treated like namul (a seasoned vegetable side dish) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Cassava leaves are almost the only way to make up for protein deficiency, the biggest weakness of a cassava diet. Eating only the root fills you up on calories while the body wastes away, but eating the leaves alongside it greatly reduces that problem. On top of that, leaves can be picked from mid-growth onward, before the roots mature, filling the long gap until harvest. However, it must not be forgotten that their cyanide content is higher than the root's. The two steps of pounding and long boiling cannot be skipped.

### 14-9. Acute Poisoning

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Onset | From tens of minutes to 6 hours after ingestion. Appears later when it must first be broken down by gut bacteria |
| Early symptoms | Dizziness, headache, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea |
| Progressive symptoms | Rapid, shallow breathing, heart palpitations, anxiety, confusion |
| Severe | Convulsions, cyanosis, loss of consciousness, respiratory paralysis, death |
| Smell | A bitter-almond smell may be present, but a substantial number of people cannot detect this smell |
| Most vulnerable | Children. Their intake per body weight is larger, so the same amount is far more dangerous |

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| Suspected right after ingestion | Stop them from eating more and remove the remaining food |
| Symptoms appear | Lay the person down somewhere with fresh air and keep them calm |
| Conscious | Give small sips of water. Do not force vomiting |
| Reduced consciousness, convulsions | Lay them on their side to keep the airway clear |
| After recovery | Discard the entire batch of cassava that caused it and review the processing procedure again |
| Underlying reality | What can be done when medical care is unreachable is limited. Prevention is the only real countermeasure |

### 14-10. Chronic Poisoning - Konzo and Neuropathy

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;What actually harms more people from cassava is not acute poisoning but chronic poisoning. Acute poisoning is conspicuous, but chronic poisoning comes on slowly.

| Category | Konzo | Tropical Ataxic Neuropathy |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | Sudden. Appears within hours to days | Gradual. Develops over years |
| Main symptoms | Both legs go stiff and walking becomes impossible | Unsteady gait, declining vision, and hearing loss |
| Most affected | Common in children and women of childbearing age | Common in people over 40 |
| Course | Does not recover. Only the worsening stops | Progressive |
| Trigger | Eating insufficiently processed bitter varieties continuously for weeks or more | Long-term exposure to insufficiently processed cassava |
| Aggravating factor | Rises sharply when combined with protein deficiency | The same |
| Treatment | None | None |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It matters to know when konzo occurs. This disease always comes together with famine. That is because, out of hunger, people soak for just one day what they would normally soak for three, and take in and eat after two days what they would normally dry for seven. A disaster is exactly the kind of situation where taking these shortcuts becomes tempting, and so it is also exactly the situation where the procedures in this section are hardest to keep.

### 14-11. The Relationship Between Protein and Detoxification

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Cyanide that enters the body is converted in the liver into thiocyanate and excreted in the urine. This reaction requires sulfur, and that sulfur comes from sulfur-containing amino acids such as methionine and cysteine. In other words, a person who eats enough protein can detoxify cyanide, but a person short on protein is hurt far more badly by the same amount of cyanide.

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| Eating only cassava | Protein is only 3.4% of calories. Detoxification capacity drops |
| Detoxification capacity drops | The same amount of cyanide causes greater damage |
| Damage accumulates | The risk of konzo and neuropathy rises |
| Result | Eating only cassava makes the poison more potent |

> **One of the most important conditions for eating cassava safely is not eating cassava alone. Beans, fish, eggs, or cassava leaves — eating any of these alongside it is what allows the body to process the cyanide.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Thiocyanate causes another problem as well. It interferes with the thyroid's uptake of iodine, and in iodine-deficient areas it increases goiter and the developmental disorders that come with it. This deserves particular attention when relying on cassava in inland areas far from the sea, where salt and seaweed are hard to obtain.

### 14-12. What You Must Never Do

| Prohibited | Reason |
|---|---|
| Eating it raw | Prohibited regardless of variety |
| Peeling only a thin layer | Leaves in place the part with the highest cyanide content |
| Boiling a large piece whole for a long time | The enzyme dies first, leaving linamarin intact |
| Boiling with the lid on | The gas cannot escape |
| Using the boiling water or soaking water | Poison is dissolved in it. Do not use it as broth |
| Giving the soaking water to livestock | Livestock are poisoned too |
| Fermenting in a sealed container | Gas builds up and becomes dangerous |
| Using flour that is not fully dried | Cyanide remains and mold develops |
| Eating it while bitterness remains | A sign that processing is not finished |
| Cutting corners on the procedure | The hungrier you are, the more strictly the procedure must be followed |
