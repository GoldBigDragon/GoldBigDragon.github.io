## Introduction

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Radish (the Korean radish, Raphanus sativus) is a root vegetable of the Brassicaceae (cabbage) family. Its original home is thought to be the Mediterranean coast and Central Asia, and it is one of the crops that people domesticated very early. Botanically, the white root we eat is an organ formed when the root and the base of the stem swell together, and its original purpose was to serve as a nutrient store that would carry the plant through winter and let it flower the following year. This one fact explains almost everything about the crop. Radish is a plant whose stored reserves, laid up for its own use, are taken by people; that is why it stores well, and also why it can no longer be eaten once the flower stalk rises.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Judged by the yardstick of disaster preparedness, radish earns an overall grade of S, with a score of 51.8. Short-term S (52.6), long-term S (51.0) and supplementary SS (62.2) make it rate high from every angle. Few crops are graded S in both the short term and the long term. It means a crop that grows fast yet holds out for a long time, one that is useful early on and does not lose its usefulness as the years go by. A vegetable with only 21kcal per 100g earns such scores not because of calories but because of balance.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The first pillar of that balance is yield. A field of 1,000m² produces 7,000kg, nearly twice the amount of other leafy vegetables. Even though the calorie density is low, the large total volume works out to 1,470kcal per m², which lifts the Calories per area grade to B.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The second pillar is storage. Buried in a root-cellar pit, radish keeps for 3-4 months, and sliced thin and dried it lasts more than a year. In a winter without electricity, the vegetables that actually become food are only those that can be stored in this way.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The third pillar is the leaves. Radish is a root vegetable and a leafy vegetable at the same time. The radish greens and siraegi (dried radish greens) come in almost the same weight as the root, and because their nutritional character differs from that of the root, the two complement each other.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The fourth pillar is Continuous-cropping tolerance, graded SS. In the classification of South Korea's Rural Development Administration (RDA), radish belongs to the group of crops that suffer no harm from continuous cropping. Most Brassicaceae crops must be rested for 2 years or more because of clubroot, but radish is rated as causing no serious problem when planted in the same spot year after year. When the field is small and there is no room for rotation, this grade is worth a great deal. Added to it are Prevalence in South Korea, graded SS, and Seed longevity, graded S. Seed can be obtained anywhere across South Korea, and seed once saved stays viable for 5 years.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The weaknesses are concentrated in three areas. The first is Cold hardiness, graded C. At 0°C the root freezes and its tissue is destroyed, so it must be pulled before a hard frost arrives. This is the exact opposite of kale, which uses the field itself as a storehouse.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The second is Perenniality, graded D. Once pulled, it is finished, with no regrowth.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The third is great vulnerability to waterlogging. When water stands, the root goes soft and rots almost at once. And there is a problem peculiar to radish: pithiness. It is the condition in which, if the right time is missed or a flower stalk rises, hollow cavities form inside the root and make it inedible; its cause is covered in Section 12.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To sum up, radish is a crop that holds one of the most secure places in a field during a disaster. While grains supply the calories and beans supply the protein, radish supplies bulk, moisture and vitamins, and becomes a stored food that carries people through the winter. It is, however, a crop that is strict about timing: sowing and harvest alike must be done on schedule, and if that window is missed, the harvest itself is lost.
