## Introduction

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sugar beet (Beta vulgaris) is a biennial plant in the family Amaranthaceae. It is the same species as Swiss chard and beetroot, a lineage that people have bred to pack sugar into the root. It was developed in late-18th-century Europe by people looking for a raw material for a sweetener to take the place of sugarcane, and it was scaled up into a national enterprise in France, where the Napoleonic Wars had cut off sugar imports. In other words, sugar beet is a crop born under blockade and broken supply lines. Today about 20% of the world's sugar comes from it.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In the disaster-preparedness evaluation, sugar beet earns an Overall grade of S, with a score of 52.4 points. It is rated S for Calories per area, at 3,850kcal per m², which is 1.47 times the self-sufficiency line. It also carries SS for Temperature range, A for Cold hardiness and SS for Infrastructure needs, and above all S for Seed longevity: sealed and stored at room temperature, its seed keeps for 5 years. This sets it decisively apart from most of the tuber crops (tubers/storage roots) in the table, which score E for Seed longevity, meaning that live tubers must be kept alive through every winter. A packet of dry seed put away in a jar can still be sown just the same several years later.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The real reason for writing a document on sugar beet, however, is not its calories but its sweetness. In a disaster, a sweetener is not a luxury. First, sugar is the fastest-absorbed form of energy. Second, sugar is a preservative: in a sugar solution stronger than 60%, most bacteria and molds cannot grow. In a world without refrigeration, the only ways to keep fruit and vegetables for months are to dry them, salt them, or steep them in sugar. Salt cannot be had when the sea is far away, but sugar comes from the field. Third, sugar is the raw material for fermentation. It becomes alcohol and vinegar, and vinegar in turn becomes a means of preserving other foods. Whether or not one can supply one's own sweetener changes the very kinds of food that can be stored.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Of course, there is a price. Getting sugar out of sugar beet consumes a great deal of fuel. The sugar in the root is around 15% and the rest is mostly water, so to obtain 1kg of sugar, several liters of water must be boiled off. The firewood this takes on an open hearth is by no means small. For that reason Section 16 covers the sugar-making procedure together with how much fuel it uses, where fuel can be saved, and whether going all the way to crystallized sugar makes sense at all. In a disaster, the right answer is often to stop at syrup.
