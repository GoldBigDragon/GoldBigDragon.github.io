## 1. Disaster-Preparedness Evaluation

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sugar beet's Overall grade is S, with a score of 52.4 points. By scenario, it rates Short-term S (53.7), Long-term S (51.2) and Supplementary B (47.4). That the short-term result is higher than the long-term one sums up the character of this crop. It needs only five months from sowing to harvest and its seed can be stored for several years, so it can be put to use right away in the first year; but because it is a biennial, seed saving takes two years, and because continuous cropping is difficult, keeping it going over the long run takes a lot of work.

### 1-3. How to Read the Strengths

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sugar beet's greatest strength is its Seed longevity of S. Most of the tuber crops (tubers/storage roots) in this table have a Seed longevity of E. Seed potatoes for potato, tubers for apios and Jerusalem artichoke, and stem cuttings for cassava must be kept alive every winter, and if even one of them dies, there is no farming the following year. Sugar beet seed is dry and can be kept in a jar at room temperature for 5 years. Since no one knows when a disaster will come, it is one of the few crops that can be secured in advance and then taken out and planted when needed.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Temperature range SS and Cold hardiness A are also of great value. The plant grows at 5-28°C and tolerates 5°C below zero. This means that in southern South Korea a cropping system is possible in which the beet is planted in autumn, overwinters, and is dug the following spring; indeed, there is a record that when it is grown this way on Jeju Island, sugar content rises to 20.8Brix and yield increases by 34% compared with a December harvest. Being able to use the winter, when fields would otherwise stand empty, for food production is especially meaningful where land area is short.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Pest-control self-reliance A is also worth noting. Few tuber crops in this table carry an A. The basis is empirical evidence that combining a resistant line with early sowing can cut the occurrence of major pests by 90% or more, and that when the crop is harvested before October, damage stays at about 1%. In other words, even without pesticides, adjusting the planting time alone can deal with a good part of the problem. This is in contrast to the potato, which is helpless against late blight.

### 1-4. How to Read the Weaknesses

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Seed saving D is the most substantial weakness. Sugar beet is a biennial: it forms its root in the first year and sends up a flower stalk and sets seed in the second. To collect seed, you must not eat the root but keep it alive through the winter and replant it the following spring. The fact that the seed lasts 5 years eases this weakness considerably, but after 5 years seed saving is unavoidable, and it then takes work spread over two years. Section 13 covers this problem in detail.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Micronutrients D is the reason sugar beet cannot be made a staple. Even when eaten in amounts large enough to meet calorie needs, only seven nutrients exceed 10% of the recommended amount. Protein density is also C, falling slightly short of the requirement line. Sugar beet is a crop that supplies calories and sweetness, not one that supplies nutrition. Eating the leaves as well changes the picture, however. Sugar beet leaves are the same as Swiss chard leaves, so they make a vegetable rich in vitamins and minerals.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fertilizer demand C means that sugar beet needs more fertilizer than other tuber crops. On top of that, it needs boron separately. When boron is short, heart rot develops, in which the inside of the root rots black; this is not caused by a pathogen but is a purely deficiency symptom. The limited means of supplying boron when there are no chemical fertilizers is a hidden difficulty in growing sugar beet.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Storability C should be considered as well. In a root-cellar pit, storage for 3 to 4 months is possible, but that is shorter than for potatoes, and the sugar is slowly consumed during storage. In other words, the longer the roots are kept, the less sugar they hold. If sugar is the goal, it is better to process them as soon as possible after harvest.

> **Sugar beet is both a calorie crop and a sweetener crop. And a sweetener widens the very range of preservation techniques available in a disaster.**

## 2. What Kind of Plant Is This?

### 2-1. Classification and Origin

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sugar beet is a biennial plant of the genus Beta in the family Amaranthaceae. It is the same species as Swiss chard, beetroot (red beet) and fodder radish, lines that diverged according to which part of the plant people selected to enlarge. Chard is the result of breeding for the leaf, beetroot for color and flavor, and sugar beet for the sugar in the root. Spinach is in the same family but belongs to a different genus.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This fact has two practical implications. First, sugar beet leaves are the same as chard leaves, so they can be eaten as they are. Even while the roots are growing, the lower leaves can be picked and used as a vegetable, filling the long gap before harvest. Second, sugar beet cross-pollinates with chard and beetroot and shares the same diseases with them. When saving seed, they must be planted far apart from one another, and in crop rotation they must not be planted one after another in the same spot.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The wild ancestor is a seashore plant of the Mediterranean and Atlantic coasts. It tolerates salt and wind, grows well in cool climates, and is weak against summer heat. This is why sugar beet slows its growth above 25°C and packs in sugar in the cool autumn.

### 2-2. Appearance

| Organ | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| Leaf | Broadly ovate and glossy, with thick, distinct veins. The shape is the same as chard |
| Leaf stalk | Thick and juicy. It can be eaten like chard |
| Root (edible part) | A white taproot, spindle-shaped. 10-15cm in diameter, 30cm or more in length |
| Root tip | Tapers thinly and reaches a depth of 1m or more |
| Flower stalk | In year 2, it rises 1m or more |
| Flowers | Small, green, inconspicuous flowers are borne in spikes. Pollinated by wind |
| Fruit | A multigerm seed: several seeds fused together into a hard cluster |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;That the seed is a multigerm seed matters at sowing. What looks like a single seed is actually a lump of two or three seeds fused together, so when it is planted in one spot, several sprouts come up at once. For this reason sugar beet must always be thinned; if it is not, the roots crowd each other out and all of them stay small. Some commercial seed has this lump ground down into single seeds, but seed saved at home remains a multigerm seed.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It is also worth remembering that the root is white. It is easily confused with red beetroot, but the red pigment stains the syrup dark red during sugar extraction and leaves an earthy smell. If sugar is the goal, the line must be a white sugar beet.

### 2-3. Growth Stages

| Stage | Timing | Key physiology | Important nutrients |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germination and seedling stage | Up to 4 weeks after sowing | Several sprouts emerge together from the multigerm seed | Phosphorus, nitrogen |
| Vegetative growth | 4-10 weeks | Leaves expand and the root begins to thicken | Nitrogen |
| Root bulking | 10-16 weeks | The root thickens in earnest | Potassium, boron |
| Sugar accumulation | 16-22 weeks | Sugar made in the leaves is stored in the root as sucrose | Potassium; nitrogen restrained |
| Maturity and harvest | Just before harvest | Sugar content reaches its peak | Stop irrigation |
| Overwintering (overwintering cropping system) | Winter | Exposure to low temperatures raises sugar content further | - |
| Bolting and flowering (year 2) | Following spring | The flower stalk rises and the sugar in the root is consumed | - |

> **Apply nitrogen early and potassium late. If nitrogen remains during the sugar accumulation stage, the plant keeps making leaves and does not send sugar to the root. This is the most common failure in growing sugar beet.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Once you understand when sugar accumulates, the knack of cultivation becomes clear. The leaves make sugar by photosynthesis, and that sugar is either used for the growth of the leaves themselves or sent down to the root for storage. When the plant judges that it still needs to grow, the sugar goes to the leaves; when it judges that it need not grow any more, the sugar goes to the root. When nitrogen is plentiful, the plant keeps trying to grow, and when it turns cool and nitrogen runs low, it switches to storage. This is why autumn cultivation and overwintering cultivation give high sugar content.

### 2-4. Nutrition per 100g

| Nutrient | Content | Notes |
|---|:---:|---|
| Energy | 43-77kcal | Varies greatly with variety and growing conditions |
| Water | about 78g | Most of the remainder is sugar |
| Carbohydrate | 10-20g | Almost all of it is sucrose |
| Sucrose (sugar) | 14-20g | This is what is extracted. With overwintering cultivation, 20 Brix or more |
| Protein | 0.8-1.8g | Low |
| Fat | 0.08-0.2g | Almost none |
| Potassium | Present | Plentiful in both root and leaves |
| Vitamin C | Present | Decreases with heating |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;What is special about sugar beet is the form in which it stores sugar. Whereas potato and cassava store starch, sugar beet stores sucrose, that is, the very substance we call sugar. Turning starch into sugar requires enzymes or acid, but with sugar beet you simply press out the juice and drive off the water. This opens up almost the only route by which a sweetener can be produced for oneself in a disaster.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The leaves should be considered as well. Sugar beet leaves are the same as chard, so they contain substantial amounts of vitamins A and K, iron and magnesium per 100g. The leaves make up considerably for the root's weakness of Micronutrients D. If you pick the lower leaves a few at a time while the roots are growing, you can have a vegetable for the five months from sowing to harvest. However, if too many leaves are picked, the photosynthetic area shrinks and sugar content drops.
