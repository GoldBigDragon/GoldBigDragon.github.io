## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Leaf mustard (Brassica juncea) is an allotetraploid leafy vegetable that arose in Central Asia from a natural cross between Chinese cabbage (Brassica rapa) and black mustard (Brassica nigra). Its overall grade is B (44.3 points, 65th of 183 valid species), and its supplementary-food grade is SS (58.7 points). At 36 kcal per 100 g, covering 2,100 kcal a day with leaf mustard alone would take 5.8 kg, so it is a crop that fills another part of the table rather than the rice bowl, and it is specialized less as fresh leaves than as a preserved food made with salt. Its pungent compound sinigrin and the breakdown product allyl isothiocyanate act as a germicide against spoilage microbes, so leaf mustard kimchi sours later and keeps longer than Chinese cabbage kimchi. Its weaknesses are micronutrients D (8 of 21 nutrients), taste D (aversion to its smell and aroma), and perenniality C (finished after one harvest). On the other hand, it offers speed of 45-60 days from sowing to harvest (20-30 days for baby leaves), a seed life of 5 years sealed at room temperature, and an annual habit that lets you save seed without overwintering, which makes it one of the few cabbage-family crops that can achieve seed self-sufficiency in the first year of a disaster. Leaf mustard is not the star of the field but its insurance.

## 2. Plant Overview
| Item | Content |
|:---|:---|
| Classification | Annual of the cabbage family (Brassicaceae), genus Brassica, prone to bolting; sown in autumn, it is a winter annual that passes the winter. It carries the chromosomes of both Chinese cabbage and black mustard, so it crosses with them at a low frequency and does not cross with the cabbage group |
| Leaves, stem | Leaf edges are toothed and the surface slightly ruffled; green, reddish-purple, and mixed colors exist |
| Roots, flowers, pods | The taproot is mostly distributed shallowly within 10-15 cm of the surface. Yellow flowers bloom, and long pods hold rows of brown to reddish-brown seeds 1-2 mm across |

| Stage | Period (after sowing) | Key points |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Germination to early vegetative growth | Up to 20 days | Seeds absorb water, cotyledons open, true leaves emerge, and roots settle in. Nitrogen |
| Rapid vegetative growth | 20-45 days | Leaves and stems grow fast. Nitrogen, potassium |
| Best harvest time | 45-60 days | Leaves are fully grown and not yet tough |
| Bolting and flowering | 60-100 days | The flower stalk rises and the leaves turn tough. Phosphorus, potassium |

> **Because leaf mustard has a short growing period, early nutrient supply almost decides the yield. There is no time to make up with top dressing, so the contest is settled at the basal fertilizer stage.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The flower stalk rises after a period of cold (0-10℃) followed by lengthening days (leaf mustard sown early in spring), and under heat and drought stress (leaf mustard sown in midsummer); in both cases the leaves turn tough and only the pungency grows, making them hard to eat. That is why cropping types divide into spring and autumn, leaving summer empty.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Per 100 g: 36 kcal, protein 3.50 g (38.9% of calories), dietary fiber 2.6 g, and beta-carotene 533 ㎍. The protein ratio is high because calories are low, but added to a diet centered on rice or potatoes (protein ratio 8-9%), it raises the protein ratio without adding calories. Beta-carotene is fat-soluble, so it is absorbed better when eaten with oil or ground perilla or sesame seeds.

## 3. Growing Environment and Varieties
| Condition | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Temperature | Optimum growth 15-20℃ (possible 10-25℃). Germination at 6-35℃ (optimum 25℃, 3-5 days). Above 25℃ leaves turn thin and tough and the pungency becomes excessive; below -5℃ the leaves freeze and turn soft |
| Light | 6 hours or more of direct sun per day. Relative yield 86% under 40% shade. Light-requiring germination, so germination rate plummets if seeds are buried deep |
| Water, soil | Total evapotranspiration about 300 mm. Roots are shallow, so growth stops when the topsoil dries, and excess water causes root rot. Fertile, water-retaining soil, pH 6.0-7.5 (below 6.0, clubroot gets severe) |

| Variety | Characteristics and use |
|:---|:---|
| Green leaf mustard (cheonggat) | Green leaves with strong pungency and strong cold and disease resistance. Most widely used for kimchi |
| Red leaf mustard (jeokgat, honggat) | Red leaves rich in anthocyanin with medium pungency. Kimchi, wraps, pickles |
| Dolsan leaf mustard (dolsangat) | A late variety with large, broad, soft leaves. Exclusively for leaf mustard kimchi |
| Cold-hardy green leaf mustard (eolcheonggat) | Strong in the cold with a mild taste. Winter cultivation and early spring harvest |
| Wild leaf mustard (deulgat) | Close to the wild type, with a strong, coarse aroma. Resistant to pests and diseases and drops its own seed |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The strength of the pungency is the strength of the storage ability. Kimchi made from leaf mustard rich in allyl isothiocyanate slows the growth of spoilage bacteria, so green leaf mustard suits kimchi to eat all winter and Dolsan leaf mustard suits kimchi to eat right at the start of winter. In disaster conditions the first criterion for choosing a variety is not yield but risk spreading. If you sow a mix of two or three varieties with different traits, such as green and red, some survive even if one is hit by disease, and harvest times are spread over 2-3 weeks.

## 4. Field Preparation and Sowing
| Item | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Preparing the soil | Dig 20-30 cm deep, break up clods and level evenly (if clods are large, light-requiring seeds get buried). Bed height 15-20 cm with a drainage ditch. Adjust pH to 6.0-7.5 and mix in wood ash if the soil is acidic |
| Fertilization (1,000㎡) | Nitrogen 8-10 kg, phosphorus 6-8 kg, potassium 6-8 kg, and compost 1,000-1,500 kg, all applied as basal fertilizer 1-2 weeks before sowing (half-rotted compost damages young roots). Skip top dressing, because by the time it takes effect it is already harvest time, and give a small amount only when growth is clearly poor |
| Substitute resources | Fully matured compost (1-1.5 kg per 1㎡), wood ash (a handful per 1㎡; it supplies potassium, corrects acidity, suppresses clubroot, and repels pests, but too much blocks absorption of micronutrients), urine diluted 10-15 times (once at 5-6 true leaves, stop 2 weeks before harvest), and bone meal |

| Cropping type | Sowing | Harvest | Notes |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Autumn crop | Late Aug-mid Sep | Late Sep-end of Oct | The most stable. 40-60 days |
| Overwintering crop | Late Oct-mid Nov | Following spring | Possible in the south. In the central region, cover with straw or fallen leaves or set windbreaks |
| Spring crop | Late March-early April | Mid April-early May | Quickly after the thaw. Bolting if late |
| Summer, highland | Early June-early July | July-Aug | Only at 400 m or higher. In low and mid-elevation open fields the flower stalk rises first, and only seed and labor are lost |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In disaster conditions the autumn crop is the core. Temperatures are heading down, so there is no risk of bolting, pest activity declines, the harvest leads straight into kimjang (winter kimchi-making) and storage, and it fills the empty space left by summer crops.

| Sowing | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Method | Cover seeds with fine soil only enough to hide them, at a depth of 0.5-1 cm, and press lightly by hand or with a board so seed and soil make contact. Row spacing 15-20 cm, final plant spacing 10-15 cm, seed rate 2-3 kg per 1,000㎡ |
| Staggered sowing | Sow in batches at intervals of 10 to 15 days (in autumn, late Aug, early Sep, and mid Sep). The only way to make up for perenniality C, finishing after one harvest |
| Seed treatment | Pick out chaff and soak in water for 3-4 hours, using only the seeds that sink. Soak in 50-55℃ water for 10-15 minutes and then cool in cold water (above 60℃ the seeds die, and below 45℃ the pathogens survive) |

## 5. Water Management and Cultivation Management
| Period | Water management |
|:---|:---|
| Sowing to germination | Lightly and often so the topsoil does not dry (once seeds swell with water, they die if they dry out) |
| Seedling stage, rapid growth | Water when the topsoil dries (roots are shallow and very weak to drying, and water in this period decides leaf size) |
| Just before harvest, rainy season | Cut back somewhat just before harvest (leaves harden, which helps storage), and secure drainage ditches in the rainy season (root rot from excess water is the greater risk) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If it wilts by day and recovers by evening, the shortage is temporary; if leaves are small and thick and the pungency grows, the drying is persistent. If lower leaves turn yellow, roots lack oxygen, and if the base turns soft and smells, that plant is hard to save. Drought stress strengthens pungency, so for kimchi leaves cut back water from 1 week before harvest. For mulch use straw, dry grass, fallen leaves, or rice hulls; when the topsoil exceeds 40℃ on a summer day, roots stop functioning, so even one layer makes a big difference.

| Management | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Thinning | First to 3-5 cm at 2-3 true leaves, second to 10-15 cm (final) at 4-5. Essential work (if crowded, stems only get long and airflow is blocked, causing downy mildew and gray mold). Do not pull; cut near the ground with scissors or a fingernail to protect the shallow roots of neighbors. The thinned baby leaf mustard is the first harvest, tender around 20 days after sowing and eaten in wraps or as fresh-salted greens |
| Weeds | The first 3 weeks decide it. Mulch with straw or dry grass after 3-4 true leaves. Scrape shallowly with a hoe (roots are shallow) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A plant that has bolted cannot be reversed, and even if the flower stalk is cut, another rises beside it, so the only sure method is to set the sowing time so that harvest ends before the flower stalk rises. Young flower stalks with buds formed taste like rapeseed greens or broccoli and are useful in spring when vegetables run short; leave only the plants to seed and eat the rest.

## 6. Pests, Diseases, and Nutrient Problems
| Pest or disease | Symptoms and conditions | Control without chemicals |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Flea beetle | Dense pinholes in leaves. Seedlings die if attacked at the cotyledon and young true-leaf stage | Insect netting from right after germination until 3-4 true leaves, wood ash |
| Aphids | Cluster on the underside of leaves sucking sap, and leaves curl | Soapy water, wood ash, protecting ladybugs |
| Cabbage white caterpillar, diamondback moth | Large holes in leaves / translucent marks on the underside of leaves | Insect netting (stops adults from laying eggs), picking by hand, rubbing off the yellow eggs on the underside of leaves |
| Clubroot | Galls form on roots and plants wilt. Acid soil, continuous cabbage-family cropping | Cannot be cured. Correct acidity, drainage, rotation |
| Downy mildew, gray mold, soft rot | White to gray mold on the underside of leaves and yellowing on top / gray mold (cool, humid conditions) / base turns soft and smells foul (hot, humid conditions, wounds) | Ventilation, spacing, removing diseased leaves and plants / drainage, avoiding wounds |

> **There is no cure for clubroot. Keeping it out is everything, and once it is in, waiting it out through rotation is the only way.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sinigrin by itself has no taste, but at the moment a leaf is wounded it meets an enzyme and turns into allyl isothiocyanate, a defensive compound. Ordinary plant-eating insects, molds, and bacteria avoid it, but adapted pests such as flea beetles and diamondback moths actually follow this smell. Clubroot resting spores survive 7-10 years in the soil, so the rule is to rest the cabbage family for 2 years or more, and diseased plants should be burned or buried far away rather than put in compost. Against butterflies and moths, put netting over the crop right after sowing or delay sowing. Make soapy water by dissolving 20 g of unscented soap in 1 L of water, wet the underside of the leaves, and repeat two or three times a few days apart. If you chop the stems and roots left after harvest finely and plow them in, the breakdown products suppress soil pathogens and nematodes.

| Symptom | Cause and remedy |
|:---|:---|
| Whole leaf yellow, small plant | Nitrogen deficiency. Matured compost, urine diluted 10-15 times |
| Leaf edges dry brown | Potassium deficiency. A handful of wood ash per 1㎡ |
| Poor growth, purplish leaves | Phosphorus deficiency (same symptom when soil temperature is low). Bone meal, wood ash |
| Large, deep green leaves but soft tissue and a flat taste | Nitrogen excess (harder to notice than deficiency; the plant becomes weak to aphids, soft rot, and downy mildew). Stop the nitrogen supply |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The pungent compound contains sulfur, so using only organic resources can leave sulfur short; if the taste gets flatter year after year, make it up with compost mixed with animal manure, oil cake, or chicken manure.

## 7. Intercropping and Crop Rotation
| Compatibility | Companions |
|:---|:---|
| Good | Legumes (peas, kidney beans, cowpeas: fix nitrogen), lettuce and spinach (different growing periods and root depths mean little competition), Welsh onion and garlic chives (the smell confuses each other's pests) |
| Bad | Cabbage-family crops such as Chinese cabbage, radish, cabbage, and kale (they share pests and diseases and risk cross-pollination), strawberries (known to inhibit each other's growth) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Going around in a 4-year cycle of leaf mustard and Chinese cabbage and other cabbage-family crops (they use up a lot of nitrogen) → legumes (fix nitrogen) → potatoes and sweet potatoes → corn, sorghum, and other grasses (deep roots bring up nutrients from the lower layer), the cabbage family is started again with a lower density of pathogens. A field with clubroot needs more than 4 years.

## 8. Harvest and Seed Saving
| Harvest | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Timing | Baby leaves 15-20 days after sowing (leaf length 5-10 cm, combined with thinning), mid-harvest at 25-35 days (15-20 cm), main harvest at 45-60 days (20-30 cm, juicy and not yet tough). Left too long, leaves turn tough and only the pungency remains, so harvest slightly early rather than late |
| Technique | On a clear day in early morning, pull up with the roots or cut with a knife leaving 1-2 cm above the ground. Almost no regrowth harvest, so rely on staggered sowing. Do not wash; air-dry the surface water in shade |

| Seed saving | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Benefit of being annual | No need to pass the winter: sown in spring, it sets seed in about 100 days, in June-July of the same year (radish, Chinese cabbage, cabbage, and kale take a year and a half from sowing). Plants sown in autumn pass the winter and bolt in March-April of the next year |
| Crossing | It self-pollinates, but cross-pollination by bees and flies is considerable. It mixes 100% with other varieties of leaf mustard (if green and red flower side by side, the colors get mixed), crosses at a low frequency with Chinese cabbage, turnip, rapeseed, and black mustard, and does not cross with cabbage, kale, broccoli, or radish |
| Preventing crossing | Isolation distance 500 m or more. Netting is especially effective (it self-pollinates, so it sets seed even without bees). Without netting, use staggered cultivation: this year let only green leaf mustard flower and eat all the red, and do the reverse next year |
| Procedure | Choose plants with large, disease-free leaves and stake them after bolting. When two thirds of the pods are brown 50-55 days after flowering, cut the whole stem and after-ripen it in shade (fully dry pods split open on their own). After full drying, winnow and seal at a moisture of 10% or less |

> **Keep at least ten plants for seed. Even for a self-pollinating crop, if seed is taken only from one or two plants over and over, genetic diversity narrows and vigor weakens.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Seed life is 5 years sealed at room temperature and a single plant yields thousands of seeds, so it is enough to save seed once every 3-4 years with proper isolation.

## 9. Storage
| Method | Conditions and period |
|:---|:---|
| Fresh, short term | Do not wash, air-dry the water, and keep in a cool place for 3-5 days, or wrap in a damp cloth and keep in a pit or under a north-facing eave for 5-7 days |
| Leaving it in the field | It withstands down to -5℃, so leave it standing until early winter and cut only what is needed. Move to pickling before serious cold |
| Leaf mustard kimchi | Salt in 3-4% brine for 3-4 hours, rinse once or twice, and drain. Mix in seasoning, press into a jar, and let the brine cover it. Several months to over 1 year. Even without seasoning, lactic fermentation proceeds if the material is under the brine at 2-3% salt |
| Salt curing | Layer with 10-15% salt and press into a jar. Several months to over 1 year. Soak in water two or three times to remove the salt before use |
| Drying, dried greens | Blanch in boiling water for 30 seconds, rinse in cold water, squeeze tightly, and dry completely in shade. 6 months to over 1 year. Soak in water and boil, then use as a seasoned side dish or in soup |

> **The key to lactic fermentation is two things: the material must be completely under the brine, and the salt concentration must not fall below 2%.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The storability of leaf mustard is D, so fresh leaves hardly last a week at room temperature. Fermentation keeps a good part of the vitamin C, but salt curing and drying keep almost none, and dried products keep dietary fiber, minerals, and beta-carotene. With plenty of salt go with salt curing; if salt is scarce, go with kimchi-type fermentation.

## 10. Cooking and Precautions
| Method | Fuel and pungency |
|:---|:---|
| Raw (wraps, fresh-salted greens) | No fuel. Strongest pungency and maximum vitamin C retention. Baby leaves suit it |
| Blanching for 30 seconds to 1 minute | Very little fuel. The heat-sensitive enzyme is destroyed, so pungency drops greatly and goitrogens decrease |
| Adding to soup or porridge | Combined with other cooking. Eating the broth too preserves nutrients |
| Stir-frying | Beta-carotene absorption improves |
| Kimchi, fermentation | No fuel. Pungency turns into savoriness and a good amount of vitamin C is kept |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In disaster conditions time is usually more plentiful than fuel, so fermentation is structurally advantageous. To keep the pungency, crush or chop the leaves, leave them at room temperature for a while, and then cook.

| Component | Problem and response |
|:---|:---|
| Mustard oil glycosides (sinigrin) | Irritate the stomach if eaten in quantity. Heating destroys the enzyme. Start with a small amount, and if the stomach is weak, eat it cooked or pickled |
| Goitrogens | On an iodine-poor diet, eating large amounts of raw cabbage-family vegetables for a long time can lower thyroid function. Mix in cooked ones (discard the blanching water) and secure dried sea mustard and kelp |
| Oxalic acid, nitrate | Oxalic acid is low and decreases further if blanched and the water discarded. Nitrate accumulates in leaves given a lot of nitrogen, so do not overapply nitrogen and avoid fertilizing right before harvest |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Leaf mustard has no acute toxicity.

## 11. Annual Work Schedule
| Month | Work |
|:---|:---|
| Jan-Feb | Check stored kimchi and salt-cured goods and seed germination rates. Turn the compost and collect wood ash in February |
| Mar-Apr | Spring sowing and removing covers from overwintered plants in late March. Thinning and baby-leaf harvest, harvest of overwintered plants, and bolting of overwintered seed plants in April |
| May-June | Main harvest of spring leaf mustard and flowering and isolation of seed plants in May. Pod set of seed plants and summer sowing in the highlands in June |
| July-Aug | Seed harvest and after-ripening in July. Autumn sowing and basal fertilizer from late August |
| Sep-Oct | Staggered autumn sowing, thinning, and scouting for flea beetles and moths in September. Autumn harvest begins in October, and overwintering sowing in the south in late October |
| Nov-Dec | Main harvest and kimjang (leaf mustard kimchi and salt curing) and covering overwintering plants in November. Managing stored goods, tidying the field, and plowing in harvest residue in December |

> **A year of leaf mustard has two peaks. Spring leaf mustard is closer to farming for seed, and autumn leaf mustard is farming for food. In the first year of a disaster, eat half of the spring leaf mustard and keep half as seed; the half you keep decides the autumn of that year and the whole of the next.**
