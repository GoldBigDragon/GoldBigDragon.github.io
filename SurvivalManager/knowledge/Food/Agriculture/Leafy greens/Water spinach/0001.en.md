## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Water spinach (Ipomoea aquatica) is a perennial vine of the genus Ipomoea in the morning glory family. Its stem is hollow and sends air down to the roots, so it does not die even on flooded land. It originates from swamps and waterways from India through Southeast Asia to southern China, and in Korea it dies in frost and lives as an annual. Its overall grade is C (41.9 points, 88th of 183 valid species) and its supplementary-food grade is SS (59.5 points), and it is a crop that takes the place of a side dish on land where other crops die: flooded ground, low ground with blocked drainage, and paddies whose water cannot be drained. Its strengths are harvest speed SS (30 days after sowing) and perenniality A, since it regrows after cutting; its weaknesses are cold tolerance E (wiped out by one frost) and storability E (2-3 days at room temperature). The plant itself has no poison, but it takes in the heavy metals and parasites in its water as they are, so the most important decision is not the seed but which water to plant in.

| Evaluation | Items |
|:---|:---|
| Strengths | SS: protein density, micronutrients, harvest speed. S: fertilization, seed saving. A: perenniality, facility burden, cooking and processing requirement, continuous-cropping tolerance, yield stability, seed longevity. B: sunlight, taste, domestic cultivation spread |
| Weaknesses | E: water consumption, cold tolerance, storability, non-food value. C: calories per area, temperature range, pest and disease pressure, pest-control self-reliance, yield retention without synthetic inputs, land-preparation and digging energy |

> **Water consumption E for water spinach means that the crop uses a lot of water, but it also means that on land where water already stands, that water does not have to be brought in anew.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Water consumption E is 1,500 L or more per ㎡ per year for flooded cultivation, including leakage and evaporation, so on land where water must be hauled in, it is the worst choice. Supplementary SS is a value in which protein density and micronutrients are calculated large because calories are small, and with calories per area C (0.50 times the self-sufficiency line) it is impossible to fill the stomach with water spinach. Pest and disease pressure C (estimated susceptibility 50.3%) is an estimate drawn from the values of the same taxonomic order, and in reality pests and diseases are very few. The grade for each item is in the Crop Evaluation Results document.

## 2. Plant Overview
| Item | Content |
|:---|:---|
| Classification | Perennial vine of the genus Ipomoea in the morning glory family. Being in the same genus as sweet potato and morning glory, stem cuttings root at the nodes, some pests and diseases are shared, and it flowers when days shorten |
| Leaves, stem | Leaves are long heart-shaped to arrowhead-shaped, 5-15 cm. The stem is a hollow cylinder, and the hollow space and the air tissue running down to the roots send air to the roots and give buoyancy, so it spreads several meters over water |
| Roots, flowers, fruit | Shallow, fibrous roots that spread; adventitious roots form at every node touching water or wet soil. Flowers are white to pale pink and trumpet-shaped (3-5 cm across). The seed coat is hard |

| Stage | Period | Key points |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Germination, seedling stage | 7 days after sowing | The hard seed coat takes up water and splits, and the cotyledons open |
| First harvest stage | 25-35 days | Stems 30-40 cm |
| Regrowth stage | 7-14 days after harvest | Side buds on the remaining nodes wake and new shoots rise |
| Flowering stage | From late August | Flower stalks rise, stems harden, leaves shrink, and taste declines |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The growing point at the stem tip suppresses the side buds on the nodes below (apical dominance), so cutting the tip wakes the side buds all at once, and the added branches become the next harvest. Per 100 g: 26 kcal, water over 90%, protein 1.67-2.5 g, carbohydrate 3.2 g, dietary fiber 1.9-2.1 g, sugars 0.66 g, potassium 489 mg, vitamin A (beta-carotene) 411 μg, vitamin C 24.6 mg, iron 2.4 mg, and calcium 87 mg. Iron and vitamin C are together in one leaf, which compensates for the low absorption of plant iron, and vitamin C is largely lost on heating.

## 3. Growing Environment and Varieties
| Condition | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Temperature | Optimum germination 20-30℃ (it hardly sprouts when the soil is below 20℃), growth 15-35℃ (optimum 25-32℃, a width of 10℃). Above 35℃ it is vigorous if water is plentiful; growth stops at 10℃, leaves are damaged around 5℃ above zero, and one frost wipes it out. Midsummer, when other leafy vegetables collapse, is its prime |
| Light | 6 hours or more a day is suitable (at 4 hours the internodes lengthen and leaves thin). Yield is still 86% in 40% shade. Shade is cool and overlaps with its temperature weakness, so a spot with half a day of sun is better |
| Water, soil | Keep flooded depth at 15-20 cm; for fields, soil moisture 80-90% of maximum water capacity. Soil depth 15-20 cm, pH 6.0-7.0, salt tolerance weak to medium |

| Type | Characteristics |
|:---|:---|
| Aquatic type (green-stem Cheonggyeong line) | Thick, largely hollow stems with great buoyancy and broad heart-shaped leaves. Suited to flooded fields, paddies, and waterside. It sets seed poorly and is propagated by cuttings; Cheonggyeong large-leaf gives the highest yield in flooded cultivation |
| Terrestrial type (white-stem Baekgol line) | Thin, firm, upright stems and narrow, elongated leaves. Suited to fields, beds, and pots, and seeds ripen well. Baekgol (white stems), Jeokgyeong (red stems), and medium-leaf Cheonggol type (both field and water) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Bringing a terrestrial type onto flooded land gives yields below expectations, and bringing an aquatic type to a field ties you to hauling water. Domestic cultivation spread is B (grown in only three or four provinces), so seed is hard to find; when you find it, secure plenty and keep it going yourself through cuttings and seed saving.

## 4. Field Preparation, Sowing, and Cuttings
| Item | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Choosing a site | Land where water does not drain is the best site (low ground, paddy plots flooded in the rainy season, corners of fields with blocked drainage, hollows where rainwater collects). But always verify where the water comes from |
| Flooded bed | Level it like a paddy (if heights differ by 3 cm or more, the shallow side dries and the deep side rots). Build a bank 25-30 cm high at the edge and press the bottom firm to reduce leakage (on sandy ground, lay clay and firm it). Fill with water 2-3 days before planting |
| Field bed | Dig 15-20 cm deep and keep beds low or flat (high beds drain fast), using furrows as water channels. Add plenty of fully matured compost and cover thickly with straw, dry grass, or leaves to cut watering to half or less |
| Fertilization (1,000㎡) | Nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium 15-20 kg each, compost 10 tons. Apply all the compost, all the phosphorus and potassium, and half the nitrogen as basal fertilizer 2 weeks before planting, and top-dress a small amount of nitrogen right after each cutting |
| Substitute resources | Wood ash (about a handful per ㎡; too much raises pH), fully matured compost (poorly rotted compost fouls the water), urine diluted 10-20 times (stop 2 weeks before harvest), herb-steeped liquid fertilizer diluted 5-10 times, water from raising ducks or fish (risk of fecal contamination) |

| Cropping type | Sowing | Notes |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Open field | Mid May-June | After frost has completely ended (below 15℃ soil it hardly sprouts and rots). Harvest June-Sep |
| Open flooded field | Late May-June | Wait for the water to warm |
| Indoor seedlings | Late April | Put out once frost ends. Harvest from June |

| Start | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Sowing | Soak in lukewarm water for 12-24 hours. Depth 0.5-1 cm, row spacing 20 cm (25-30 cm for flooded), final plant spacing 10-15 cm. In flooded cultivation, drain the water, sow and cover with soil, and once sprouts reach about 5 cm raise the water little by little, always keeping the shoot tips out of the water. 25-35 days to first harvest |
| Cuttings | Cut healthy, thick stems to 15-20 cm (4-5 nodes), strip the leaves from the part to be buried, and insert at a slant with 3-4 nodes buried. Stems placed in a water jar show roots in 3-5 days. Establishment 5-7 days (do not let them dry), 20-25 days to first harvest |
| Thinning | At 2-3 true leaves to 5 cm, at 4-5 to 10-15 cm. Replant the thinned seedlings or eat them cooked |

## 5. Water Management and Cultivation Management
| Period | Flooded cultivation | Field cultivation |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Germination, seedling stage | Drain and keep the soil just moist, then raise the water gradually to a height that does not submerge the shoot tips | Keep the topsoil from drying, once or twice a day |
| Vegetative growth | Keep at 15-20 cm | Soak when the topsoil dries, 1-2 times a day |
| Around harvest | Keep as is | Water well right after cutting |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;With plenty of water the stems are tender; with too little, the fibers harden and turn tough. If the flower stalk rises early from water shortage, drought stress has triggered flowering, which can be used for seed saving. Conversely, young shoots that are fully submerged dissolve within a few days, water that stagnates and rots turns black and smells and the roots turn brown, and shallow water heats above 40℃ at midday and cooks the roots. Keep the water at 15 cm or more and top it up in the morning.

| Method | Characteristics |
|:---|:---|
| Flooded cultivation | Grown with 15-20 cm of impounded water. Highest yield, and the water blocks weed germination, soil pests, and fungi so the work of weeding, watering, and soil pests vanishes all at once; the labor is only once, when firming the ground |
| Field cultivation | Planted on beds and watered. Yield is 60-80% of flooded, weeding and daily watering are needed, and there are root rot and soil pests |
| Floating cultivation | On deep water, weave a raft, lay aquatic plants, straw, reeds, and leaf mold on it, insert cuttings, and anchor with stakes or stones. No fertilizer is needed, but it takes in the heavy metals and pathogens of polluted water directly |

| Management | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Pinching, training | When stems are 20-25 cm, cut the tips to increase side shoots, and spread the stems evenly to secure airflow (reducing fungal disease) |
| Removing flower buds | Removing buds on sight from late August extends the harvest period by 2-3 weeks |

## 6. Pests, Diseases, and Nutrient Problems
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Water spinach has very few pests and diseases. Flooding excludes soil pests and soil-borne fungi, the growing period is a short 30 days, and every cutting removes the pests on the leaves with it.

| Pest or disease | Symptoms and conditions | Control without chemicals |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Downy mildew, white rust | Grayish-white mold or white pustules on the underside of leaves and yellow spots on top. Cool and humid conditions, dense planting, rainy season | Improve airflow by tidying stems, remove affected leaves (remove the plant if severe), water at the roots |
| Root rot, damping-off | Roots turn brown and the plant wilts / young shoots constrict at the base and fall over. When water stagnates or rots, dense sowing | Improve drainage in fields, change the water in flooded fields, avoid dense sowing, avoid poorly rotted manure |
| Aphids, spider mites | Suck sap, leaves curl and sooty mold follows / leaves turn pale with webbing (when dry) | Wash off with a strong stream of water, soapy water, protect natural enemies / spray water often to raise humidity |
| Moth caterpillars, snails | Chew the leaves | Pick by hand at dusk, a band of wood ash |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In flooded fields, anything like soapy water that is sprayed builds up, harms the roots, and kills fish first, so prefer catching by hand and removing affected leaves.

| Symptom | Cause and remedy |
|:---|:---|
| Yellowing from the lower old leaves and a small plant | Nitrogen deficiency. Diluted urine, herb-steeped liquid fertilizer, fully matured compost |
| New leaves yellow with only the veins green | Iron deficiency (when pH is high it cannot use iron even if it is in the soil). Lower pH and reduce wood ash |
| Lush leaves, soft stems that snap, pests gathering | Nitrogen excess. Cut nitrogen and wash it out with water |
| Leaf edges dry and roots turn brown | Excess fertilizer concentration. Wash with plenty of water |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If damage starts from old leaves, nutrients that move easily within the plant (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, magnesium) are short; if from new leaves, nutrients that do not move easily (iron, calcium, boron) are short. Nitrate in leafy vegetables given much nitrogen turns into nitrite and is dangerous to infants, so harvest in the afternoon of a sunny day and discard the blanching water of heavily fertilized crops.

## 7. Intercropping and Crop Rotation
| Compatibility | Companions |
|:---|:---|
| Good | Legumes (cowpea, mung bean), rice (using the inner edge of the paddy bank and the channels, with rice supplying calories and water spinach the side dish), water dropwort and watercress (they use the same water) |
| Poor | Morning glory family such as sweet potato and morning glory (shared pathogens and pests), root vegetables (radish, carrot, burdock), garlic and onion (they rot in standing water and their water needs are opposite) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The basis of continuous-cropping tolerance A is a 2-year rest from the morning glory family. Rotate field plots as water spinach → legumes → cabbage family or gourd family → water spinach, and flooded plots as water spinach → rice or water spinach → legumes after draining → water spinach. Soil long under water has no oxygen, so organic matter rots less and iron and manganese change into forms harmful to roots; therefore drain and dry the ground for one year.

## 8. Harvest, Seed Saving, and Overwintering
| Harvest | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Timing | First harvest 25-35 days after sowing and 20-25 days after cuttings (stems 30-40 cm). After that every 7-14 days (side shoots 20-30 cm), four or more times in one summer until frost |
| Judging | Firmness matters more than length. If pressing the stem with a finger dents it easily, it is tender. Beyond 40 cm it turns tough from the base, and once flower buds appear the fibers harden quickly |
| How to cut | Leave 5-10 cm from the base (two or three nodes) and cut at a slant with a sharp knife or scissors (twisting it off tears the stem and lets disease in). Cutting in early morning gives the plumpest stems and slower wilting |
| After harvest | Move to shade at once, rinse several times in running water, and drain. Put tough bases and damaged leaves in compost, and set aside thick, sound stems as cuttings |

> **The key to harvesting water spinach is not how much you cut but how much you leave. The number of nodes left decides the size of the next harvest.**

| Carrying on | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Two routes | One frost kills it down to the roots, so keep both seed and living stems as insurance for each other |
| Seed saving | Choose a healthy plant with clear traits in early August and leave it uncut. Reducing water brings flowering forward (from flowering to seed set takes over a month, so September flowers may not ripen before first frost). When pods dry brown, pick, dry in shade, rub, and winnow. It is mainly self-pollinating, so varieties do not mix easily. Sealed in a cool, dry, dark place, 2-3 years |
| Overwintering by cuttings | Two weeks before frost, put several healthy stems of 20-30 cm in a jar of water by a sunny window. Keep it from falling below 10℃ (below 5℃ it dies), in the warmest spot, split among several small containers; change the water every 3-5 days, and cut and replant long stems to multiply them. In mid May after frost ends, put them outside for only a few daytime hours to acclimate, then move them out |

## 9. Storage
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Storability E is because water is over 90% and the tissue is tender. The surest answer is not to store: it does not spoil while standing in the field, so cut only what you will eat that day. Storage is needed only when handling what is left all at once just before frost.

| Method | Procedure and period |
|:---|:---|
| Leave in shade | Without washing, shake off water and leave in a shady place. 1-2 days |
| Stand bases in water | The stems keep drawing up water (change the water often so the bases do not rot). 2-3 days |
| Drying | Blanch 30 seconds in boiling water, rinse in cold water, squeeze, spread thin and dry thoroughly in shade and wind (10% of the volume), and seal. Vitamin C is almost gone, so it is for soup. 6 months-1 year |
| Pickling | Layer with 3-5% salt by weight and press (2-3 months), or make kimchi or soy-pickled vegetables (several months) |

## 10. Cooking and Precautions
| Method | Time and characteristics |
|:---|:---|
| High-heat stir-fry | 2-5 minutes. Uses the least fuel, keeps relatively more vitamin C, and oil helps absorb beta-carotene |
| Blanching, soup | Blanching 30 seconds-1 minute (water-soluble vitamins leach out, so use that water as broth), soup and stew 5 minutes (add last) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Cook the stems first for about 1 minute and add the leaves later; tough stems are better sliced thin on the diagonal or split in half.

| Hazard | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Heavy metals | Cadmium (factory wastewater, mine drainage, phosphate fertilizer, sewage sludge: kidney and bone damage, decades to excrete), lead (water pipes, paint, batteries, road dust: nervous system, children's brain development), arsenic, mercury. They cannot be removed by washing or cooking and cannot be told by appearance |
| Intestinal fluke, liver fluke, blood fluke | The first two attach as encysted larvae to the surface of aquatic plants and settle in the intestine or in the liver and bile ducts if eaten raw (diarrhea, abdominal pain / fever, pain in the upper right abdomen). Blood flukes penetrate the skin from larvae in the water, infecting during work in water with bare feet and hands |
| Fecally contaminated bacteria | Cook especially thoroughly anything from water mixed with feces (including water used to raise ducks or fish) |

> **The most important decision for water spinach is made neither when choosing seed nor when fertilizing, but when deciding which water to plant in. If you do not know where the water came from, it is better not to use that site.**

| Water | Judgment |
|:---|:---|
| Pooled rainwater, well water, mountain stream water, paddy irrigation water | Relatively safe (check upstream for paddy irrigation water) |
| Lower reaches of streams, urban drains, puddles beside roads | Avoid (lead from road dust and domestic wastewater) |
| Water below factories, mines, and landfills, long-stagnant water, water with an oil film | Never use |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Encysted larvae are weak to heat and die with a brief blanching in boiling water, so heat it thoroughly; rinsing alone is not enough. When working in water, cover bare skin with long boots and clothing. Oxalic acid is far lower than in spinach or chard, and because potassium is 489 mg per 100 g, a person with reduced kidney function does better to blanch and discard the water.

> **Water spinach is a vegetable that must be eaten cooked. Do not apply to this crop the habit of eating washed greens raw like lettuce. The cost of eating it cooked is part of the vitamin C; the cost of not cooking it is parasites.**

## 11. Annual Work Schedule
| Month | Work |
|:---|:---|
| Jan-Feb | Rest. Indoor cutting care (change water, keep 10℃ or above, cut and replant long stems) |
| Mar-Apr | Prepare compost, check seed condition. In April, for flooded plots tidy the site and repair the banks, for fields dig and add compost, and start seedlings indoors |
| May-June | After mid May, fill with water, sow or take cuttings, acclimate cuttings. In June keep water at 15-20 cm, mulch, pinch, and take the first harvest |
| July-Aug | In July repeat harvests, top-dress after harvest, secure airflow in the rainy season, watch for downy mildew. In early August choose the seed-saving plants and stop harvesting them, and from late August remove flower buds |
| Sep-Oct | In September watch the flowers and pods of the seed plants and reduce water to help flowering. In October finish harvesting before first frost, gather seed, bring in cuttings, and blanch and dry what is left |
| Nov-Dec | In November drain flooded plots and let the ground dry, compost the remaining stems in fields, and store seed. In December start indoor cutting care |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The water spinach year divides into five months in the field and seven months indoors. If you do not set aside seed-saving plants in early August there is no seed, and if you do not bring in cuttings in October there are no living stems.

> **The harvest of the five summer months rests on the judgment in early August to leave a few plants and on the hand in October that cuts and brings in a few stems. For this crop, the skill of carrying on is harder than the skill of harvesting.**
