## 5. Field and Water-Site Preparation, and Fertilizer

### 5-1. Choosing the Site

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Choosing a site for water spinach follows the opposite logic from other crops. Normally one looks for land that drains well, but water spinach looks for land that does not drain. Low ground near a stream or reservoir, a paddy plot that is flooded every monsoon season (jangma, late June to July in South Korea), a field corner that stays damp because its drainage ditch is blocked, and a hollow where rainwater collects are all candidates. Land that other crops have given up on is first-class land for this one.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There is one condition, however. It is necessary to know where that water came from. As Section 14 covers in detail, water spinach takes into its body whatever is in the water. Water with a factory or a waste landfill upstream, water that has run in off a road, and water that has leaked from a livestock barn or a human-waste pit are not suitable for a site. Spots where rainwater has pooled, irrigation water used in rice paddies, and well water are relatively safe.

### 5-2. Building a Flooded Plot

| Step | Details |
|---|---|
| Leveling the bed | Level it flat, like a rice paddy. If the difference in height reaches 3cm or more, the water depth varies, so the shallow side dries out and the deep side rots |
| Building the bank | Pile soil along the edge to a height of 25-30cm and firm it down. This is the height needed to hold 15-20cm of water, plus a margin |
| Firming the bed | Press down the bed inside the bank with the feet or a flat piece of wood. This slows the rate at which water leaks away |
| Adding organic matter | Spread well-rotted compost evenly and mix it in before firming |
| Filling with water | Let water in 2-3 days before planting and wait for the soil to settle |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The bed is firmed to reduce leakage. On sandy ground, water drains away by several cm a day, and where water is scarce this loss becomes a direct burden. On ground mixed with clay, firming alone seals it considerably, and on sandy ground a layer of mud is laid over the bed and firmed. Where there is no plastic sheeting, mud and firming are the only means.

### 5-3. Preparing a Field Plot

| Item | Method |
|---|---|
| Tillage | Till and level to a depth of 15-20cm. The roots are shallow, so there is no need to dig deeper |
| Raised bed | Make a low raised bed or simply leave the ground flat. A high bed drains quickly, which is a disadvantage |
| Furrow | Used as a channel for supplying water. Keeping water in the furrows keeps the soil of the raised bed moist |
| Organic matter | Add plenty of well-rotted compost. The aim is to increase water-holding capacity |
| Mulching | Cover thickly with straw, dry grass or fallen leaves. The key is to prevent evaporation |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In field cultivation, success or failure is decided by water-holding capacity. Water spinach has shallow roots and wilts at once when the topsoil dries. Two things cut the number of waterings to half or less: adding plenty of organic matter so that the soil holds water, and covering the soil thickly so that the sun cannot heat the ground directly. Where plastic mulching is not possible, straw and dry grass are not a substitute but actually the better choice. They rot into organic matter, and they do not raise soil temperature excessively the way plastic does.

### 5-4. Fertilizer Rates

| Fertilizer | Per 1,000m² | Per m² |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Nitrogen | 15-20kg | 4.5-6g |
| Phosphorus | 15-20kg | 4.5-6g |
| Potassium | 15-20kg | 4.5-6g |
| Compost | 10 tonnes | 3kg |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The S for Fertilizer demand in the evaluation table was assigned on the basis of a combined nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium of about 14g. That is low for a leafy vegetable, and compared with the 24g that kale requires it is at the 60% level. In flooded cultivation this requirement drops even lower, because the water carries nutrients and organic matter decomposes slowly in waterlogged soil, releasing nitrogen little by little.

| Category | Timing | What | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base fertilizer | 2 weeks before planting | All of the compost, all of the phosphorus and potassium, half of the nitrogen | Phosphorus hardly moves in water, so it must be mixed into the soil in advance |
| Top-dressing | After every harvest | A small amount, mainly nitrogen | Nitrogen is needed for new shoots to grow from the cut |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The timing of top-dressing for water spinach is unusual. Other crops are fed according to their growth stage, but water spinach is fed according to harvest. Each time it is cut, the plant loses a large part of its body and has to start again from the remaining base, so the time just after cutting is when nitrogen is needed most. If harvesting and top-dressing are tied together as a single action, it is not forgotten.

### 5-5. When There Is No Chemical Fertilizer

| Substitute resource | Nutrients supplied | How to use | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wood ash | Potassium, phosphorus, calcium | Sift it and scatter about a handful per m² | It is alkaline, so adding too much raises the pH. In flooded plots it dissolves and spreads in the water, so use less |
| Well-rotted compost | All-round | Add plenty as base fertilizer | If compost that is not fully rotted is put into standing water, the water goes foul and the roots are damaged |
| Diluted urine | Nitrogen, potassium | Dilute 10-20 times with water and pour around the roots | Undiluted urine burns the roots. Do not use it from 2 weeks before harvest |
| Liquid fertilizer from soaked grass | Nitrogen | Soak grass in water and ferment for 2 weeks, then dilute 5-10 times | The smell is strong, and if it is not fully decomposed it harms the roots |
| Bone meal | Phosphorus, calcium | Burn and crush it, then mix into the base fertilizer | The effect appears slowly |
| Fermented fish or shellfish | Nitrogen, phosphorus | Ferment, dilute and use | The smell attracts animals |
| Water from raising ducks or fish | Nitrogen, phosphorus | Let it flow as is into the flooded plot | There is a risk of fecal contamination, so the crop must always be eaten cooked |

> **In flooded cultivation, if fertilizer that is not fully rotted is put into the water, decomposing microbes use up all the oxygen in the water. Water spinach holds on because it sends air down through its stems, but when the water turns black and begins to smell, that is a sign the roots are being damaged.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The method in the last row, of raising ducks or fish together with the crop, is an arrangement that has long been used in Southeast Asia. What the fish excrete becomes fertilizer for the water spinach, and the water spinach absorbs nitrogen and phosphorus from the water and slows the water's going foul. In a disaster, this cycle makes it unnecessary to obtain fertilizer separately, but it also means that the part people eat comes into direct contact with feces. Water spinach grown this way must never be eaten raw, and the reason is given in Section 14.

## 6. Sowing and Stem Cuttings

### 6-1. Two Ways to Start

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There are two ways to start water spinach: sowing seed and cutting a stem and sticking it in the ground. Both work well, and which is more advantageous depends on the situation.

| Method | Time to first harvest | What is needed | Advantageous when |
|---|:---:|---|---|
| Sowing | 25-35 days | Seed | Starting for the first time, or filling a large area all at once |
| Cuttings | 20-25 days | Live stems | There is no seed, plants are already being grown, or a quick increase is wanted |

### 6-2. Sowing Time

| Cultivation type | Sowing time (South Korea) | Harvest time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open-field cultivation on dry land | Mid-May to June | June to September | After the risk of frost has completely ended |
| Open-field flooded cultivation | Late May to June | June to September | Wait for the water to warm up |
| Transplanting after raising seedlings indoors | Sow in late April | From June | Move outdoors once frost is over |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The mid-May benchmark for South Korea comes from soil temperature. Water spinach seed germinates poorly when the soil is below 20°C and hardly at all below 15°C, so if sown early it rots in the soil. In terms of air temperature, it is when the daytime high reaches 25°C and the nighttime low does not fall below 15°C.

### 6-3. Seed Treatment and Sowing

| Item | Standard | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Soaking | 12-24 hours in lukewarm water | The coat is hard and takes a long time to take up water. Soaking brings germination forward by several days and makes it even |
| Scarifying the coat | Rub on a rough stone or sand | Used for old seed or unusually hard seed. It makes a way for water to get in |
| Hot-water treatment | 10-15 minutes in 50-55°C water | Reduces pathogens on the coat. If the temperature is exceeded, the seed dies |
| Sowing depth | 0.5-1cm | About twice the seed diameter. If it is deeper, the sprout cannot break through the soil |
| Row spacing | 20cm | Widened to 25-30cm in flooded cultivation |
| Plant spacing | 10-15cm | The final spacing, reached by thinning |
| Sowing method | Drill sowing or hill sowing | For hill sowing, put two or three seeds in each spot and thin later |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In hot-water treatment the temperature is decisive. Above 55°C the seed embryo dies, and below 45°C the pathogens survive. If there is no thermometer, use as a guide the point at which the water feels hot when the hand is dipped in but does not scald. Water spinach's germination rate is naturally high, however, so skipping this treatment does not cause serious problems.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In flooded cultivation the sowing method is a little different. If seed is scattered while the water is held, the seeds float about and gather on one side. So the seed is first sown with the water drained or lowered to a very shallow level, covered lightly with soil, and after the sprouts have grown to about 5cm, the water is raised little by little. Young sprouts that are completely submerged suffocate and die, so the water level is always adjusted so that the tips of the sprouts stay above the water.

### 6-4. Stem Cuttings

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Cutting stems and sticking them in the ground is just as important as seed in propagating water spinach. It makes direct use of the ability to produce adventitious roots at every node, and the principle is the same as planting sweet potato vine tips.

| Item | Method | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing cuttings | Cut 15-20cm lengths from healthy, thick stems | A tip that is too young roots poorly, and a base that is too old has no buds |
| Number of nodes | Cut so that 4-5 nodes are included | Roots grow from the 3-4 buried nodes, and leaves grow from the nodes that stick out |
| Removing leaves | Strip the leaves from the end that will be buried | If leaves rot in the soil or water, the rot spreads to the stem |
| Planting depth | Stick in at a slant so that 3-4 nodes are buried | The more nodes are buried, the more roots form |
| Establishment period | 5-7 days | Do not let them dry out during this time |
| Rooting in water | Stand them in a bowl of water and roots appear in 3-5 days | Roots can be checked before planting, so there are fewer failures |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The significance of cuttings in a disaster is that they allow seed to be saved. One packet of seed sets up twenty plants, and the stems cut from those plants then make a hundred. If a few thick, sturdy stems are picked from those cut at harvest and stuck in the ground, they become the next field. What is special about this crop is that harvesting and propagation are done in the same motion.

> **A stem of water spinach cut for eating can become the next plant just as it is. Even if the seed runs out, a single living stem is enough to fill the field again.**

### 6-5. Thinning

| Round | Timing | Spacing left |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | 2-3 true leaves | 5cm between plants |
| 2nd | 4-5 true leaves | 10-15cm between plants |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Young plants removed in thinning are not thrown away. If the roots are attached, they survive transplanting to another spot, and the young leaves are tender and actually taste better. Even so, the rule of not eating it raw still holds.

## 7. Water Management - Where Standing Water and Dry Field Part Ways

### 7-1. Why Water Decides Success or Failure

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For water spinach, water is different in character from water for other crops. Ordinarily water is the medium that dissolves nutrients and carries them to the roots, but for water spinach, water in addition directly determines the tenderness of stems and leaves. When water is plentiful, the cells fill with water and grow larger, and the cell walls stretch while staying thin, so the tissue is tender. When water is short, the cells stay small and hard and the fibers thicken, so the tissue turns tough. The qualifier "off-putting texture" attached to the Taste criterion in the evaluation table comes close to describing water spinach grown under water shortage.

| Stage | Flooded cultivation | Field cultivation |
|---|---|---|
| Sowing to germination | Keep the water shallow or drained, with the soil just moist | Morning and evening, so the topsoil does not dry out |
| Seedling stage | Raise it gradually to a height at which the sprout tips are not submerged | Once a day, twice on hot days |
| Vegetative growth stage | Maintain 15-20cm | Water thoroughly when the topsoil dries, 1-2 times a day |
| Around harvest | Keep as it is | Water generously right after cutting |

### 7-2. What Appears When Water Is Short

| Symptom | What is happening |
|---|---|
| Leaves droop at midday and recover in the evening | A temporary shortage. The roots cannot supply water as fast as transpiration takes it away, but this is not yet damage |
| Leaves still not open in the morning | A severe shortage. The cells have lost water and their function is declining |
| Stems become thin and hard | A chronic shortage. The fibers harden and the stems become difficult to eat |
| Leaves shrink and nodes shorten | Growth has stopped. Yield falls sharply |
| Flower stalks rise early | Drought stress has pulled the trigger that sends the plant toward reproduction |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The last row can be used for seed saving. If the plants meant for seed are deliberately given less water to stress them, flowering is brought forward. Even in a year when the day-shortening conditions are not met, flowers can be obtained this way. However, harvesting leaves from the same plants has to be given up.

### 7-3. When There Is Too Much Water

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Having too much water is rare for water spinach, but it does happen. There are three cases.

| Case | What is the problem | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Young sprouts completely submerged | The leaves cannot get above the water, so photosynthesis and air supply are cut off | They dissolve away within a few days |
| Water stagnates and goes foul | Organic matter that is not fully rotted, or feces, uses up all the oxygen | The water turns black and smells, and the roots turn brown |
| Water too hot | Shallow water warms to more than 40°C at midday | The roots are cooked and the leaves look as if they had been blanched |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The third case actually becomes a problem in South Korean summers. In a shallow spot where the water is only 5cm deep, the water heats up to a high temperature at midday. The standard of keeping the water 15cm or deeper has, besides the purpose of submerging the roots, the purpose of preventing the water temperature from rising and falling sharply. This is because the more water there is, the more slowly its temperature changes.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Where it is hard to bring in fresh water, it is better to top up the water in the morning. Pouring in cold water at midday mixes it with the heated water and changes the temperature abruptly, shocking the roots, while pouring it in the evening lowers the water temperature through the night, which is a burden for a tropical crop.

## 8. Crop Management - Built to Regrow After Cutting

### 8-1. Four Ways of Growing

| Method | What it is | Water demand | Yield | Suitable site |
|---|---|:---:|:---:|---|
| Flooded cultivation | Grown with 15-20cm of water held on the plot | Very high | Highest | Rice paddies, flooded ground, pools |
| Field cultivation | Planted on raised beds and watered like an ordinary vegetable | High | Medium | Ordinary vegetable plot |
| Floating cultivation | A raft is floated on the water and the crop is grown on it | Water already present | High | Reservoirs, deep pools, water tanks |
| Container cultivation | Planted and grown in tubs or boxes | Medium | Low | Rooftop, balcony, spots without soil |

### 8-2. How Flooded and Field Cultivation Differ

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;These two are not merely two ways of growing the same crop; they are, in effect, close to different kinds of farming. The differences are summarized below.

| Item | Flooded cultivation | Field cultivation |
|---|---|---|
| State of the roots | Submerged, breathing through the stem's aerating tissue | Breathing the air in the soil |
| Shape of the roots | Dense white fine roots in the water | Short, sparse roots in the soil |
| Posture of the stems | Creep and extend sideways over the water | Grow standing at a slant |
| Weeds | Almost none appear. The water blocks weed seed germination | They appear steadily. Weeding is needed |
| Soil pests and diseases | Almost none. The water drives out soil-dwelling pests and fungi | Root rot and soil pests occur |
| Fertilizer | Less is needed, since the water carries nutrients and flooded soil releases nitrogen | Must be applied as prescribed |
| Effort of water management | Building the plot takes effort, and after that almost none | Water must be applied every day |
| Yield | Highest | About 60-80% of flooded cultivation |

> **The advantage of flooded cultivation lies not in the harvest size but in the fact that three kinds of labor, weeding, watering and dealing with soil pests and diseases, disappear all at once. The labor goes in only once, when the ground is firmed.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The absence of weeds matters more than it seems. In a disaster, most field work is weeding, and without machines or herbicides it all has to be done by hand. If 15cm of water is held, most weed seeds cannot sprout, and those that do sprout are submerged and die. This is one of the reasons rice farming has been sustained over so many years, and water spinach enjoys the same benefit as a leafy vegetable.

### 8-3. Floating Cultivation

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In water too deep for the crop to stand rooted in the bottom, there is a method of floating a raft. The floating vegetable gardens of Southeast Asia work on the same principle.

| Element | How to make it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Floating frame | Bind bamboo, thick branches, hollow containers or pieces of polystyrene foam into a raft | The framework that holds the growing layer up on the water |
| Growing layer | Pile aquatic plants, straw, reeds and leaf mold thickly on top | It only has to give the roots something to hold on to; no soil is needed |
| Planting | Stick cuttings into the growing layer | The roots pierce the layer and go down into the water |
| Anchoring | Tie the raft down with stakes or stones | Keeps it from being carried off by wind and current |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This method is valuable where there is no soil at all or where the bottom cannot be touched. The growing layer decays over time into fertilizer, and because the roots absorb nutrients in the water directly, the crop grows for quite a long time without separate fertilizer. If there are fish in the water, the cycle works even better. However, where the water is polluted, the roots are in direct contact with the water and take in heavy metals and pathogens as they are, and this risk is greater than in soil cultivation.

### 8-4. Pinching and Training

| Task | Timing | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| First pinching | When stems are 20-25cm | Cut off the tip | Releases apical dominance and increases side branches. Harvests grow larger afterward |
| Training | As stems lengthen | Set the direction and spread them out evenly | Prevents them from bunching on one side and shading one another |
| Removing flower buds | From late August in South Korea | Pick them off as soon as they are seen | Keeps resources from going to seed and lengthens the leaf-harvest period |
| Tidying overgrown stems | As needed | Clear out stems that have grown too far and tangled | Secures ventilation and reduces fungal diseases |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It is worth knowing that removing flower buds lengthens the harvest period. Flowering and setting seed cost a plant considerable resources, and those resources are drawn out of the leaves and stems. If the buds are picked off continuously, the plant judges that reproduction has failed and turns back to producing leaves. However, the day-length signal itself cannot be changed, so the time that can be gained by this method is about 2 to 3 weeks.
