## 2. What Kind of Plant Is This?

### 2-1. Classification and Origin

| Item | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Scientific name | Lactuca sativa |
| Taxonomy | Magnoliophyta Magnoliopsida / Asterales / Asteraceae / Lactuca |
| Life cycle | Annual |
| Origin | The region stretching from the Mediterranean coast to western Asia |
| Wild ancestor | Prickly lettuce (Lactuca serriola) |
| Same-family relatives | Dandelion, mugwort, sunflower, burdock, chicory |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In practice, the fact that lettuce belongs to the daisy family means three things. First, it does not share pests and diseases with cabbage-family crops. Neither the cabbage white butterfly, nor flea beetles, nor clubroot touches lettuce. In a field crowded with cabbage-family crops, lettuce acts as a safe zone. Second, it has diseases of its own that are peculiar to the daisy family: sclerotinia rot and downy mildew, which also pass to chicory, garland chrysanthemum, and burdock. Third, cutting the stem releases a white latex. It is the same white sap that comes out when you snap a dandelion, and it is a trait common to daisy-family plants.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The Lactuca in the scientific name comes from lac, the Latin word for milk, and refers to this white latex. The main components of the latex are the bitter substances lactucin and lactucopicrin, which together are called lactucarium. Its original purpose is to drive off herbivores. This is why lettuce suffers less insect damage than other leafy vegetables, and also why it becomes inedible once the flower stalk rises. Section 14 covers these compounds in detail.

### 2-2. Appearance

| Organ | Characteristics |
|:---|:---|
| Leaf | Varies by variety: round, crinkled, or elongated leaves. Green, red, or mixed colors |
| Stem | Extremely short normally, so the leaves look as if they grow in a cluster. Grows upright once the plant bolts |
| Root | A straight taproot goes down, while fine roots spread shallowly |
| Flower | Yellow. A flower head (capitulum) made up of many small flowers, typical of the daisy family |
| Fruit | An achene. Brown or black, with a pappus at the tip |
| Latex | White sap comes out when the stem or leaf veins are cut |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The fact that a lettuce stem is hardly visible in normal times is the foundation of cut-and-come-again harvesting. The stem is compressed to a very short length with the growing point at its top, so the leaves arise in a radial whorl and overlap. The further out a leaf is, the older it is, having emerged first; the further in, the newer. So when you pick from the outside, the plant loses only old leaves, and the growing point remains and keeps pushing up new leaves. Conversely, if you cut out the center, that plant is finished.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The pappus at the tip of a lettuce seed has the same structure as that of a dandelion seed. It is a device for spreading on the wind, and during seed saving it becomes a nuisance. Ripe seed is easily blown away by the wind, so if you miss the timing, nothing is left to harvest. At the same time, thanks to the pappus, it is comparatively easy to separate out the empty seeds by winnowing.

### 2-3. Growth Stages

| Stage | Timing (from sowing) | Key physiology | Nutrients used most |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Germination and seedling stage | Sowing to day 7 | The seed checks light and temperature before it sprouts | Phosphorus, nitrogen |
| Vegetative growth stage | Days 7-30 | Leaves multiply quickly and build up the plant | Nitrogen |
| Best harvest window | Days 30-60 | Leaves reach full size, starting with the outer ones | Balance of nitrogen and potassium |
| Cut-and-come-again harvest stage | Days 40-110 | Picking the outer leaves makes new leaves rise from the inside | Nitrogen |
| Bolting and flowering stage | Days 60-100 | The stalk shoots up and the leaves turn bitter | Phosphorus, potassium |
| Seed-setting stage | 2-3 weeks after flowering | Seeds with a pappus ripen | Phosphorus, potassium |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The decisive turning point in this table is bolting. Until then, lettuce spends all its resources on making leaves, but once bolting begins it changes direction toward raising a stalk and producing flowers. At this point the nutrients left in the leaves move to the stalk and flowers, and at the same time the concentration of lactucarium rises sharply. The leaves toughen, the bitterness intensifies, and they become inedible.

> **Bolting is not a disease but a normal stage in lettuce's life. The lettuce people eat is only the early part of that life, and half of growing technique lies in how far this turning point can be delayed.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Three conditions trigger bolting. The first is high temperature. When days above 25°C continue, lettuce switches to reproductive growth. The second is long days. As the days lengthen, the same signal gets stronger. The third is stress. When water is short, the roots are damaged, or crowding makes competition severe, the plant hurries to leave seed. All three conditions coincide in midsummer, which is why summer lettuce does not work.

### 2-4. Nutrition per 100g

| Component | Content | Notes |
|:---|:---:|:---|
| Energy | 20kcal | 13-20kcal depending on variety |
| Water | About 95% | The root cause of poor storability |
| Protein | 1.88g | Protein share of calories 37.6% |
| Sugars | 1.22g | |
| Vitamin A (beta-carotene equivalent) | 354-717 μg | Differs greatly by variety |
| Folate | 49 μg | |
| Vitamin K | Present | Involved in blood clotting and bone metabolism |
| Potassium | Present | |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It is practically important that vitamin A content differs by a factor of two between varieties. Beta-carotene is higher the darker the leaf color. Dark green leaves have more than pale green leaves, and outer leaves have more than the pale inner leaves. In varieties that fill with a white heart, like head lettuce, that white part contains almost no beta-carotene. If you grow lettuce for nutrition in a disaster, it is better to choose a type with dark, open leaves.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The red color of red lettuce is anthocyanin. It acts as an antioxidant and deepens in cold and strong light. This is why red lettuce grown in autumn in the same field is darker in color than that grown in spring. Mixing green and red lettuce widens the range of compounds obtained from the same area.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The sedative effect of lettuce has long been known. Lactucarium has been reported to have a mild sedative effect, and the rule of thumb that eating lettuce makes you sleepy came from this. However, the concentration of lactucarium in the young lettuce people usually eat is very low, not at a level where any real pharmacological effect can be expected. The concentrated form is the latex that comes from the stem of bolted lettuce.

## 3. Growing Conditions

### 3-1. Temperature

| Category | Temperature | Explanation |
|:---|:---:|:---|
| Optimal for germination | 15-20°C | Seedlings emerge within 3-5 days |
| Germination limit | 25°C or above | Germination rate drops sharply |
| Optimal for growth | 15-20°C | Leaves are tender and yield is high |
| Growth range | 15-22°C | Narrow, with a width of 10°C |
| Bolting induction | When 25°C or more persists | The flower stalk rises and bitterness sets in |
| Cold limit | Tolerates light frost | In deep sub-zero cold the leaves freeze and turn mushy |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most unusual thing about lettuce's temperature requirements is on the germination side. For most crops, germination speeds up as temperature rises, but lettuce does the opposite: above a certain temperature it stops germinating. This phenomenon is called high-temperature dormancy.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is not a malfunction but a design. It makes sense when you think from the standpoint of wild lettuce that grew in a Mediterranean climate. In that region summers are hot and dry, and rain falls from autumn to spring. A seed that sprouted in summer would clearly wither and die. So lettuce seed does not sprout when the soil is warm, and waits. When temperatures fall and rain begins, it germinates then. This decision mechanism is still intact, so if you sow lettuce in midsummer, the seeds fall asleep in the ground.

| Countermeasure | Method | Principle |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Seed priming | Soak the seeds in water for a few hours, leave them in a cool place for a day, then sow | The seeds experience cold and dormancy is broken |
| Evening sowing | Sow after sunset and water thoroughly | Takes advantage of the drop in soil temperature overnight |
| Shading | Make shade over the sowing spot with a bamboo screen or branches | Lowers soil temperature by a few degrees |
| Pre-sprouting on wet cloth | Sprout the seeds in a cool place, then move and sow them | Finishes the germination stage outside the field |
| Changing the timing | Give up on summer sowing and wait for autumn | The most reliable |

> **Summer lettuce sowings fail not because the soil is poor or water is short, but because the seed went to sleep from the heat. Once you know the cause, the response changes.**

### 3-2. Light

| Item | Standard | Notes |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Sunlight requirement | 6-8 hours a day | |
| Tolerance of partial shade | Good | Relative yield of leafy vegetables at 40% shade is 86% |
| Seed germination | Light-germinating | Seeds must receive light to germinate |
| Summer | Shading is actually beneficial | Lowers soil temperature and delays bolting |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The fact that lettuce seed needs light to germinate is where mistakes most often happen in sowing. Because germination starts only when the seed receives light, covering it thickly with soil leaves it asleep. A sowing depth of 2-3mm, just enough to hide the seed, is right. Compared with burying radish 1-2cm deep, it is a different order of magnitude.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Light-requiring germination and high-temperature dormancy are two sides of the same decision mechanism. The seed sprouts when it lies shallow, where light reaches it, and the soil is cool. When it is buried deep or the soil is hot, it waits. It is, in effect, a double lock that opens only when both conditions are right.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lettuce's good tolerance of partial shade can be used in laying out a field in a disaster. Put lettuce in the shade of a building, under trees, or in spots that get no sun all day, and give the sunny spots to radish or potatoes. In the period close to summer, shade actually extends lettuce's life. This is where the layout of planting lettuce on the north side of tall crops such as maize or sunflowers comes from.

### 3-3. Water and Soil

| Item | Standard | Notes |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Total evapotranspiration | About 350mm | On the low side among leafy vegetables |
| Irrigation interval | Every 3-5 days in spring and autumn, every 2-3 days in summer | Adjust to soil condition |
| Soil acidity | pH 6.0-6.6 | Growth worsens at pH 5 or below, or 8 or above |
| Soil | Rich in organic matter and well drained | Fairly wide soil adaptability |
| Bed height | 15-20cm | Ensures drainage |
| Rooting depth | Spreads shallowly | Affected immediately when the topsoil dries |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lettuce is not very particular about soil. It generally grows in any soil that is airy and holds moisture. In a disaster, this trait is highly valuable. Lettuce grows in places that are not fields: yards, flower beds, even cracked pots. However, because its roots spread shallowly, it is sensitive to the condition of the topsoil. When the soil dries out, the leaves toughen and the bitterness intensifies, and if that state continues, bolting comes sooner.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The principle of water management is evenness. A lettuce leaf is tissue that is 95% water, so the water supply shows up immediately in the condition of the leaf. When a dry spell drags on, the leaves are small, thick, and bitter. Conversely, when water pools, the roots cannot get oxygen and soft rot and sclerotinia rot begin at the base. Building raised beds and mulching with straw or dry grass is the way to deal with both problems at once.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mulching is especially effective on lettuce. One reason is that its roots are shallow and sensitive to fluctuations in soil temperature; another is that it stops raindrops from splashing soil onto the undersides of the leaves. The spores of sclerotinia rot and gray mold sit in the soil and are then splashed up by rainwater and stick to the leaves. A layer of straw between the ground and the leaves cuts off this route.

## 4. Varieties

### 4-1. Characteristics by Type

| Type | Characteristics | Role in a disaster |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Leaf lettuce (chima, "skirt" lettuce) | Does not form a head; the leaves spread open | Allows cut-and-come-again harvesting, so it is the most advantageous |
| Head lettuce | Forms a firm, tight heart. Needs spacing of 30cm or more | One-time harvest. Covered in a separate document |
| Romaine lettuce | Upright, with elongated leaves | Semi-heading. Cut-and-come-again harvesting works to some extent |
| Butterhead lettuce | Forms a soft, semi-heading head | Leaves are tender, so storability is even worse |

| Variety | Characteristics | Suitable environment |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Cheongchima (green chima) | Green leaves. The most widely used type | Spring, autumn |
| Jeokchima (red chima) | Red leaves. High in anthocyanin | Spring, autumn |
| Cheongchungmyeon / Jeokchungmyeon (green / red crinkled type) | Broad leaves with many wrinkles | Spring, autumn |
| Jangsu | Grows even in low-light conditions | Partial shade, indoors |
| Tomallin | Thick, fibrous leaves that do not wilt easily | When storage and transport are needed |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The type at the center in a disaster is leaf lettuce. The reason is cut-and-come-again harvesting. Head lettuce is a one-time crop whose firm head is harvested whole, so once it is pulled, that is the end, but leaf lettuce, picked only by its outer leaves, carries on for two to three months. The total harvest from the same area is larger, and because the harvest is spread out, there is no storage burden.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Varieties with thick, fibrous leaves, like the Tomallin type, are less pleasant to chew but store better. They are an option that raises the Storability E grade, if only slightly. They are worth considering when the field is far from home or when you cannot go out to the field every day.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Varieties that grow even in shade, like the Jangsu type, prove their worth when grown somewhere other than a field. Even in spots short of sunlight, such as between buildings, along the foot of a wall, or on an indoor windowsill, they give a fair yield. If a disaster strikes in a city, this trait can be decisive.

### 4-2. Selection Criteria by Situation

| Situation | Choice | Reason |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Early in a disaster, fastest harvest | Cheongchima, Jeokchima | Young leaves can be harvested in 25-30 days |
| Picking over a long period | Leaf lettuce types in general | 2-3 months through cut-and-come-again harvesting |
| Spots short of sunlight | Shade-tolerant types such as Jangsu | Grows even in partial shade |
| When food must be kept for a few days | Thick-leaved types such as Tomallin | Wilts less easily |
| When widening the range of nutrients | Jeokchima | Adds anthocyanin |
| Seed self-reliance first | Fixed-trait varieties, landraces | Self-pollination keeps traits true when seed is saved |
| Spreading risk | Mixed planting of two or three varieties | Bolting time and disease resistance differ, which reduces the risk of total loss |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Planting several varieties mixed together is useful against downy mildew. The empirical papers on organic lettuce mentioned in the evaluation basis also tested mixed planting of varieties. Because resistance to disease differs between varieties, if you plant only one, the whole field collapses when a pathogen strain to which that variety is vulnerable arrives. If you plant a mix, even if one is hit, the rest remain.

> **Because lettuce is a self-pollinating crop, seeds do not get mixed even when several varieties are planted side by side. Unlike cabbage-family crops, which force a choice between mixed planting and seed saving, lettuce can do both at once.**
