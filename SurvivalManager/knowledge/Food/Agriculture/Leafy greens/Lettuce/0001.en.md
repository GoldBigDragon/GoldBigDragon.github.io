## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lettuce (Lactuca sativa) is a leafy vegetable of the aster family; unlike kale or leaf mustard of the cabbage family, it belongs to the same family as dandelion, mugwort, and sunflower. Its ancestor is prickly lettuce, which grew wild around the Mediterranean and western Asia. Its overall grade is D (37.7 points, 135th of 183 valid species), and its supplementary-food grade is SS (62.1 points). Calories per area is D (even a harvest of 3,000 kg per 1,000㎡ reaches only 0.23 times the self-sufficiency line), storability is E (2-3 days at room temperature without power), the lowest of the crops evaluated, and temperature range is also C (15-22℃). But lettuce is not a food to pile up in a storehouse; it is a food that goes from the field straight to the mouth. It is eaten as is after washing, so it needs no fuel (cooking and processing SS), it keeps growing for two to three months if you pick only the outer leaves (perenniality A), and because it self-pollinates, seed saved from it keeps its traits (seed saving S). Its weaknesses are heat dormancy, in which seeds do not germinate above 25℃, flower stalks and bitterness that come with heat, a seed life of only 2-3 years, and a 2-3-year rest from sclerotinia rot of the aster family. Lettuce is not the pillar of the field but its daily fare.

## 2. Plant Overview
| Item | Content |
|:---|:---|
| Classification | Annual of the aster family (Asteraceae), genus Lactuca. Cabbage-family pests and clubroot do not attack it, but sclerotinia rot and downy mildew, specific to the aster family, also pass to chicory, garland chrysanthemum, and burdock |
| Leaves, stem | Round, ruffled, or elongated leaves in green, red, and mixed colors. The stem is normally extremely short, so the leaves look bunched together, and it grows upright when it bolts. Cutting it releases a white latex (lactucarium) |
| Roots, flowers, fruit | The taproot goes down but the fine roots spread shallowly. Yellow flower heads. An achene with a pappus (tuft of fluff) at the tip |

| Stage | Period (after sowing) | Key points |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Germination, seedling stage | Up to 7 days | Seeds check light and temperature before germinating |
| Vegetative growth | 7-30 days | Leaves increase quickly and form a plant. Nitrogen |
| Harvest, regrowth harvest | 30-110 days | Outer leaves mature first, and when picked, new leaves rise from the inside |
| Bolting and flowering | 60-100 days | The stem shoots up and the leaves turn bitter. Phosphorus, potassium |

> **Bolting is not a disease but a normal stage in the life of a lettuce. The lettuce people eat is only the early part of that life, and half of cultivation technique lies in how long you can delay this turning point.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There are three conditions for bolting: heat, with days above 25℃ continuing; long days, as daylight lengthens; and stress, such as water shortage, root damage, or crowding. Midsummer is where all three overlap, so lettuce does not work in summer. Once bolting begins, the concentration of lactucarium rises sharply and the leaves turn tough and bitter.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Per 100 g: 20 kcal, water about 95% (the root cause of poor storability), protein 1.88 g (37.6% of calories), folate 49 ㎍, and vitamin A (as beta-carotene) 354-717 ㎍. Beta-carotene is higher the darker the leaf and the further out it grows, so for nutrition a strain with dark, open leaves is advantageous.

## 3. Growing Environment and Varieties
| Condition | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Temperature | Optimum germination 15-20℃ (3-5 days), and the germination rate plummets above 25℃. Optimum growth 15-20℃, possible range 15-22℃; if 25℃ or more continues, bolting and bitterness follow |
| Light | 6-8 hours a day. Relative yield 86% under 40% shade. Light-requiring germination. In summer shade is actually advantageous (it lowers soil temperature and delays bolting) |
| Water, soil | Total evapotranspiration about 350 mm. Well-drained soil rich in organic matter, pH 6.0-6.6, bed height 15-20 cm. Roots are shallow, so the topsoil drying out affects it right away |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lettuce seed stops germinating above a certain temperature. It is a design that keeps seedlings that sprout in summer from dying of drought in the hot, dry Mediterranean, and together with its light requirement it is a double lock: the seed sprouts only in a shallow spot that light reaches when the soil is cool. Because it adapts well to partial shade, put it in the shade of buildings, under trees, or on the north side of tall crops such as corn and sunflowers, and give the sunny spots to other crops.

| Variety | Characteristics |
|:---|:---|
| Leaf lettuce (chima type) | Leaves open out and allow regrowth harvest, so it is the core in disaster conditions. Green chima (most widely grown), red chima (anthocyanin), and green and red chukmyeon (crinkled) |
| Head lettuce, romaine, butterhead | Head lettuce is a single-harvest crop that ends once pulled; romaine is semi-heading and allows some regrowth harvest; butterhead has tender leaves and even worse storability |
| Jangsu, Tomalin | Jangsu also grows in partial shade and indoors; Tomalin has thick, fibrous leaves that do not wilt easily |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For the fastest harvest in the early disaster period choose green or red chima (baby leaves in 25-30 days), and for seed self-sufficiency choose fixed varieties and landraces. Lettuce self-pollinates, so seed does not mix even when several varieties are planted side by side; sowing a mix of two or three varieties makes bolting time and disease resistance differ, which reduces the risk of total loss and is also useful against downy mildew.

## 4. Field Preparation and Sowing
| Item | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Preparing the soil | Dig 20-30 cm deep, break clods finely, and level (the seed is barely 3 mm, so if it falls between clods and is buried deep, it does not sprout). Roots are shallow, so crushing only the top 10 cm is enough for it to grow; build beds if drainage is poor |
| Fertilization (1,000㎡) | Nitrogen 10-12 kg, phosphorus 6-8 kg, potassium 6-8 kg, compost 1,000-1,500 kg, and lime 100-150 kg, all applied as basal fertilizer 1-2 weeks before sowing. Skip top dressing in principle (high nitrogen makes leaves soft, bitter, and weak to disease), and give it thinly right after picking leaves only when regrowth harvest continues past two months |
| Substitute resources | Fully matured compost (1-1.5 kg per 1㎡), wood ash (a handful per 1㎡, so that pH does not exceed 6.6), urine diluted 15-20 times (weaker than for other leafy vegetables), eggshell powder |

| Cropping type | Sowing | Harvest | Notes |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Spring crop | Late March-mid May | Late April-late June | The most common. 4-6 weeks of harvest, ending in bolting. Seed can be saved only in spring |
| Autumn crop | Early Aug-early Sep | Early Sep-early Nov | The most advantageous in disaster conditions. 8-10 weeks, ending with frost |
| Summer, winter | Not recommended | | Summer: germination failure and premature bolting; winter: only under protected conditions in the south |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The trap of the autumn crop is that in the heat of early August, once soil temperature exceeds 25℃ the seeds go dormant, so the real skill of autumn sowing is getting seeds to germinate while avoiding the heat.

| Heat dormancy response | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Cold priming | Soak seeds in water for 4-6 hours, wrap in a damp cloth, and leave for a day in a cool place (well water, cellar, north-facing shade). The most reliable. Sow right after treatment and keep the soil from drying (seeds that have taken up water die if they dry out) |
| Other methods | Pre-germinate in a damp cloth and transplant to sow, sow after sunset, make light shade with screens, branches, or straw (remove when seedlings sprout), and sprinkle cold water in the daytime to lower soil temperature |

| Sowing | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Method | Cover with fine soil to a depth of 2-3 mm, just enough to hide the seeds, or only press them in. Row spacing 20 cm. Sow thickly and then thin |
| Staggered sowing | It cannot be stored, so sow in three to four batches at intervals of 10 to 15 days. Even if one crop fails to disease, the next batch remains |
| Seed treatment | Soak seeds in 50-55℃ water for 10-15 minutes and cool in cold water. Freshly harvested seed has stronger heat dormancy, so keep two years' worth overlapping and use the older seed first |

## 5. Water Management and Cultivation Management
| Period | Water management |
|:---|:---|
| Sowing to seedling stage | Lightly and often so the topsoil does not dry |
| Growth stage | Every 3-5 days in spring and autumn, every 2-3 days in summer. Water also after picking leaves (if it dries, recovery slows and bolting may be hastened) |
| Just before harvest, rainy season | Cut back somewhat just before harvest, and secure drainage ditches in the rainy season |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When water is short, lettuce thickens its leaves and raises its lactucarium concentration, so it turns bitter. If white mold and black grains appear at the base, it is sclerotinia rot and that plant is discarded. Water in early morning so the leaves dry during the day (mold diseases occur in proportion to how long the leaves stay wet). Straw mulch cuts the route by which rain splashes soil and sticks spores to the underside of leaves, but it becomes a hiding place for slugs, so use it together with ventilation and drainage.

| Management | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Thinning | First to 5 cm at 2-3 true leaves, second to 15-20 cm (final) at 4-5. If late, plants grow only upward and airflow is blocked, causing mold diseases. Do not pull; cut near the ground with scissors or a fingernail. The thinned baby lettuce is the first harvest (around day 25 it has almost no bitterness and goes into wraps and salads) |
| Weeds | No hilling needed. Mulch with straw or dry grass after 3-4 true leaves, and when weeds are small, scrape only the surface with a hoe held shallow |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Once bolting begins and a stem shoots up from the center, the harvest of that plant is over, and even if the flower stalk is cut, another rises beside it. Young flower stalks and the leaves above them become greens if blanched and soaked, and a few plants are left to save seed. To delay bolting, lower soil temperature with shade, mulch, and watering and avoid drought stress, root damage, and crowding; if you must get through summer, put it on the north side of tall crops or in the shade of buildings.

## 6. Pests, Diseases, and Nutrient Problems
| Pest or disease | Symptoms and conditions | Control without chemicals |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Sclerotinia rot | The base turns soft, with white hyphae and black grains (sclerotia). Cool, humid conditions, continuous Asteraceae cropping | Deep plowing, mulch, 2-3-year rotation, removing diseased plants |
| Downy mildew | Yellow-brown spots on the upper side of leaves and white mold on the underside. Cool conditions, leaves staying wet long | Ventilation, morning watering, variety mixing, removing diseased leaves |
| Gray mold, crown rot, bacterial leaf spot | Gray mold (cool, humid) / browning at the crown and collapse (excess water) / blackish-brown spots on leaf edges (hot, humid, wounds) | Humidity control, spacing / drainage, keeping the area around the base clear / ventilation |
| Aphids, thrips | Suck sap and carry viruses / silvery marks | Soapy water, protecting ladybugs / yellow sticky traps, removing weeds |
| Moth caterpillars, slugs | Chew leaves / holes and slime trails | Catching in early morning, insect netting / catching at night, bands of ash, rice hulls, or eggshells |

> **When you find a plant with sclerotinia rot, lift it together with the soil around its roots and carry it out of the field. It must not go on the compost pile. The sclerotia survive the fermentation heat of compost and spread over the whole field.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The problem of lettuce is not insects but molds. In a field where sclerotia have built up, avoid garland chrysanthemum, chicory, and burdock too, and bring in grasses or legumes for 3-5 years. Lettuce leaves are tender, so do not exceed 20 g of soap per 1 L for soapy water, spray it away from midday, and rinse with clean water a few hours later.

| Symptom | Cause and remedy |
|:---|:---|
| Whole leaf yellow, small plant | Nitrogen deficiency. Matured compost, urine diluted 15-20 times |
| Inner leaf tips dry brown (tipburn) | Calcium does not reach the tips of new leaves because of uneven water movement. Even watering and spacing |
| Soft, tender leaves, or dark green leaves with strong bitterness | Nitrogen excess or drought stress. Tell them apart by flower stalk, soil dryness, and leaf color, then cut nitrogen or water |

## 7. Intercropping and Crop Rotation
| Compatibility | Companions |
|:---|:---|
| Good | Spring onion, Welsh onion, carrots (the smell confuses pests), radish (lettuce vacates the spot in 40-60 days), corn and sunflowers (on the north side, their shade delays bolting) |
| Bad | Asteraceae such as garland chrysanthemum, chicory, and burdock (they share sclerotinia rot and downy mildew), continuous lettuce cropping (sclerotia build up) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Going around in a 4-year cycle of lettuce and other Asteraceae → legumes (fix nitrogen) → grasses such as corn and sorghum → cabbage-family crops such as radish and Chinese cabbage, lettuce is started again with a lower density of sclerotia.

## 8. Harvest and Seed Saving
| Harvest | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Timing | Baby leaves (salad, wraps) at 25-30 days (combined with thinning), leaf-picking harvest at 30-40 days (outer leaf length 15-20 cm), whole-plant harvest at 40-60 days |
| Picking | Pick fully grown leaves from the bottom outer side, no more than half of all leaves at a time, always leaving six or more center leaves, and cut near the stem with scissors (tearing by hand leaves a wound that is an entry for pathogens). Water after picking |
| Repeat harvest | Every 10-14 days, 2-3 times on average and 4-5 if conditions are good. Two to three months from one plant, ending when the flower stalk rises. Ten to twenty plants can supply one person's daily needs |

> **Always leave six or more center leaves. The leaves you leave are the photosynthesis factory, and that factory makes the next leaves. If you get greedy and pick them all, that plant simply stops.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The growing point is at the top of the short stem in the center of the plant, so it remains when outer leaves are picked, but if the center is cut that plant is finished (the reason head lettuce is a single-harvest crop). Autumn lettuce continues until frost, so it lasts longer than spring lettuce.

| Seed saving | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Pollination | Pollination is complete at the bud stage before the flower opens, so it is self-pollinating and the cross-pollination rate is 0-5% (usually around 1%). Isolation of 1-2 m is enough, and even one plant will do |
| Timeline | Bolting begins 60-80 days after sowing, flowering at 90-110 days, seeds ripen 12-24 days after flowering, and harvest at 120-150 days. In an autumn crop frost comes first and seed cannot ripen, so seed is saved from the spring crop |
| Pappus | When the pappus swells, it scatters within a day or two, and the ripening time even within one plant spreads over more than 3 weeks. Strip only the swollen flowers every 2-3 days (highest recovery), cover with a breathable paper bag or cloth bag just before the fluff shows (best balance; avoid plastic), or once more than half have swollen, cut the whole stalk and hang it upside down to after-ripen |
| Cleaning, storage | A single plant yields 3,000-10,000 seeds. Dry thoroughly and keep in a sealed container in a cool, dark, non-humid place. Life is 2-3 years sealed at room temperature and 3-5 years if cool and dry. Check germination every year with 20 seeds |

> **Choose seed plants from the plants whose flower stalks rise latest. This is because late bolting, that is, the trait of holding out late in the heat, is inherited. If you repeat selection by this standard for several years, the lettuce in that field gradually becomes stronger in summer.**

## 9. Storage
| Method | Procedure and period |
|:---|:---|
| Stem in water | Cut 1 cm off the base and stand it in a dish with shallow water. 3-5 days. Change the water daily and recut the base |
| Damp cloth, damp straw | Wrap unwashed in a damp cloth and keep in a cool place for 3-4 days / lay in layers between damp straw for 3-5 days |
| Cellar, pit | In a cool underground space (10-15℃ all year) with a damp cloth, 5-7 days. Do not seal off airflow completely, and do not mix in spoiled leaves |
| Processing | Blanch and dry (6 months to 1 year, texture collapses), water kimchi (1-2 weeks), salt-pickled type (salt 10% or more, several months, only for soup) |

> **The way to solve lettuce's storage problem lies not in storage technique but in the sowing plan. If you sow in batches 10 to 15 days apart and use only the outer leaves, the need to store never arises. Lettuce standing in the field is the best storehouse.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lettuce is over 95% water, with thin, wide leaves that lose water quickly, and the tissue is held up by water pressure inside the cells, so it wilts when only a little leaves, and the latex at the cut browns and spoilage begins. The only techniques for getting it through a few days are keeping its water and lowering its temperature, and because washing leaves a water film that lets spoilage bacteria spread, wash just before eating.

## 10. Cooking and Precautions
| Method | Tips |
|:---|:---|
| Wraps | Use the whole leaf to wrap rice and side dishes. The simplest way to break the monotony of a grain-centered diet |
| Fresh-salted greens | Chop coarsely, salt briefly, and toss with seasoning. The leaves wilt and shrink in volume so you eat more, which makes it especially practical in disaster conditions |
| Salad, water kimchi | Mixing in oil or perilla powder raises absorption of the fat-soluble vitamins A, E, and K / left in 2-3% brine for a day or two, a good part of the vitamin C remains |
| Blanching, lettuce stalks | Blanching in boiling water for 20-30 seconds removes bitterness and softens, which is better when leaves are tough from bolting / peel lettuce stalks, soak in salted water to remove bitterness, and stir-fry or toss with seasoning; salted and packed in soybean paste or soy sauce, they keep for months |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lettuce can be eaten after washing alone, so it needs no cooking fuel. Vitamin C, folate, and some B vitamins are heat-sensitive and water-soluble, so a good part is lost on blanching, but lettuce has no such loss.

| Component | Effect and response |
|:---|:---|
| Lactucarium | The bitter component of the white latex that comes out when the stem is cut. It has a mild sedative effect but not at a level felt from the amount eaten. It rises sharply right after bolting and is hard to eat in the flowering period; bitterness is not a sign of toxicity but a sign that the time has passed |
| Nitrate | Increases with shade, low light, and excess nitrogen. A problem when lettuce from a shaded field given too much nitrogen is eaten in large amounts daily without other vegetables. Stop nitrogen sources a few days before harvest, give sunny spots to lettuce first, harvest in the afternoon, and alternate with other leafy vegetables |
| Oxalic acid | Content is low and not a practical problem |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lettuce has no acute toxicity.

## 11. Annual Work Schedule
| Month | Work |
|:---|:---|
| Jan-Feb | Check seeds and germination rates, turn compost, plan rotation |
| Mar-Apr | Spring sowing in late March (basal fertilizer, building beds). Thinning and transplanting, harvesting baby leaves, and scouting for slugs in April |
| May-June | Main harvest in May, regrowth harvest at 10-14 day intervals. Bolting begins in June; mark seed plants and clear the rest |
| July-Aug | Flowering and seed set of seed plants in July, summer crops in the empty space. Seed harvest and cleaning in August, with autumn sowing from the beginning of the month (dealing with heat dormancy) |
| Sep-Oct | Thinning and transplanting, second sowing, and scouting for downy mildew in September. Main harvest in October, the month with the longest regrowth harvest |
| Nov-Dec | Continued harvest, straw covering and windbreaks, and tidying in late November. Clearing the field and processing residue in December (diseased plants out of the field) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Plant in spring to secure seed and in autumn to eat, and the summer and winter in between are filled by other crops such as purslane and water spinach, kale and leaf mustard.

> **Of the twelve months of the year, lettuce is on the table for a little over four. Knowing this and planning what will fill the other eight months matters more in disaster conditions than the skill of growing lettuce well.**
