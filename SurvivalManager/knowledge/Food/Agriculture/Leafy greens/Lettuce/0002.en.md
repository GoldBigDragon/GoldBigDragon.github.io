## 1. Disaster-Preparedness Evaluation

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lettuce's Overall grade is D, with a score of 37.7. Broken down by scenario, it is Short-term D (38.5), Long-term D (36.9), and Supplementary SS (62.1). The difference between Overall and Supplementary is more than 24 points. Among the crops covered in this document, the gap between these two values is one of the largest. To understand lettuce as a crop, you first have to see why this gap arose.

### 1-3. Between Overall D and Supplementary SS

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The criteria that pulled the Overall score down are clear: Calories per area D, Storability E, Tillage & digging energy D, and Non-food value E. All four are different ways of saying the same thing: lettuce is of almost no use as a calorie resource or as a stockpile resource.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Tillage & digging energy D is worth taking a closer look at. An energy return of 4.1 times means that the harvest gives back 4.1 times the energy spent on working the field. That is low compared with kale's 12.9 times and leaf mustard's 7.5 times. Growing lettuce is not a losing venture, but it is a thin-margin business. Where people have to dig their fields with shovels, this number becomes the yardstick for deciding where to spend labor.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Conversely, the criteria that raised the Supplementary score are Protein density SS, Micronutrients S, Cooking & processing SS, Taste S, and Infrastructure needs SS. All five say that lettuce excels as a side dish and as a nutritional supplement.

| Perspective | Where lettuce stands |
|:---|:---|
| Source of calories | Almost meaningless. 20kcal per 100g |
| Stored food | Does not work. 2-3 days |
| Micronutrient supply | Meets 16 of 21 nutrients. Upper tier |
| Fuel saving | Top tier, since no cooking is needed |
| Seed self-reliance | Top tier, thanks to self-pollination |

> **The purpose of planting lettuce is not to fill a storehouse but to put something green on tonight's dinner table. As far as that purpose goes, few crops can replace it.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Let us look a little more closely at the number behind Micronutrients S, 16 nutrients. Of the 21 nutrients, lettuce exceeds 10% of the daily recommended amount for 16, including vitamin A, vitamin E, vitamin K, thiamin, riboflavin, pantothenic acid, folate, and calcium. These are exactly the slots that are the first to go empty in a diet built only from grains, tuber crops (tubers/storage roots), and beans. Vitamin K and folate can hardly be obtained from grains, and even from stored vegetables a considerable amount is lost during cooking. Lettuce eaten raw suffers no such loss.

### 1-4. How to Read the Strengths

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Three criteria carry an SS: Protein density, Infrastructure needs, and Cooking & processing. S went to Micronutrients, Speed to harvest, Taste, Prevalence in South Korea, and Seed saving.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Seed saving S is the quietest but most decisive strength in a disaster. Lettuce is a self-pollinating crop. Fertilization with its own pollen is already complete before the flower opens, so even if bees visit, the genes do not mix. This is the exact opposite of kale and radish, which require an isolation distance of 500m to 1km and need several plants flowering together. With lettuce, you can take seed from a single plant, and even if another variety flowers next to it, the seed generally stays pure. It is among the easiest crops for seed self-reliance.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Perenniality A is practical as well. If you pick only the outer leaves, new leaves keep rising from the growing point in the center, and you can harvest for 2-3 months. This trait neutralizes a large part of the fatal weakness of Storability E. For poor storage to become a problem, you would have to harvest a lot at once, but with lettuce you pick on the day only as much as you will eat that day. In effect, the field itself serves as the refrigerator.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Water use A, with total evapotranspiration of about 350mm, is also worth noting. It is less than the 500mm of radish and the 500mm of iceberg lettuce. Where water has to be hauled by hand, this difference translates into labor. It is one of the reasons leaf lettuce is better suited to a disaster than head lettuce.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Taste S is not an item to pass over lightly either. The assessment is that there is no off-putting smell or aroma and no problem with texture. This contrasts with leaf mustard's Taste D. Whether people can keep eating a crop in a disaster is hardly reflected in nutrition calculations, but there is no reason to plant a crop that goes uneaten.

### 1-5. How to Read the Weaknesses

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;E went to Storability and Non-food value; D to Calories per area and Tillage & digging energy; and C to Temperature range, Pest & disease pressure, and Yield retention without synthetic inputs.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Storability E, 2 to 3 days at room temperature without power, is the lowest grade. The reason lies in the structure. Lettuce is more than 95% water. The leaves are thin and broad, so their volume is small relative to their surface area, and the cell walls are thin, so the leaves go limp as soon as water escapes. From the moment of harvest, the leaves keep respiring and burning their own sugars, and in the process they give off heat and the tissue breaks down. Ways of slowing this process are covered in Section 15, but fundamentally the right way to use lettuce is not to assume storage at all.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Temperature range C, the narrow band of 15-22°C, is the second weakness. Compared with kale's 5-30°C and Swiss chard's 5-30°C, it is less than half as wide. This narrow band pins lettuce's cropping seasons to spring and autumn. In summer it will not germinate, and in winter it does not grow. Out of the whole year, the period in which lettuce can be harvested in South Korea is a little over four months.

> **Lettuce is a cool-season crop, and once that season passes, the flower stalk rises and the leaves turn bitter. This change cannot be reversed, and delaying that moment is half of the art of growing lettuce.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;What Continuous-cropping tolerance B and Pest-control self-reliance B point to are diseases typical of the daisy family. Sclerotinia rot, a fungal disease, leaves hard black lumps called sclerotia in the soil, and these lumps survive for several years. Downy mildew spreads rapidly in cool, damp conditions, and according to the evaluation basis, mulching alone does not give commercial control and losses reach at least 10% at moderate incidence. Where no pesticides are available, the answer to these diseases lies not in control after the fact but in preventive conditions: ventilation, drainage, and crop rotation.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Seed longevity B, 2-3 years sealed at room temperature, is another item to note. It is short compared with the 5 years of cabbage-family crops. Daisy-family seeds have thin coats and contain fat, so they oxidize quickly. In a disaster, this means seed has to be saved more often, and fortunately, because lettuce is self-pollinating, that burden is not large.
