## 5. Field Preparation and Fertilizer

### 5-1. Preparing the Field

| Item | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Tillage | Till to a depth of 20-30cm, break up the clods, and level the soil finely |
| Raised bed | 15-20cm high. Dig drainage channels alongside |
| Soil acidity | Adjust to pH 6.0-6.6 |
| Organic matter | Mix in well-rotted compost 1-2 weeks before sowing |
| Surface preparation | Because the seeds are very small, level the surface finely |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;What sets lettuce apart from other crops in field preparation is how finely the surface is leveled. A lettuce seed is very small, just over 3mm long, and the sowing depth is no more than 2-3mm. If the clods are large, seeds fall into the gaps and get buried deep, and because the seed needs light to germinate, they will not sprout. Breaking up and leveling the soil as finely as if sifting it, at least at the sowing spot, determines the germination rate.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Deep tillage itself is not essential for lettuce, because its roots spread shallowly. That is also why the Tillage & digging energy in the evaluation table was calculated on the basis of 10cm deep tillage. If labor must be conserved, lettuce will grow as long as the top 10cm of soil is well broken up. However, if the field drains poorly, the work of building raised beds cannot be skipped.

### 5-2. Fertilizer Rates

| Fertilizer | Per 1,000m² | Per m² | When applied |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---|
| Nitrogen (N) | 10-12kg | 10-12g | All as base fertilizer |
| Phosphate (P) | 6-8kg | 6-8g | All as base fertilizer |
| Potassium (K) | 6-8kg | 6-8g | All as base fertilizer |
| Compost | 1,000-1,500kg | 1-1.5kg | 1-2 weeks before sowing |
| Lime | 100-150kg | 100-150g | For correcting acidic soil |

| Category | Timing | Details |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Base fertilizer | 1-2 weeks before sowing | All of the compost and fertilizer |
| Top-dressing | Skipped as a rule | A small amount only on poor soil or when cut-and-come-again harvesting runs long |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Because lettuce has a short growing period and its roots do not reach deep, plenty of fertilizer is applied as base fertilizer and almost no top-dressing is given. The Fertilizer demand criterion in the evaluation table, graded B and set at about 22g of nitrogen, phosphate, and potassium combined, reflects this figure.

> **Top-dressing lettuce actually makes it taste worse. When nitrogen concentration rises, the leaves turn soft, the bitterness intensifies, and the plants become weak against disease. Unless the soil is poor, base fertilizer alone is enough.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The exception is when cut-and-come-again harvesting is carried on for a long time. If you keep picking leaves for more than two months, nutrients really do leave the field. In that case, give a dilute top-dressing once every 2-3 weeks, applying it right after picking the leaves and not in the few days before harvest. This is because nitrate can remain in leaves picked right after nitrogen is applied.

### 5-3. Substitute Fertilizers for a Disaster

| Substitute resource | Nutrients supplied | How to use | Caution |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Well-rotted compost | Nitrogen, phosphate, potassium, and trace elements generally | Apply 1-1.5kg per m² as base fertilizer | Compost that is not fully rotted damages young roots |
| Wood ash | Potassium, phosphate, calcium | Scatter a handful per m² and mix with the soil | It is alkaline, so adjust so that pH does not exceed 6.6 |
| Diluted urine | Nitrogen | Dilute 15 to 20 times with water | Lettuce is sensitive to nitrogen, so use it more dilute than for other crops |
| Bone meal | Phosphate, calcium | Mix into compost and apply as base fertilizer | It decomposes slowly, so apply it in advance |
| Eggshell powder | Calcium | Crush finely and mix into the field | Used to prevent leaf tip burn |
| Leaf mold from fallen leaves and grass | Organic matter | Work into the field or use as mulch | Use only fully decomposed material |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When using diluted urine on lettuce, make it weaker than for other crops. For most leafy vegetables a dilution of 10-15 times is recommended, but for lettuce 15 to 20 times is safe. Too much nitrogen softens the leaves, intensifies the bitterness, and attracts aphids. Moreover, since lettuce is a vegetable eaten raw, you need to pay more attention to nitrate accumulation.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Wood ash supplies potassium and calcium but is strongly alkaline. The pH 6.0-6.6 that lettuce requires is on the lower side compared with cabbage-family crops, so if you apply wood ash generously, as you would for radish, the pH rises too far. It is right to apply a little and watch the result.

## 6. Sowing - A Seed Ruled by Light and Temperature

### 6-1. Cropping Seasons

| Cropping season | Sowing time (South Korea) | Harvest time (South Korea) | Notes |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Spring crop | Late March to mid-May | Late April to late June | The most common |
| Autumn crop | Early August to early September | Early September to early November | The most advantageous in a disaster |
| Summer crop | Not recommended | | Germination failure and early bolting |
| Winter crop | Only under protected conditions in southern South Korea | | Growth is extremely slow |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There are three reasons why autumn cropping is advantageous in a disaster. First, temperatures are falling throughout the growing period, so there is no pressure to bolt. Spring cropping is the opposite: temperatures rise, so at some point the flower stalk is bound to rise. Second, lettuce grown in cool conditions has thicker leaves and a sweeter taste. Third, the period of cut-and-come-again harvesting is longer. If spring lettuce is done in a little over a month, autumn lettuce goes on for more than two months.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;However, autumn sowing has a trap. Early August in South Korea is still hot, and that heat triggers high-temperature dormancy. If the soil temperature at the time of sowing exceeds 25°C, the seeds go to sleep. So the real skill of autumn sowing lies in how to get the seeds to germinate while avoiding the heat.

### 6-2. Getting Past High-Temperature Dormancy

| Method | Procedure | Principle |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Cold priming | Soak the seeds in water for 4-6 hours, wrap them in a wet cloth, and leave them in a cool place for a day | Seeds that have absorbed water experience cold, and dormancy is broken |
| Pre-sprouting | After the seeds sprout slightly between sheets of wet cloth, carefully move and sow them | Finishes the germination stage outside the field |
| Evening sowing | Sow after sunset and water thoroughly | Takes advantage of the drop in soil temperature overnight |
| Shading | Make shade over the sowing spot with a bamboo screen, branches, or straw | Lowers soil temperature by a few degrees |
| Cooling with water | Sprinkle cold water right after sowing and during the day | Lowers soil temperature through the heat of evaporation |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Cold priming is the most reliable and takes the least effort. When seeds that have absorbed water experience cold, the hormonal signal that was suppressing germination is released, and afterwards they sprout even when sown in hot soil. Even without a refrigerator, any cool spot will do, such as well water, a cellar, or north-facing shade. However, seeds that have taken up water die if they dry out, so after treatment they must be sown right away and the soil must be kept from drying.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Shading works even if it covers only the sowing spot. Because the seeds need light to germinate, do not make it completely dark; lay a bamboo screen or branches loosely so that they cast a light shade. Remove it as soon as the seedlings emerge.

### 6-3. Sowing Method

| Item | Standard | Notes |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Sowing depth | 2-3mm | Light-germinating. Seeds buried deep will not germinate |
| Soil covering | Fine soil, only enough to barely hide the seeds | Sometimes the seeds are not covered at all and only pressed down |
| Row spacing | 20cm | |
| Sowing method | Row sowing or spot sowing | Broadcast sowing is also possible if the aim is harvesting young leaves |
| Seed rate | Sow densely, then thin | Germination may be low, so allow a margin |
| Firming | Press lightly with a hand or a board | Seeds must be in close contact with the soil to absorb water |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Another method is to sow with no soil covering at all and only press the seeds down lightly. This can be better for light-germinating seed, but there is a risk that the seeds will dry out or be washed away by rain. The compromise is 2-3mm. Scattering a very thin layer of dry grass over it reduces evaporation while still letting light through.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Not sowing everything at once is a basic skill of lettuce growing. Because it is a crop that cannot be stored, a harvest that bunches up ends up being wasted. If you split the sowing at intervals of 10 to 15 days, the harvest is spread out by the same amount. For autumn sowing in South Korea, for example, you divide it into three sowings: early August, mid-August, and early September.

### 6-4. Seed Treatment and Storage

| Treatment | Procedure | Effect |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Selection | Choose plump seeds of even color | Raises the germination rate |
| Hot-water soak | Soak in 50-55°C water for 10-15 minutes, then cool in cold water | Kills seed-borne pathogens |
| Cold storage | Keep sealed in a cool place at 0-4°C | Lengthens seed longevity |
| Desiccant | Put in roasted and cooled rice or charcoal | Keeps moisture out |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lettuce seed longevity is rather short, at 2-3 years when sealed at room temperature. This is because daisy-family seeds have thin coats and contain fat, so they oxidize quickly. Keeping them cool noticeably extends their life. Without a refrigerator, look for a place that stays cool all year round, such as a root-cellar pit, an earthen cave, a north-facing underground space, or the area near a well. However, damp actually kills them faster, so sealing and a desiccant must go together.

## 7. Water Management

### 7-1. Water by Growth Stage

| Period | Management | Notes |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Sowing to germination | Water lightly and often so the surface soil does not dry | The seeds lie shallow and dry out easily |
| Seedling stage | Water lightly and often | Roots are shallow |
| Growth stage | Every 3-5 days in spring and autumn, every 2-3 days in summer | Adjust by looking at the soil condition |
| Cut-and-come-again harvest stage | Water after picking leaves | New leaves need moisture to rise |
| Just before harvest | Reduce somewhat | Leaves firm up and wilt more slowly |
| Monsoon season (jangma, late June to July in South Korea) | Secure drainage channels | Sclerotinia rot and gray mold begin in excess moisture |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;What to remember about watering lettuce is the relationship between dryness and bitterness. When water is short, lettuce thickens its leaves and raises the concentration of lactucarium. This is the general response of a stressed plant increasing its defensive compounds, and to the human mouth it shows up as bitterness. If lettuce is bitter, it was short of water, it was hot, or bolting has begun.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The best time to water is early morning, because the moisture on the leaves dries during the day. If you water in the evening, the leaves stay wet all night, and that is the condition for fungal disease. In lettuce, downy mildew and gray mold occur almost in proportion to how long the leaves stay wet.

### 7-2. When Water Is Short or Excessive

| Condition | Symptom | Result |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Too little | Leaves droop in the daytime and recover in the evening | Temporary. Recovery is still possible |
| Too little | Leaves become small and thick, and bitterness intensifies | Lactucarium increases |
| Too little | Leaf margins dry out and the flower stalk rises | Recovery is difficult |
| Too much | Lower leaves turn yellow | Lack of oxygen at the roots |
| Too much | White mold and black grains are seen at the base | Sclerotinia rot. That plant must be discarded |
| Too much | Leaves turn soft and gray mold grows | Gray mold |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If you see hard black grains at the base, it is sclerotinia rot. These grains are sclerotia, and once they fall into the soil they survive for several years and infect the next crop. When you find them, dig up the plant along with the surrounding soil, carry it out of the field, and burn it or bury it far away instead of putting it into the compost.

### 7-3. Mulching

| Material | Effect | Caution |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Straw | Conserves moisture, suppresses weeds, blocks rain splash | Use only a thin layer right after sowing |
| Dry grass and fallen leaves | Same effects as straw | Grass with seeds mixed in invites weeds |
| Rice hulls | Light and long-lasting | Do not mix into the soil; only lay on top |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The greatest benefit of mulching in lettuce is blocking rain splash. The spores of sclerotinia rot and gray mold sit in the soil, and when raindrops hit the ground they are splashed up and stick to the undersides of the leaves. A layer of straw between the ground and the leaves cuts off this route. Lettuce is a crop whose leaves grow close to the ground, so this problem is especially serious, and that is why mulching works better than it does for other crops.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;However, as the evaluation basis points out, mulching alone cannot stop downy mildew, because downy mildew spreads by airborne spores. Mulching is one of several measures and is meaningful only when used together with ventilation, drainage, and mixed planting of varieties.

## 8. Crop Management

### 8-1. Thinning

| Round | Timing | Spacing left |
|:---|:---|:---:|
| 1st | 2-3 true leaves | 5cm |
| 2nd | 4-5 true leaves | 15-20cm (final) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If thinning is not done on time, lettuce grows only upward. Competing with neighboring plants for light, it stretches its leaf stalks, and the result is a thin, long stem with a few small leaves attached. Moreover, when plants are crowded, the leaves overlap and ventilation is poor, and that humid air becomes the condition for downy mildew and gray mold. Where no pesticides are available, securing spacing is also a means of pest and disease control.

> **Young lettuce that has been thinned out is not thrown away; it is the first harvest. Young leaves at around 25 days after sowing are tender and have almost no bitterness, so they can go straight into ssam (leaf wraps) or a salad.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When thinning, do not pull the plants; cut them near the ground with scissors or a fingernail. Lettuce's fine roots are shallow and tangled with one another, so pulling one shakes the neighboring plants too. A plant whose roots have been loosened spends several days recovering, and that stress can even bring bolting forward.

### 8-2. Weeds and Hilling

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lettuce does not need hilling. The harvested part does not grow in the soil, and because the growing period is short, harvest ends before the plant gets big enough to fall over. On the contrary, soil heaped around the base can start sclerotinia rot right there, so it is better to leave the area around the base clear so that air can circulate.

| Weed control | Method | Timing |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Hand weeding | Pull the weeds between plants | Together with thinning |
| Mulching | Cover with straw or dry grass | After 3-4 true leaves |
| Shallow hoeing | Insert a homi (Korean hand hoe) shallowly and scrape only the surface | While weeds are small |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When weeding, take care not to put the homi in deep. Most of lettuce's fine roots lie within 5-10cm of the surface, and if these roots are cut, the plant wilts and recovers slowly. Scrape shallowly starting from the side farthest from the plant, and pull the weeds right next to the plant by hand.

### 8-3. Managing to Delay Bolting

| Factor | Effect on bolting | Response |
|:---|:---|:---|
| High temperature | The strongest signal | Lower soil temperature with shading, mulching, and irrigation |
| Long days | Promoted as days lengthen | In spring cropping, hurry the harvest |
| Drought stress | The plant hurries to leave seed | Water evenly |
| Root damage | Acts as stress | Thin and weed carefully |
| Crowding | Competition stress | Thin on time |
| Nitrogen deficiency | The switch to reproductive growth comes sooner | Apply enough base fertilizer |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Once bolting starts, it cannot be reversed. When a stalk shoots up from the center and the leaves begin to stand upright, it is right to consider the harvest from that plant finished. Even if you cut off the flower stalk, another rises from beside it, and in the meantime the leaves keep getting more bitter.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Bolted lettuce does not have to be thrown away entirely. If young flower stalks and the leaves on them are blanched and soaked, much of the bitterness comes out. Blanch them in boiling water, leave them in cold water, and squeeze out the water, and they can be eaten as namul (a seasoned vegetable side dish). And leave a few plants as they are to save seed. Section 13 covers this.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When you have to get lettuce through the summer, the practical responses are shading and placement. Putting lettuce in the shade on the north side of tall crops, in the shade of buildings, or under trees lowers the soil temperature by a few degrees and delays bolting by that much. The old layout of planting lettuce between maize or sunflowers rests on this principle.
