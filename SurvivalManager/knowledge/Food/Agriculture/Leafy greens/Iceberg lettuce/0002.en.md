## 1. Disaster-Preparedness Evaluation

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Iceberg lettuce's overall grade is E, 35.7 points. Broken down by scenario: short-term D (36.4), long-term E (35.1), supplementary S (57.1). That places it among the lower-scoring crops this document covers, and the long-term E in particular signals that iceberg lettuce has almost no place in a food system meant to sustain people over the long haul.

### 1-3. Side by Side with Lettuce

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The fastest way to understand iceberg lettuce accurately is to line its criteria up against lettuce, the same species. The two crops are genetically almost identical and grown in similar ways, so every criterion where they differ traces back to a single trait: heading.

| Criterion | Lettuce | Iceberg Lettuce | Reason for the Difference |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---|
| Overall | D (37.7) | E (35.7) | |
| Calories per area | D | D | Yield is higher, but calorie density is lower |
| Micronutrients | S (16 nutrients) | A (15 nutrients) | Inner leaves get no light, so they contain less chlorophyll and vitamins |
| Speed to harvest | S (50-60 days) | A (85-95 days) | Heading takes more time |
| Perenniality | A (2-3 months) | D (one-time) | Harvesting the head removes the growing point |
| Water use | A (350mm) | B (500mm) | Filling out a large head takes more water |
| Temperature range | C (15-22°C) | D (15-20°C) | Heading conditions are narrower than growth conditions |
| Storability | E (2-3 days) | D (1 week) | Outer leaves wrap and protect the inside |
| Tillage & digging energy | D (4.1×) | C (5.2×) | Yield per area is higher, so the energy-return multiple is higher |
| Prevalence in South Korea | S (8 provinces) | A (5-7 provinces) | Growing regions are limited |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In this table, iceberg lettuce wins only three cells: storability, tillage and digging energy, and yield per area, which is not in the table. It loses six. From a disaster-preparedness standpoint, this is where the conclusion comes from that it is hard to find a reason to plant iceberg lettuce instead of lettuce.

> **Iceberg lettuce is not an upgraded version of lettuce. It is a different strategy branching off from the same species, and under disaster conditions, that strategy is mostly on the losing side.**

### 1-4. Reading the Weaknesses Precisely

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The only criterion marked E is non-food value, while D was given to calories per area, perenniality, temperature range, and storability. Of these, the ones that actually matter are perenniality D and temperature range D.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Perenniality D, with its basis of "a heading type, so the harvest is one-time," outweighs the others. The core strength of leafy vegetables lies in yielding a little at a time over a long period. Lettuce keeps giving for two to three months, Swiss chard for several months, kale for more than a year - that is their strength. Iceberg lettuce gives up that strength and bets everything on a single moment. For the three months it grows in the field, nothing edible comes out of it, and then, the moment those three months end, everything arrives at once. A storability of D - one week unpowered at room temperature - is not enough to handle that all-at-once arrival.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Temperature range D - heading limited to 15-20°C, a span of 8°C - is the narrowest among the crops this document covers. Compared with Swiss chard's 25°C or kale's 25°C, it is only a third as wide. This narrow window means two things. First, the planting window is pinned to a narrow band in spring and fall. Second, even planting within that band fails if the year's weather does not cooperate. If it is transplanted in fall and heat continues into late September, heading never starts; if it is planted in spring and June turns hot early, heading stops partway through. The statistical rating of yield stability A measures variation across years when cultivation went normally, and it does not capture the risk of total loss from a failed head.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Micronutrients A, 15 nutrients, is also worth noting. That is only one nutrient fewer than lettuce's 16, but the number behind it matters more. Reaching 10% of the daily recommended intake actually requires eating 15.0kg, versus 10.5kg for lettuce. In other words, eating the same amount of iceberg lettuce gets you only about 2/3 of the micronutrients you would get from lettuce. The reason lies in heading itself. The inner leaves are shaded by the outer ones and receive no light, so they have almost no chlorophyll and stay white. No chlorophyll means little photosynthesis happened in that leaf, and beta-carotene, vitamin K, and several other compounds are correspondingly reduced as well. The very reason iceberg lettuce's heart is crisp and mild is the same reason its nutrition is diluted.

### 1-5. The Strengths That Remain

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The criteria marked SS are three: protein density, infrastructure needs, and cooking & processing. S went to taste and seed saving. These five are traits iceberg lettuce shares with lettuce. Since it is washed and eaten as is, it needs no fuel; it needs no infrastructure; and being self-pollinating, its traits stay stable even when you save your own seed.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Iceberg lettuce has two strengths of its own. One is the storability D already noted. Unpowered, at room temperature, one week is a low figure in absolute terms, but not low among leafy vegetables. Swiss chard is also one week, and lettuce is 2-3 days. Putting it in a cool root-cellar pit extends this period further.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The other is yield per area. 4,000kg per 1,000m² is 33% more than lettuce's 3,000kg. Calorie density is low, so calories per area is still D, but the fact that the energy-return multiple under tillage & digging energy rose from lettuce's 4.1× to 5.2× is thanks to this yield. In a situation where turning the field over depends on human muscle, getting more volume for the same labor is genuinely valuable.

> **If there is a reason to plant iceberg lettuce, it is exactly one: to leave fresh vegetables for a few weeks after frost. Short of that purpose, planting lettuce in the same spot is better on every criterion.**
