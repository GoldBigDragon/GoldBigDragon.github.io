---
## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Chili pepper (Capsicum annuum) is an annual fruit vegetable of the nightshade family, genus Capsicum. It is native to Central and South America. The evaluation table judged that it cannot qualify as a food crop because its calories per area are graded E. Even a harvest of 300 kg from 1,000㎡ gives only 255 kcal per ㎡, 0.10 times the self-sufficiency line, which falls short of the 15% exclusion line, and its no-gate score is 30.5 (rank 183 of 183 valid species). Even so, chili is grown because it is a tool, not a food. Fully dried chili keeps for 1-2 years at room temperature and can be ground and used at once without heating, so when salted food is too salty to eat and grain is too bland to swallow, a spoonful of red pepper powder turns it into something edible. Capsaicin is antibacterial and also repels insects. The weak point is disease. Anthracnose has no cure, so in some years the whole red pepper crop is lost in the rainy season. A realistic goal is to plant twenty to thirty plants to secure a year's red pepper powder.

## 1. Disaster Preparedness Evaluation
| Category | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Verdict | Excluded (calories per area E, below 15% of the self-sufficiency line). No-gate score 30.5 |
| Strengths | SS: cooking and processing (used at once without heating). S: micronutrients, sunlight response (relative yield 102% under 40% shading), storability, yield stability, domestic cultivation spread, seed saving |
| Weaknesses | E: calories per area, cold tolerance. D: harvest speed (160-180 days in direct-sowing terms, 70 days of that for seedlings), fertilization (42 g per ㎡), facility burden (stakes and rain shelters), pest-control self-reliance (no cure for anthracnose, unprotected loss rate 50.1%), continuous-cropping tolerance (5-7 years of rest), non-food value, taste. C: water consumption (total evapotranspiration 750 mm), temperature range, seed longevity |

> **A square meter of chili field is a square meter of potato given up. When people are hungry that trade is a bad one; when there is food but it is hard to eat, it is a good one.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fresh chili has 44-78 mg of vitamin C per 100 g, but because only a few grams to a few dozen grams are used a day, its nutritional contribution is minor. The grade for each item is in the Crop Evaluation Results document.

## 2. Plant Overview
| Species | Representatives | Cross-pollination |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Capsicum annuum | Landrace chili, bell pepper, paprika, jalapeño | Cross completely within the same species |

> **The hot chili, green chili, bell pepper and paprika we grow are all the same species. If planted in one field they exchange pollen, and the seed from them gives a mix of hot and sweet the next year.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The stem stands upright, is woody at the base, and is 60-100 cm tall; the first branching point, where it splits at a certain node, is called the first fork. Because the taproot is cut at transplanting, the fine roots crowd within 20 cm of the surface, making the plant shallow-rooted. The flowers are white and mainly self-pollinating. The fruit is a berry, hollow, with flat seeds 3-4 mm across attached to the placenta. As a nightshade it shares diseases with eggplant, tomato and potato.

| Stage | Timing | Key point |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Seedling | 70-80 days from sowing to transplanting | 5-7 true leaves; transplant when the first flower bud appears |
| Establishment and vegetative growth | 1-5 weeks after transplanting | No visible growth during establishment is normal. Afterwards the stem thickens and the first fork forms |
| Flowering and fruit set | 40-50 days after transplanting | The first flower opens at the first fork |
| Green pepper | 20-30 days after flowering | Fruit has reached full size |
| Red pepper | 45-50 days after flowering | Turns red; capsaicin and pigment accumulate |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Green and red pepper are different moments of the same fruit, and which to harvest is decided by the year's disease situation and need.

| Nutrition per 100 g | Fresh (red) | Dried |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Calories | 85 kcal | 280-330 kcal |
| Protein / dietary fiber | 3.12 g / 4-5 g | 10-13 g / 25-30 g |
| Vitamin C | 44-78 mg | Mostly lost |
| Others | Beta-carotene, potassium, capsaicin (0-0.5% depending on variety, concentrated in the placenta) | Concentrated |

> **To use chili as a source of vitamin C, it must be eaten raw. Do not expect anything from winter red pepper powder.**

## 3. Growing Environment and Varieties
| Condition | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Temperature | Germination 25-30℃ (very slow at 20℃ or below), seedlings day 22-30℃ and night 15℃ or higher (flower bud differentiation is poor if it stays below that), growth 25-32℃, fruiting 20-25℃. Above 35℃ pollen vigor falls and flowers drop, growth stops at 10℃ or below, and frost kills it |
| Light | 6 hours or more a day (minimum 4 hours). Midsummer direct sun causes sunscald on the fruit |
| Water | About 750 mm total evapotranspiration per crop. Soil moisture 60-70% of maximum. Excess water is more dangerous than dryness |
| Soil | Loam to sandy loam that both drains and holds water, soil depth 30 cm or more, pH 6.0-6.5. Weak to salts. Bed height 20-30 cm |

> **Raising the beds in a chili field is the only structural defense against blight. Without chemicals, bed height decides that year's harvest.**

| Variety | Characteristics |
|:---|:---|
| Maeuntan, Speed Kaltan | Large-fruited early types. Relatively resistant to anthracnose and viruses. F1 hybrids |
| Bugang, Muhanjilju, Urigeon | Standard mid-season types. Dry well. Muhanjilju is very hot |
| Landrace Chilseongcho and Subicho lines | Fixed lines, so seed can be saved and carried on, and the aroma is rich, but they are weak to anthracnose |
| Cheongyang type | Small-fruited. Very hot, so a small amount serves for seasoning and insect repellence |
| Bell pepper and paprika | Not hot, for children and the elderly. Weak to anthracnose |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;With no chemicals at all, an anthracnose-resistant line is the only defense, but such varieties are usually F1 hybrids whose seed cannot be saved, while fixed landraces are weak to anthracnose. The realistic answer is to plant both lines, kept apart from each other. To raise the harvest before the rainy season, choose early-maturing varieties.

## 4. Field Preparation, Sowing, and Transplanting
| Item | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Soil preparation | Plow 25-30 cm deep, make beds 20-30 cm high, and dig drainage ditches along the field edge and at the ends of the ridges. First check whether nightshades were planted within the past 3-5 years. Lime 2-3 weeks before transplanting, well-rotted compost 1-2 weeks before (if applied on the same day, nitrogen is lost) |
| Fertilizing (per 1,000㎡) | Compost 1,500-2,000 kg, nitrogen 19.0 kg (50% basal, 50% top dressing), phosphate 11.2 kg (all basal), potassium 14.9 kg (60% basal, 40% top dressing) |
| Top dressing | Start about 30 days after transplanting and repeat every 20-25 days, four times in all. The third is mainly potassium. Never right beside the plant |
| Substitute resources | Well-rotted compost 2-3 kg per ㎡, urine diluted 10-15 times (poured into the furrows and stopped 2 weeks before harvest), wood ash a handful per ㎡, bone meal, fermented oil cake diluted 10 times, grass mulch, burned eggshell or shell powder (calcium) |

> **The idea that more nitrogen means more fruit is a mistake. Too much nitrogen makes only the leaves lush and the flowers drop, and in plants whose interior has turned dark, anthracnose and soft rot take hold.**

| Work | Central region | Southern region |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Sowing (start of seedlings) | Mid to late February | Early to mid February |
| Transplanting | Early to mid May (after the late frosts are over) | Late April to early May |
| Harvest | Green pepper from mid June, red pepper late July to October | Same |

| Seedlings and transplanting | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Sowing | Soak in 30℃ water for 10 hours, wrap in wet cloth and sprout at 25-30℃ (4-7 days). Depth 0.5-1 cm. Transplant once at 3-4 true leaves (35-40 days after sowing), and harden off from 1 week before transplanting by putting the plants outside during the day |
| Without heating | Manure-heated hotbed of horse manure, rice straw and rice hulls trodden in (20-30℃ for 3-6 weeks), a south-facing window, heat storage with black water barrels (raises night temperature by 3-5℃), double covering (raises it 2-4℃), the upper end of an ondol room (night 18-22℃, prone to leggy growth) |
| When seedlings are impossible | Sow in April and transplant in mid June. It becomes a year of green peppers only |
| Transplanting | Slightly deeper than the seedling soil. Single-row planting: ridges 80-90 cm, plants 35-40 cm apart (about 3 plants per ㎡); double-row planting: ridges 150 cm. Pour water into the hole and plant after it drains. Set 1.5 m stakes at the same time as transplanting. Plant on a cloudy afternoon |

## 5. Water Management and Cultivation Management
| Period | Water management |
|:---|:---|
| Right after transplanting to establishment | Water thoroughly and keep from drying out for 3-4 days |
| After establishment to before flowering | When the topsoil dries, at 5-7 day intervals. Keeping it slightly dry makes the roots spread wide |
| Flowering and fruit set, swelling and coloring | Every 3-4 days, evenly. Uneven watering causes blossom-end rot |
| Rainy season | Stop irrigation and check the drainage ditches |
| Late harvest | Taper off gradually (watering late delays coloring) |

> **When a single plant suddenly wilts and dies, you want to give more water, but this is usually the worst choice. The cause of sudden summer wilting in chili is far more often blight than lack of water, and more water carries the zoospores to neighboring plants.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When water is short, flowers and young fruit drop first and the fruit turns thin and wrinkled. Blossom-end rot, in which the fruit tip sinks and rots black, is not a pathogen but the result of interrupted calcium transport, and the remedy is not adding more calcium but keeping soil moisture even with mulch. When water is scarce, use straw mulch, evening watering, furrow watering (so anthracnose spores are not splashed), and drip watering with a bottle poked with holes at the bottom stuck into the soil.

| Management | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Side shoots | 30-40 days after transplanting, when the shoots are as long as a thumb, snap off by hand the side shoots below the first fork. Removing them too early slows early growth. Pick off the first fruit at the first fork while it is young |
| Training | One-line training (simple but branches droop), two-line training (strings in two or three tiers on stakes at both sides), X-shaped training (1.8 m stakes crossed; good ventilation and support). Use straw rope, kudzu vine or cloth strips for ties |
| Rain shelter | Without plastic: cultivation under eaves or along walls, thatch or straw-mat roofs, boards or roof tiles over the part where fruit is concentrated, straw mulch. There is no complete substitute |

> **Without chemicals, pruning chili is not for making larger fruit but for letting wind pass through the plant. The places where moisture lingers are where anthracnose starts.**

## 6. Pests, Diseases, and Nutrient Problems
| Disease | Symptoms and conditions | Response without chemicals |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Anthracnose | Water-soaked round spots on ripening fruit that sink in and form black spore masses. Hot, humid rainy season | No cure. Pick diseased fruit at once and burn it. Resistant varieties, rain shelter, straw mulch, wide spacing |
| Phytophthora blight | The stem base narrows to dark brown and the plant wilts suddenly. Excess water, poor drainage | High beds and drainage ditches are the only structural remedy. Remove diseased plants at once |
| Bacterial wilt, virus disease | Wilts at midday and recovers in the morning, repeatedly, then dies (hot, soil-borne). Leaves mottled, fruit deformed (carried by aphids and thrips) | Neither can be cured. Remove diseased plants, long rotation, control the vectors |
| Bacterial spot, gray mold, powdery mildew | Spots on leaves, flowers and fruit, gray mold, white powder | Ensure ventilation, shake off spent flowers, Bordeaux mixture as prevention, diluted milk or baking soda solution |

> **Anthracnose has no cure. All you can do is reduce infection itself, and the surest way to avoid it is to pick green pepper before anthracnose gets going. Grown without chemicals, some years give a generous harvest and some years lose half.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The pests are aphids (soapy water, protect ladybugs), thrips (blue sticky boards), tobacco budworm (pick off and destroy fruit with holes and catch the larvae by hand), two-spotted spider mite (spray water hard on the underside of leaves) and stink bugs (hand picking in the early morning). Control materials are Bordeaux mixture (dissolve 1 kg of copper sulfate and 1 kg of quicklime separately, let cool, combine and make up to 100 L), soapy water (20 g of unscented soap in 1 L of water), diluted milk, wood ash, and wood vinegar diluted 300-500 times.

| Symptom | Cause and action |
|:---|:---|
| Yellowing from the lower leaves and small plants | Nitrogen deficiency. Urine diluted 10-15 times, rotted compost |
| Leaf margins scorched and small fruit | Potassium deficiency. Wood ash |
| Underside of leaves and stem purplish | Phosphorus deficiency (in cold periods the cold soil can also block uptake). Bone meal |
| Yellow between the veins, veins stay green | Magnesium deficiency. Dolomite powder |
| Flowers open and then drop | One of boron deficiency, heat, excess nitrogen or water shortage. Check in turn |
| Only leaves lush and few flowers | Excess nitrogen. Cut off nitrogen and supplement potassium with wood ash |

## 7. Intercropping and Crop Rotation
| Compatibility | Crop |
|:---|:---|
| Good | Legumes (kidney bean, cowpea), marigold (suppresses root-knot nematodes), spring onion, garlic and garlic chive (suppress soil pathogens), perilla and shiso (the strong scent confuses pests) |
| Poor | Eggplant, tomato and potato (share blight and bacterial wilt completely), maize (shade, nutrient competition), gourd family (vines block ventilation) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Rotate chili in year 1, a legume in year 2, the onion genus in year 3, a grain (maize, sorghum, foxtail millet) in year 4 and a leaf or root vegetable in year 5, and resume chili from year 6. If it is planted on the same spot for 3 years or more, in some year half of it will suddenly die of blight.

## 8. Harvest and Seed Saving
| Item | Green pepper | Red pepper |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Harvest point | 20-30 days after flowering, when it has grown to full size and is glossy (every 3-5 days) | 45-50 days after flowering, when it turns deep red and starts to wrinkle (every 7-10 days) |
| Exposure to anthracnose | Short | Long |
| Storage | 1-2 weeks at room temperature | 1-2 years if dried |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Pick in the early morning after the dew has dried, leaving the calyx on (if picked wet, mold appears in storage), and take diseased fruit separately out of the field. Fruit near the bottom is close to the soil and has a high anthracnose risk, so pick it green, and ripen the upper fruit, which gets good sun and wind, to red. In a year when the rainy season looks long, bring the decision forward.

| Seed saving | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Preventing crossing | Bees carry pollen at a rate of 2-50%. Keep 100-500 m from other lines, or cover with a net or cloth bag before the flowers open. Planting only one line is simplest |
| Seed fruit | In July pick healthy, vigorous plants and mark fruit on the lower part. In September-October leave until fully red and wrinkled, then ripen 1-2 weeks in shade |
| Separating and drying | Split the fruit, strip the seed from the placenta and discard what floats in water. Dry in a single layer in shade for 1-2 weeks. Seal in a glass jar or metal tin at 8% moisture or less with a desiccant (roasted and cooled rice, charcoal, wood ash) |
| Lifespan | 1 year at room temperature unsealed, 2-3 years sealed with a desiccant, 4-5 years dry and sealed at 10℃ or below. Each year place 20 seeds on wet cloth and count germination after ten days; if below 70%, raise the sowing rate or collect new seed |
| Disinfection | 50-55℃ water for 10-15 minutes then cool in cold water, discard what floats in water, coat with wood ash |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If you touch the placenta with bare hands while stripping seed, capsaicin soaks into the skin and stings for hours. Wrap your hands in cloth or plastic, and after the work wipe them with oil or milk and then wash.

## 9. Drying and Storage
| Method | Conditions | Time |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Sun drying | On a clear, windy day spread on mats or strung on thread and hung | 7-15 days |
| Shade drying | Well-ventilated shade. The color stays good, but it fails in the rainy season | 15-30 days |
| Fire drying | Residual heat over the hearth, the warm end of an ondol floor, beside the stove | 2-5 days |
| Mixed | A few days in the sun, finished beside the fire. Realistic in the rainy season | 5-10 days |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Use only fully red peppers with no disease; if wet, dry them for a day, then spread in a single layer without overlap or string and hang them. Turn them once or twice a day and bring them in at night so they do not get dew. They are dry when they snap if bent and the seeds rattle when shaken.

> **There is one test for dryness. Does it snap when bent? If it bends, it is not dry, and it will mold within a month. Dry it another half day even if it seems a waste.**

| Storage | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Dried pepper | Moisture 12% or less, a dark, cool, dry place (an underground cellar, under a north-facing wall), earthenware jar, double-wrapped sack or airtight container, with charcoal, roasted and cooled rice or dry wood ash as desiccant. 1-2 years at room temperature. Open once a month to check for dampness and mold. If damp, spread it in the sun for half a day and pack it again. Block Indian meal moth with airtightness and dryness |
| Green pepper | Brined in 10-15% salt water for several months, soy sauce pickles for several months to 1 year, lactic fermentation in brine for several months (useful in a year when it is hard to ripen peppers to red) |

## 10. Cooking, Processing, and Precautions
| Use | Method |
|:---|:---|
| Red pepper powder | Remove the calyx from dried peppers and grind in a mortar or millstone. No fuel needed |
| Green pepper | Wash and eat raw, dipped in soybean paste or sauce (the only way to get its vitamin C) |
| Kimchi seasoning and soup | Mix red pepper powder with salt and vegetables. Whole peppers go into soup and are removed |
| Protecting stored grain | Put a few dried peppers or a piece into grain sacks, jars or seed containers (weevils, Indian meal moth) |
| Repellent | Soak dried peppers in water for a day, strain, dilute and spray on the field (soft-bodied insects, stink bugs). Red pepper powder scattered along paths drives off rats and moles |

> **Chili only slows spoilage; it does not restore spoiled food. If spoiled food is covered with hot seasoning, you cannot smell the spoilage and it becomes more dangerous.**

| Precaution | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Toxicity | No acute toxicity. The solanine-group compounds are at a level with no practical effect |
| Capsaicin | Dissolves in oil, not in water. Causes severe pain in the eyes and wounds; wipe off with milk or oil. After grinding red pepper powder or stripping seed, always wash the hands, and do not touch the face before that |
| Mold | Discard any with a damp, musty smell or black or blue spots. Cutting out the moldy part is not safe even when only a part is affected |

## 11. Annual Work Schedule
| Month | Work |
|:---|:---|
| Jan-Feb | Check seed and germination rate, gather hotbed materials and trim stakes, sow (central region mid to late month, south early to mid month), start hotbed heating |
| Mar-Apr | Manage seedlings and transplant into pots at 3-4 true leaves, lime the field, compost and basal fertilizer, raise the beds, harden off. Transplant in the south in late April |
| May | Transplant in the central region and set stakes at the same time, lay straw mulch |
| June | Remove side shoots below the first fork and pick off the first fruit, first top dressing, green pepper harvest from mid month |
| July | Check drainage in the rainy season, watch for anthracnose and remove diseased fruit at once, second top dressing, red pepper harvest from late month |
| Aug-Sep | Harvest red pepper every 7-10 days and dry it, third and fourth top dressings, mark seed fruit and let it ripen fully |
| Oct-Nov | Finish harvest and pickle the remaining green pepper, ripen the seed fruit, pull the plants and burn diseased residue, clean the seed and put the dried pepper away |
| December | Check the condition of dried pepper, decide next year's spot (confirm the nightshade rest) |

> **Five things not to forget: high beds and drainage, save on nitrogen, pick green pepper before anthracnose, dry until it snaps when bent, and rest nightshades for 5-7 years.**
