## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Winter squash (Cucurbita moschata), called maetdol-hobak in Korean after the millstone it resembles, is an annual vining plant of the gourd family, genus Cucurbita, also known as old pumpkin or Cheongdung pumpkin. It is native to Central and South America, and the young fruit eaten as young squash and the fully ripened fruit harvested as old pumpkin are different moments of the same crop. The overall grade in the evaluation table is A (48.4 points) and the supplementary grade is S (55.0 points). Fruit vegetables almost without exception stay at D for storability, but winter squash alone received A. Well-ripened and cured squash keeps three or four months at room temperature without a refrigerator, and up to six months under good conditions. Its calories per area are also B (4,000 kg per 1,000㎡, 1,520 kcal per ㎡, 0.58 times the self-sufficiency line), and its high beta-carotene supplies vitamin A in winter. Its continuous-cropping tolerance is SS (a continuous-cropping harmless group), and the seed keeps 6-10 years sealed at room temperature. The ripe fruit, young squash, leaves and shoots, flowers and seeds can all be eaten. Its weak points are that it is harvested only once a year, all at once in autumn (perenniality D), that it takes 120-150 days from sowing to full ripeness, that frost kills it (cold tolerance E), that the vines run 3-5 m, that protein is short (P:E 9.3%), and that it depends on bees for pollination. The arrangement that suits it best is a few plants in leftover spaces such as the edge of a field or under a wall, sending the vines outward.

> **The storability of winter squash comes not from storage technique but from the structure of the fruit itself. Unripe squash does not last however well it is kept, and the success of storage is decided not in the storehouse but by the point at which the harvest is timed.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Squash supplies calories and vitamin A but not protein, so the way to make up for this weakness is to plant beans next to it and not throw away the squash seeds. The grade for each item is in the Crop Evaluation Results document.

## 2. Plant Overview
| Species | Group | Representatives | Characteristics |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| C. moschata | Oriental | Winter squash, old pumpkin, young squash, butternut | Heat-tolerant and stores well. Stem end is a widely flared pentagon |
| C. maxima | Western | Kabocha, yakhobak | Likes cool climates and has high sugar. Stem end is round and corky |
| C. pepo | Pepo | Zucchini, spaghetti squash | Short vines and early, but does not store. Stem end is a five-angled star |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Different species hardly cross, while plants of the same species mix completely. The vines creep 3-5 m over the ground, and adventitious roots form at the nodes. The flowers are large, yellow and trumpet-shaped, with separate male and female flowers, opening early in the morning and closing by midday. Male flowers come first and far more of them, so do not worry that no female flowers appear early on. The fruit is flattened and round with deep furrows, green when young, turning yellow-brown with a white bloom as it ripens. One fruit holds 200-500 flat seeds 1.5-2 cm long.

| Stage | Timing | Key point |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Germination and seedling | From sowing to 4-5 true leaves (3-4 weeks) | Phosphorus |
| Vine elongation | May-July | Grows over 10 cm a day. Only male flowers open. Nitrogen |
| Flowering and fruit set | July-August | Female flowers open and are pollinated. Phosphorus, potassium, boron |
| Fruit swelling | August-September | Nearly full size 20-30 days after flowering. Potassium |
| Maturity | September-October | The rind hardens, starch accumulates and the color changes. Reduce watering |

> **The month after the fruit reaches full size decides storability. Size is finished 30 days after flowering, but the rind turning woody, the stem drying and the white bloom appearing take another 20-30 days after that. If harvested without waiting, the storability grade A disappears.**

| Nutrition per 100 g | Old pumpkin (raw) | Pumpkin seeds (dried) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Calories | 38 kcal (water about 88 g) | 550-570 kcal |
| Protein / fat | 0.88 g / 0.1 g | 25-30 g / 45-50 g |
| Others | Very high beta-carotene (orange), vitamin C 10-15 mg (much lost in cooking), potassium 200-400 mg | Very high magnesium; rich in iron, zinc and vitamin E |

## 3. Growing Environment and Varieties
| Condition | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Temperature | Germination 25-30℃ (no germination at 15℃ or below), growth 18-30℃, fruiting 22-28℃ (20-30℃). Above 35℃ pollen vigor falls and fertilization fails, and a single frost kills the leaves black |
| Light | 6 hours or more a day (minimum 4 hours; below that there are few female flowers and the fruit is small). Each fruit needs 20-25 leaves |
| Water | About 550 mm total evapotranspiration per crop. Soil moisture 60-70% of maximum |
| Soil | Well-drained sandy loam to loam, soil depth 30 cm or more, pH 5.5-6.8. Bed height 20-30 cm |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Squash likes a spot rich in manure, but with too much nitrogen only the vines grow and no female flowers appear. It is true that it thrives planted beside a compost pile, but if that spot has plenty of nitrogen and little phosphorus and potassium, only the leaves become lush.

| Variety | Characteristics |
|:---|:---|
| Traditional winter squash, Cheongdung pumpkin | Flattened with deep furrows and deep orange flesh. Best storability, and a fixed line, so seed can be saved and carried on |
| Improved winter squash | Higher yield and reinforced disease resistance. If an F1 hybrid, seed cannot be saved |
| Butternut | Long-necked, bell shape with a small seed cavity and a high flesh ratio. The same species, so it crosses with winter squash |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For winter food and seed saving, use the traditional line; for small land, butternut or the pepo group; for cool highlands, kabocha. Seed from F1 hybrids scatters the next year.

## 4. Field Preparation, Sowing, and Transplanting
| Item | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Soil preparation | Plow 25-30 cm deep, make beds 20-30 cm high, and dig drainage ditches along the field edge and at the ridge ends (where water pools, blight comes). A spot with 6 hours or more of sun where the vines can run 3-5 m (field edge, under a wall) |
| Fertilizing (per 1,000㎡) | Compost 1,500-2,000 kg, nitrogen 10-15 kg, phosphate 8-12 kg, potassium 10-15 kg, lime 100-150 kg (3-4 weeks before planting). Basal fertilizer 2 weeks before planting: all the compost + all the phosphate + 50% of the nitrogen and potassium |
| Top dressing | First at vine elongation (a month after transplanting), a little, mainly nitrogen; second right after fruit set, mainly potassium. Stop 20-30 days before harvest (late nitrogen leaves the rind less hardened and lowers storability) |
| Substitute resources | Well-rotted compost mixed half and half with soil in the planting pit, urine diluted 10-15 times (only at vine elongation), a handful of wood ash after fruit set, bone meal, leaves and grass at the bottom of the pit |

> **Nitrogen needs particular care in squash. If the leaves are deep green, the vines thick and the internodes long but there are no female flowers, it is almost certainly excess nitrogen.**

| Cropping | Sowing | Transplanting | Harvest |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Direct sowing in the open | Mid to late April | - | Early September to mid October |
| Seedlings then transplanting | Late March to mid April | Early to late May | Early September to mid October |

> **Winter squash takes 120-150 days from sowing to full ripeness. It must be planted in April to ripen in September; if planted after May, the rind does not harden before frost and only half-finished squash that cannot be stored is left.**

| Planting | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Seedlings | Soak overnight, wrap in wet cloth and sprout at 25-30℃. Depth 2-3 cm, day 22-28℃ and night 15℃ or higher, 3-4 weeks, transplant at 3-4 true leaves. Do not grow them large |
| Layout | Row width 3.5-4 m (for untended growing), plants 60-100 cm apart (30-40 cm on narrow land), 300-400 plants per 1,000㎡. Sow 2-3 seeds per hole and leave only the strongest at 2-3 true leaves. Pour water into the pit and plant after it drains |
| Handling the vines | Leave them sprawling (no work, and adventitious roots form at the nodes), turn them one way, train them up a fence or wall (fruit does not touch the soil and rots less), train them onto a roof or trellis (the fruit must be supported with rope), or let them climb trees |

## 5. Water Management and Cultivation Management
| Period | Water management |
|:---|:---|
| Sowing to germination | Keep from drying out (the seed is large and thick) |
| Seedling stage | Every 3-4 days |
| Vine elongation | Every 5-7 days |
| Flowering and fruit set, fruit swelling | Every 4-5 days, generously (if water is short the female flowers drop, and most of the fruit's weight is water taken in at this time) |
| Maturity (20-30 days before harvest) | Taper off gradually and finally stop (raises storability) |
| Rainy season | Stop irrigation and check the drainage ditches |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Leaves that droop at midday and recover in the evening are normal. If they do not recover in the evening and the female flowers wilt yellow and drop, it is water shortage; if the base of the vine splits lengthwise, oozes and wilts, it is vine-splitting wilt from excess water. Frequent rain in autumn lowers storability, so secure the best possible drainage of the beds and harvest after several clear days. When water is scarce, use straw or grass mulch, soil over the nodes, and planting in pits.

| Management | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Pinching | The mother vine bears female flowers late and rarely, so the fruit is taken from the child vines. At 5-6 true leaves cut the mother vine's growing tip and keep only the two strongest child vines, or cut at the 5th-6th node and train 3-4 child vines in all directions. If left alone, the fruit is small and the number uneven |
| Fruit set | Take fruit from the second or third female flower after the plant has gained vigor (around nodes 13-18 of the child vine), and pick off fruit that set too early and poorly shaped fruit while young. About 2 fruit per plant |
| Protecting the fruit | Lay rice straw, boards or tile pieces under the fruit to prevent rot. Turn it gently once or twice at maturity so the color is even. In a heat wave cover with leaves or straw. Do not move the vines often |

| Hand pollination | Method |
|:---|:---|
| When needed | Rain keeps bees from flying, few bees nearby, a heat wave above 35℃, rain in the early morning. If not pollinated, the female flower wilts yellow and drops |
| Method | From just after sunrise until 8 a.m., pick a fully open male flower with its stalk, remove the petals, and rub the stamens on the stigma of the female flower to coat it evenly. One male flower pollinates two or three female flowers |
| Checking | If the ovary swells after three or four days, it worked; if it wilts yellow, it failed |

> **If female flowers appear but fruit does not set, try hand pollination before adding more fertilizer. Adding more nitrogen makes only the vines lush and falls into a vicious circle in which female flowers decrease further.**

## 6. Pests, Diseases, and Nutrient Problems
| Disease | Symptoms and conditions | Response without chemicals |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Powdery mildew | White powder on the leaf surface. August-September when dry and humid weather alternate | Egg-yolk oil, diluted milk, baking soda solution, thinning lower leaves, removing diseased leaves early |
| Downy mildew | Angular yellow spots bounded by veins, mold on the underside. Humid, cool | Ensure ventilation, preventive Bordeaux mixture |
| Vine-splitting wilt, blight | The base splits lengthwise and oozes, and the plant wilts. Roots and stems rot. Excess water | No cure. Remove diseased plants at once, high beds and drainage |
| Mosaic virus | Leaves mottled and curled and fruit deformed (carried by aphids) | No cure. Control aphids, remove diseased plants |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The pests are aphids (soapy water, protect ladybugs), two-spotted spider mite (spray water hard on the underside of leaves), cucumber leaf beetle (net at the seedling stage, hand picking), cotton leaf roller (remove rolled leaves) and root-knot nematode (marigold intercropping, rotation). Egg-yolk oil is made by mixing 1 egg yolk into 100 mL of water, pouring in 60 mL of cooking oil, stirring hard to emulsify and adding it to 20 L of water (powdery mildew and mites; sprayed at midday it burns the leaves). Also used are 1 g of baking soda in 1 L of water, Bordeaux mixture (dissolve 1 kg of copper sulfate and 1 kg of quicklime separately, let cool, combine and make up to 100 L), soapy water (20 g of unscented soap in 1 L of water) and wood ash.

| Symptom | Cause and action |
|:---|:---|
| Yellowing from the lower leaves and thin vines | Nitrogen deficiency. During vine elongation, urine diluted 10-15 times (not after fruit set) |
| Leaf margins scorched and small fruit | Potassium deficiency. Wood ash |
| Slow growth and small, purplish leaves | Phosphorus deficiency. Bone meal, wood ash |
| Only vines vigorous, long internodes, no female flowers | Excess nitrogen. Cut off nitrogen, supplement potassium with wood ash, pinch |
| Fruit tip soft and rotting | Impaired calcium transport or excess water. Better drainage, even watering |
| Flesh pale and not sweet | Potassium shortage, low light or early harvest. Wood ash, thin leaves, harvest later |

## 7. Intercropping and Crop Rotation
| Compatibility | Crop |
|:---|:---|
| Good | The Three Sisters planting with maize and pole beans (maize is the support for the beans, pole beans fix nitrogen, squash covers the ground and suppresses weeds; allow generous spacing), legumes, marigold, perilla and shiso |
| Poor | Other gourd-family crops (cucumber, watermelon, oriental melon; they share pests and diseases), potato (shares the blight line), short leaf vegetables (covered by the vines) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Rotate winter squash in year 1, winter squash again (possible) or a legume in year 2, a grain or leaf vegetable in year 3, and resume winter squash in year 4. It is better to put in a different crop once every three years.

## 8. Harvest and Seed Saving
| Item | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Timing | 50-60 days after flowering, early September to mid October before the first frost. Young squash 7-10 days after flowering, when a fingernail goes into the rind |
| Signs of ripeness | The rind has changed completely from green to yellow-brown, the white bloom has risen evenly, the stem is dry and woody with lengthwise cracks (the surest sign), a fingernail leaves no mark, and tapping gives a dull sound |
| Cutting | On a clear, dry day cut with a knife or pruning shears leaving 3-5 cm of stem (do not twist), and carry them one at a time without wounds. With 3-5 cm of dried stem attached it keeps three or four to six months, with a short stem two or three months, and where the stem has fallen off and left a hole it rots from that spot within weeks |

> **If the stem is still green and soft, the squash is not ripe however yellow the rind. When the stem has dried woody and corky, it is in a state that can be stored.**

| Seed saving | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Crossing | C. moschata (winter squash, young squash, butternut) cross completely among themselves, and maxima and pepo hardly cross. Cucumber, oriental melon, watermelon and bottle gourd are of other genera and do not cross. Species are told apart by the shape of the stem end |
| Prevention | Plant only one line of one species. Isolation of 500 m or more is hard, so use the long seed life and let only one line flower in a year. The surest way is to bag the male and female flowers on the afternoon before flowering, hand-pollinate from just after sunrise to 8 a.m. the next morning, rebag the female flower and remove the bag two days later |
| Number of plants | To avoid inbreeding depression, take one or two fruit each from at least five, preferably ten or more plants |
| Procedure | In August choose well-shaped, disease-free fruit and mark them. Ripen fully, longer than squash for eating, and after-ripen 2-4 weeks after harvest. Scrape out the seed, rub it in water to remove the pulp and discard what floats. Spread in a single layer on cloth or a mat and dry 1-2 weeks in shade (not directly in the sun) |
| Lifespan | 6-10 years sealed at room temperature. Once every few years place 20 seeds on wet cloth and check germination |

## 9. Storage
| Step | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Curing | Right after harvest, 25-30℃ and about 80% humidity for 10-14 days. Spread them in a single layer without touching on a sunny porch, under the eaves, at the far end of an ondol room, or in a shed without plastic |
| Main storage | 10-15℃ (12-14℃ is best), humidity 60-70%, a cool, dry, ventilated place (shed, attic, under the porch, north-facing room). Keep in a single layer on shelves or straw without touching, and every 2-3 weeks pick out any that have gone soft. Do not use a refrigerator (chilling injury at 5℃ or below) |

> **Squash that has not been cured does not last past three or four months, while cured squash keeps six months. This difference of just over ten days decides the food of late winter. Starch turns to sugar, so it is sweetest one to two months after harvest.**

| Method | Details | Period |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Whole storage | After curing, 10-15℃ | 4-6 months |
| Dried strips (hobakgoji) | Peel, slice thin and dry in the sun (for wounded or unripe squash) | 6 months to 1 year |
| Seeds | Wash, dry and seal | Many years |

## 10. Cooking and Precautions
| Part | Cooking |
|:---|:---|
| Ripe fruit | Steamed, porridge, soup, mash. Needs 30 minutes of heating and is not suited to eating raw |
| Young squash | Stir-fried, stew, pancake |
| Leaves and shoots | Steamed for wraps, blanched as greens. Snapping the leaf stalk and peeling the skin toward the stem strips off the tough fibers with it. Placed on top of rice and steamed with it, no extra fuel is needed. Pick only three or four leaves at a time from one plant |
| Flowers | Male flowers in pancakes or soup (picking female flowers loses the fruit) |
| Seeds | Dry and roast over low heat and eat as they are, or grind into porridge. Roasted, ground and pressed, 100 g of seed gives 30-40 g of oil, and the oil cake is eaten or used as fertilizer. One squash gives 50-100 g of dried seed |
| Rind | Edible if boiled for a long time, otherwise livestock feed |

| Precaution | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Cucurbitacin | The bitter defense compound of the gourd family, almost gone from cultivated varieties, but it can appear from seed crossed with ornamental pumpkins, from squash gone wild, and under extreme drought or heat. If one bite gives a bitterness that stings the tongue, spit it out at once and discard the whole squash. Cooking does not remove it, and vomiting and diarrhea follow. The same applies to leaves and shoots |
| Mold | If it turns soft and smells in storage, discard it |

## 11. Annual Work Schedule
| Month | Work |
|:---|:---|
| Jan-Feb | Check the stored squash, pick out soft ones and keep eating, check seed, decide where to plant, turn compost |
| Mar-Apr | Start seedlings in late March and lime the field, dig pits and add compost, direct sow in mid April |
| May | Transplant early to late month, pinch the mother vine at 5-6 true leaves |
| June | Train the vines and tidy child vines, first top dressing, start picking leaves to eat |
| July | When female flowers open, hand-pollinate in the early morning, pick off the fruit of the first female flower |
| August | Fruit set management and laying supports under fruit, second top dressing (potassium), watch for powdery mildew, mark seed fruit |
| September | Reduce water, harvest ripe ones from early month and start curing |
| October | Harvest everything before the first frost, finish curing and move into main storage |
| Nov-Dec | Clear the vines and leaves, chop them and plow them in, take seed from seed fruit and clean it, check the storehouse temperature so nothing freezes |

> **Five things not to forget: sow in April, save on nitrogen, hand-pollinate when there are no bees, harvest and cure after waiting for the stem to dry, and discard it if it tastes bitter.**
