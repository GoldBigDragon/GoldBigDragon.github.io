## 2. What Kind of Plant Is This?

### 2-1. Taxonomy and Origin

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Eggplant is a perennial herb in the genus Solanum, family Solanaceae, but in temperate regions it cannot survive winter and is grown as an annual. Its scientific name is Solanum melongena. The same genus includes potato (Solanum tuberosum), and the same family includes chili pepper (Capsicum), tomato (Solanum lycopersicum), and tobacco. In practice, this lineage means one thing above all: these plants share their diseases wholesale.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Bacterial wilt, late blight, Verticillium wilt, tobacco mosaic virus, aphids, thrips, and the tobacco cutworm all move freely among Solanaceae crops without discriminating. If chili peppers are planted the following year where eggplant once stood, pathogens left in the soil catch the peppers. The same happens if eggplant is planted where potatoes were dug up. Continuous-cropping tolerance D in the evaluation table compresses this fact into a single grade, and all of Section 11's rotation design comes from it.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Eggplant originates from the subtropical belt spanning eastern India to Myanmar. Its wild ancestor was a thorny, small-fruited shrubby plant, and long selection by people removed the thorns and enlarged the fruit. This origin decides the growing conditions. First, being a heat-loving plant, it is helpless against frost: growth stops below 10°C, and a single frost blackens and kills an entire field. Second, being a plant from a region with a distinct rainy season, it drinks a great deal of water — total evapotranspiration reaches 750mm, and flowers and young fruit drop as soon as it dries out. Third, traces of its perennial nature remain, so once it is established it keeps flowering and fruiting continuously until frost. This third trait is why the renewal pruning in Section 8 works.

### 2-2. Appearance

| Organ | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| Leaves | Broadly ovate with shallow serrations on the margin. The surface is covered in fine hairs, making it rough to the touch. In many varieties the veins and petiole are tinged purple |
| Stem | Grows upright and becomes woody and firm toward the base. It forks in two at a certain node, and this first branch point is called the crotch (bangadari). Height 60-120cm |
| Roots | Has a taproot, but in practice it is shallow-rooted, with fine roots concentrated within 30cm of the surface. This is why it is weak against drought |
| Flowers | Pale purple or white, star-shaped, and face downward. Each flower has both pistil and stamens, and self-pollination predominates |
| Fruit | A berry. Shapes range from long and cylindrical to round depending on the variety; the skin is typically dark purple, with purple, white, and green types also occurring |
| Seeds | Flat, disc-shaped, 2-3mm across, pale brown, and scattered through the flesh inside the fruit |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Understanding the crotch (bangadari) is half of growing eggplant. Once the stem has grown to a certain node, it opens its first flower at the tip, and the stem forks in two at that point. Each of the two branches grows on, flowers, and forks again. Because this structure keeps repeating, an eggplant plant keeps doubling its branches endlessly as it grows. Left unmanaged, the stems multiply into dozens of branches, and once that happens, the nutrients reaching each stem are spread too thin, the fruit turns small, and seeds form early inside it. Training in Section 8 is the work of managing this structure.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Shallow roots produce two consequences. One is vulnerability to drought: the plant reacts as soon as the top 10cm of soil dries out. The other is that, because the roots are shallow, a hoe used for weeding or hilling can easily cut them. The trick is to pull weeds by hand around the base of the plant and to scrape the soil only along the middle of the row. That said, the renewal pruning in Section 8 deliberately cuts these roots to induce new ones — but that is a matter of different timing and purpose.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The structure of the flower is also worth knowing. Eggplant flowers face downward, and a mix of long-styled flowers, where the pistil extends beyond the stamens, and short-styled flowers, where the pistil is short, appear together. Long-styled flowers fertilize well and become good fruit, while short-styled flowers often fail to fertilize and drop. When nutrition is short or heat stress is present, the proportion of short-styled flowers rises. If a plant has plenty of flowers but no fruit is setting, the cause is usually a shortage of water or fertilizer, or the heat of high summer.

### 2-3. Growth Stages

| Stage | Timing | Key Physiology | Key Nutrients |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seedling stage | Sowing to transplanting (70-80 days) | Grows to 5-7 true leaves. The best time to transplant is around when the first flower bud forms | Nitrogen, phosphorus |
| Transplanting and establishment | 1-2 weeks after transplanting | New roots emerge and take hold in the soil. It is normal for the plant to appear not to be growing during this period | Phosphorus |
| Vegetative growth | 2-5 weeks after transplanting | The stem thickens, leaves spread, and the crotch (bangadari) forms | Nitrogen |
| Flowering and fruit-set | 40-50 days after transplanting | The first flower opens at the crotch. Flowers then follow on every new branch | Phosphorus, boron |
| Fruit enlargement and harvest | From 10-20 days after flowering until frost | Fruit enlarges rapidly. Picking it brings on the next flower right away | Balance of potassium and nitrogen |
| Decline and renewal | Late July-early August | Vigor drops from heat and continuous fruiting. Renewal pruning at this point carries the harvest into September-October | Nitrogen, potassium |

> **Once eggplant's reproductive growth begins, it does not stop until frost. The more fruit is picked, the sooner the next flower comes; if fruit is left on the plant, that plant pours its nutrients into that fruit and makes no new flowers.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This trait explains the whole logic of the harvesting technique in Section 12. From the plant's point of view, the purpose of growing fruit is to leave seed, and once the seed has fully ripened, that purpose is achieved and there is no more reason to keep flowering. When a person keeps picking the fruit, the plant remains in a state where its purpose is not achieved and keeps flowering. Perenniality B in the evaluation table comes from this principle, and in practice a single plant produces 20-40 fruit before frost.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The fact that the seedling stage takes 70-80 days is worth stressing again. This length comes from how slowly eggplant grows at the seedling stage: after the cotyledons emerge, it takes a week for one more true leaf to appear. At low temperatures this pace slows further, nearly stopping at 15°C. So a night temperature of at least 15°C, and preferably above 20°C, must be maintained throughout the seedling stage, and this is the hardest part of the whole process in a disaster situation.

### 2-4. Nutrition per 100g

| Nutrient | Amount | Notes |
|---|:---:|---|
| Calories | 19-25kcal | 19kcal by the Korean Food Composition Table's raw-eggplant figure. Low even among vegetables |
| Water | 92g | The direct cause of the low calorie count |
| Protein | 1.13g | 23.8% of calories. Small in absolute amount but high in density |
| Carbohydrates | 5.7-6.0g | A large share is dietary fiber |
| Fat | 0.1-0.2g | Almost none |
| Dietary fiber | 3.0g | High among vegetables |
| Potassium | 230mg | The most notable mineral in eggplant |
| Calcium | 15-20mg | An ordinary level |
| Iron | 0.3-0.4mg | Low |
| Magnesium | 14mg | An ordinary level |
| Vitamin C | 2.2mg | Very low. Eggplant is not a source of vitamin C |
| Vitamin B6 | 0.1mg | An ordinary level |
| Folate | 22 µg | An ordinary level |
| Nasunin (anthocyanin) | Concentrated in the skin | Causes the deep purple color; an antioxidant pigment |
| Chlorogenic acid | Present in the flesh | The substance responsible for browning on a cut surface |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Two things should be read from this table. First, vitamin C is essentially absent at 2.2mg per 100g. Set beside kale's 90mg or chili pepper's 44-78mg, it is an order of magnitude different — eggplant should not be relied on to prevent winter scurvy. Second, eggplant's nutritional character lies not in any particular vitamin but in potassium, dietary fiber, and the pigment in its skin.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Nasunin is the anthocyanin-class pigment that makes the skin purple. From the plant's standpoint it is a sunshade protecting the fruit's interior from strong ultraviolet light; from a human standpoint it is an antioxidant. The practically important fact is that this compound exists only in the skin. Peeling the eggplant removes half of its nutritional character. Eating it with the skin on is the rule, and a tough-skinned, overripe fruit has simply missed its harvest window to begin with.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Chlorogenic acid is why a cut eggplant browns quickly. It is an enzymatic reaction with oxygen in the air that changes the color — the fruit has not spoiled. Still, it looks unappealing and tastes astringent, so slicing and immediately soaking it in water or salted water slows the reaction. In Section 15's drying process, how this browning is handled makes the difference in quality.

## 3. Growing Conditions

### 3-1. Temperature

| Category | Limit | Optimum | Notes |
|---|:---:|:---:|---|
| Germination temperature | 15-35°C | 28-30°C | Below 20°C, germination is very slow and uneven |
| Seedling temperature (day) | 20-32°C | 22-30°C | At low temperatures the rate of true-leaf emergence drops by half |
| Seedling temperature (night) | 15°C or above | 18-20°C | Frequent drops below 15°C harm flower bud formation |
| Growing temperature | 17-35°C | 22-30°C | Growth slows noticeably below 17°C |
| Fruit-setting temperature | 20-30°C | 23-28°C | Temperature range C in the evaluation table comes from this 10°C span |
| Heat limit | Above 35°C | - | Pollen loses vigor and flowers drop |
| Cold limit | Below 10°C | - | Growth stops, and if it continues the plant dies |
| Lethal | Below 0°C | - | A single frost blackens and kills the whole plant |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The fact that the fruit-setting range spans only 10°C, from 20-30°C, is what sets eggplant's whole schedule. In South Korea's climate, this condition is met from mid-June to mid-September, with May and October as margins on either side. Planting before the last frost ends in death, and the first frost ends the season. This is why transplanting is timed for after the last frost has passed.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The heat of high summer also causes problems. Above 35°C, pollen loses its power, fertilization fails, and unfertilized flowers drop. This is why a stretch appears from late July to early August where flowers open but no fruit sets, and the plant's vigor also drops during the same period. This is why renewal pruning is done at this point: since fruit is not setting anyway, that stretch of time is put to use for recovery instead.

### 3-2. Light

| Category | Standard | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sunlight needed | 6+ hours a day | Fruit-set stabilizes and fruit color deepens |
| Minimum sunlight | 4 hours a day | The plant grows but becomes leggy and fruit-set worsens |
| At 40% shade | Relative yield 102% | A common value for fruit vegetables; the basis for the S grade in the evaluation table |
| Strong-light stress | Direct midsummer sun + 35°C | Sunscald appears on the fruit skin, leaving white patches |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The fact that eggplant maintains its yield even at 40% shade is useful for laying out a plot in a disaster situation. A spot with sun all day should go to grain, beans, and potatoes first, while eggplant does its part even in a spot with sun for only half the day. In fact, in high summer it is even better to block the western afternoon sun, since that reduces sunscald on the fruit skin. Even so, below 4 hours a day the stem only grows long and flowers drop, so being tolerant of shade should not be read as being fine in full shade.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fruit color is also tied to light. The purple of the skin is anthocyanin, and anthocyanin forms only with light. A fruit hidden behind leaves comes out pale or blotchy. This does not affect edibility, but if many fruits show poor color, it is a sign the foliage has grown too dense and it is time to clear some lower leaves.

### 3-3. Water and Soil

| Item | Standard | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Total evapotranspiration | About 750mm per season | High even among fruit vegetables; the basis for water use C in the evaluation table |
| Soil moisture | 70-80% of maximum water-holding capacity | Drying causes flower and fruit drop; waterlogging causes root rot |
| Soil texture | Organic-rich loam, sandy loam | Needs both water retention and drainage |
| Soil depth | 30cm or more | Fine roots concentrate near the surface, so the topsoil layer must be thick |
| pH | 6.0-7.0 | Around 6.5 is best |
| pH lower limit | 5.5 | Below this, phosphorus and calcium uptake worsens |
| Salt tolerance | Weak | Growth is suppressed in fields where salts have accumulated |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Eggplant needs a lot of water while at the same time being unable to tolerate standing water. This seemingly contradictory demand comes from its shallow roots. Because the roots are concentrated in the topsoil, water runs short the moment the topsoil dries, and for the same reason, water pooling in the topsoil suffocates the roots. The solution is to raise the bed. Building the ridge up 20cm or more lets water drain below the root layer while moisture still remains within the ridge itself. In South Korea's climate, with its long monsoon, a raised bed is not optional — it is a precondition.

> **The first sign eggplant sends under drought is not the leaves but the flowers. Before the leaves wilt, the flowers and pea-sized young fruit have already dropped. By the time the leaves droop, that week's harvest is already gone.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;One more thing is worth adding about pH. Eggplant is not an especially acid-sensitive crop, but once pH drops below 5.5, phosphorus binds with iron or aluminum into a form the plant cannot use. Since fruit-setting absolutely requires phosphorus, this fixation shows up directly as poor fruit-set. Where lime cannot be obtained, wood ash and ground, burned shells or eggshell powder serve as substitutes, and this continues in Section 5.
