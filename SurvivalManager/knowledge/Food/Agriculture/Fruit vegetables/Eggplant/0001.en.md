## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Eggplant (Solanum melongena) is an annual fruit vegetable of the nightshade family, genus Solanum. Its origin is the subtropics from eastern India to Myanmar, so it loves heat, is weak to cold, drinks a great deal of water, and a single frost ends the whole field. The overall grade in the evaluation table is E (31.7 points). It has 19 kcal per 100 g, and even a harvest of 3,500 kg from 1,000㎡ gives calories per area of only 0.25 times the self-sufficiency line; to fill 2,100 kcal a day on eggplant alone, you would have to eat 11 kg. Its supplementary grade, however, is A (50.2 points). Low calories pushed its protein density up to S. Its yield stability is also S (year-to-year variation 5.7%), so the hot, humid midsummer, when Chinese cabbage-family leaf vegetables melt away, is eggplant's main stage. Its weak points are temperature (cold tolerance E, 70-80 days of seedlings), continuous-cropping injury (5-7 years of rest), and storability (92% of the fruit is water). Eggplant is not the center of the field; it is a crop of which five or six plants stand in a corner of the field, picked and eaten in summer, with the surplus sliced and dried to carry into winter.

## 2. Plant Overview
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A perennial of the nightshade family (genus Solanum), but in temperate zones it cannot survive the winter and is grown as an annual. It shares its pests and diseases with tomato, chili and potato. The stem stands upright and is 60-120 cm tall, and the first branching point, where it splits in two at a certain node, is called the first fork. The fine roots crowd within 30 cm of the surface, making it shallow-rooted and weak to drought. The flowers are light purple and star-shaped, and mainly self-pollinating. The fruit is a berry, with black-purple skin as the standard.

| Stage | Timing | Key point |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Seedling | 70-80 days from sowing to transplanting | 5-7 true leaves; transplant when the first flower bud appears |
| Establishment and vegetative growth | 1-5 weeks after transplanting | During establishment it is normal for it to seem not to grow. Afterwards the stem thickens and the first fork forms |
| Flowering and fruit set | 40-50 days after transplanting | The first flower opens at the first fork |
| Swelling and harvest | From 10-20 days after flowering until frost | Once fruit is picked, the next flower follows at once |
| Decline and renewal | Late July to early August | Vigor falls with heat and continuous fruiting. Renewal pruning carries the harvest through September-October |

> **Eggplant must be picked to keep setting fruit. If fruit is left on, the plant pours all its nutrients into making seed and makes no new flowers.**

| Nutrition per 100 g | Content |
|:---|:---|
| Calories | 19 kcal (water 92 g) |
| Protein | 1.13 g (23.8% of calories) |
| Carbohydrates / dietary fiber | 5.7-6.0 g / 3.0 g |
| Minerals and vitamins | Potassium 230 mg, calcium 15-20 mg, iron 0.3-0.4 mg, magnesium 14 mg, vitamin C 2.2 mg (not a source), folate 22 µg |
| Compounds | Nasunin (anthocyanin in the skin), chlorogenic acid (the cause of browning on cut surfaces) |

## 3. Growing Environment and Varieties
| Condition | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Temperature | Germination 28-30℃ (very slow at 20℃ or below), seedlings day 22-30℃ and night 15℃ or higher, growth 22-30℃, fruiting 20-30℃. Growth stops at 10℃ or below, and a single frost kills the whole plant |
| Light | 6 hours or more a day (minimum 4 hours). Midsummer direct sun combined with 35℃ causes sunscald on the fruit |
| Water | About 750 mm total evapotranspiration per crop. Soil moisture 70-80% of maximum. Flowers and fruit drop if dry, roots rot if soggy |
| Soil | Loam to sandy loam rich in organic matter, soil depth 30 cm or more, pH 6.0-7.0 (around 6.5 is best; below 5.5 phosphorus and calcium uptake are poor). Weak to salts |

> **Under drought, the first signal eggplant sends is not in the leaves but in the flowers. Before the leaves wilt, flowers and pea-sized young fruit have already dropped. If you water after the leaves have gone limp, that week's harvest is already gone.**

| Variety | Characteristics |
|:---|:---|
| Shinheuksanho, Heukjinju | Long-berried early types. Set fruit well and early yield is stable. Both open field and rain shelter |
| Cheonhadaejang | Slender long berry (36-41 cm), mid-season. Thin, deeply glossy skin. Suited to hot, dry periods |
| Soebbulgaji, Paengpaengi eggplant | Thick, short berries. Thick skin and relatively cold-resistant; Paengpaengi keeps long after harvest |
| Traditional Joseon landrace | Short, plump berries. Low yield, but a fixed line, so seed can be saved and replanted, and the aroma is rich |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For early harvest use Shinheuksanho and Heukjinju, for cool regions Soebbulgaji and Paengpaengi eggplant, and for drying use long, thin-skinned lines. Seed saving grade S only means that eggplant is self-pollinating; seed taken from F1 hybrids scatters the next year, so if you look years ahead, secure one fixed line such as the traditional Joseon landrace.

## 4. Field Preparation, Sowing, and Transplanting
| Item | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Soil preparation | Plow 25-30 cm deep, make beds 20 cm or higher, and dig drainage ditches along the field edge and at the ridge ends (where water pools, blight explodes). Lime 2-3 weeks before transplanting; applied at the same time as compost, nitrogen is lost |
| Fertilizing (per 1,000㎡) | Well-rotted compost 1,500-2,000 kg, nitrogen 19.0 kg (10.2 kg basal + 8.8 kg top dressing), phosphate 11.2 kg (all basal), potassium 15.0 kg (9.0 kg basal + 6.0 kg top dressing) |
| Top dressing | Start about 30 days after transplanting and repeat every 20-25 days, four times in all (the third together with renewal pruning) |
| Substitute resources | Well-rotted compost 2-3 kg per ㎡, urine diluted 10-15 times (poured into the furrows and stopped 2 weeks before harvest), wood ash a handful per ㎡, bone meal, fermented oil cake diluted 10 times, grass mulch |

> **Urine is rich in nitrogen but short of phosphorus. If you keep giving only urine, only the leaves grow lush and fruit does not set. Wood ash and bone meal must supply potassium and phosphorus together to keep the balance.**

| Work | Timing |
|:---|:---|
| Sowing (start of seedlings) | Central region mid to late February, south early to mid February |
| Transplanting | Central region early to mid May (after the late frosts are completely over), south late April |
| First harvest | 50-70 days after transplanting, late June. Harvest ends at first frost, mid to late October |

| Seedlings and transplanting | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Seedlings | Germinates in 3-5 days at 25-30℃. Harden off from 1 week before transplanting by putting plants outside during the day. Without heating: a manure-heated hotbed (horse manure, rice straw and rice hulls trodden in, 20-30℃ for 3-6 weeks), a south-facing window, heat storage with black water barrels (raises night temperature by 3-5℃) |
| When seedlings are difficult | Sow in early April and transplant in mid June. The first harvest slips to August and the harvest period shrinks to a little over two months, but farming does not vanish altogether |
| Transplanting | Shallow, so that the seedling's soil is just visible (planted deep, the stem rots). Single-row planting: ridges 80-90 cm, plants 45-50 cm apart (2.2-2.8 plants per ㎡); double-row planting: ridges 150 cm with 60 cm or more between rows. Plant on a cloudy afternoon and set stakes at the same time as transplanting |

## 5. Water Management and Cultivation Management
| Period | Water management |
|:---|:---|
| Right after transplanting to establishment | Water the holes thoroughly and keep from drying out for 3-4 days |
| After establishment to before flowering | Every 4-5 days when the topsoil dries. Keeping it slightly dry makes the roots go deep |
| Flowering and fruit set, swelling and harvest | Every 2-3 days, generously (most of the fruit's weight is water taken in at this time) |
| Rainy season | Stop irrigation and check the drainage ditches |
| Right after renewal pruning | Generously |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When water is short, flowers and young fruit drop first and the fruit becomes thin and tough; if leaves that drooped by day do not recover in the evening, that week's harvest is lost. When water is scarce, use straw or grass mulch (stretches the watering interval 1.5-2 times), drip watering with a bottle poked with holes at the bottom, evening watering, furrow watering, and reuse of rinse water and rice-washing water. Do not spray the leaves; always water the soil.

| Management | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Training and pruning | Snap off by hand every side shoot that comes out below the first fork. Grow three stems: the two stems that split at the first fork plus the single strongest side shoot just below it, and thin only the stems that cross inward. When harvest begins, remove the leaves within 30 cm of the ground one after another |
| Working method | On a clear morning, by hand where possible. If a knife is used, heat it in fire or dip it in boiled water between plants (bacterial wilt and viruses travel on the blade) |
| Leaf thinning | About three leaves are needed to grow one fruit. Do not remove more than 20% of all the leaves at one time |
| Training | One-line training (simple but the stem droops), two-line training (strings in two tiers on stakes at both sides), X-shaped training (1.8 m stakes crossed) |
| Renewal pruning | When vigor flags in late July to early August, cut each main stem leaving only two or three nodes from the bottom, and thrust a spade 20 cm deep 25-30 cm from the plant to cut the old roots (root pruning). Add compost and diluted urine and water generously; 2-3 weeks later new shoots come out and fruit sets again from mid September |

> **The saying that autumn eggplant is delicious is a physiological fact. As temperatures fall, fruit grows slowly, and slowly grown fruit has tight cells, so the flesh is soft and has few seeds. Renewal pruning is the preparation for getting that autumn eggplant.**

## 6. Pests, Diseases, and Nutrient Problems
| Disease | Symptoms and conditions | Response without chemicals |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Bacterial wilt | Wilts suddenly at midday and revives in the morning, repeatedly for days, then dies. A cut stem oozes white bacterial fluid. Hot and humid, soil-borne | No cure. Pull at once with the roots and burn, and remove the soil at that spot as well. Rotate for 5-7 years |
| Phytophthora blight | The stem base touching the ground narrows to dark brown and the plant wilts suddenly. Excess water, rainy season | High beds and drainage ditches are the only remedy. Preventive Bordeaux mixture |
| Verticillium wilt, virus disease | One half of a leaf yellows and wilts. Leaves mottled and fruit deformed (carried by aphids and thrips) | No cure. Remove diseased plants, long rotation, control the vectors |
| Gray mold, leaf mold, powdery mildew | Gray mold, gray-brown mold on the leaf underside, white powder | Shake off spent flowers, ensure ventilation, thin lower leaves, no excess nitrogen, milk (1 part to 1-2 parts water) or baking soda solution |

> **If a plant with bacterial wilt is left in the field or put on the compost pile, the bacteria spread through the whole field. Pull it and burn it, or bury it deep far away, and always wash the hands and tools that pulled it.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The pests are aphids (soapy water, protect ladybugs), two-spotted spider mite (spray water hard on the underside of leaves), thrips (sticky boards, straw mulch), tobacco budworm (pick off and destroy fruit with holes and catch the larvae by hand) and stink bugs (hand picking in the early morning). Control materials are Bordeaux mixture (dissolve 1 kg of copper sulfate and 1 kg of quicklime separately, let cool, combine and make up to 100 L; preventive), soapy water (20 g of unscented soap in 1 L of water), wood ash, wood vinegar diluted 300-500 times, and tobacco leaf or chili infusion. Burn diseased residue and do not plant diseased seedlings.

| Symptom | Cause and action |
|:---|:---|
| Yellowing from the lower leaves and small plants | Nitrogen deficiency. Urine diluted 10-15 times, rotted compost |
| Leaf margins scorched and small fruit | Potassium deficiency. Wood ash |
| Underside of leaves and stem purplish | Phosphorus deficiency (in cold periods the cold soil can also block uptake). Bone meal, wood ash |
| Many flowers but they drop | Boron deficiency or heat stress. Diversify compost materials, shade |
| Yellow between the veins, veins stay green | Magnesium deficiency. Dolomite powder |
| New leaves small and deformed, growing point dies | Looks like calcium deficiency but is usually lack of water. Check water first, then burned shell powder |
| Only leaves lush and deep green, few flowers | Excess nitrogen (the most common problem in eggplant). Cut off nitrogen and supplement potassium with wood ash |

## 7. Intercropping and Crop Rotation
| Compatibility | Crop |
|:---|:---|
| Good | Legumes (kidney bean, cowpea, pea), marigold (suppresses root-knot nematodes), basil and perilla (the strong scent confuses aphids and thrips), spring onion and garlic |
| Poor | Tomato, chili and potato (share pests and diseases completely; avoid planting even next door), pumpkin (vines block ventilation) |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Rotate eggplant in year 1, a legume in year 2, a leaf vegetable or the onion genus in year 3, a grain or root vegetable in year 4 and a legume or green manure in year 5, and resume eggplant from year 6. On small land, divide the field into six plots and move one plot each year, or strip 30 cm of soil from a badly diseased spot and fill it with fresh soil.

> **If eggplant is planted on the same spot for 3 years or more, it is common for the first two years to be fine and then half to wilt and die suddenly in the third. Rotation is something done not after the disease appears but before it appears.**

## 8. Harvest and Seed Saving
| Item | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Timing | 10-20 days after flowering (shorter when hot). Berry types are about 15-20 cm long, but judge by condition, not length. When the skin is glossy and firm to the press; once the gloss is gone it is already late |
| Method | Pick the first fruit early at egg size so the plant's body grows first. 2-3 times a week, in the early morning, cut above the calyx with scissors or a knife (twisting tears the stem) and leave 3-5 mm of calyx |
| After harvest | Wash just before eating. 10-12℃ is best, and at 5℃ or below chilling injury dents the skin. Keep only what will be eaten that day and slice and dry the rest at once |

| Seed saving | Standard |
|:---|:---|
| Isolation and plant number | Keep 30-50 m from other eggplant varieties. At least 3-5 seed plants (genetic breadth). One fruit holds hundreds to over 1,000 seeds |
| Fruit | In late July to August choose fruit set on the lower part of the plant when it was vigorous and mark it with string. Leave until the skin turns yellow-brown and loses its gloss (60-70 days after flowering) and after-ripen 1-2 weeks in cool shade. Fruit set just before frost does not ripen its seed |
| Separating and drying | Split the fruit, soak in water and rub out the seed, and use only the seed that sinks. Spread on cloth and dry in shade for 1-2 weeks (not directly in the sun). Done when it snaps if broken |
| Storage | Seal in a glass jar or metal tin with a desiccant (roasted and cooled rice, charcoal, dry wood ash, burned and cooled salt) in a cool, dark place. 3-4 years sealed at room temperature. Each year place 20 seeds on wet cloth and check germination after a week |
| Disinfection | 50-55℃ water for 10-15 minutes then straight into cold water, discard what floats, coat with wood ash |

## 9. Storage and Drying
| Method | Details | Period |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Fresh | Unwashed, wrapped in newspaper, in shade at 10-15℃ | 5-7 days |
| Drying (dried eggplant slices) | Slice thin and dry completely in sun and wind. Volume and weight fall to a tenth, the most practical | 6 months to 1 year |
| Salting | Salt at 10-15%. Soak in water before eating | Several months |
| Pickles | Packed in soy sauce, soybean paste or chili paste | Several months to 1 year |
| Lactic fermentation | Brine at 2-3% salt with the fruit kept under the liquid. Tends to go soft, so tricky | Several weeks to several months |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For dried slices, wash flawless fruit, wipe off the water completely, and cut into rounds 5-7 mm thick or split lengthwise into four. Spread in a single layer without overlap on a basket or mat, dry 2-4 days in a sunny, breezy place, and bring in at night to avoid dew. Dry until it snaps when broken by hand (if even slightly soft, it molds), and keep in an airtight container with a desiccant in a dark, cool place. Open once a month to check, and if damp, dry it in the sun again for half a day. In the rainy season or on days without sun, dry it over a fire.

> **Success in drying is decided on the first day. If you start spreading on a cloudy, humid day, black mold appears on the third day and you must throw it all away. Choose a clear, windy day to start.**

## 10. Cooking and Precautions
| Cooking | Fuel and tips |
|:---|:---|
| Steaming | Within 10 minutes. It floats, so press it down with a plate for even cooking. Uses no oil, the most economical |
| Stir-frying | Soaks up oil and tastes better, but uses a lot of oil |
| Grilling | Grill whole in embers and peel the skin. Uses leftover fire |
| Soups and stews | Combined with other cooking |
| Dried eggplant | Soaking for 20-30 minutes brings it back to nearly half its original volume. Stir-fry or season. Do not peel |
| Raw | Not recommended (astringent and alkaloids remain) |

> **If oil is scarce, steaming is the right answer for eggplant. Tear the steamed eggplant and dress it with soy sauce or soybean paste, garlic and sesame salt: 10 minutes of fuel and a few drops of oil.**

| Precaution | Details |
|:---|:---|
| Solanine and solamargine | Defense compounds of the nightshade family. Eating much causes stomach irritation and nausea. Do not eat strongly bitter fruit (common in fruit that is too young, fruit gone yellow after missing the harvest, and fruit grown under severe water shortage). Cooking reduces some |
| Leaves and stems | Alkaloid content is far higher, so do not eat them; return them to compost |
| Chlorogenic acid | Not toxic; the cause of astringency and browning. Slice and soak in water or light salt water |
| Others | Some people may get itching or hives, so start with a small amount |

## 11. Annual Work Schedule
| Month | Work |
|:---|:---|
| Jan-Feb | Check seed and germination rate, gather hotbed materials, sow (central region mid to late month, south early to mid month) and start hotbed heating |
| April | Basal fertilizer and compost, make beds, harden off. Transplant in the south in late month |
| May | Transplant in the central region in early to mid month and set stakes, remove side shoots below the first fork |
| June | Pick the first fruit while young, first top dressing, straw mulch, first harvest from late month |
| July | Harvest 2-3 times a week, second top dressing, check drainage in the rainy season, prepare for renewal pruning from late month |
| August | Renewal pruning and root pruning in early month, third top dressing, start drying |
| September | Harvest autumn eggplant from the new shoots, fourth top dressing, seed fruit ripens fully |
| Oct-Nov | Finish harvest and slice and dry all remaining fruit, after-ripen seed fruit, pull the plants and burn diseased ones and compost healthy stems, clean the seed |
| December | Check next year's rotation plan and the condition of the dried eggplant |

> **Five things not to forget: transplant after the late frosts, steady water, eggplant must be picked to keep setting fruit, drying decided on the first day, and rest nightshades for 5-7 years.**
