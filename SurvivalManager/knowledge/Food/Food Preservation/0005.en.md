## 4. Long-Term Meat Preservation Recipes
---
### 4-1. Beef Jerky
| Item | Content |
| :---: | :--- |
| Principle | Reduces water activity to 0.75 or lower to inhibit the growth of most bacteria. |
| Materials | Lean meat with minimal fat (such as flank, eye of round, or top round), salt (2% of meat weight) |
| Storage | Store in a place protected from moisture and light inside a sealed container after complete dehydration. |
| | For storage, it is recommended to seal it in a Mylar bag with an oxygen absorber or use a metal container. |
| | Homemade jerky has a shorter shelf life than expected due to the absence of preservatives. For long-term storage purposes, making pemmican is preferable. |

| Step | Instructions |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Remove as much surface and internal fat and tendons from the lean meat as possible. (Fat does not dry out and is the primary cause of rancidity and foul odors) |
| 2 | Slice the lean meat uniformly against the grain to a thickness of 5 mm or less. |
| 3 | Rub the salt evenly over the meat. (Ingredients like soy sauce, spices, and sugar are optional, but should be minimized as they increase moisture and reduce preservation capacity) |
| 4 | Cure the meat under refrigeration (4°C or lower) for at least 3 hours to allow the salt to penetrate deeply. (If refrigeration is unavailable, cure in a shaded area at room temperature for 2 hours) |
| 5 | Dehydrator Drying: Dry at 65°C for at least 12 hours. |
| | Sun Drying: Cover with a thin cloth or mesh to block dust and pests, and dry in a well-ventilated area with direct sunlight for at least 3 days. (Bring indoors at night as it absorbs moisture) |
| | Common: Turn the meat over multiple times during the process to ensure uniform drying. |
| 6 | After drying is complete, flash-bake or heat-treat the meat until the internal temperature reaches at least 71°C to eradicate foodborne pathogens (such as Salmonella and E. coli). |
| 7 | The finished jerky should have no stickiness or moisture on the surface, and it should crack rather than snap when bent. |

### 4-2. Pemmican
| Item | Content |
| :---: | :--- |
| Principle | Combining completely dehydrated meat powder with animal fat high in oxidative stability results in a low moisture content that allows for long-term storage at room temperature. |
| Materials | Lean meat with minimal fat, beef fat (tallow), salt (2% of meat weight), (Optional) completely dehydrated fruit powder (within 5% of total weight) |
| Storage | Store in a sealed state in a location protected from moisture and light. |
| | Avoid high-temperature environments to prevent the fat from melting or spoiling. |

| Step | Meat Powder Preparation Instructions |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Remove as much surface and internal fat and tendons from the lean meat as possible. (Fat does not dry out and carries a high risk of rancidity) |
| 2 | Slice the lean meat as thinly as possible. |
| 3 | Rub the salt evenly over the meat. |
| 4 | Cure the meat under refrigeration (4°C or lower) for at least 3 hours to allow the salt to penetrate deeply. (If refrigeration is unavailable, cure in a shaded area at room temperature for 2 hours) |
| 5 | Dehydrate the cured meat completely using a food dehydrator (65°C or higher) or sunlight until it is brittle enough to snap. |
| 6 | After drying is complete, thoroughly roast the meat until the internal temperature reaches at least 71°C to destroy foodborne pathogens (such as Salmonella and E. coli). |
| 7 | Allow the heat to dissipate, then grind the meat using a mortar and pestle or a blender into a fine powder (meat powder) that easily crumbles by hand. |

| Step | Beef Fat Rendering Instructions |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Chop the beef fat into small pieces and render the fat by heating it with water, or by frying it over low heat without water. (Pork fat is disadvantageous for long-term storage due to low oxidative stability, so beef fat must be used) |
| 2 | Strain out the water and protein residues using a sieve or cloth to collect only the clear tallow. |
| 3 | Place the clear tallow in a pot and simmer over low heat until all microscopic moisture in the oil evaporates completely and no bubbles form. |

| Step | Pemmican Molding Instructions |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Mix the prepared meat powder and dried fruit powder uniformly. (Fruit powder decreases shelf life, so limit it to within 5% of the total weight, and add a small amount of salt only if additional seasoning is needed) |
| 2 | Pour the warm, melted rendered beef tallow into the meat powder at an approximate 1:1 ratio (or until the meat powder becomes moist) and mix until it reaches a clay-like consistency. (Too much fat causes it to run, while too little prevents binding) |
| 3 | Press the mixture firmly into molds before the fat solidifies to shape it into bars or blocks. |
| 4 | Once the beef tallow solidifies completely and the shape hardens, seal each piece individually to prevent air exposure. |

### 4-3. Smoked Meat
| Item | Content |
| :---: | :--- |
| Principle | Forms an antibacterial and antioxidant barrier of smoke components (phenols, acetic acid, etc.) on the meat surface to suppress microbial growth and lipid oxidation. |
| Materials | Lean meat with low fat, salt (4% of meat weight), smoker, thermometer, smoking materials (hardwood logs/chips such as oak or fruitwood) |
| Storage | Store in a cool, dry, and dark place in a dried and sealed condition. |
| | Unsealed or improperly dried smoked meat must be kept refrigerated. |

| Step | Preparation Instructions |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Rub the salt evenly over the meat. |
| 2 | Cure the meat under refrigeration (4°C or lower) for at least 48 hours to allow the salt to penetrate deeply. (Without prior curing, the meat will spoil during smoking) |
| 3 | Once curing is complete, wipe away surface moisture so that smoke can readily adhere to the surface, and dry the meat sufficiently until a tacky, firm skin (pellicle) forms on the exterior. |
| 4 | Ignite the smoker using only hardwood logs or chips such as oak or fruitwood, as softwoods (like pine) generate bitterness and toxicity due to resin and soot. |
| 5 | Apply smoke for several hours to several weeks, adhering to the temperature standards for each smoking method below, depending on the target preservation period and cooking style. (*See table below for detailed temperatures) |
| 6 | After smoking, age the meat for a certain period in a cool, dry place protected from dust and pests to allow the smoke components to penetrate evenly to the interior and stabilize moisture. |
| 7 | Safety Verification: The finished smoked meat must be firm and dry. Because the low-temperature, low-oxygen environment generated during smoking is highly conducive to *Clostridium botulinum* proliferation, the entire batch must be discarded immediately if salinity, dryness, or temperature controls fail to meet targets. |

| Smoking Method | Target Temperature | Smoking Time | Preservation & Characteristics |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| Cold Smoking | 30°C or lower | Several days to weeks | For room-temperature long-term storage. High-salinity curing and complete drying are mandatory; this method applies only smoke without heat to drastically remove moisture. |
| Warm Smoking | 40 to 60°C | 2 to 5 hours | For short-term storage. The meat structure softens but is not fully cooked, meaning subsequent reheating is required, and its preservation capacity is relatively low. |
| Hot Smoking | 65 to 90°C | Several hours | For immediate consumption and short-term storage. This method completely cooks the interior of the meat while drying it (barbecue style); it yields excellent flavor but cannot be stored long-term. |
| Roast Smoking | 90°C or higher | Short duration | A method where meat is quickly roasted at high temperatures to impart only a smoke aroma, offering virtually no preservation effect. |
