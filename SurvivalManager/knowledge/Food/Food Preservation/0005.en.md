## 4. Long-Term Preservation Recipes for Meat
---
### 4-1. Jerky
| Item | Content |
| :---: | :--- |
| Principle | Lowering water activity to 0.75 or below suppresses the growth of most bacteria. |
| Materials | Nearly fat-free lean meat (eye of round, inside round, etc.), salt (2% of the meat's weight) |
| Storage | After drying completely, seal it and store it in a place shielded from moisture and light. |
| | When storing, seal it in a Mylar bag together with an oxygen absorber, or use a metal container. |
| | Homemade jerky has no preservatives, so its shelf life is shorter than you'd think; if the goal is long-term storage, it is better to make pemmican. |

| Step | Method |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Remove as much fat and sinew as possible from the surface and interior of the lean meat. (Fat does not dry, and is the main cause of rancidity and bad odor) |
| 2 | Cut the lean meat against the grain evenly into slices of 5 mm or less. |
| 3 | Rub salt evenly into the meat. (Soy sauce, spices, sugar, and so on are optional, but they can lower keeping quality by adding moisture, so keep them to a minimum) |
| 4 | Let it cure for at least 3 hours refrigerated (4 °C or below) so the salt penetrates inside. (If refrigeration is not possible, cure it for 2 hours in room-temperature shade) |
| 5 | Dehydrator drying: Dry at 65 °C for at least 12 hours. |
| | Sun drying: Cover with a thin cloth or net to keep out dust and pests, and dry for at least 3 days in a place with direct sunlight and good ventilation. (At night it absorbs moisture, so always bring it indoors) |
| | Common: Turn it several times along the way to dry evenly. |
| 6 | After drying, lightly roast or heat-treat it until the internal temperature of the meat reaches at least 71 °C to kill food poisoning bacteria (Salmonella, E. coli, and so on). |
| 7 | The finished jerky should have no stickiness or moisture on the surface and should crack without breaking when folded. |

### 4-2. Pemmican
| Item | Content |
| :---: | :--- |
| Principle | The combination of completely dried meat powder and animal fat of high oxidative stability has low moisture content and can be stored at room temperature for the long term. |
| Materials | Nearly fat-free lean meat, beef fat (tallow), salt (2% of the meat's weight), (optional) completely dried fruit powder (within 5% of total weight) |
| Storage | Store sealed in a place shielded from moisture and light. |
| | Avoid high-temperature environments so the fat does not melt or spoil. |

| Step | How to make the meat powder |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Remove as much fat and sinew as possible from the surface and interior of the lean meat. (Fat does not dry and has a high risk of rancidity) |
| 2 | Cut the lean meat as thin as possible. |
| 3 | Rub salt evenly into the meat. |
| 4 | Let it cure for at least 3 hours refrigerated (4 °C or below) so the salt penetrates inside. (If refrigeration is not possible, cure it for 2 hours in room-temperature shade) |
| 5 | Dry the cured meat completely until it snaps crisply, using a dehydrator (65 °C or above) or the sun. |
| 6 | After drying, roast it thoroughly until the internal temperature of the meat reaches at least 71 °C to kill food poisoning bacteria (Salmonella, E. coli, and so on). |
| 7 | After the heat cools, pound it in a mortar or grind it in a blender into a fine powder (meat powder) that crumbles easily by hand. |

| Step | How to render beef fat |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Cut beef fat into small pieces and heat it with water, or fry it over low heat without water, to melt out the oil. (Pork fat has low oxidative stability and is disadvantageous for long-term storage, so always use beef fat) |
| 2 | Strain out water and protein residue with a sieve or cloth and collect only the clear oil. |
| 3 | Put the clear oil in a pot and heat it over low heat, boiling it until the fine moisture in the oil has completely evaporated and no bubbles form at all. |

| Step | How to shape pemmican |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Mix the prepared meat powder and dried fruit powder evenly. (Fruit powder lowers keeping quality, so keep it to within 5% of total weight, and add a small amount of salt only when the seasoning is lacking) |
| 2 | Pour in the warmly melted rendered beef fat at about a 1:1 ratio with the meat powder (or until the meat powder becomes moist), mixing until it reaches a clay-like consistency. (Too much oil makes it run, and too little keeps it from clumping) |
| 3 | Before the fat hardens, put it in a mold and press firmly to shape it into bars or lumps. |
| 4 | When the beef fat has hardened completely and the shape is firm, seal each piece individually so that air cannot get in. |

### 4-3. Smoked Meat
| Item | Content |
| :---: | :--- |
| Principle | The antibacterial, antioxidant film of smoke components (phenols, acetic acid, and so on) forms on the meat surface and suppresses microbial growth and fat rancidity. |
| Materials | Low-fat lean meat, salt (4% of the meat's weight), smoker, thermometer, smoking wood (hardwood logs/chips such as oak and fruit trees) |
| Storage | Store dry and sealed in a cool place shielded from moisture and light. |
| | Smoked meat that is not dry and sealed must always be refrigerated. |

| Step | Method |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Rub salt evenly into the meat. |
| 2 | Let it cure for at least 48 hours refrigerated (4 °C or below) so the salt penetrates inside. (Without prior salting, it spoils during smoking) |
| 3 | Wipe the surface moisture off the cured meat so that smoke adheres well to the surface, and dry it sufficiently until a sticky, firm film (pellicle) forms on the outside. |
| 4 | Softwoods (pine and the like) produce bitterness and toxicity from resin and soot, so always light the smoker with hardwood logs and chips such as oak or fruit trees. |
| 5 | According to the target preservation period and cooking method, observe the temperature standards for each smoking method below and expose the meat to smoke for several hours to several weeks. (※ See the table below for detailed temperatures) |
| 6 | Age the smoked meat for a period in a cool, dry place free of dust and pests so that the smoke components permeate evenly inside and the moisture stabilizes. |
| 7 | Safety check: Finished smoked meat should be firm and dry. The low-temperature, low-oxygen environment during smoking is a condition in which botulinum bacteria multiply easily, so if you judge that control of the set salinity, dryness, or temperature failed, discard the entire batch immediately. |

| Smoking method | Controlled temperature | Smoking time | Keeping quality and characteristics |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| Cold smoking | 30 °C or below | Days to weeks | For long-term room-temperature storage. High-salt curing and complete drying are essential, and it is a smoking method that applies only smoke without heat to drive off moisture to the extreme. |
| Warm smoking | 40-60 °C | 2-5 hours | For short-term storage. The meat tissue becomes tender but not fully cooked, so reheating and cooking are needed later, and keeping quality is relatively low. |
| Hot smoking | 65-90 °C | Several hours | For immediate eating and short-term storage. It dries and fully cooks the inside of the meat at the same time (barbecue style), with good flavor but no long-term storage. |
| Roast smoking | 90 °C or above | Short time | A method of quickly roasting the meat at high temperature while adding only the smoke aroma, with almost no preservative effect. |
