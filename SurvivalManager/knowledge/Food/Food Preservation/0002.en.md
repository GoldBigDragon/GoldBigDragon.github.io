## 1. The 7 Essentials of Food Preservation
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To prevent food from spoiling and keep its quality, environmental factors such as temperature, humidity, light, and oxygen must be tightly controlled. In particular, managing water activity, which suppresses the growth of microorganisms, is the key.

---
### 1-1. Temperature
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In most chemical and microbial reactions, when the storage temperature rises 10 °C above room temperature, shelf life tends to be halved, and when it falls 10 °C, shelf life tends to double. Therefore, apart from some crops sensitive to chilling, food should be stored in the coolest environment possible.

---
### 1-2. Humidity
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Moisture is like an ocean to microorganisms, and it promotes rancidity. Water activity expresses the free water in food available to microorganisms, and for long-term preservation it is best to lower water activity to 0.6 or below.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When packaging food, drying and sealing are important so that moisture does not get in, and only foods with low moisture content should be packaged. In a humid environment, it is good to add silica gel (a moisture absorber) as an auxiliary measure or to keep a dehumidifier.

---
### 1-3. Light
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Light promotes rancidity, so food should be stored in a dark environment shielded from light.

---
### 1-4. Oxygen
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Oxygen oxidizes food and degrades its taste and quality, so it is best to remove oxygen with oxygen absorbers or vacuum packaging. At this time, oxygen absorbers should be put in quickly as soon as the package is opened and sealed, and individually packaged products are best.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Even if oxygen is removed, fat rancidity in nuts is strongly affected by temperature, so nuts must always be stored at low temperature as well.

---
### 1-5. Hygiene
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Food must be packaged in a hygienic environment. A very small amount of spoilage can quickly spoil all the food.

---
### 1-6. Contact with Metal
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Food should avoid contact with metals such as iron, copper, and nickel, with the exception of stainless steel.

---
### 1-7. Packaging Materials
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;High-quality packaging such as Mylar bags and Number 10 cans can protect food to some extent from light, moisture, and oxygen.
