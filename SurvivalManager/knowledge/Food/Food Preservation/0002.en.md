## 1. The 7 Core Principles of Food Preservation
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To prevent spoilage and maintain food quality, environmental factors such as temperature, humidity, light, and oxygen must be carefully controlled. In particular, managing water activity is one of the most important factors in suppressing microbial growth.

---
### 1-1. Temperature
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In most chemical and microbial reactions, when storage temperature rises 10°C above room temperature, shelf life tends to be reduced by half. Conversely, lowering the temperature by 10°C generally doubles shelf life. Therefore, with the exception of certain cold-sensitive crops, food should be stored in the coolest environment possible.

---
### 1-2. Humidity
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Moisture is like an ocean for microorganisms and also accelerates rancidity. Water activity refers to the amount of free water available for microbial growth inside food. For long-term preservation, it is recommended to keep water activity below 0.6.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When packaging food, proper drying and airtight sealing are essential to prevent moisture intrusion. Only foods with low moisture content should be packaged for long-term storage. In humid environments, it is recommended to use silica gel desiccants or operate a dehumidifier as additional protection.

---
### 1-3. Light
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Light accelerates oxidation and rancidity, so food should be stored in a dark, light-blocked environment.

---
### 1-4. Oxygen
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Oxygen oxidizes food and degrades both flavor and quality. Using oxygen absorbers or vacuum sealing is recommended to remove oxygen during storage. Oxygen absorbers should be inserted immediately after opening and sealed quickly, and individually packaged absorbers are preferable.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Even if oxygen is removed, the fats in nuts are still highly affected by temperature. Therefore, nuts should always be stored at low temperatures as well.

---
### 1-5. Sanitation
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Food should always be packaged in a sanitary environment. Even minor contamination or rancidity can rapidly spoil an entire batch of food.

---
### 1-6. Contact with Metals
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Food should avoid contact with metals such as iron, copper, and nickel, with the exception of stainless steel.

---
### 1-7. Packaging Materials
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;High-quality packaging materials such as Mylar bags or Number 10 cans can help protect food from light, moisture, and oxygen.
