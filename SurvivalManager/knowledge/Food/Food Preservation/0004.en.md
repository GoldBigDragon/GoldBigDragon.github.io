## 3. Canning in Glass Jars
---
### 3-1. Sterilizing Glass Jars
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Thorough sterilization and sealing are crucial when canning in glass jars. During boiling water sterilization, care must be taken to prevent the jars from shattering due to sudden temperature changes.

| Step | Instructions |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Place the thoroughly cleaned glass jars into a pot and fill it with cold water until the jars are completely submerged. |
| 2 | Heat the pot and, once the water begins to boil, maintain the boil for at least 10 minutes to sterilize the jars. (The jars must be placed in the water while it is still cold before boiling). |
| 3 | Because the sealing rubber on some metal lids can degrade during prolonged heating, lids should be sterilized separately from the jars in hot water at 80°C. |
| 4 | Once sterilization is complete, place the jars and lids upside down on a clean tray to air dry. The interiors of the jars must be completely dry with no remaining moisture. |

---
### 3-2. Sealing Food
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Filling the sterilized glass jars with hot contents and processing them extends their shelf life at room temperature.

| Step | Instructions |
| :---: | :--- |
| 5 | Fill the sterilized glass jars with the food while it is still hot. Leave about 2 cm of headspace from the rim of the jar. |
| 6 | Wipe away any food residues or foreign substances from around the rim of the jar using a sterilized cloth. Any remaining debris can prevent a perfect seal. |
| 7 | Securely tighten the lid to seal the jar. |
| 8 | Submerge the sealed jars back into boiling water and process them over low heat for 90 minutes (sterilization and simmering). This process helps reduce microorganisms and create a vacuum seal. (*Note: Low-acid foods such as meats and vegetables must undergo 'pressure canning' instead of this step.) |
| 9 | Allow the jars to cool down gradually in a well-ventilated area to prevent them from shattering. |
| 10 | After cooling, verify that the lids have properly sealed, and store the jars in a cool, dark place. |

---
### 3-3. Pressure Canning
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For low-acid foods with a pH of 4.6 or higher—such as meats, braised meat in soy sauce (Jangjorim), and vegetables—simply boiling or processing them in boiling water (100°C) is insufficient to destroy *Clostridium botulinum* spores. *Clostridium botulinum* can proliferate even in oxygen-free environments (sealed jars) and produces a fatal toxin.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To store low-acid foods safely at room temperature for the long term, they must be processed at temperatures of 121°C or higher for at least 20 minutes using a pressure canner or a pressure cooker that elevates the boiling point by increasing internal pressure. Low-acid foods processed simply by boiling below 100°C cannot be stored at room temperature for extended periods; they must be kept refrigerated and consumed within a few days.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In disaster situations where the power supply is disrupted, do not attempt to preserve low-acid foods like meats and vegetables through jar canning. Instead, preserve them using salting or complete dehydration methods (such as jerky or pemmican).
