## 3. Making Jarred Preserves
---
### 3-1. Sterilizing Glass Jars
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When making jarred preserves, thorough sterilization and sealing are important, and when sterilizing glass jars in boiling water, take care that jars do not break from sudden temperature changes.

| Step | Method |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Put the cleanly washed jars in a pot and fill it with cold water until the jars are completely submerged. |
| 2 | Heat the pot, and when the water starts to boil, keep heating for at least 10 minutes to sterilize the jars. (You must put the jars in while the water is cold and then bring it to a boil) |
| 3 | Some metal lids suffer deterioration of the sealing rubber when heated for a long time, so sterilize the lids separately from the jars in 80 °C hot water. |
| 4 | Place the sterilized jars and lids upside down on a clean tray and let them air dry. At this time, they must be dried completely so that there is no moisture inside the jar. |

---
### 3-2. Sealing Food
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Filling hot contents into boiling-water-sterilized glass jars and letting them steep extends room-temperature shelf life.

| Step | Method |
| :---: | :--- |
| 5 | Put hot food into the boiling-water-sterilized glass jar. At this time, leave about 2 cm of space at the mouth of the jar. |
| 6 | Wipe contents and foreign matter around the mouth of the jar with a sterilized cloth. If foreign matter remains, a perfect seal may not form. |
| 7 | Close the lid tightly to seal it. |
| 8 | Put the sealed jar back into boiling water and simmer it over low heat for 90 minutes (sterilization and steeping). This process helps reduce microorganisms and form a vacuum. (※ Low-acid foods such as meat and vegetables must undergo "pressure sterilization" instead of this step.) |
| 9 | To prevent the jar from breaking, cool it slowly in a ventilated place. |
| 10 | After cooling, check that the lid is properly sealed and store it in a cool, dark place. |

---
### 3-3. Pressure Sterilization
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Low-acid foods with a pH of 4.6 or higher, such as meat, braised beef in soy sauce (jangjorim), and vegetables, cannot have botulinum spores killed merely by boiling in boiling water (100 °C) or steeping. Botulinum bacteria can multiply even in an oxygen-free environment (a sealed jar) and produce a deadly toxin.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To store low-acid foods safely for the long term at room temperature, you must use a pressure canner or pressure cooker that raises the internal pressure to raise the boiling point, and process at 121 °C or higher for at least 20 minutes. Low-acid foods processed by simply boiling at 100 °C or below cannot be stored at room temperature for the long term, and must be premised on refrigerated, short-term storage (consumed within days).

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When the power supply is cut off due to a disaster, do not attempt jarred preserves at all for low-acid foods such as meat and vegetables; preserve them by salting or complete drying (jerky, pemmican).
