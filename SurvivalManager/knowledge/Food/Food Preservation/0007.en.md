## 6. Long-Term Preservation Recipes for Grains
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Grains have low moisture content to begin with, so they can simply be stored in plastic bottles with oxygen absorbers, but in long-term storage, pests such as weevils and the rancidity of fat components are the biggest problems.

---
### 6-1. Hardtack / Ship's Biscuit
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most cost-effective long-term grain preservation recipe in human history, eaten for years by sailors of the Age of Sail and soldiers during the American Civil War.

| Item | Content |
| :---: | :--- |
| Principle | Removing the moisture from the flour lowers water activity to 0.3 or below so microorganisms cannot multiply, and since no fat (butter, oil) or sugar is added, there is no organic matter to go rancid. |
| Materials | 3 cups of flour (all-purpose or bread flour), 1 cup of water, 2 teaspoons of salt (no oil, sugar, or yeast) |
| Storage | After cooling completely, wear plastic gloves so germs from your hands do not get on it, and store it in a Mylar bag or airtight container with an oxygen absorber. |
| Cautions | It is as hard as stone, so chewing it as is can break your teeth. You must soak it in water, coffee, or broth, or break it up and boil it into porridge. |

| Step | Method |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Mix salt into the flour and knead while pouring in water little by little. |
| 2 | Knead until the dough no longer sticks to your hands and feels stiff. |
| 3 | Flatten the dough to a thickness of 1 cm with a rolling pin. |
| 4 | Cut it with a knife into rectangles of about 5 cm by 5 cm. |
| 5 | Poke holes densely all over the dough with a fork or chopsticks. (Passages for the inner moisture to evaporate) |
| 6 | In a household oven, bake at 160 °C for about 30 minutes, then flip and bake another 30 minutes. Bake it as if drying it to the core without burning, until the surface is brownish and it is completely hard to the touch. |
| 7 | Take the baked biscuits out and dry them for 3 days in a well-ventilated place to remove even the remaining moisture inside. |

### 6-2. Byeokgokdan
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A grain reserve food in pill form devised in the East so that, in war or famine, a single pellet could let a person forget hunger and endure without cooking.

| Item | Content |
| :---: | :--- |
| Principle | Grain is steamed once and dried to inactivate its enzymes, ground into powder, kneaded with "honey," a natural preservative and calorie source, and sealed. The high sugar concentration of honey causes strong osmotic pressure and blocks microbial growth. |
| Materials | Grains that are easy to get nearby such as soybeans, rice, glutinous rice, barley, jujube, and chestnuts (or sesame), and natural honey (sugar syrup and oligosaccharide syrup have too much moisture for long-term storage) |
| Storage | Wrap each byeokgokdan pellet in paper or hanji, and put them in a jar or airtight container in a cool place. |

| Step | Method |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Wash the prepared grains (soybeans, rice, and so on) clean and steam them thoroughly in a steamer. (If the grains are cooked in advance, they can be eaten later without recooking) |
| 2 | Spread the steamed grain in a sunny, breezy place and dry it until it is hard. |
| 3 | Put the thoroughly dried grain, jujube (pits removed), dried chestnuts, and so on into a mortar or grinder and grind them into a very fine powder. |
| 4 | Pour natural honey into the grain powder little by little and knead until it feels like firm clay. (Too much honey makes it sticky, so add only enough to make it hold together) |
| 5 | Roll the dough into balls 2 cm in diameter to make pills. |
| 6 | Dry the rolled byeokgokdan for 2 days in a well-ventilated shaded place until the surface is slightly firm, then wrap in paper or hanji and store in an airtight container. |

### 6-3. Misutgaru

| Item | Content |
| :---: | :--- |
| Principle | Grain is roasted at high temperature to reduce internal moisture to 5% or below and gelatinize the protein and starch. With no moisture, microorganisms cannot grow. |
| Materials | Barley, glutinous rice, brown rice, beans, and so on (oil-rich grains such as perilla seeds and sesame go rancid quickly because of their oil and should be excluded) |
| Storage | Put in a Mylar bag with an oxygen absorber and store in a cool place. |

| Step | Method |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Wash the grain clean in water and soak it in cold water for three or four hours. |
| 2 | Put the soaked grain in a steamer and steam until fully cooked. |
| 3 | Spread the steamed grain on a drying tray and dry until the moisture is gone. |
| 4 | Roast the dried grain over low heat until it smells toasty. |
| 5 | Cool the roasted grain completely and grind it finely into powder in a blender or mortar. |
