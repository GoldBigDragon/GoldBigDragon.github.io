## 6. Long-Term Preservation Recipes for Grains
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Grains inherently have a low moisture content, making them easy to store in plastic bottles alongside oxygen absorbers. However, pest infestations, such as weevils, and the rancidity of lipid components pose the greatest challenges during long-term storage.

---
### 6-1. Hardtack / Ship's Biscuit
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is the most cost-effective long-term grain preservation recipe in human history, relied upon for years by sailors during the Age of Discovery and soldiers during the American Civil War.

| Item | Content |
| :---: | :--- |
| Principle | Removing moisture from the flour lowers the water activity to 0.3 or below, rendering microbial proliferation impossible. Additionally, the exclusion of fats (butter, oil) and sugar leaves no organic matter susceptible to rancidity. |
| Materials | 3 cups flour (all-purpose or bread flour), 1 cup water, 2 teaspoons salt (Do not use oil, sugar, or yeast) |
| Storage | Allow to cool completely, then store in Mylar bags or airtight containers alongside oxygen absorbers. Wear vinyl gloves during handling to prevent contamination from pathogens on hands. |
| Precautions | These biscuits are rock-hard and can break teeth if chewed directly. They must be softened in water, coffee, or broth, or crushed and boiled into a porridge-like consistency before consumption. |

| Step | Instructions |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Mix the salt into the flour, and gradually add water while kneading the dough. |
| 2 | Knead until the dough no longer sticks to your hands and develops a stiff, firm consistency. |
| 3 | Use a rolling pin to flatten the dough to a uniform thickness of 1 cm. |
| 4 | Use a knife to cut the dough into squares measuring approximately 5 cm by 5 cm. |
| 5 | Use a fork or a chopstick to poke dense holes across the entire surface of the dough squares. (These serve as pathways for internal moisture to evaporate). |
| 6 | Bake in a home oven at 160°C for approximately 30 minutes, then flip and bake for another 30 minutes. Bake thoroughly as if drying the dough from the inside out without burning it; the exterior should turn golden brown and feel completely rigid to the touch. |
| 7 | Remove the baked biscuits from the oven and dry them in a well-ventilated area for 3 days to eliminate any remaining internal moisture. |

### 6-2. Byeokgokdan (Grain Pill)
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is a ball- or pill-shaped emergency grain supply developed in East Asia to enable individuals to endure wartime or famine by staving off hunger with just a single pill, without requiring additional cooking.

| Item | Content |
| :---: | :--- |
| Principle | Involves steaming and drying grains to inactivate enzymes, grinding them into a powder, and kneading the mixture with natural honey, which acts as both a natural preservative and a calorie source, before sealing. The high sugar concentration of honey creates a powerful osmotic pressure that blocks microbial growth. |
| Materials | Commonly available grains such as soybeans, rice, glutinous rice, and barley; jujubes, chestnuts (or sesame seeds); natural honey (Sugar syrup or oligosaccharides cannot be used for long-term storage due to their high moisture content). |
| Storage | Wrap each grain pill individually in paper or Hanji (traditional Korean paper), place them in a jar or airtight container, and store in a cool location. |

| Step | Instructions |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Thoroughly wash the prepared grains (soybeans, rice, etc.) and steam them fully in a steamer basket or traditional earthenware steamer. (Cooking the grains beforehand ensures they can be consumed later without re-cooking). |
| 2 | Spread the steamed grains in a well-ventilated, sunny area and dry them thoroughly until they become rigid. |
| 3 | Place the completely dried grains, pitted jujubes, and dried chestnuts into a mortar and pestle or a grinder, and pulverize them into a very fine powder. |
| 4 | Gradually pour the natural honey into the grain powder and mix until the dough reaches a firm, clay-like consistency. (Add honey sparingly just to bind the mixture, as too much will make it sticky). |
| 5 | Shape the dough into round balls with a diameter of approximately 2 cm to form pills. |
| 6 | Dehydrate the shaped grain pills in a well-ventilated, shaded area for 2 days until the surface hardens slightly, then wrap them in paper or Hanji and store them in an airtight container. |

### 6-3. Misutgaru (Roasted Grain Powder)
| Item | Content |
| :---: | :--- |
| Principle | Roasting grains at high temperatures reduces internal moisture to 5% or below and gelatinizes the proteins and starches. The absence of moisture prevents the growth of microorganisms. |
| Materials | Barley, glutinous rice, brown rice, soybeans, etc. (Oil-rich grains such as perilla seeds or sesame seeds must be excluded, as their fat content accelerates rancidity). |
| Storage | Place in a Mylar bag along with an oxygen absorber and store in a cool location. |

| Step | Instructions |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Thoroughly wash the grains in water and soak them in cold water for three to four hours. |
| 2 | Place the soaked grains into a steamer and steam them until they are completely cooked. |
| 3 | Spread the steamed grains out on a colander or drying tray to eliminate surface moisture. |
| 4 | Roast the dehydrated grains over low heat until they emit a savory, aromatic fragrance. |
| 5 | Allow the roasted grains to cool completely, then grind them into a fine powder using a blender or a mortar and pestle. |
