## 3. Kiln Principles and Air Control
### 3-1. Two Kinds of Kiln
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Kilns divide into two kinds depending on where the heat needed for carbonization comes from.

- Partial-combustion type: burns part of the wood put into the kiln and uses that heat to carbonize the rest. Pit kilns, earth kilns, and traditional brick kilns belong here. No separate fuel is needed and the structure is simple. A certain proportion of the wood is used as fuel, so the yield is low, and combustion and carbonization happen at the same time in the same space, which makes air control tricky.
- Indirect-heating type (retort): puts the wood in a sealed vessel and heats the vessel from outside. The wood never meets air, so there is no loss to burning away; the yield is high, and the degree of carbonization can be controlled directly by temperature. It needs metal or refractory material to make the vessel and separate fuel to heat it.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A retort can send the volatile gas given off by the wood back into the fire to be burned. Smoke drops greatly, and from the middle of carbonization onward that gas alone sustains the heating, so almost no outside fuel is needed. It can be built from two oil drums.

---
### 3-2. Comparing Kiln Types
| Type | Key structure | Yield | Time required | What you need |
|:---|:---|---:|:---|:---|
| Pit kiln | Dig a hole, fill it with wood, light it, and cover with soil | 12-20% | 1-3 days | A shovel |
| Earth kiln | Stack wood on the ground and seal it with grass and soil | 15-25% | 3 days to 2 weeks | Shovel, grass, clayey soil |
| Drum kiln | Fill an oil drum with wood, fire it from below, and control it with air holes | 20-30% | 6-10 hours | A 200 L oil drum |
| Brick kiln | Build a semi-permanent kiln with firebricks, with vents and a chimney | 25-35% | 2-7 days | Bricks, clay, skill |
| Retort | Heat a sealed vessel from outside and reuse the exhaust gas for combustion | 30-40% | 4-8 hours | Two metal drums |

- Pit kiln: choose it when a shovel is your only tool. The ground acts as the cover and insulation, so no separate material is needed. Soil flows between the wood and mixes into the charcoal, and where the water table is high, water rises from the bottom and it fails.
- Earth kiln: can be built larger than a pit, and air control is a little easier. The covering soil dries and cracks, and air entering through the cracks often turns the charcoal to ash, so keep filling the cracks throughout carbonization.
- Drum kiln: the realistic compromise in a disaster. The seal is reliable, so the failure rate is low, results come within a day, and the drum can be used repeatedly. There are two ways: stand the drum upside down and drill several air holes in the lower side, or lay the drum on its side and attach a chimney to one end.

---
### 3-3. Controlling the Amount of Air
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The heart of charcoal making is the amount of air. With too much oxygen, even the carbonized charcoal burns and turns to ash, and with none, the temperature does not rise and carbonization stops. The narrow range between these two failures is the working zone.

| Air condition | What happens in the kiln | Product |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Too much | The carbon burns completely with oxygen into carbon dioxide | White ash. No charcoal left |
| Right amount | Only part burns, and the heat it gives off pyrolyzes the rest | Charcoal |
| Too little | The temperature falls below 280 °C and decomposition stops | Black outside, wood inside |
| Fully blocked (when heating) | Pyrolysis proceeds on external heat alone | High-quality charcoal. The retort method |
| Fully blocked (when cooling) | Combustion stops and the charcoal is preserved | Finishing carbonization and safety |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It is operated with two openings.

- Lower air hole: sets the amount of oxygen coming in. Narrowing it suppresses combustion.
- Upper chimney: sets the amount of smoke going out. Narrowing it raises the pressure inside the kiln, which reduces the air intake and lowers the temperature.
- In early carbonization the water has to be driven off, so open both holes wide. From the middle stage on, once the exothermic reaction sustains itself, narrow them gradually.

> **Caution:** When you judge that carbonization is finished, seal all the openings completely with soil and wet ash, and open the kiln only after letting it cool for at least a day. If you open a kiln holding hot charcoal too early, the sudden air makes all the charcoal ignite at once.

---
### 3-4. Smoke Color and Kiln Temperature
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When you have no thermometer, judge the stage inside the kiln by the chimney smoke. Each stage gives off different substances, so the color and smell differ.

| Appearance of the smoke | Substance coming out | Stage in the kiln |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Thick white smoke, droplets forming at the chimney end | Steam | Drying stage. Around 100 °C |
| Thin white smoke with a sour smell | Acetic acid, methanol, steam | Torrefaction. 170-280 °C |
| Thick yellow or brown smoke with a pungent, oily smell | Tar and heavy volatiles | Full carbonization. 280-400 °C |
| The smoke thins and turns bluish | Carbon monoxide and light gases | Carbonization nearly complete |
| Almost no smoke, just a heat shimmer | Carbon dioxide and air | Carbonization complete. Time to seal |

- When you see blue smoke, prepare to seal. If you leave it past this stage, the charcoal already made burns away and the yield falls.
- If you cannot tell, hold a dry twig near the chimney. If tar is coming out, the twig gets an oily film, and if carbonization is finished, it does not.

> **Caution:** Blue smoke and clear exhaust are the stage at which the most invisible carbon monoxide comes out. Do not put your face right in front of the chimney to check. Observe from beside the chimney, standing where the wind blows from behind you.

---
### 3-5. Causes of Carbonization Failure
| Failure pattern | Direct cause | Root cause |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Almost no charcoal, only ash | Air keeps coming in | The covering soil cracked, the seal failed, the chimney was open too wide |
| Black outside, wood inside | Center temperature did not reach 280 °C | Wood too thick, high moisture, too little carbonization time |
| Temperature does not rise | Heat is spent boiling water | Green wood, wet ground, rained on |
| The chimney clogs and smoke backs up | Tar hardens and blocks the passage | A lot of conifer used, low chimney temperature |
| Fire remains when opened after cooling | Air reached uncombusted carbon | Incomplete seal, too little cooling time |
| Charcoal crumbles into small pieces | Overheating weakened the structure | Local overheating, uneven draft |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The recurring root causes are the water in the wood and the gaps in the kiln. Dry the wood well and seal the kiln firmly, and most failures disappear.
