## 4. Carbon Monoxide, By-Products, and Non-Fuel Uses
### 4-1. Carbon Monoxide Poisoning
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;After typhoons and power outages, whole families die from lighting charcoal fires indoors, in the same pattern everywhere in the world. The cause is carbon monoxide (CO).

- Generation: when oxygen is short, carbon picks up only one oxygen atom and becomes CO. A charcoal fire burns at the surface and quickly consumes the surrounding oxygen, so in an enclosed space CO inevitably builds up.
- Danger: CO binds to hemoglobin in the blood more than 200 times more strongly than oxygen and blocks oxygen transport. Even though you breathe, oxygen does not reach the tissues. There are no warning signs such as choking, coughing, or smell. The first symptoms are headache and drowsiness, and if you take it for tiredness and lie down, you lose consciousness right there.

| Concentration in air | Exposure time | Symptoms |
|---:|:---|:---|
| 50 ppm | 8 hours | No clear symptoms in healthy adults |
| 200 ppm | 2-3 hours | Mild headache, fatigue, nausea |
| 400 ppm | 1-2 hours | Severe headache. Life-threatening after 3 hours |
| 800 ppm | 45 minutes | Dizziness, vomiting, convulsions. Loss of consciousness within 2 hours |
| 1,600 ppm | 20 minutes | Headache and dizziness. Death possible within 1 hour |
| 6,400 ppm | 1-2 minutes | Convulsions and respiratory arrest. Death within 15 minutes |

> **Caution:** Even with no flame or smoke, a charcoal fire keeps producing CO. The most CO comes out when it is smoldering red without a flame. Do not light a charcoal fire in a tent, inside a car, in a room with the windows closed, or in a cellar. Leaving a window slightly open does not solve it.

- Ventilation: two openings on opposite sides must be open so that air actually passes through. Opening just one window by a hand's width creates no flow.
- CO has almost the same density as air and spreads evenly through the whole room. Getting low does not avoid it.
- If poisoning is suspected, first move the person into outside air. Everyone, including those with no symptoms, goes out together. It takes several hours for CO to come off hemoglobin even in fresh air.
- The typical shape of this accident is that the person who goes in to rescue becomes the second victim.

### 4-2. Wood Vinegar and Tar
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The smoke that escapes during carbonization becomes a liquid when cooled. After a few days it separates into three layers. A light oily layer on top, a brown aqueous solution (wood vinegar) in the middle, and black, sticky tar settled at the bottom.

| By-product | Main components | Use | Cautions |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Wood vinegar (crude) | Water, acetic acid, methanol, phenols | Not used as is right after collection | Mixed with substances of carcinogenic concern |
| Wood vinegar (refined) | An aqueous solution centered on acetic acid | Soil improvement, repelling pests, removing odors | Must be left to settle for 6 months or more to separate the upper oil layer and the lower tar |
| Wood tar | Phenols, creosote | Wood preservation, rope waterproofing, caulking boat seams, adhesive raw material | Strongly irritates skin and is flammable |
| Pitch | Tar boiled down and hardened | Waterproof sealing, base for arrowhead adhesive | Easily catches fire during heating |

- In a disaster the value of by-products lies in preservation and waterproofing. A post coated with wood tar rots far more slowly even when buried in the ground, and tar worked into rope or cloth repels water. Pitch, tar boiled down further, is a basic raw material for adhesives.
- Do not drink wood vinegar or apply it to wounds. Unrefined wood vinegar is mixed with substances of carcinogenic concern such as polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, and there is no way to separate them reliably at home.

### 4-3. Using Charcoal Without Burning It
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;During carbonization the cell structure of the wood stays intact and only the contents leave, so the charcoal retains densely packed fine passages made by the original vessels and cell walls. This structure gives charcoal a very large surface area.

| Use | How it works | Limits |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Odor removal | Odor molecules physically cling to the pore surfaces | The effect is gone once the pores are full. Drying in the sun restores some |
| Dehumidifying | The pores take up water vapor and release it when dry | Small capacity, so not enough for a large space |
| Improving the taste and smell of water | Adsorbs dissolved organic matter and chlorine | Hardly captures bacteria and viruses |
| Soil improvement | The pores hold water and nutrients and become habitat for microbes | Added as is, it takes up nutrients at first. Mix with compost and let it mature |
| Gunpowder ingredient | Supplies carbon as fuel to potassium nitrate, the oxygen source | Charcoal fired at a low temperature from low-density wood is suitable |
| Writing and marking | Carbon particles are left on the surface | Washes off with water |
| Aid in wound care | Adsorption reduces exudate | No disinfecting effect. Harmful for infected wounds |

- Activated carbon and charcoal are different. Activated carbon is charcoal treated again with steam or chemicals at 800-1,000 °C to widen the pores, and its surface area is several hundred to 1,500 m² per gram. The surface area of ordinary charcoal is a few tens of times smaller. Most figures about water purification and deodorizing are for activated carbon and do not apply directly to charcoal you fire yourself.
- Adsorption is not decomposition. Charcoal only holds things; it does not eliminate them. When the pores are full, adsorption stops, and charcoal used in water for a long time becomes a breeding ground for bacteria that feed on the organic matter it has captured. Replace the charcoal layer in a water filter regularly.

### 4-4. Storage
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Charcoal does not rot or decay, but it is weak to moisture and impact.

- Moisture: because of its pore structure it keeps absorbing water from the air. In the rainy season more than 10% of its weight can become water, and in that state it lights poorly, smokes, and loses firepower. Wet charcoal recovers after a few days of drying. Keep it in airtight containers or thick sacks, raised off the ground.
- Impact: charcoal knocking together in transport breaks, and powder blocks airflow and lowers its value as fuel. Powder is used as adhesive filler, soil conditioner, and gunpowder ingredient.

> **Caution:** Freshly made charcoal can still hold heat inside even when it looks cool. A good many charcoal-making fires are cases where charcoal thought to be cold was put in sacks and taken into a shed, and ignited a few hours later. Spread charcoal taken from the kiln on a noncombustible floor outdoors for at least a day, and store it only after checking by hand that it is completely cold.
