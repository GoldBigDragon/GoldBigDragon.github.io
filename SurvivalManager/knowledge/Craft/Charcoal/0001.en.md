## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Charcoal is a fuel made by heating wood with almost no air to drive off water and volatile components, leaving only carbon. It gives off almost no smoke, can exceed 1,000 °C with just blowing air, and keeps for years if kept out of the rain. In exchange, it loses more than half of the wood's energy and 75-85% of its weight.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Assuming electricity, gas, and logistics are cut off, this document covers the principle of carbonization, kilns and air control, carbon monoxide safety, and by-products and uses, centered on methods that work with only a shovel and soil.
