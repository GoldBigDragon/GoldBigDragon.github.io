## 3. Tanning Methods
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There are three methods possible with natural materials: brain tanning, vegetable tannin tanning, and alum tanning. They differ in the substance placed between the fibers and the nature of its bond with collagen.

---
### 3-1. Brain Tanning
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;More than half of an animal's brain by weight is lipid, and a good part of that is phospholipids such as lecithin. A phospholipid is friendly to water at one end and to oil at the other, so it acts as an emulsifier that holds oil in water as fine droplets.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Pure oil cannot soak into wet hide, but an emulsion made by dissolving brain in water goes into every corner between the fibers along with the water. As the hide dries and the water evaporates, only the phospholipids and fat remain as a thin film on the fiber surfaces, keeping the fibers from touching.

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Solution temperature | 40-50 °C. If too hot, it damages the collagen, and if cold, the fat hardens and does not penetrate |
| Number of repetitions | Soak, squeeze, and work it two or three times or more. One pass does not reach the middle |
| Nature of the bond | A physical coating rather than a chemical bond, so it can wash out |
| Required follow-up steps | Staking and smoking. If either is missing, the hide hardens again |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The rule of thumb that "every animal has just enough brain to tan its own hide" is mostly right but not exactly proportional. Some species, such as humans and pigs, have large brains relative to body weight, and with thick hides like cattle's one brain may not be enough.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If there is no brain, substitute a material that supplies phospholipids or emulsifiable fat.

| Substitute | Active component | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Egg yolk | Lecithin, fat | Closest in composition to brain, so it works well |
| Liver | Phospholipids, fat | Grind it and dissolve it in water |
| Bone marrow | Mostly fat | Weak emulsifying power, so use soap or yolk with it |
| Animal fat and soap | Fat, surfactant | Soap takes over the role of the emulsifier |
| Vegetable oil and eggs | Fat, lecithin | Avoid oils that go rancid easily |

---
### 3-2. Vegetable Tannin Tanning
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Tannins are polyphenols found in bark, leaves, and fruit. Each molecule carries several hydroxyl groups, which build hydrogen-bond bridges across two neighboring collagen chains and fix the collagen structure. If brain tanning is a physical treatment that coats the fiber surfaces with an oil film, tannin tanning bonds chemically with collagen. The tanning components do not wash out even when wet, resistance to bacterial breakdown rises, and the shrinkage temperature goes up to 75-85 °C.

| Material | Tannin content | Characteristics |
| --- | :---: | --- |
| Oak bark | 8-16% | The most widely used. Gives a fine light brown |
| Chestnut bark and wood | 10-13% | Similar to oak and extracts well |
| Sumac and other Anacardiaceae leaves | 25-30% | High content and a light color, but avoid if you are sensitive to lacquer |
| Pine and spruce bark | 5-18% | Easy to find, but the color is red and resin mixes in |
| Alder bark | Highly variable | Extracts well from young trees |
| Willow bark | Medium | Easily found near water |
| Acorn cups | 25-35% | Can be gathered in large quantities in autumn |

- Concentration: start with a dilute solution and raise the strength a little at a time. If you soak the hide in a strong solution from the start, the tannin bonds with the surface collagen instantly and forms a dense layer that blocks penetration inward. The hide ends up tanned only at the surface and rawhide inside, and when it dries, the middle layer hardens and splits. Starting dilute lets it penetrate slowly and deeply, and as the concentration is raised the bonding is completed. The rate at which you raise the concentration is the quality.
- Duration: proportional to the thickness of the hide. A few weeks for rabbit and goat hides, one to two months for deer hide, and six months to a year for thick cowhide.
- Replacement: when you first put a hide in, almost all the tannin in the solution is used up in a day. Change it often at first and lengthen the interval later.
- Judge progress by cutting the hide and seeing whether the cross-section color has soaked evenly to the middle.

---
### 3-3. Alum Tanning
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Alum is a double salt of aluminum and potassium. Aluminum ions released in water coordinate with the carboxyl groups of collagen and fix the fibers. It finishes in a few days and the result is white and soft, so it was traditionally used for gloves and thin leather.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The bond between aluminum and collagen is reversible. When wet again, the aluminum ions come off and wash out, so a single rain undoes the tanning and the hide hardens again as it dries. Strictly speaking it is sometimes classed as a separate category rather than tanning. Soaking in plenty of oil so that water cannot enter makes up for it to some extent.

| Method | Time required | Materials | Properties of the finished leather | Water resistance |
| --- | :---: | --- | --- | :---: |
| Brain tanning | A few days | Brains, egg yolk, liver, oil | Very soft, cushiony, and stretchy | Secured by smoking |
| Vegetable tannin | A few weeks to a year | Bark, acorn cups | Firm, shape-holding, and thick | Good |
| Alum | A few days | Alum | White, soft, and thin | Very poor |
| Smoking only | One to two days | Smoke | Meaningful only as the finish for other methods | Contributes as a supplement |
| No tanning (rawhide) | Drying time only | None | Very hard and stiff | Softens when wet |

- Brain tanning: parts that touch the body and fold often, such as the insides of clothing and shoes
- Tannin tanning: parts that must hold their shape, such as shoe soles, belts, and sheaths
- Alum tanning: used only in a limited way, for thin leather that will not touch water
- Combining: the two methods are often used together, for example by tanning with tannin and then working oil in. You get the stability of tannin and the flexibility of oil.
