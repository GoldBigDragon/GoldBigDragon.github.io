## 1. The Principle of Tanning
### 1-1. The Structure of Hide
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Skin has three layers, and only the dermis is used as leather. The other two layers are removed.

| Layer | Composition | Handling in tanning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Epidermis | Keratinized cells, hair, keratin | Removed along with the hair. If left, the leather is stiff and blotchy |
| Dermis | A net of collagen fiber bundles interwoven in three dimensions | The layer you keep. The substance of leather |
| Subcutaneous tissue | Fat, loose connective tissue, thin fascia | Scrape it all off. If left, it turns rancid and chemicals cannot penetrate |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The collagen fibers of the dermis are interwoven obliquely, so the hide is tough whichever way it is pulled, and the fibers can slide past one another, so it folds softly.

---
### 1-2. Why It Rots and Why It Hardens
- Rotting: wet dermis is a culture medium with protein, water, and a suitable temperature. When bacteria, fungi, and the tissue's own enzymes break down the collagen, the fibers snap and holes form. At summer temperatures, the hair begins to slip within half a day of skinning; this is not dehairing but the start of putrefaction.
- Hardening: in wet dermis water acts as a lubricant between the fibers. When the water evaporates, the fiber surfaces come into direct contact, and neighboring fibers bond at the hydrogen-bonding sites on the collagen surface, so the whole net becomes one hardened mass.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Tanning is putting a substance between the fibers so that the fibers cannot stick directly to one another even after the water is gone. Brain tanning fills it with phospholipids and oil, vegetable tanning with tannin molecules, and alum tanning with aluminum complexes. The more firmly the filling bonds to the fibers, the higher the water resistance and heat resistance of the finished leather.

---
### 1-3. Rawhide and Tanned Leather
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Rawhide is hide from which only hair and fat have been removed, stretched taut and dried without tanning. As it dries, all the fibers fuse, and it becomes a very hard board that does not warp.

| Category | Rawhide | Tanned leather |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Treatment | Dehaired and fleshed, then stretched and dried | Dried while being worked, after the tanning agent has penetrated |
| Dry state | Very hard and stiff | Flexible and foldable |
| When wet | Softens and stretches, and when it dries again it shrinks and hardens | Keeps its shape, and returns to its original state when dry |
| Resistance to rot | Safe only when dry | Does not rot easily even when wet |
| Main uses | Cord, lashing, drum skins, boxes and containers, sheaths, shields | Clothing, shoes, gloves, sacks, pouches |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A rawhide cord wrapped on wet and dried shrinks and tightens, giving a binding strength that knots alone cannot. It is used to attach an axe head to its handle or to fix an arrowhead. It softens again in rain, so apply oil or resin to keep the water out.

---
### 1-4. The Scale of Tanning
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The degree of tanning is measured by the shrinkage temperature. When collagen is soaked in water and heated, at some temperature the triple helix unwinds and it suddenly shrivels; the stronger the bond between the tanning agent and the collagen, the higher this temperature.

| State | Approximate shrinkage temperature | Meaning |
| --- | :---: | --- |
| Untanned rawhide | 55-65 °C | Shrivels in hot water and becomes gelatin |
| Leather tanned with oil and brains | 60-70 °C | The bond is weak, so heat resistance barely rises |
| Leather tanned with vegetable tannin | 75-85 °C | Tannin builds bridges and stabilizes the structure |
| Leather tanned with alum | 70-80 °C | Stable when dry but weak against water |

- Brain tanning has a low degree of tanning chemically and is closer to oiling the fibers so they do not stick. It is the softest but weak against heat, and when wet the tanning components wash out and it can harden again. Smoking compensates for this, so smoking is essential in brain tanning.
- When you have no instruments, cut the thick part and look at the cross-section. If the color has soaked evenly from the edge to the inside, the agent has penetrated completely. If a band of the original color remains in the middle, it is incomplete, and if you dry it as it is, that layer hardens and the whole hide stiffens.
