## 1. Principles of Tanning
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;All techniques for processing hide stem from the properties of a single layer called the dermis. Once you understand what that layer is composed of and what happens when it loses water, you realize that all tanning stages are merely different approaches to solving a single problem.

---
### 1-1. Structure of Hide
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Animal skin consists primarily of three layers. Among these, only the middle layer is intended for human use, while the other two are targets for complete removal.

| Layer | Composition | Treatment in Tanning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Epidermis | Keratinized cells, hair, and keratin | Removed along with hair. Remaining parts make leather stiff and stained |
| Dermis | A 3D intertwined mesh of collagen fiber bundles | Retained exclusively. This constitutes the actual leather |
| Subcutaneous Tissue | Fat, loose connective tissue, and thin fascia | Scraped off entirely. Remaining portions go rancid and block agent penetration |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The collagen fibers of the dermis are not arranged parallel in a single direction, but are intertwined diagonally with one another. This intertwining creates the unique properties of leather. Because some fibers bear the load regardless of which direction it is pulled, it is tough in all directions; and because the fibers can slide past one another, it folds smoothly. This structure is what distinguishes leather from paper or wooden boards.

---
### 1-2. Why It Rots, and Why It Hardens
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If left wet, a skinned hide rots; if simply dried, it hardens.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The reason for rotting is simple. Collagen is a protein, and wet dermis serves as a culture medium equipped with protein, water, and suitable temperatures. As bacteria and fungi begin breaking down collagen, fibers sever and holes form in the hide. On top of this, autolysis caused by the tissue's own enzymes overlaps. In summer temperatures, hair begins falling out within half a day of skinning, which indicates the onset of putrefaction rather than progress in unhairing.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The reason for hardening is even more interesting. In a wet dermis, collagen fibers are surrounded by water molecules, which serve as a lubricant between fibers. However, as water evaporates, fiber surfaces come into direct contact with one another. Because collagen molecule surfaces feature countless hydrogen bonding sites, adjacent fibers bond right on the spot. When thousands of contact points attach simultaneously, the intertwined mesh turns into a solid mass, and the hide hardens like a wooden board.

> **Tanning is the act of filling the spaces between fibers so that even after water escapes, the fibers cannot directly adhere to one another.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;From this perspective, the differences between various tanning methods are merely differences in what is packed between the fibers. Brain tanning introduces phospholipids and oils, vegetable tanning introduces tannin molecules, and alum tanning introduces aluminum complexes between the fibers. How firmly the packed substance bonds with the fibers determines the water resistance and heat resistance of the finished leather.

---
### 1-3. Rawhide vs. Tanned Leather
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fiber adhesion is not always a problem. Utilizing that property as it is results in rawhide. Rawhide is hide that has only had hair and fat removed, dried under tension without tanning; as it dries, the fibers adhere completely, forming an extremely hard, warp-resistant board.

| Classification | Rawhide | Tanned Leather |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Processing | Stretched and dried after unhairing and fleshing | Worked and massaged while drying after tanning agent penetration |
| Dry State | Extremely hard and stiff | Flexible and foldable |
| Wet State | Softens, stretches, and hardens again upon re-drying | Retains shape and returns to original state when dried |
| Decay Resistance | Safe only in a dry state | Does not rot easily even when wet |
| Primary Uses | Thongs, bindings, drum heads, containers, sheaths, shields | Clothing, footwear, gloves, sacks, pouches |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Rawhide thongs are particularly valuable in toolmaking. Wrapping them wet and allowing them to dry causes them to shrink and tighten, creating a binding force unattainable through knotting alone. This is why they are used to secure axe heads to handles or attach arrowheads. However, because rawhide softens again when exposed to rain, water must be blocked using oil or resin.

---
### 1-4. Measuring the Degree of Tanning
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The shrinkage temperature serves as an objective measure for judging how thoroughly a hide has been tanned. When collagen is immersed in water and heated, the triple helix unravels and shrinks rapidly at a certain temperature; this temperature rises as the bond between collagen and the tanning agent strengthens.

| State | Approximate Shrinkage Temperature | Significance |
| --- | :---: | --- |
| Untanned Rawhide | 55–65°C | Shrinks and turns into gelatin when immersed in hot water |
| Brain- and Oil-Tanned Leather | 60–70°C | Bonds are weak, so heat resistance barely increases |
| Vegetable Tannin-Tanned Leather | 75–85°C | Tannins cross-link and stabilize the structure |
| Alum-Tanned Leather | 70–80°C | Stable in dry conditions, but vulnerable to water |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;From a chemical standpoint, brain-tanned leather has a very low degree of tanning, functioning practically by coating the fibers with oil to prevent adhesion. Thus, while offering unmatched softness, it is weak against heat, and if soaked in water, tanning agents wash out, causing it to harden again. The final step—smoking—counteracts this weakness, which is why smoking is a necessity rather than an option in brain tanning.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In situations where testing equipment is unavailable, shrinkage temperature cannot be directly measured, but an alternative judgment method exists. When a thick portion of the hide is cut and its cross-section examined, an even color distribution from edge to center indicates complete agent penetration. If a stripe of original color remains in the middle, tanning is incomplete. Drying the hide with this inner band remaining causes that specific layer to harden, making the entire leather stiff.
