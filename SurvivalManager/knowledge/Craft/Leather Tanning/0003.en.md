## 2. Preparation Steps: Skinning, Dehairing, Fleshing, Neutralizing
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The purpose of the preparation steps is to leave only the collagen fibers of the dermis and open a path for the agent to pass through. Most tanning failures come not from the tanning step but from rushing through these steps.

---
### 2-1. Skinning
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Hide begins to rot the moment it is removed. The limit for handling is a few hours at summer temperatures, and a day even on a cool day.

- Minimize knife work. The more you use the knife, the more the dermis is nicked, and those nicks become holes or thin spots in the finished leather.
- There is a plane between the subcutaneous tissue and the dermis that opens easily. Pushing in with a fist or a blunt stick strips off a good part even without a knife, and it leaves almost no damage, which favors large animals.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If you cannot process it right away, take the water out so bacteria cannot grow, and buy time.

| Method | Principle | Conditions and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salting | Salt draws water out of the tissue by osmotic pressure | Rub 30-50% of the hide's weight in salt evenly onto the flesh side. With too little salt, it rots from the middle |
| Drying | Evaporates water to lower water activity | Dry it stretched taut in the shade with airflow. Dried in direct sunlight, only the surface hardens and the inside rots |
| Freezing | Lowers the temperature to stop bacteria and enzymes | Possible only in winter. Repeated freezing and thawing damages the fibers |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A dried hide must later be revived by soaking in water (soaking). The longer a hide has been dried, the more days it takes for water to soak in, and during that time rot begins if the water is warm. Change the water at least once a day during soaking.

---
### 2-2. Dehairing
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The hair roots are embedded deep in the dermis, so pulling the hair tears the dermis with it. Instead, the protein holding the hair roots (keratin) is dissolved with alkali so that the hair falls out by itself. This is because strong alkali breaks the disulfide bonds that hold keratin together.

- Lye: wood ash steeped in water. The main component is potassium carbonate.
- Calcium hydroxide: shells or limestone burned and mixed with water. Its alkalinity is high and lasts longer, so it suits dehairing better (the reason quicklime is used in industrial processes).

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Target pH | 11-13. Lower than that and the hair does not come out |
| Time required | Three to ten days depending on temperature. Longer when cold |
| How to tell it is done | The hair slides off in handfuls when rubbed by hand |
| If overdone | The alkali also hydrolyzes the collagen, so the hide turns slimy and tears when pulled |
| Side effect | The fiber bundles swell and open, opening a path for the agent to enter |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The side effect is in fact the second purpose, and it influences the result more. When the dermis swells in alkali, the gaps between the fiber bundles widen and the non-collagen substances that held the fibers together dissolve away. A hide that has not been through this will only soak in at the surface however long it is kept in the tanning liquor, leaving the inside as it was.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;An alternative that does not use alkali is natural fermentation. Soak the hide in lukewarm water for a few days and let bacteria break down the epidermis first, and the hair comes out. No materials are needed. However, it is very hard to judge when to stop, and if you are even a little late, the dermis is broken down too and the hide is ruined, and the smell is extreme. If you can get alkali, do not use it.

---
### 2-3. Fleshing
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is the work of scraping off the fat, subcutaneous tissue, and thin membranes stuck on the flesh side of the hide. It is the hardest of the preparation steps, the one most often rushed, and the most common cause of failure.

| What happens if left | Why |
| --- | --- |
| The tanning agent cannot penetrate | Fat repels water, so water-soluble agents cannot get through |
| Only that part hardens stiff | Fibers the agent did not reach fuse as they dry |
| It turns rancid over time | Leftover fat oxidizes, producing a bad smell and discoloring the hide |
| Mold grows | Nutrient-rich subcutaneous tissue feeds mold |
| Uneven drying | Parts of different thickness dry at different speeds and warp |

- Method: drape the hide over a log or round post, press it in place with your body, and scrape with a dull tool as if pushing the material off.
- Tools: a sharp edge cuts the dermis, so a blunt bone or dull iron is more suitable than a sharp knife.
- Why the post is round: the contact surface is narrow, so pressure is concentrated. On a flat board the force spreads and the tissue does not come off.
- Remove the epidermal layer on the hair side too. The thin, slippery film that remains on the surface after dehairing does not let water through and blocks the penetration of the agent; if left, only that side becomes glossy and stiff.

---
### 2-4. Neutralizing
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A hide that has finished dehairing is soaked with alkali, and no tanning method works on it. Tannin is oxidized by alkali and only gives color without bonding, and the emulsion of brain tanning cannot form a stable film under alkaline conditions. The leftover alkali also keeps breaking down collagen.

1. Wash in several changes of water to remove most of the alkali.
2. Neutralize the remaining alkali with a weak acid. Vinegar, sour fruit juice, or fermented grain water is enough. Do not use strong acid, because it hydrolyzes collagen.

| Judgment | Method |
| --- | --- |
| Whether alkali remains | If it feels slippery like soap to the hand, it remains |
| Whether the swelling has gone down | The swollen, taut hide becomes limp and thinner |
| Number of washes | Change once a day when the temperature is low, two or three times when high |
| Sign of completion | The hide hangs limp in water, and clear water comes out when you squeeze it by hand |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When neutralizing is done, squeeze the hide to remove a good deal of water. If the spaces between the fibers are full of water, there is no room for the tanning agent to enter. Twist it, or hang it over a pole and press, until water no longer drips, and then move on to the tanning step.
