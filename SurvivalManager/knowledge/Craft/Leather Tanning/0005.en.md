## 4. Staking and Smoking: Tools and Work Environment
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Successfully penetrating tanning agents into the hide does not automatically produce soft leather. The final two steps—staking and smoking—remain, and far from being optional, these are the critical phases that turn all preceding efforts into tangible results.

---
### 4-1. Staking
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Staking is the process of stretching, massaging, and rubbing the hide continuously as it dries. Why this strenuous labor is necessary even when tanning agents are already present between the fibers becomes clear when examining the forces at play during drying.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;As water remaining between the fibers evaporates, surface tension pulls adjacent fibers toward one another. While this force appears minuscule at any single point, the astronomical number of contact points combines into a substantial aggregate force. Even if tanning agents coat the fiber surfaces, leaving the hide undisturbed during drying allows surface tension to press the fibers tightly together; at certain points, the agent film gets displaced, causing fibers to adhere directly. Staking is the mechanical breaking apart of this adhesion as fast as it occurs.

| Element | Details |
| --- | --- |
| When to Begin | As soon as surface moisture vanishes and no longer wets the hands |
| How Long to Continue | Until the leather is completely dry and feels warm to the touch (usually several hours to a full day) |
| Frequency | Continuously without rest; leaving it unattended for even 15 minutes causes those spots to harden |
| Areas That Harden First | Thin regions like the neck and legs, as well as the edges |
| If Skipped | No matter how thoroughly tanning agents were applied, the result will be rigid, board-like leather |

> **Skipping staking renders all preceding processes meaningless. What ultimately determines the success or failure of tanning is not the chemical agent, but the physical labor spent during drying.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If rigid spots remain after the leather has dried completely, the situation can still be remedied. Re-wet those specific areas, submerge them in tanning solution, and repeat the staking process from the beginning. Conversely, if left untouched to harden completely over an extended period, fiber adhesion becomes permanent, making recovery extremely difficult.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A method to make staking easier is leveraging mechanical advantage. Securing a stake with a dull blade pointing upward and draping the hide over it while pulling with both hands concentrates far greater force along a narrow line than kneading by hand. Tying a rope taut between two trees and working the hide back and forth over it like sawing operates on the same principle and is particularly effective when working large hides alone.

---
### 4-2. Smoking
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Even after completing staking, brain-tanned leather retains one critical vulnerability: re-exposure to water followed by drying causes it to stiffen again. Because the oil film is merely deposited on fiber surfaces rather than chemically bound to collagen, incoming water rearranges or washes away the oil, allowing fibers to adhere once more.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Smoking resolves this issue chemically. Smoke produced by incomplete combustion of wood contains aldehydes such as formaldehyde and glyoxal, alongside various phenolic compounds. Aldehydes react with amino groups on collagen side chains to form covalent cross-links that bridge adjacent chains. Collagen fixed in this manner maintains its structural integrity even in the presence of water, retaining its initial flexibility after wetting and drying. Phenolic compounds additionally inhibit mold and bacteria while repelling insects.

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Requirement | Dense smoke without open flames |
| Good Fuels | Punky rotten wood, damp hardwood chips, pine cones, dry moss |
| Fuels to Avoid | Fresh softwood branches (resins deposit soot on the leather) |
| Temperature | Warm enough to comfortably hold a hand over; excessive heat cooks and shrinks the leather |
| Duration | 20 minutes to an hour per side, then repeated on the reverse |
| Signal of Completion | The color transforms uniformly from tan to dark brown |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most efficient method for smoking is sewing the hide into a bag or cone shape open at the bottom, suspending it over smoldering embers so smoke fills the interior. Allowing smoke to leak out reduces the amount reaching the leather, prolonging the process. Because open flames burn or heat-shrink the leather in seconds, cover embers with ash to extinguish flames and maintain pure smoke generation.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Leather tanned with vegetable tannins already possesses stable chemical bonds, making smoking unnecessary. Smoking as an indispensable finishing touch applies specifically to brain-tanned and oil-tanned leather types.

### 4-3. Tools and Work Environment
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;All tools required for tanning can be fashioned from natural materials. The common rule for these tools is that they must not be sharp, as sharp implements cut into the dermis.

| Tool | Form | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fleshing Beam | A peeled log mounted at an angle | Drapes the hide for body-weight securing; its curved surface concentrates scraping pressure |
| Scraper / Flesher | Dull-edged metal or a split shinbone of a large animal | Scrapes off fat, subcutaneous tissue, and the epidermal layer |
| Tanning Frame | A square frame fashioned from thick branches | Suspends the hide taut using cords laced through perimeter holes |
| Staking Post / Stake | A post topped with a smooth, rounded, dull tip | Drapes and stretches the hide to break fiber adhesion |
| Soaking Vat | A hollowed-out log, large clay vessel, or pit lined with hide | Holds alkaline and tannin solutions |
| Smoking Bag | A hide sewn into a tubular enclosure | Traps smoke to ensure even contact across the hide |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The workspace must be located away from living quarters due to odors and wastewater. Vats undergoing unhairing emit strong sulfur compounds and ammonia odors, fat and tissue from fleshing attract flies in large numbers, and the smell of putrefaction draws predators. Alkaline solutions and spent tanning liquors kill aquatic life if discharged directly and harm those drawing water downstream.

| Item | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| Distance from Dwellings | At least several dozen meters downwind |
| Distance from Water Sources | At least 50 meters from drinking sources; never dump directly into flowing streams |
| Wastewater Disposal | Collect in a pit and cover with soil; neutralize alkaline waste with ash and earth |
| Debris Disposal | Bury or burn fat and tissue scraps |
| Skin Protection | Alkaline solutions cause skin chemical burns; handle with long sticks and rinse immediately upon contact |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Precautions when handling alkaline solutions mirror those in soap making. Calcium hydroxide and lye dissolve skin fats to cause chemical burns, and eye contact causes permanent damage. Because pain onset is often delayed, damage can occur unnoticed; if contact occurs, rinse immediately and thoroughly regardless of pain.

### 4-4. Process Differences Between Fur and Leather
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Processing fur hides with hair intact precludes several steps used in standard leather tanning. These differences stem entirely from the constraint of preserving hair.

| Process | Leather (Hair Removed) | Fur (Hair Preserved) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unhairing | Removes hair using alkalis | Omitted entirely |
| Use of Alkalis | Essential | Prohibited (causes hair slip and damage) |
| Fiber Opening | Achieved via alkaline swelling | Substituted with repeated physical kneading and mild acids |
| Fleshing | Performed on both flesh and hair sides | Flesh side only; hair side remains untouched |
| Tanning Methods | All methods applicable | Brain, oil, and alum methods primary; tannins stain the hair |
| Drying and Staking | Applied across the entire hide | Flesh side massaged only, avoiding crushing the fur |
| Smoking | Both sides | Flesh side only; hair absorbs heavy smoke odors |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The prohibition against using alkalis represents the greatest constraint in fur processing. Because the step that opens spaces between fibers is omitted, tanning agent penetration is poor and must be compensated for through repeated physical kneading and multiple applications of oil. This explains why fur processing requires far more manual labor than standard leather of equivalent size.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The condition of the fur depends heavily on the season. Pelts taken from animals harvested between late autumn and winter are dense and firmly rooted; during spring and summer, molting leaves the fur sparse and prone to slipping upon light rubbing. Hides intended for fur must be selected seasonally. The acquisition and processing of wild animal hides and furs are governed by wildlife protection and management laws; ensure compliance with applicable national legislation within your jurisdiction.
