## 4. Staking, Smoking, Tools, and the Work Environment
### 4-1. Staking
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Staking is pulling, working, and rubbing the hide the whole time it dries. Even when the tanning agent has already gone in, during drying the water between the fibers evaporates and surface tension pulls neighboring fibers together. Left alone, the film of agent is pushed aside at some points and the fibers stick directly. Staking mechanically pulls apart this sticking as soon as it forms.

| Element | Details |
| --- | --- |
| When to start | Once the surface moisture is gone and nothing comes off on your hands |
| When to finish | Until the hide is completely dry and feels warm. Usually a few hours to a day |
| Frequency | Without pause. Left for even 15 minutes, that spot begins to harden |
| Where it hardens first | Thin parts such as the neck and legs, and the edges |
| If skipped | You get a board-like hide no matter how well you applied the agent |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Skipping staking makes all the earlier steps meaningless. What decides the success of tanning is not the agent but the labor while it dries.

- If stiff parts remain after it is fully dry, you can reverse it. Re-wet that part, soak it in the tanning liquor, and stake again from the start. If it is left completely hardened for a long time, the sticking sets and recovery is hard.
- Leverage makes it easy. Fix a dull edge facing up on top of a post stood upright, drape the hide over it, and pull with both hands, and far more force than hand-working is concentrated on a narrow line.
- Tying a rope between trees and rubbing the hide back and forth over it like sawing works on the same principle and is effective when handling a large hide alone.

### 4-2. Smoking
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Even after staking, brain-tanned hide stiffens again when wet and dried. This is because the oil film does not bond with collagen but sits on the surface, so when water comes in it rearranges or washes out and the fibers stick again.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The smoke given off when wood burns incompletely contains aldehydes such as formaldehyde and glyoxal, and various phenolic compounds. Aldehydes bond covalently to the amino groups on collagen side chains and link two neighboring chains. Collagen fixed this way does not lose its structure when water enters, so it stays supple through wetting and drying. The phenolic compounds suppress mold and bacteria and repel insects.

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| What you need | Smoke but no flame |
| Good fuels | Rotten, crumbly wood, pieces of wet hardwood, pinecones, dry moss |
| Fuels to avoid | Green conifer twigs. The resin sticks as soot |
| Temperature | Bearable to the hand. If hot, the hide cooks and shrivels |
| Time | 20 minutes to an hour on one side, then turn over and repeat |
| Sign of completion | The color changes evenly from yellow-brown to deep brown |

- The most efficient way is to sew the hide into a bag shape with only the bottom open and place smoldering embers underneath so that the smoke fills the bag. If smoke leaks, less reaches the hide and only the time lengthens.
- If a flame rises, the hide burns or shrivels from the heat in an instant. Cover the embers with ash to kill the flame and keep only smoke.
- Hide tanned with tannin is already chemically bonded, so smoking is not essential. What is not finished without smoking is the brain-tanning and oil-tanning family.

### 4-3. Tools and the Work Environment
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;All the tools can be made from natural materials. What they have in common is that they are not sharp. Sharp tools cut the hide.

| Tool | Form | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fleshing post | A debarked log set up at a slant | The hide is draped over it and pressed in place with the body. The round surface concentrates pressure |
| Fleshing scraper | A dull-edged piece of iron, or a split shin bone of a large animal | Pushes off fat, subcutaneous tissue, and the epidermal layer |
| Tanning frame | A square frame woven from thick branches | Holes are made along the edge of the hide and it is lashed taut with cord |
| Staking pole | A stake whose end is rounded and blunted | The hide is hooked on it and pulled to break the sticking of the fibers |
| Soaking vessel | A hollowed-out log, a big jar, or a pit lined with hide | Holds the alkali liquor and tannin liquor |
| Smoking bag | A tube sewn from hide | Traps the smoke so that it touches the hide evenly |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Keep the work site away from your shelter. The vat used for dehairing gives off a strong smell of sulfur compounds and ammonia, the fat and tissue from fleshing draw flies in great numbers, and the smell of rot attracts predators. If you let the alkali liquor and spent tanning liquor flow away as they are, aquatic life dies and people downstream who draw water are harmed.

| Item | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| Distance from shelter | At least tens of meters, on the downwind side |
| Distance from water | 50 m or more from water sources. Do not dump directly into running water |
| Waste liquid | Collect in a pit and cover with soil. Neutralize alkali liquor with ash and soil |
| Debris | Bury or burn fat and bits of flesh |
| Skin protection | Handle alkali liquor with a long stick and wash right away if it touches you |

> **Caution:** Calcium hydroxide and lye dissolve the fat in the skin and burn it, and in the eyes they lead to permanent damage. Pain appears late, and people often do not realize they are already injured, so if contact has occurred, wash for a long time immediately, regardless of pain.

### 4-4. Differences in Process Between Fur and Leather
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fur that is finished with the hair on has to keep its hair, so some steps cannot be used.

| Process | Leather (hair removed) | Fur (hair kept) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dehairing | Hair removed with alkali | Not done |
| Using alkali | Essential | Cannot be used. The hair falls out and is damaged |
| Opening the fibers | Solved by alkali swelling | Replaced by repeated working and weak acid |
| Fleshing | Both flesh side and hair side | Flesh side only. The hair side is left alone |
| Tanning method | All methods possible | Mainly brain, oil, and alum. Tannin stains the hair |
| Drying and staking | Over the entire surface | Work only the flesh side, taking care not to mat the hair |
| Smoking | Both sides | Flesh side only. The smell soaks strongly into the hair |

- Because alkali cannot be used, the step that opens the fibers is missing and the agent penetrates poorly. Make up for it with repeated working and several rounds of oiling. For the same size, fur takes far more effort.
- The condition of the hair depends on the season. Animals taken between late autumn and winter have the thickest hair and it does not come out easily. In spring and summer the hair thins from shedding and comes out just from rubbing by hand. For fur, you have to choose the season.
- In ordinary times, acquiring or processing the hides and fur of wild animals is regulated by the Act on the Protection and Management of Wildlife. Check the relevant laws of your country and act only within them.
