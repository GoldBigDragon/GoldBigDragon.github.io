## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This document contains **knowledge for securing disinfectant supplies**. When a disaster drags on, rubbing alcohol is among the first supplies to run out, and it serves as a prerequisite for wound care, tool sterilization, and medical procedures as a whole. At the same time, alcohol functions as a fuel, extraction solvent, and preservative, giving it versatile applications if it can be produced.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;However, this knowledge carries greater danger than virtually any other hygiene topic. There are two primary risks. One is physical: alcohol vapor ignites at low temperatures, and sealed devices explode under pressure. The other is a far quieter and far more lethal risk: **methanol**. Methanol is indistinguishable from ethanol in both taste and smell; after ingestion, no symptoms appear for over half a day before progressing to blindness and death. This is why discarding foreshots is emphasized so heavily throughout this document.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Illicit distillation for consumption is illegal in most countries, including the Republic of Korea.** This document compiles fundamental principles assuming a scenario where medical disinfectants and fuel cannot be obtained, and does not encourage or guide homebrewing for drinking. Furthermore, once the principles are understood, it becomes clear that drinking distillate produced without precise measurement is gambling against the odds of methanol poisoning.
