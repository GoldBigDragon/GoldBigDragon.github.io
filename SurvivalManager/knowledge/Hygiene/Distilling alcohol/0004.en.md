## 3. Three Pathways of Concentration - Distillation, Freezing, and Desiccation
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To raise the 12–18% ABV of the fermentation wash to the 60%+ required for disinfection, water must be removed. There are three methods, each operating on different physical principles and leaving behind **different impurities.** This difference is what determines safety.

---
### 3-1. Distillation - Utilizing Differences in Boiling Points

| Substance | Boiling Point at 1 atm |
| --- | --- |
| Methanol | Approx. 64.7°C |
| Ethanol | Approx. 78.3°C |
| Water | Approx. 100°C |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Ethanol boils at 78.3°C, while water boils at 100°C. When a liquid mixture is heated, the component with the lower boiling point enters the vapor phase in greater proportions; collecting and condensing that vapor yields a liquid richer in alcohol than the original. That is the essence of distillation.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;However, it is not the case that only ethanol comes off at 78.3°C and only water at 100°C. When two liquids are mixed, they evaporate together at intermediate temperatures, and as temperature rises, the proportion of water vapor increases. Thus, distillation is **a shift in proportions, not an absolute separation.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Furthermore, this shift encounters an insurmountable barrier.

> **Ethanol and water form an azeotrope at approximately 95.6% concentration. At this concentration, the vapor composition matches the liquid composition, meaning further distillation cannot raise the ABV any higher.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In practice, even reaching this limit is difficult. A single distillation using a simple setup yields roughly 40–60% ABV depending on the wash strength, and redistilling raises it slightly higher. Reaching the 70% required for disinfection usually demands two to three distillation runs. With each run, volume decreases significantly.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The apparatus structure is divided into three distinct parts, each performing a clear role.

| Part | Function | Factors Determining Performance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heating Vessel (Still) | Heats the wash to produce vapor | Ability to **slowly** maintain temperature between 78–90°C |
| Vapor Tube (Column) | Transfers vapor to the condenser | Length and slope; longer tubes allow heavier components to fall back |
| Condenser | Cools vapor back into liquid | Cooling surface area, temp difference, vapor residence time |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If condenser efficiency is low, alcohol vapor escapes without turning into liquid, causing loss and accumulating flammable vapor in the surroundings. Cooling is achieved by submerging the tube in cold water or running cold water along the outside of the tube. Arranging the **cooling water to flow in the direction opposite to the vapor** maintains a temperature gradient across the entire length, greatly increasing efficiency.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Here arises the most critical safety principle:

> **No point in a distillation apparatus must ever be completely sealed. If the vapor outlet becomes blocked, internal pressure rises rapidly until the apparatus explodes, spilling hot alcohol over open flames.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Bends in the vapor tube causing liquid pooling, clogged condensers, or overly tight seals at joints all lead to the exact same catastrophe. Vapor must always have an open path to escape. Additionally, ethanol has a low flash point, igniting even at temperatures slightly above ambient. Because alcohol vapor is heavier than air, it flows along the floor and can ignite from a distant spark. Thus, **avoiding direct-fire heating and using a double-boiler (water bath)**, operating outdoors or in well-ventilated areas, and keeping distance between fire and apparatus are mandatory rules.

---
### 3-2. Freeze Concentration - A Simple Yet Dangerous Method
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Water freezes at 0°C, whereas ethanol freezes at a much lower temperature. Freezing the fermentation wash causes water to turn into ice first; skimming away that ice increases the alcohol concentration of the remaining liquid. It requires neither fire nor complex apparatus—only cold weather.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;However, this method carries a lethal flaw.

> **While distillation separates components by boiling point, freezing removes only water and separates nothing else. Methanol and fusel oils do not freeze with the water; they remain with the ethanol and concentrate alongside it.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In other words, freeze concentration **concentrates impurities along with the alcohol.** While distillation removes a significant portion of methanol by discarding foreshots, freeze concentration offers no cut point to discard anything. This method can be considered only under the strict premise that it will be used solely for disinfection and never ingested.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There are performance limitations as well. As alcohol concentration rises, the freezing point drops, requiring progressively lower temperatures. Under realistic natural cold conditions, exceeding ~30% ABV is difficult. Disinfectant-level concentration cannot be reached by freezing alone.

---
### 3-3. Desiccants - Bypassing the Azeotrope
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Due to the azeotrope, distillation caps out at 95.6%, but chemically binding water allows going beyond that limit. While laboratories use zeolites or anhydrous salts, **quicklime (calcium oxide)** is the realistic option in disaster situations. Quicklime reacts with water to become slaked lime, trapping water without reacting with ethanol.

| Method | Achievable Concentration | Characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single Distillation | 40–60% | Simple setup; allows discarding foreshots |
| Repeated Distillation | 70–90% | High volume loss; consumes substantial fuel |
| Freeze Concentration | ~30% | Concentrates impurities together; extremely dangerous for drinking |
| Desiccant Treatment | 95%+ | Bypasses azeotrope; risk of chemical contamination |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;However, for disinfection purposes, there is virtually no reason to surpass the azeotropic limit. As shown earlier, **70% is optimal for disinfection, whereas 95% is actually weaker.** Laboriously producing near-absolute alcohol only to dilute it back with water is an unnecessary task. Desiccant concentration is meaningful for fuel or anhydrous solvent applications, which are generally irrelevant in disaster scenarios.
