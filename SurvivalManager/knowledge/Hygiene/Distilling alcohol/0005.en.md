## 4. Foreshots and Methanol, Checking ABV, and Applications
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Distillate is not the exact same substance from beginning to end. Because various compounds with different boiling points come off sequentially, its chemical composition varies greatly depending on when it is collected. Segmenting these fractions and discarding the initial portion is the single most critical step in distillation.

---
### 4-1. Why Foreshots Must Be Discarded
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Liquid emerging at the beginning of distillation is called foreshots. Compounds with boiling points lower than ethanol concentrate heavily in this initial fraction.

| Fraction | Primary Components | Handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foreshots (Heads) | Acetone, acetaldehyde, ethyl acetate, **methanol** | Discard completely |
| Heart (Hearts) | Primarily ethanol | Collect and utilize |
| Tails | Fusel oils (higher alcohols), water | Low toxicity, but causes headaches and ABV drops |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It is necessary to understand where methanol originates. When pectin in plant cell walls is broken down by enzymes, methanol is cleaved off. Consequently, raw materials high in pectin—particularly **fruit peels and seeds, and stone fruits like apples and plums**—generate significant methanol, whereas grain fermentations yield relatively little. Fermenting with peels and seeds included is precisely what increases this risk.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Methanol itself is not overwhelmingly toxic. The problem lies in how the human body metabolizes it. Enzymes in the liver convert methanol into formaldehyde, which is then converted into **formic acid**. This formic acid paralyzes cellular energy production, destroys the optic nerve, and causes metabolic acidosis in the blood. In short, it is not the methanol itself, but the human body attempting to break it down that creates the deadly poison.

> **Methanol cannot be distinguished from ethanol by taste or smell. Immediately after ingestion, no symptoms appear, and vision begins to blur only after 12–24 hours have passed. By then, damage is irreversible. As little as 10 mL can cause blindness, and 30 mL can be fatal.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The long latent period is extended even further when co-ingested with ethanol, as liver enzymes prioritize metabolizing ethanol, delaying the breakdown of methanol. This explains the classic clinical progression where vision loss begins the day after drinking. Group methanol poisoning incidents recurring worldwide all share this exact mechanism, and treatment requires antidotes and hemodialysis—**treatments unobtainable in a disaster situation.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The standard for how much to discard should be set conservatively. Common rules of thumb involve discarding a fixed percentage of the initial wash volume per distillation run, or unconditionally discarding a generous initial portion. In the absence of precise measurement equipment, **discarding more than feels wasteful** is the only safe judgment. Discarded foreshots should be stored separately, labeled for fuel or cleaning use, and never kept in beverage containers.

---
### 4-2. How to Check ABV
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Knowing whether produced alcohol meets the required concentration for disinfection is practically essential, because anything falling below 60% cannot be trusted as a disinfectant.

| Method | Principle | Accuracy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alcohol Hydrometer | Reads concentration via density, as ethanol is lighter than water | Most accurate; requires temperature correction |
| Volume & Weight Measurement | Compares weight against an equal volume of water | Usable provided scales are precise |
| Bubble Persistence | Estimates ABV by how long bubbles remain after shaking | Relies on experience; rough estimate |
| Spoon Combustion Test | Judges by whether it ignites | **Inaccurate and dangerous** |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Although the spoon combustion test is widely known, it is unsuitable for determining disinfectant concentration. Alcohol begins to ignite around 50% ABV, but this threshold fluctuates wildly depending on temperature, container type, and flame conditions. Most importantly, **ignition merely proves it has exceeded 50%, not that it has reached the 60–70% required for disinfection.** Furthermore, alcohol flames are virtually invisible in bright light, easily leading to burns and fires.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Caution is also required when using a hydrometer. Hydrometers are calibrated assuming the liquid consists solely of ethanol and water. If residual sugars or other solutes remain, added weight causes falsely low ABV readings. Therefore, do not use it directly on fermentation wash; **use it on post-distillation distillate.**

---
### 4-3. Non-Disinfectant Uses and Legal Issues
| Application | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Disinfection | Adjust to 60–90% for use on hands, tools, and surfaces |
| Fuel | Used in alcohol stoves with virtually no soot output |
| Extraction Solvent | Used in making tinctures to extract active botanical compounds |
| Preservation | Used for specimen preservation and seed surface sterilization |
| Cleaning | Dissolves grease, oil, and resins |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When used as an extraction solvent, requirements run opposite to disinfection. Water-soluble compounds favor lower ABV, while lipophilic (oil-like) compounds favor higher ABV; concentrations are adjusted according to purpose. When used as fuel, lower water content yields better heat output, making higher ABV advantageous.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Finally, **unlicensed production of alcoholic beverages—especially distilled spirits—is illegal in most countries, including the Republic of Korea.** Making it for disinfection does not automatically create an exception, and sale or distribution is treated even more severely. This document compiles fundamental principles assuming a scenario where medical supplies and fuel cannot be obtained due to severed supply chains, and is not knowledge for normal times.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;And anyone who has followed the principles through to the end will reach a clear conclusion: in a situation where the exact amount of foreshots to discard cannot be verified by instruments, where residual methanol cannot be detected by human senses, and where no medical means exist to treat poisoning, **liquid produced via this method must be used strictly for disinfection and fuel, and NEVER ingested.** This is not merely a legal issue, but a matter of probability.
