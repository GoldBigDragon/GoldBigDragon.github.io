## 1. Conditions for Mold Growth
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mold is not a plant but a fungus. It cannot photosynthesize, so it must break down and eat organic matter, and breakdown needs water. Spores endure dryness for years and germinate when conditions are met. Spores cannot be eliminated, so the response to mold is not a fight to eliminate spores but a fight to deny them conditions.

---
### 1-1. Germination Conditions
| Condition | Threshold | Meaning in reality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moisture | Surface relative humidity of 70% or more | Above 80%, proliferation becomes explosively faster |
| Temperature | Generally 0-50 °C, optimum 20-30 °C | The temperature comfortable for people is also optimal for mold |
| Nutrients | Cellulose, starch, protein, oil | Paper, wood, glue, dust, skin oil, food residue are enough |
| Time | 24-48 hours | Once this window passes, visible colonies begin |

- Temperature and nutrients are effectively uncontrollable. A temperature people can tolerate also suits mold, and a single layer of dust is enough nutrition. That mold blooms even on surfaces like cement or glass is because it feeds on the dust and skin oil on the surface. Moisture is the only variable people can act on.
- What matters is not the humidity of the air but the humidity of the surface. Mold feeds not on water in the air but on water attached to surfaces. Even if the hygrometer in the middle of a room reads 55%, the surface relative humidity of a cold north wall can exceed 90%. This is why, when wallpaper is peeled off, the rest of the room is fine and only one wall is pitch black.
- Water activity is a value from 0 to 1 expressing how freely available the water in a material is. Mold generally requires 0.8 or more, and xerophilic molds such as Aspergillus grow even near 0.7. Food cured in salt or sugar does not spoil easily because they hold on to the water and lower water activity, not because they kill mold.

---
### 1-2. 24-48 Hours
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If wet materials are dried within 24-48 hours after flooding or a leak, mold mostly does not grow. This figure comes from the time it takes for spores to germinate and extend hyphae.

| Elapsed time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0-24 hours | Spores absorb water and prepare to germinate. Nothing is visible yet |
| 24-48 hours | Germination and hyphal extension begin. Still inconspicuous |
| 3-7 days | Colonies form, and color and odor appear |
| 1-2 weeks and after | Spores are produced and spread through the air, and it spreads elsewhere |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;By the time it is visible, it is already the third stage. The contest against mold is decided in the first two days, when nothing is visible.

- The first thing to do in a flooded house is not to look for mold but to drain the water and let air through. If you leave wet carpets and mattresses and the insulation in water-filled walls as they are and wipe only the surface, you waste two days.
- Water in places you cannot see is easy to miss. Inside walls, under floors, behind furniture, and inside insulation stay wet for weeks even after the surface is dry, and mold growing there gives off spores and odor without being seen. If the surface is dry but a musty smell persists, that is the signal.

---
### 1-3. Humidity Control Over Removal
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Wiping off removes what has already grown, while removing moisture removes everything that would grow in the future. Cleaning alone also cannot fully remove it. The visible black stains are the spore structures the hyphae have pushed outward, and the body of the mold is the hyphae extending into the material. Even if you wipe off the spores, the hyphae remain, and if water enters again it returns to its original state within days.

| Approach | Range of effect | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surface cleaning | Visible colonies and spores | Days to weeks if moisture remains |
| Applying a disinfectant | Surviving hyphae on the surface | Ends when the agent dries. Almost no residual effect |
| Removing porous material | Even hyphae embedded inside | Permanent |
| Drying and humidity control | All species, all locations | Permanent as long as conditions hold |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The order is always the same. Cut off the source of water, dry, and only then wipe or discard. Cleaning without cutting off the water repeats the same job endlessly. In a disaster, time and stamina are the most expensive resources, so keep to this order.
