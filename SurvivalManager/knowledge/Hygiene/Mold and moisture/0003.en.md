## 2. Health Effects and Mycotoxins
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mold harms people by three routes, and the responses differ. Inhaling spores and fragments, eating toxins the mold has made, and, rarely, infection in which the mold itself grows in the body.

---
### 2-1. Inhaling Spores
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mold spores are generally 2-10 μm, so they are not filtered out in the nose and throat and reach deep into the bronchi and lungs. Fragments of hyphae and fine surface structures are blown up with them.

| Reaction | Content | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Irritation | Stinging eyes, nose, and throat, cough, headache | Possible in anyone |
| Allergy | Rhinitis, conjunctivitis, triggering and worsening of asthma attacks | Repeatedly in sensitized people |
| Hypersensitivity pneumonitis | Fever and shortness of breath after repeated inhalation, lung fibrosis | When exposed continuously to high concentrations |
| Fungal infection | Pulmonary aspergillosis and the like | Limited to people with suppressed immunity |

- The World Health Organization concludes that damp, moldy indoor environments are linked to respiratory symptoms, the onset and worsening of asthma, and increased respiratory infections. This link is tied not to specific mold species but to dampness itself. The distinction that only black mold is dangerous and white mold is fine does not hold. Mold growing is an indicator that the space is continuously wet, and that dampness also increases mites and bacteria.
- The most dangerous moment is the moment of cleaning. Rubbing a dry mold surface with a brush or dusting it with a dry rag sends the surface spores up all at once. Wet the surface first, then begin.

---
### 2-2. Mycotoxins
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Some molds, as they grow, make toxic substances (mold toxins, mycotoxins) as secondary metabolites. Mycotoxins are chemicals the mold has excreted, not the body of the mold, so even if you kill the mold, the toxin remains.

| Toxin | Producing mold | What is mainly contaminated | Toxicity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aflatoxin | Aspergillus flavus and others | Peanuts, corn, nuts, grain, chili powder | Liver toxicity and liver cancer, a powerful carcinogen |
| Ochratoxin A | Aspergillus, Penicillium | Grain, beans, raisins, coffee | Kidney toxicity |
| Patulin | Penicillium expansum | Apples with rotten parts and their processed products | Digestive irritation, immunotoxicity |
| Deoxynivalenol | Fusarium | Wheat, barley, corn | Vomiting and digestive symptoms |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most important property is heat stability. Aflatoxin is barely broken down at ordinary cooking and heating temperatures, and meaningful destruction requires temperatures well above 250 °C. None of boiling, cooking rice, stir-frying, or baking removes mycotoxins.

> **Caution:** Boiling kills bacteria but mycotoxins remain. Moldy grain does not become safe by heating.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In a disaster where food is scarce, what is hardest to throw away is exactly what molds readily. Grain sacks, corn, peanuts, and dried chili are items where aflatoxin readily forms, and their high caloric density makes them hard to give up. Aflatoxin poisoning causes liver failure when acute and liver cancer when chronic, and there is no means of detoxification.

---
### 2-3. Moldy Food
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Whether you can cut out only the moldy part and eat the rest depends on how deep the hyphae have gone in. Hyphae spread much wider than the visible stain, and the softer the tissue and the more water it has, the farther they extend.

| Category | Examples | Judgment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Firm, low water | Hard vegetables, hard cheese, dry salami | Cut out with a generous margin away from the mold, and the rest can be used |
| Soft, high water | Bread, soft fruit, jam, yogurt, cooked food | Hyphae are spread throughout, so discard it all |
| Kernels and powders | Grain, nuts, flour, chili powder, dried beans | A toxin problem, so suspect the whole sack, not only what is visible |
| Liquids | Soup, juice, fermented liquid | Discard it all |

- Judge grain especially conservatively. Mycotoxins can be produced before mold becomes visible, and they are not distributed uniformly even within a single sack, so testing only a part tells you nothing. If there is a musty smell, discolored kernels, clumping, or traces of crushed flour, exclude that sack from human consumption.
- Feeding it to livestock is not a safe disposal either. Aflatoxin fed to dairy cows comes out in the milk in the form of a metabolite, and in poultry and pigs it causes growth reduction and death. Discard contaminated grain rather than using it as feed. Bury or burn it, and fix storage conditions first so that the next harvest does not end up in the same place.
