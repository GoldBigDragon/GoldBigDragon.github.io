## 2. Health Effects and Mycotoxins
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mold harms human health through three distinct pathways, each with different properties: first, **inhaling** spores and fragments; second, **ingesting** toxic compounds produced by mold; and third, though rare, **infection**, where mold grows inside the human body. Because countermeasures differ for each pathway, they must be clearly distinguished.

---
### 2-1. Problems of Inhaling Spores
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mold spores generally measure 2–10 μm in size. This size range allows them to bypass filtration in the nose and throat, reaching deep into the bronchi and lungs. Fragmented hyphae and microscopic structural debris resembling fine glass shards are also aerosolized alongside spores.

| Reaction | Details | Target Population |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Irritation | Stinging in eyes, nose, and throat; coughing; headaches | Can manifest in anyone |
| Allergy | Rhinitis, conjunctivitis, triggering and exacerbation of asthma attacks | Repeatedly in sensitized individuals |
| Hypersensitivity Pneumonitis | Fever, dyspnea, and lung fibrosis following repeated inhalation | Chronic exposure to high concentrations |
| Fungal Infection | Pulmonary aspergillosis, etc. | Strictly limited to immunocompromised individuals |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The World Health Organization states that damp, moldy indoor environments are linked to respiratory symptoms, onset and exacerbation of asthma, and increased respiratory infections. Crucially, this association is **linked to dampness itself, rather than specific mold species.** The notion that black mold is dangerous while white mold is harmless holds no scientific validity. The very presence of mold serves as an indicator that the space is persistently damp, and that dampness simultaneously fosters dust mites and bacteria.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most hazardous moment occurs **during the cleaning process.** Scrubbing dry moldy surfaces with a brush or dusting with a dry cloth causes accumulated surface spores to launch into the air all at once. Mold abatement must always begin **after thoroughly wetting the surface.**

---
### 2-2. Mycotoxins
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Certain molds produce toxic secondary metabolites during growth, known as mycotoxins. A critical fact to understand is that mycotoxins are **chemical compounds secreted by mold, not the mold organism itself.** Killing the mold leaves the toxins intact on the surface.

| Toxin | Producing Mold | Primary Contaminants | Toxicity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aflatoxin | *Aspergillus flavus*, etc. | Peanuts, corn, tree nuts, grains, chili powder | Hepatotoxicity, liver cancer; potent carcinogen |
| Ochratoxin A | *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium* | Grains, beans, raisins, coffee | Nephrotoxicity |
| Patulin | *Penicillium expansum* | Apples with decayed spots and processed apple products | Gastrointestinal irritation, immunotoxicity |
| Deoxynivalenol | *Fusarium* | Wheat, barley, corn | Vomiting and gastrointestinal distress |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most critical property of mycotoxins is their **thermal stability.** Aflatoxin undergoes almost no decomposition under standard cooking and heating temperatures, requiring temperatures well over 250°C for significant breakdown. Neither boiling, steaming, roasting, nor baking destroys mycotoxins.

> **Boiling kills bacteria, but mycotoxins remain. Moldy grains do not become safe through heating.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This fact is particularly cruel in disaster scenarios because the items most susceptible to mold are precisely those most painful to discard during food shortages. Sacks of grain, corn, peanuts, and dried chili peppers are all prime candidates for aflatoxin contamination, yet their high caloric density makes them difficult to abandon. However, aflatoxin poisoning causes acute liver failure and chronic liver cancer, with no medical antidote available.

---
### 2-3. Moldy Foods
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Whether discarding only the moldy portion of food is acceptable depends on **how deeply the hyphae have penetrated.** Hyphae spread far beyond visible surface stains, penetrating further in softer, higher-moisture foods.

| Category | Examples | Judgment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hard & Low Moisture | Hard vegetables, hard cheeses, hard salami | Safe to trim generously around the mold spot and consume the remainder |
| Soft & High Moisture | Bread, soft fruits, jams, yogurt, cooked foods | Discard entirely; hyphae permeate the entire volume |
| Whole Grains & Powders | Grains, nuts, flour, chili powder, dried beans | Discard entirely; suspect the whole batch due to invisible toxins |
| Liquids | Soups, juices, fermented liquids | Discard entirely |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Decisions regarding grains must be exceptionally conservative. Mycotoxins can be synthesized before mold becomes visible to the naked eye, and because toxin distribution is non-uniform within a single sack, sampling a portion provides no guarantee. If a sack exhibits a musty odor, grain discoloration, clumping, or crushed powder residue, it must be excluded from human consumption.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Feeding contaminated grain to livestock is equally unsafe. Aflatoxin ingested by dairy cows transfers into milk as toxic metabolites, while in poultry and swine it induces growth stunting and mortality. Ultimately, the proper disposal of contaminated grain is **waste destruction, not animal feed.** Bury or incinerate the contaminated batch, and correct storage conditions to protect future harvests.
