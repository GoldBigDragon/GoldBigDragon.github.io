## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mold spores float everywhere. The reason they do not grow in ordinary times is that it is not damp enough, and heating, ventilation, and drainage maintain this dryness. When flooding or a power outage releases these restraints, mold appears within days.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Removing mold is a matter of moisture, not cleaning. Mold on a wet wall comes back even if wiped, and if you dry the wall, it spreads no further even without wiping.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This document covers why 70% relative humidity is the borderline, why the 24-48 hours after flooding are decisive, why wiping gypsum board is useless while wiping tile finishes the job, and why moldy grain is not safe even if boiled. On the premise that there is no electric dehumidifier or blower, it sets out the principles of handling moisture with airflow, temperature difference, and natural materials.
