## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mold is not a problem created by a disaster; rather, a disaster **unlocks the inhibitory mechanisms that previously prevented mold proliferation**. Mold spores float everywhere at all times, settling on walls, clothing, and grains at this very moment. In normal times, the sole reason these spores fail to grow is that **surfaces are not damp enough to support growth**. Heating, ventilation, and drainage merely maintain that state of dryness.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This is why mold appears within days following flooding or power outages. It emerges not because surfaces were left unwashed, but because they remained wet. Missing this direction of cause and effect derails the entire response. No matter how many times mold on a wall is wiped away, as long as the wall remains wet, the mold will continuously return. Conversely, drying the wall stops mold proliferation even without wiping. **Mold removal is a matter of moisture control, not cleaning.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This document is not a checklist of mold removal methods. Instead, it examines why 70% relative humidity serves as the critical threshold, why the 24–48 hour window post-flooding is decisive, why wiping drywall (plasterboard) is futile while wiping tiles resolves the issue, and why moldy grain remains unsafe even after boiling. Operating under the assumption that electric dehumidifiers and blowers are unavailable, this document outlines the principles of managing moisture using airflow, temperature differentials, and natural materials alone.
