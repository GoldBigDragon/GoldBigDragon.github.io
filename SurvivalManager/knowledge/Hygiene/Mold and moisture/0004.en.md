## 3. Responses by Material
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The branching point in dealing with mold is not which agent you use but whether that surface absorbs water. The body of the mold is the hyphae extending into the material, so whether to solve it by wiping or discard it depends on whether the material is one the hyphae can enter.

---
### 3-1. Non-Porous and Porous
| Category | Material | Penetration of hyphae | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Non-porous | Glass, metal, glazed tile, hard plastic, ceramics | Attach only to the surface | Scrub and dry completely, and you are done |
| Semi-porous | Wood, concrete, brick, mortar | Shallowly beneath the surface | Scrape or shave off and dry thoroughly |
| Porous | Gypsum board, insulation, ceiling tile, carpet, cloth, paper, books | Completely inside | Removal is the rule |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There are three reasons wiping porous material is useless.

- The hyphae are already inside the material, so they survive wiping the surface.
- The cleaning liquid soaks into the material and actually supplies more water.
- Even if the mold is killed, dead spores and hyphal fragments remain as allergens. Disinfection does not remove allergic reactions.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mold in gypsum board and insulation is not something to wipe but something to cut out.

- Cloth and clothes: cotton and linen that can be boiled can often be revived with boiling water, alkaline washing, and thorough sun drying. Stains remain. Quilts, mattresses, cushions, and leather do not dry through to the inside and are hard to recover.
- Books and paper: after drying completely, brushing off the spores with a soft brush keeps it from spreading further. Stains already formed and weakened fibers cannot be reversed.
- Set the extent of removal wider than the visible mold. The hyphae have already extended beyond the stain, and the area the water soaked is always wider than the area that changed color.
- Wrap the cut-out pieces in plastic or cloth on the spot and carry them away. Do not scatter spores around the whole house while carrying them.

---
### 3-2. Cleaning and Suppression
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Killing and physically removing are different. Scrubbing the surface hyphae and spores off with a brush and water is the most reliable measure, and the agents below are supplementary means to reduce what remains and slow recurrence.

| Material | Principle of action | Actual range of effect | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vinegar (5% acetic acid) | Disrupts cell membranes and enzymes through acidity | Effective on some species. Suited to non-porous surfaces | Does not work on some species and does not reach deep into porous material |
| Salt water (saturated) | Lowers water activity through osmotic pressure | Suppression, preservation | Cannot kill, and salt remains when it dries |
| Lye (potassium carbonate) | Denatures proteins and lipids with strong alkali | Strong cleaning power removes it together with grease | Damages skin and metal, so rinsing is needed |
| Boric acid, borax | Interferes with metabolism and remains on the surface | Soaks into wood for lasting suppression | Toxic, so forbidden around food and children |
| Alcohol 70% | Denatures proteins and dissolves membranes | Acts quickly for surface disinfection | Evaporates, with no residual effect |
| Sunlight (ultraviolet) | Damages DNA of surface spores | The surface of cloth and objects hung to dry | Does not reach shaded inner parts |

- Vinegar is the most widely used but not a cure-all. It works well on some molds and hardly at all on others. Diluted with water its effect drops quickly, so use it close to undiluted. It has almost no residual toxicity and no harm besides the smell, so it is a reasonable choice in spaces where people continue to live.
- Sunlight is the cheapest means in a disaster. The part that actually does the work is drying and temperature more than ultraviolet. Ultraviolet cannot pass through thick objects, so hang clothes inside out and stand books with the pages fanned open. Leaving them until completely dry is the key.

---
### 3-3. Bleach
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sodium hypochlorite bleach is a powerful disinfectant on non-porous surfaces. On porous materials only the water soaks in and the chlorine stays on the surface so the effect drops sharply, and it actually supplies moisture to the inside. It is in the same vein that the US Environmental Protection Agency does not recommend routine use of biocides in mold cleanup. The basic approach is to scrub off with detergent and water and dry completely, and bleach is limited to smooth surfaces such as tile, toilets, and countertops.

> **Caution:** Mixing bleach with ammonia-containing cleaners produces chloramine gas, and mixing it with vinegar or acidic cleaners produces chlorine gas. Both gases can kill a person in a confined space.

- Mixing often happens even without any intention to mix. Using another cleaner right after mopping a floor with bleach, reusing the same bucket without rinsing, or using the two one after the other in a bathroom is enough.
- Urine also contains ammonia, so do not pour bleach on places where urine has pooled.
- Use only one cleaner at a time, and rinse thoroughly with water before using the next.

---
### 3-4. Respiratory Protection During Work
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mold removal work exposes you to far higher concentrations than living in a space with mold. This is because spores are released in large numbers while scraping, tearing, and cutting.

| Protection | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| Wet it first | Wetting the surface with water or cleaning liquid keeps spores from flying up. The most effective measure |
| Ventilation | Open windows and doors to create a flow to the outside. A direction blowing toward the inside of the house spreads contamination |
| Respiratory protection | At minimum cover the nose and mouth with several layers of cloth, but use it understanding its limits |
| Eyes and skin | Spores, alkali, and chlorine all irritate eyes and mucous membranes, so cover up and wear gloves |
| After work | Take off clothes on the spot and wash them separately, and wash your body |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;How far a cloth mask blocks spores, and why it is useless against gases such as the smell of mold, are in the Improvised respiratory protection document. Wetting the surface so that spores do not rise in the first place is far more effective than raising the grade of the mask.
