## 1. Disinfection Levels and Cleaning
### 1-1. Distinguishing the Terms
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Four words that get mixed together in daily life reach different levels. If you do not decide the level you need, you waste resources on excessive means or are falsely reassured by inadequate ones.

| Term | Level reached | Typical methods |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cleaning | Physical removal of dirt and microbes | Water, soap, brush, running water |
| Sanitizing | Reducing microbe numbers to a public-health-safe level | Hot water rinse, dilute chlorine |
| Disinfection | Killing or inactivating pathogenic microbes other than spores | Boiling, chlorine, alcohol, iodine |
| Sterilization | Eliminating all microbes, including spores | Autoclave 121 °C for 15 minutes or more, dry heat 160 °C or more for a long time |

- Spores: a dormant state in which some bacteria wrap themselves in a thick shell when the environment worsens. They withstand boiling water, chlorine, and alcohol alike. Tetanus, botulinum, and anthrax bacteria are examples. To be sure of killing them, you must apply pressure to reach 121 °C or more.
- In a disaster, disinfection is enough in most cases. Sterilization is practically impossible and usually not needed. What kills people in a disaster is diarrheal disease and wound infection, and since most causes are bacteria, viruses, and protozoa that do not form spores, boiling catches them.

| Pathogen | Resistance | Characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lipid-enveloped viruses | Weakest | Easily destroyed by soap and alcohol |
| Ordinary bacteria | Weak | Killed well by boiling and chlorine |
| Non-enveloped viruses | Medium | Relatively resistant to alcohol |
| Tuberculosis bacteria | Medium to high | High resistance because of their lipid cell wall |
| Protozoan cysts | Strong | Not killed well by chlorine. Must be boiled or filtered |
| Bacterial spores | Strongest | Withstand boiling water and most agents |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If you are worried about Cryptosporidium in water, do not increase the chlorine; boil or filter it. If soil has gotten into a wound, removing the foreign matter comes before applying disinfectant.

---
### 1-2. Why Cleaning Comes Before Disinfection
- If disinfectant is sprayed on a dirty surface, most of its potency is consumed by organic matter, not microbes. Organic compounds in blood, feces, food residue, grease, and soil react immediately with oxidizing disinfectants such as chlorine and hydrogen peroxide and use them up (the chlorine demand in water management).
- Organic matter also acts as a shield. Disinfectant does not reach bacteria inside dried blood or a grease film, so only the surface is disinfected and the inside is left as is.
- Cleaning alone gives a considerable reduction. Running water and friction wash microbes off the surface, and soap destroys microbes with a lipid envelope. Without disinfectant, cleaning alone does a considerable share.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Disinfection works only on a clean surface. Disinfecting without washing uses up the agent and gets no result.

### 1-3. Variables That Determine Effectiveness
| Variable | Content | Failure examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concentration | Is there enough active ingredient | Bleach that has decomposed in long storage, an overly diluted solution |
| Contact time | Did it stay in contact long enough | Wiping off right after spraying |
| Organic load | Is the surface clean | Disinfecting with blood and soil left on |
| Temperature and pH | Are the reaction conditions right | Chlorine in cold water, chlorine in alkaline water |

- What most often breaks down in a disaster is contact time. Disinfection is a chemical reaction and needs time. Water treated with chlorine must wait 30 minutes, and disinfectant sprayed on a surface is left wet until it dries. Skipping it because you are in a hurry is the same as nothing having happened.
- Chemical reactions are generally faster at higher temperatures, so in cold water wait longer even at the same concentration.
- Chlorine is sensitive to pH. When the water tilts alkaline, the strongly germicidal form decreases and effectiveness drops noticeably. This is why disinfecting with chlorine dishes washed in lye without rinsing them makes it work worse.
