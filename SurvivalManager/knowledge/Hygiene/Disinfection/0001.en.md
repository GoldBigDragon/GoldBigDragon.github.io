## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Disinfection has levels. Each pathogen resists differently, and even the same agent gives different results depending on concentration, contact time, and surface condition. There are three common failures.

- Spraying disinfectant on a dirty surface. Organic matter uses up the disinfectant first and it never reaches the microbes.
- Not allowing contact time. If you wipe it off right after spraying, most disinfectants disappear before they act.
- Using it on a target it does not work on. Chlorine is weak against Cryptosporidium oocysts, and alcohol cannot kill bacterial spores.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This document covers the principles of disinfection (boiling, pasteurization, solar disinfection, chlorine, alcohol, lye, lime). It centers on disinfection possible with fire, sunlight, and ash, on the premise that electricity and manufactured agents are cut off.
