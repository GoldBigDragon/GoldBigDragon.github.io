## 2. Physical Disinfection
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Physical disinfection leaves no residue, leaves no taste or smell, builds no resistance, and has no shelf life. It needs resources that remain even in a disaster, such as fuel or sunlight. Large-scale treatment is hard, and it takes time and labor.

---
### 2-1. Heat
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Heat kills microbes by denaturing proteins, damaging nucleic acids, and breaking down cell membrane structure. Moist heat is far more efficient than dry heat. With water present, proteins unfold at a much lower temperature, so 100 °C when wet does about the same work as 160 °C when dry. That is why boiling is the basic form of disinfection.

| Condition | Time | Level reached |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 °C (boiling water) | 1 minute | Kills most waterborne pathogens. Spores survive |
| 1,000 m above sea level or higher | 3 minutes | The boiling point falls, so compensate with more time |
| 72 °C | 15 seconds | High-temperature short-time pasteurization |
| 63 °C | 30 minutes | Pasteurization. Kills pathogenic microbes |
| 121 °C (pressure cooker) | 15 minutes or more | Kills even spores (sterilization) |

- 63 °C for 30 minutes: the water does not have to be boiled. Cholera, dysentery, and typhoid bacteria, hepatitis A virus, and even Giardia and Cryptosporidium cysts die if given enough time in the 60 °C range. Holding water too hot to keep a hand in for 30 minutes kills most waterborne pathogens without boiling. It makes a big difference when fuel is short.
- The weakness of this method is that you cannot confirm it without a thermometer. Official guidance is based on a boiling state you can verify by eye (rolling with large bubbles). At high altitude the boiling point falls, so extend the time.
- Heat does not remove chemical contamination. For water containing heavy metals, pesticides, or salt, boiling evaporates water and actually raises the concentration. Mycotoxins also remain. Distill such water, or do not use it.

---
### 2-2. Solar Disinfection
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Solar disinfection, in which water in a clear plastic bottle is left in the sun, uses no fuel. Of the ultraviolet, the long-wavelength UV-A damages the DNA and cell structure of microbes, and at the same time the water temperature in the bottle rises so that heat works together. When the two actions overlap, the effect is greater than the sum of each.

| Condition | Standard | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Container | Clear plastic bottle, 2-3 L or less | Glass blocks a considerable part of ultraviolet |
| Turbidity | 30 NTU or less | Suspended matter shades the UV so it does not reach the water inside |
| Clear day | At least 6 hours, including noon | The time of day when UV is strongest is needed |
| Cloudy day | Two days in a row | The amount of UV drops greatly |
| Water temperature | At 50 °C or more, shortened to around 1 hour | Heat acting together makes it much faster |
| Position | Lay it down and blacken half | The water layer must be thin for light to pass and for heat to build up |

- Ultraviolet works only if it penetrates. In water like muddy water, where light does not get in even a few centimeters, only the surface is disinfected and the inside is left as is. Let turbid water settle first, or strain it through cloth. A field estimate is whether you can read newspaper print through the bottle.
- Disadvantages: large-scale treatment is hard. It depends entirely on the weather. Chemical contamination remains. It has no residual disinfecting power, so recontamination is possible. Drink solar-disinfected water from the bottle it was in, and do not transfer it to another container.

---
### 2-3. Drying and Freezing
- Drying: it deprives microbes of the water they need to live. Most bacteria die slowly on a dry surface, and hanging things to dry in the sun adds ultraviolet and heat, increasing the effect. Drying laundry and cooking utensils completely in the sun is itself a disinfection measure.
- Things that resist drying: bacterial spores survive for years. Roundworm eggs retain infectivity in soil for years. Protozoan cysts also endure for a long time. Hantavirus in dried rodent droppings can be blown up as dust and infect people. Dry is not the same as safe.
- Freezing is not disinfection. Freezing does not kill microbes; it puts them to sleep. They multiply again when thawed, and freezing is in fact a way of preserving microbes for a long time. Many bacteria and viruses live a long time with only their metabolism stopped at low temperature (the reason strains are stored at tens of degrees below zero in laboratories). Ice crystals tear cells and kill some, but the proportion is not consistent, so it cannot serve as a means of disinfection.
- Exception: some parasites (such as anisakis in fish) die if held for enough time at a sufficiently low temperature. This does not apply to bacteria and viruses.
