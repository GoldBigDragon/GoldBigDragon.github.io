## 4. Gases, Smoke, and Criteria for Judgment
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Up to this point, the discussion has focused on particulates. This chapter addresses the exact point where filtration reaches its theoretical limit—namely, gases. It also explains why fire smoke, the single most lethal respiratory hazard in disasters, is not a problem that can be solved with a mask.

---
### 4-1. What Activated Carbon Captures and Fails to Capture
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Activated carbon does not filter out particles mechanically; it **captures** gas molecules via adsorption. Treating charcoal at high temperatures creates countless microscopic pores inside, resulting in an extraordinarily vast surface area where passing gas molecules adhere to the surface.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;However, not all gas molecules adhere equally. **Larger, heavier molecules with higher boiling points** tend to remain on the surface, whereas smaller, lighter molecules with lower boiling points detach shortly after adhering.

| Category | Gas | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Effectively Captured | Organic solvent vapors, benzene, toluene, odorous substances, certain pesticide vapors | Molecules are large with high boiling points |
| Requires Special Treatment | Ammonia, chlorine, sulfur dioxide, hydrogen cyanide | Chemical additives are required to capture them via chemical reactions |
| **Fails to Capture** | **Carbon monoxide**, methane, carbon dioxide, nitrogen | Molecules are small, nonpolar, and have extremely low boiling points |

> **Carbon monoxide cannot be filtered out by activated carbon. No matter how thick the layer of charcoal is, carbon monoxide passes straight through.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To eliminate carbon monoxide, it must be **converted into another substance** rather than trapped. Smoke hoods in specialized gear contain catalysts that oxidize carbon monoxide into carbon dioxide; however, these catalysts are sensitive to moisture, have a limited shelf life, and cannot be manufactured at home. In a disaster, the only response to carbon monoxide is **evacuating the area altogether**, not relying on filtration.

---
### 4-2. Breakthrough
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The amount of gas activated carbon can adsorb is finite. Once the surface area is completely saturated, any incoming gas passes straight through; this point is known as **breakthrough**.

| Factor | Conditions Accelerating Breakthrough |
| --- | --- |
| Concentration | Higher gas concentrations saturate the media faster |
| Respiration Rate | Heavier breathing increases the volume of gas passing through per unit time |
| Humidity | Water vapor occupies adsorption sites first |
| Temperature | Higher temperatures cause adsorbed molecules to desorb |
| Storage | Simply opening the packaging causes it to adsorb airborne substances and deplete capacity |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The greatest danger is that **there is no reliable way to notice breakthrough when it happens.** If you begin to smell the gas again, it means gas is already passing through—a post-facto notification rather than a warning. Furthermore, for gases that have no odor at all, such as carbon monoxide, or for substances whose toxic concentration is lower than the odor threshold, even this signal is entirely absent.

---
### 4-3. How Fire Smoke Actually Kills
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A vast majority of fire casualties perish not from burns, but from inhaling smoke. Yet the deadly components in that smoke are not substances that a mask can mitigate even partially.

| Cause of Death | Mechanism | Effect of Improvised Mask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carbon Monoxide | Binds to hemoglobin, blocking oxygen transport | None |
| Hydrogen Cyanide | Paralyzes cellular energy production itself | None |
| Oxygen Deficiency | Fire consumes oxygen, causing atmospheric levels to plunge | None |
| Superheated Gases | Causes respiratory burns and edema, blocking airways | Virtually none |
| Irritant Gases | Gases like hydrogen chloride and acrolein cause airway swelling | Virtually none |
| Soot Particles | Airway irritation and deposition | Partially effective |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Hydrogen cyanide is especially vital to understand. It is generated when nitrogen-containing materials burn—materials abundant in modern living spaces. Polyurethane foam insulation and mattresses, nylon carpets and clothing, as well as wool and silk, all belong to this category. This aligns with the reason mentioned earlier in "Waste Disposal" regarding why foam plastics must never be burned. Hydrogen cyanide and carbon monoxide act synergistically, causing loss of consciousness far faster than either gas alone.

> **What kills people in fire smoke is not soot particles. Covering your mouth with a cloth against visible black smoke merely creates the illusion of protection while you inhale lethal, invisible, odorless gases.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Advice to stay low while moving must also be understood accurately. Because hot smoke and soot rise, staying low provides a clear advantage in visibility and heat exposure. However, carbon monoxide has a density similar to air and mixes uniformly throughout the space. **Crawling on the floor does not allow you to dodge carbon monoxide.** A low posture merely buys a little extra time to exit; the ultimate destination must always be the outside.

---
### 4-4. Criteria for Judgment
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Finally, the concepts discussed throughout this document are summarized into a single decision matrix.

| Scenario | Correct Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Fire Smoke | Evacuate immediately. A mask is only a secondary aid during escape, never a justification to stay or re-enter |
| Confined Space | Do not enter. Ventilate first, and pull collapsed victims out from the outside |
| Toxic Gas Leak | Move crosswind or upwind toward higher ground. If indoors, seal windows and gaps |
| Collapse Dust / Volcanic Ash | Filtration is genuinely effective. Face seal fit and layered fabric provide meaningful protection |
| Mold / Waste Cleanup | Open windows for ventilation, dampen surfaces with water first, and cover mouth/nose to prevent particle dispersion |
| Infectious Droplets | Filtration is effective. Face seal fit and wearing duration dictate performance |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**If the hazard consists of particulates, filtration helps; if it is gas or oxygen deficiency, filtration does not help.** In fire smoke or toxic gas leak situations, masks must never serve as the basis for decision-making. Conversely, in volcanic ash, cleanup, or infectious droplet scenarios, wearing a mask offers meaningful protection.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Under all circumstances, two actions take precedence over filtration: **leaving the space, and exchanging the air.** A mask merely reduces the intake of contaminated air slightly; ventilation reduces the amount of contaminated air itself; and evacuation avoids breathing it altogether. The moment this hierarchy is inverted, improvised respiratory protection ceases to be a protective tool and becomes the reason you remain in a deadly place far too long.
