## 3. Materials and Fit
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Before choosing materials, there is a fundamental truth to understand: the laboratory performance of mask fabric and the actual protection obtained in practice are not equal values, and the gap between the two is defined by **face seal fit**.

---
### 3-1. Why Fit Is More Important Than Filter Rating
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When wearing a mask, air enters through two distinct pathways: passing through the filter fabric, or leaking through gaps between the mask and the face. The critical problem is that air leaking through gaps **undergoes zero filtration**.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Even if a mask fabric filters 99% of particles, if 10% of the inhaled air leaks through edge gaps, that 10% reaches the lungs in an unpurified state. No matter how clean the remaining 90% is, overall protection caps out around 90%. Conversely, even if the filter media captures only 70%, if there are zero edge leaks, the full 70% protective efficacy is reliably achieved.

> **Fitting a mask tightly against the face without gaps always makes a bigger difference than raising the fabric rating by one level.**

| Gap Location | Cause | Improvement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bridge of Nose | Fabric fails to follow the contours of the nasal ridge | Insert a wire or thin metal strip and press to fit |
| Edges of Cheeks | Loose straps cause fabric to lift | Tighten straps and fold edges to create a flat seal |
| Under the Chin | Gap opens when talking or moving | Size the mask to wrap under and enclose the chin |
| Facial Hair | Air passes freely through beard hair fibers | Fundamental face seal fit is impossible |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Facial hair in the bottom row offers no workaround. No mask can establish a proper seal over a beard; this is a geometric impossibility, not a matter of personal preference. In heavy dust environments, user seal checks are simple: cover the mask faceplate with your hands and inhale deeply. If the **fabric pulls inward toward your face**, a proper seal is established; if you feel air entering from the sides, that spot is a leak path.

---
### 3-2. Approximate Performance by Material
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The values below represent **approximate ranges of raw fabric media** reported in published studies. Because values vary significantly based on test conditions and particle sizes, they should not be taken as absolute figures—and above all, **these do not reflect worn performance on a human face.**

| Material | Approx. Filtration Range | Breathing Resistance | Evaluation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Surgical Mask | High (approx. 90% range) | Medium | Baseline comparison standard |
| Vacuum Cleaner Filter Bag | High (approx. 85–95%) | **Very High** | Good filtration, but extremely hard to breathe; glass fiber risk |
| Dishcloth / Heavy Cotton Towel | Medium-High (approx. 70–85%) | High | Practical for short durations only |
| Cotton Blend Shirt | Medium (approx. 70%) | Medium | Relatively balanced filtration and breathability |
| Pillowcase / Linen | Medium (approx. 55–60%) | Low | Suitable for prolonged wear |
| Cotton T-Shirt | Medium (approx. 50–70%) | Low | Most practical choice for layering |
| Silk / Scarf | Low (approx. 50% range) | Low | Blocks large dust particles only |
| Coffee Filter | Medium | **Very High** | Excessive resistance forces air to leak around edges; low practical efficacy |
| Nonwoven Fabric (Mask Grade) | High | Low | Reaches this performance level only when electrostatic charge is intact |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;What should be gathered from this table is not a simple ranking, but the **underlying trade-off**. Moving toward the top yields better filtration at the expense of breathing ease, while moving toward the bottom increases breathability at the cost of filtration. Vacuum filter bags serve as a prime example: while their filtration efficiency rivals surgical masks, breathing resistance is so severe that air bypasses via edge leakage, making long-term wear impossible. Furthermore, some models contain glass fibers, posing an inhalation hazard themselves. **Researchers recommended cotton T-shirts and pillowcases not because their filtration was superior, but because they represent reusable combinations.**

---
### 3-3. How Far Does a Wet Cloth Actually Help?
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Advice to wet a towel and cover one's mouth during fire or toxic gas emergencies is widely known. There is partial scientific basis for this, and understanding the exact boundaries of that basis is critical.

| Mechanism | Principle | Limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Particle Capture | Water fills gaps between fibers, narrowing passage paths; particles adhere to wet fibers without re-entrainment | Breathing resistance rises sharply; dries out quickly |
| Water-Soluble Gas Absorption | Ammonia, chlorine, hydrogen chloride, and sulfur dioxide dissolve readily in water, capturing a portion in wet fibers | Water volume in the cloth is minimal, saturating rapidly |
| Heat Barrier | Slightly cools hot air, delaying respiratory tract burns | Cannot block the thermal source itself |

> **A wet cloth has no effect whatsoever on carbon monoxide and hydrogen cyanide. Even for water-soluble gases, its effect lasts only a few dozen seconds to a few minutes—just enough time to buy an escape window.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Accurately understanding this limitation is essential because relying on a wet towel to **remain in place** leads to fatal accidents. The proper function of a wet towel is for breathing while moving through smoke to exit a building, never for staying inside smoke to perform tasks.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There are also conditions regarding the water used for dampening. Placing a cloth soaked in contaminated water against your face directly introduces pathogens from that water into your respiratory tract. When moistening cloths for mold abatement or waste cleanup, strictly use clean water; never use urine or stagnant water under any circumstances.
