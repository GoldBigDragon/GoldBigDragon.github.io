## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A mask is a filter, not a shield. A filter sorts by size, so it works on particles such as dust, ash, and droplets, but not on gases that are mixed with air at the molecular level, such as carbon monoxide and chlorine. In a space short of oxygen, no filtering device can create oxygen that is not there.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Rather than how to make an improvised mask, this document covers the principles and limits of how filtration works. It explains why 0.3 μm particles pass most easily, why a tight fit matters more than the filter grade (KF94, KF80, and so on), how far a wet towel helps, and why activated carbon cannot capture carbon monoxide.

> **Principle:** Leaving and ventilating come before filtering.

---

## 1. What a Mask Blocks and What It Does Not
> **Caution:** An improvised mask is partly effective against particles such as dust, ash, smoke soot, and droplets, but is practically powerless against gases and vapors such as carbon monoxide, chlorine, ammonia, and hydrogen sulfide. In a space short of oxygen, no mask is of any use.

---
### 1-1. Types of Hazard
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Tell the three hazards apart, because the responses are completely different.

| Hazard type | Examples | Improvised mask | What is needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Particles | Soil dust, volcanic ash, collapse dust, soot, mold spores, droplets, dried-droppings dust | Partly effective | A mask worn with a tight seal |
| Gases and vapors | Carbon monoxide, chlorine, ammonia, hydrogen sulfide, sulfur dioxide, hydrogen cyanide | Nearly powerless | An adsorbent or catalytic cartridge for that gas |
| Oxygen deficiency | Wells, septic tanks, cellars, silos, storage tanks, enclosed fire spaces | Completely powerless | Equipment that supplies air, or not entering |

- Particles and gases are separated by size. Soil dust is several μm, smoke soot is around 0.1 μm, and droplets are several to tens of μm, which fibers can catch. Gas molecules are on the order of 0.0003 μm, a thousand to ten thousand times smaller than particles, and no fiber structure can filter them out. Gases must be captured or reacted chemically, and the means differs for each gas.
- Every filtering respirator assumes that there is breathable oxygen in the surrounding air. A mask filters out bad things but cannot add good ones. In a space where oxygen is below 19.5%, a mask means nothing, and the false sense of protection makes people go in deeper.

---
### 1-2. Gases the Senses Do Not Warn About
| Gas | Sensation | Why it is dangerous |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carbon monoxide | Colorless, odorless, non-irritating | There is no signal. It starts with headache and drowsiness, and the person simply falls asleep |
| Hydrogen sulfide | Rotten-egg smell at low concentrations | At high concentrations it paralyzes the sense of smell, and the smell disappears |
| Methane, nitrogen | Odorless | Not toxic, but they displace oxygen and suffocate |
| Carbon dioxide | Odorless | Pools in low places and accumulates in enclosed spaces |
| Chlorine, ammonia | Strong irritation | Can be noticed by smell, but by then exposure has already occurred |

- Hydrogen sulfide: if the smell was there and then vanished, it may mean not that it has become safe but that the concentration has risen to a lethal level. This is the typical course of accidents in septic tanks, sewers, compost piles, and long-standing stagnant water.
- Carbon monoxide: it binds to hemoglobin more than 200 times more strongly than oxygen and blocks oxygen transport, yet there is no feeling of suffocation. Instead drowsiness comes on and the person cannot evacuate on their own. This is the same principle as the danger of mosquito coils in "Insect and rodent control."

---
### 1-3. Confined-Space Accidents
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Wells, septic tanks, underground storage, silos, water tanks, and cellars that have been closed for a long time do not exchange air, so oxygen is consumed and other gases build up. This is because decomposing organic matter eats oxygen and gives off carbon dioxide, hydrogen sulfide, and methane.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The accident follows almost the same pattern every time. One person goes in and collapses, the person who goes in to rescue collapses, and the next person goes in too. The person who has collapsed loses consciousness quietly, without showing distress, so people outside do not notice the danger.

> **Caution:** Rushing in when someone has collapsed in a confined space is not rescue but increasing the number of dead. A towel or mask does nothing in this situation.

- What you can do: do not go in. Pull the person out from outside with a hook or rope. Change the air first. Open the cover and ventilate for a good while, or blow air in from above to push the heavy gas up.
- The old method of lowering a candle or match to see whether the flame goes out only shows oxygen deficiency, cannot detect carbon monoxide, and becomes an ignition source if methane is present. It is not a reliable test.

## 2. Principles of Particle Filtration
### 2-1. Filtration Mechanisms
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Filtration cannot be explained by sieving alone. If it were sieving only, stopping a 0.1 μm particle would need 0.1 μm holes, and you could not breathe. In reality five mechanisms work together.

| Mechanism | How it works | Size caught best |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sieving | Particles larger than the gaps between fibers are simply blocked | Several μm and above |
| Inertial impaction | Heavy particles cannot follow the airflow and hit the fibers | 1 μm and above |
| Interception | Particles following the flow brush a fiber and stick | 0.5-1 μm |
| Diffusion | Light particles wander by Brownian motion until they touch a fiber | 0.1 μm and below |
| Electrostatic attraction | Charged fibers pull particles in | The whole size range, regardless of size |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Inertial impaction and interception work better the larger the particle, and diffusion works better the smaller it is. The two trends are opposite, so a valley where both are weak forms between them. That valley is at roughly 0.3 μm, and this size passes most easily (the most penetrating particle size).

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The 95% in the N95 grade is the worst-case performance measured at the size that passes most easily. Particles larger or smaller than this are filtered better. The claim that viruses smaller than 0.3 μm pass through does not hold. The smaller they are, the stronger the diffusion, so they are actually caught better.

---
### 2-2. Static Electricity and Breathing Rate
- A good part of the performance of manufactured masks comes from the charge held in the fibers. When a nonwoven fabric made of very fine fibers is electrified, passing particles are drawn in and stick. Because filtration improves without shrinking the pores, breathing resistance stays low.
- This charge disappears with water, alcohol, detergent, high humidity, and long storage. That is why washing a mask or disinfecting it with alcohol lowers its performance. A homemade cloth mask has no charge to begin with, so only purely mechanical filtration remains.
- When air passes quickly, small particles stay near the fibers for a shorter time, so the chance of being caught by diffusion falls. The harder you breathe, the lower the filtration performance, so a mask worn while running or carrying heavy loads blocks less than one worn at rest.

---
### 2-3. Number of Layers
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Layering cloth raises filtration performance, but breathing resistance rises ever more steeply. Past a certain point there is only loss.

| Layers | Filtration performance | Breathing resistance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 layer | Low | Low |
| 2 layers | Clearly higher | Clearly higher |
| 3-4 layers | A little higher | Much higher |
| More than that | Almost stalled | Rises sharply |

- When resistance rises, air flows toward the side of lower resistance and leaks in through gaps at the mask edge. The paradox arises that the better the fabric, the more the leakage grows.
- When breathing is hard, people take the mask off or wear it pulled down. Even when worn, if exhaled air is breathed in again, carbon dioxide accumulates and causes headache and dizziness. For people with heart and lung disease, the elderly, and young children, this is itself a danger.
- The criterion for the number of layers is not the filtration rate but whether you can keep breathing in that state for as long as needed. It depends on whether it is for passing through briefly or for working for hours.

## 3. Materials and Fit
### 3-1. Why Fit Matters More Than Fabric Grade
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When you wear a mask, air enters by the path through the mask and by the path leaking through gaps between face and mask. Air that leaks through gaps passes no filtration at all. Even if the fabric filters 99% of particles, if 10% of the air you inhale comes in through gaps, the overall performance stays at about 90%. Even if the fabric filters only 70%, with no leakage the full 70% is achieved. Sealing the mask to the face without gaps makes a bigger difference than raising the fabric grade one step.

| Where gaps form | Cause | Improvement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beside the nose | The cloth cannot follow the curves of the nose bridge | Insert wire or a thin metal strip and press it to fit |
| Edges of the cheeks | Loose straps let the cloth lift | Tighten the straps and fold the edges to make a flat surface |
| Under the chin | Opens when talking or moving | Size it so that it wraps under the chin |
| Beard | Air passes between the hairs | A tight seal is fundamentally impossible |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;With a beard, no mask will seal. To check the fit, cover the mask with your palm and inhale: if the cloth is sucked toward the face, it is sealed, and if you feel air coming in somewhere, that spot is the leak.

---
### 3-2. Approximate Performance by Material
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;These are rough ranges reported in published studies for the fabric itself. They vary greatly with measurement conditions and particle size, and they are not the performance when worn.

| Material | Approximate filtration range | Breathing resistance | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Surgical mask | High (around 90%) | Medium | The reference for comparison |
| Vacuum cleaner bag | High (about 85-95%) | Very high | Good filtration but hard to breathe through, and fiberglass risk |
| Dishcloth, thick cotton towel | Medium-high (about 70-85%) | High | Practical only for short periods |
| Blended cotton shirt | Medium (about 70%) | Medium | A comparatively good balance of performance and breathing |
| Pillowcase, linen | Medium (about 55-60%) | Low | Suitable for long use |
| Cotton T-shirt | Medium (about 50-70%) | Low | The most workable for layering |
| Silk, scarf | Low (around 50%) | Low | Only stops large dust |
| Coffee filter | Medium | Very high | High resistance makes air leak around the sides, so it is of little practical use |
| Nonwoven fabric (for masks) | High | Low | This performance comes only while the charge is alive |

- The better a material filters, the harder it is to breathe through, and the easier it is to breathe through, the less it filters. This trade-off is the crux.
- A vacuum cleaner bag filters nearly as well as a surgical mask, but its breathing resistance is so high that air leaks around the edges and it cannot be worn for long. Some products contain fiberglass and are themselves an inhalation hazard.
- Researchers recommended cotton T-shirts and pillowcases not because they filter best but because they are a combination that can be used many times.

---
### 3-3. How Far a Wet Cloth Helps
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The advice to wet a towel and cover the mouth in a fire or toxic gas situation has partial grounds.

| Action | Principle | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capturing particles | Water fills the pores between fibers, narrowing the passage, and particles stick to the wet fibers and are not re-raised | Breathing resistance rises greatly and it soon dries |
| Absorbing water-soluble gases | Ammonia, chlorine, hydrogen chloride, and sulfur dioxide dissolve well in water, so some are caught by the water in the cloth | The amount of water in the cloth is tiny, so it saturates quickly |
| Blocking heat | Cools hot air a little and delays burns to the airway | Does not stop the heat source itself |

> **Caution:** A wet cloth has no effect on carbon monoxide or hydrogen cyanide. Even for water-soluble gases, the effect lasts tens of seconds to a few minutes, that is, only enough to buy time to get out of the place. Deciding to stay because you trust a wet towel leads to accidents.

- Proper use: use it while passing through smoke to get outside. Do not use it to do anything in the smoke.
- The water used to wet it: a cloth wetted with contaminated water and held to the face brings pathogens straight into the airways. Use clean water to wet cloth when cleaning mold or droppings, and never use urine or stagnant water.

## 4. Gases and Smoke, and Criteria for Judgment
### 4-1. What Activated Carbon Captures and What It Does Not
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Activated carbon does not filter; it grabs. Charcoal treated at high temperature gets countless fine pores inside, its surface area becomes extremely large, and gas molecules passing through stick to this surface. The larger, heavier, and higher-boiling the molecule, the better it stays, and small, light, low-boiling molecules stick but soon come off.

| Category | Gas | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Captured well | Organic solvent vapors, benzene, toluene, odor compounds, some pesticide vapors | Large molecules with high boiling points |
| Needs special treatment | Ammonia, chlorine, sulfur dioxide, hydrogen cyanide | Chemicals must be added so that a chemical reaction catches them |
| Not captured | Carbon monoxide, methane, carbon dioxide, nitrogen | Small, nonpolar molecules with very low boiling points |

> **Caution:** Carbon monoxide is not filtered by activated carbon. However thickly the charcoal is packed, it passes straight through.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Carbon monoxide must be converted into another substance rather than captured. The smoke hoods of professional equipment contain a catalyst that oxidizes carbon monoxide to carbon dioxide, but this catalyst is weak to moisture, has a limited life, and cannot be made at home. In a disaster the only response to carbon monoxide is to leave the space.

---
### 4-2. Breakthrough
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The amount of gas that activated carbon can capture is not unlimited. Once the surface is full, gas that comes in afterward passes straight through, and this point is breakthrough.

| Factor | Conditions that speed up breakthrough |
| --- | --- |
| Concentration | The higher the gas concentration, the faster it saturates |
| Breathing volume | The harder you breathe, the more passes per unit time |
| Humidity | Water vapor takes up the adsorption sites first |
| Temperature | At higher temperatures, adsorbed molecules come off |
| Storage | Even just leaving the package open adsorbs substances from the air and uses it up |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most dangerous thing is that there is no good way to notice breakthrough. If the smell starts again, gas is already passing through, and it is not a warning but a notice after the fact. For odorless gases such as carbon monoxide, and for substances whose harmful concentration is below the concentration at which they can be smelled, even this signal is absent.

---
### 4-3. How Fire Smoke Kills
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A good share of fire deaths are caused not by burns but by inhaling smoke. The components in that smoke that kill people are hardly blocked by a mask at all.

| Cause of death | Action | Effect of an improvised mask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carbon monoxide | Binds to hemoglobin and blocks oxygen transport | None |
| Hydrogen cyanide | Paralyzes the cells' energy production itself | None |
| Oxygen deficiency | The fire consumes oxygen and its concentration falls | None |
| Hot gases | Cause burns and swelling of the airway, which then closes | Almost none |
| Irritant gases | Hydrogen chloride, acrolein, and others make the airway swell | Almost none |
| Soot particles | Irritate and deposit in the airway | Partly effective |

- Hydrogen cyanide is produced when materials containing nitrogen burn. Modern homes are full of polyurethane foam insulation and mattresses, nylon carpets and clothes, wool, and silk. This is the same reason "Waste disposal" says foamed plastic must not be burned. Hydrogen cyanide and carbon monoxide act together and make a person lose consciousness far faster than either alone.
- Blocking the visible black smoke with cloth only lets you breathe in the colorless gases that actually kill you while feeling protected.
- Staying low: hot smoke and soot rise, so low places are better for visibility and heat. But carbon monoxide has a density similar to air and mixes evenly through the whole space, so lying down cannot avoid carbon monoxide. Staying low only buys a little time to get out, and the destination is always outside.

---
### 4-4. Criteria for Judgment
| Situation | Correct judgment |
| --- | --- |
| Fire smoke | Leave immediately. A mask is only an aid while leaving and is no grounds for going back |
| Confined space | Do not enter. Ventilate first, and pull out the person who has collapsed from outside |
| Toxic gas leak | Move with the wind at your back, to higher ground, upwind. If indoors, close the windows and seal the gaps |
| Collapse dust, volcanic ash | Filtering really helps. A tight seal and several layers of cloth matter |
| Cleaning mold or droppings | Open the doors to ventilate, wet the surface with water first, and cover with cloth so that particles do not spread into the air |
| Infectious droplets | Filtering helps. Fit and wearing time decide performance |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If the hazard is particles, filtering helps; if it is a gas or lack of oxygen, it does not. In fire smoke and toxic gas leaks, a mask cannot be the basis of a decision. In volcanic ash, cleaning, and infectious droplets, a mask gives a degree of protection.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In every case, two things come before filtering: leaving the place and changing the air. A mask makes you breathe a little less of the contaminated air, ventilation reduces the contaminated air itself, and leaving means not breathing it at all. If the order is reversed, an improvised respirator becomes a reason to stay longer in a dangerous place.
