## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This document is structured opposite to other hygiene documents. While most documents begin with what can be done, respiratory protection becomes lethal knowledge unless **what it cannot do** is established first. Those who entered smoke with a piece of cloth covering their mouth, those who descended into septic tanks wearing a towel, and those who returned to fire scenes believing in a mask all started from the exact same misconception.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The root of this misconception is treating a mask **as a shield rather than a filter**. A filter is a device that separates what passes through from what is blocked based on size. While this distinction works for airborne clusters like dust, ash, and droplets, it has no room to operate on gases like carbon monoxide and chlorine that are mixed at the molecular level just like air. If air passes through, those gases pass through together. Furthermore, in an oxygen-deficient space, no filtration device can generate non-existent oxygen.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Therefore, this document does not cover how to craft improvised masks well, but rather addresses the physics of filtration and the exact point where that physics ends. It explains why particles around 0.3 μm penetrate most easily, why face seal fit is far more crucial than filter media ratings (KF94, KF80, etc.), how far wet towels actually help, and why activated carbon fails to capture carbon monoxide. The conclusion is predetermined from the start: **evacuation and ventilation take precedence over filtration.**
