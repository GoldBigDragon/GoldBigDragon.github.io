## 3. Physical Insect Killing
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Pyrethrum is a chemical insecticide that acts on nerves. It works fast but breaks down in light so is not persistent, and with repeated use resistant individuals survive and it stops working. Physical insect killing attacks not the insect's nerves but the structure of its body. Insects can evolve to withstand a chemical by increasing detoxifying enzymes, but they have no way to evolve against water leaving their bodies or proteins cooking in heat. It is slow and troublesome, but its potency does not wane, which is a decisive advantage when there are no supplies.

---
### 3-1. Diatomaceous Earth and Ash
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;On the outermost surface of an insect exoskeleton is a wax layer called the epicuticle. This lipid film, less than 1 μm thick, is effectively the only barrier against evaporation of water from the body. An insect has a very large surface area for its volume, so when this film is damaged, water leaves it at a rate that cannot be compared with a human's.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Diatomaceous earth is a powder made by grinding a sediment of hardened siliceous shells of diatoms, and it breaks down this film in two ways.

- At the microscopic level, sharp particles scrape off the wax layer while the insect crawls.
- The countless pores in the particle surface absorb the lipid components of the wax.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Water leaks through the scratched spots, the film thins and dehydration accelerates, and in the end the insect loses its body fluids and dies. When wet the effect disappears. Water fills the pores of the particles so there is no place to adsorb wax, the powder clumps and does not stick to the insect's body, and humid air replaces the water the insect lost.

> **Diatomaceous earth is an insecticide only when dry.** Wet diatomaceous earth is just mud.

- It is not a quick-acting drug. It takes hours to days to kill and works only if insects walk over the powder, so sprinkle it along lines they must cross, such as passages and wall corners.
- If piled thick, insects avoid it and go around. Spread it thin enough that the floor color shows through.

| Material | Action | Potency | Cautions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diatomaceous earth | Abrasion and wax adsorption | Strongest | Ineffective when wet; avoid inhaling dust |
| Wood ash (fine) | Abrasion, moisture absorption, alkaline irritation | Medium | Particles are coarse, so sift before use |
| Dry fine soil, yellow clay | Abrasion, moisture absorption | Weak | Only as an aid to grain storage |
| Slaked lime powder | Alkaline corrosion and drying | Medium | Too irritating, unsuitable for living spaces |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The silica in diatomaceous earth is mostly amorphous and is known to carry a low risk of silicosis, but fine dust irritates the airways and eyes. When sprinkling, cover your nose and mouth with a wet cloth and keep the wind at your back.

### 3-2. Heat and Sunlight
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lice and scabies mites are weak to heat because their proteins are denatured by heat. Enzymes and structural proteins generally begin to lose their folded structure at around 50 °C, and once unfolded they do not go back even when the temperature falls. Eggs withstand more than adults, but the limit lies in the same place.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Body lice live not on the human body but in the seams of clothing. They hide in folds to attach eggs and move to the skin only to suck blood. Scabies mites also remain in bedding and clothing for days. Lice and scabies live in clothes and bedding more than on people. If you wash only your body and put on the same clothes again, you will be bitten again the next day.

| Treatment | Conditions | Principle and effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Boiling | A few minutes in boiling water | Kills both adults and eggs. Most reliable |
| Hot-water wash | 60 °C or more for 30 minutes | Kills adults and eggs |
| Heat drying, ironing | 50 °C for 10 minutes or more | Heat applied directly to the seams works on scabies mites |
| Sealed isolation | 1-2 weeks in plastic | Body lice starve in 1-2 days, scabies mites in 2-3 days |
| Hanging in the sun | Cover with black cloth at midday | The real action is the rise in internal temperature, not ultraviolet |

- When hanging in the sun, spread thin and turn once, and cover with black cloth to raise the temperature.
- The most common reason washing alone fails is that the water temperature does not rise. Cold-water washing does not kill eggs attached to the seams, and they hatch a few days later. Even when fuel is short, boiling the clothes and washing the body in cold water is better than the reverse.

### 3-3. Four Conditions for Stopping Grain Pests
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Rice weevils and azuki bean weevils lay eggs inside the kernels. They cannot be seen from outside, so assume that grain that comes in already has eggs, and set the goal as keeping the eggs from developing. Cutting off just one condition for development is enough.

| Condition | Threshold | Principle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moisture | Grain moisture 13% or less; almost stopped at 12% or less | Larvae get the water they need for development from the grain |
| Temperature | Development stops at 15 °C or less; dies on prolonged exposure near 0 °C | Cold-blooded, so metabolic rate is directly tied to temperature |
| Oxygen | After sealing, the insects consume it themselves until it runs out | Suffocate in a few weeks in a completely sealed container |
| Physical contact | Mix ash or diatomaceous earth at 0.5-1% of the grain weight | Dehydrates them as they move and covers egg-laying surfaces |

- If you have no tool to measure dryness, bite a kernel with your teeth. If it is soft and gets pressed flat, moisture remains; if it cracks with a snap, it is at a level that can be stored.
- If grain that is not dry enough is sealed, moisture is trapped and mold grows instead of insects. If sealed while hot, condensation forms inside the container as it cools. Dry it during the day, and seal it in the evening after it has cooled.
- The traditional method of mixing in ash works in three ways at once. Ash particles dehydrate the insects, fill the spaces between kernels to hinder movement and egg-laying, and the hygroscopic nature keeps the grain dry. Wash it in water before eating.
- Grain that already has insects is poured into water, and the hollowed-out empty kernels float up and are skimmed off.
