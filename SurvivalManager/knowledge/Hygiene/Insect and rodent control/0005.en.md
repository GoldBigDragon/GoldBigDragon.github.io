## 4. Rodent Control
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Insects are so numerous that removing them individually has little meaning. Rats are fewer in number but they learn, are wary, and change their environment. They gnaw to widen holes, avoid new objects, and remember where food is. Rodent control is a matter of order, not tools.

---
### 4-1. Removing Food and Shelter
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most common failure is setting traps first. The number of rats a place can support is set by food and shelter. Even if traps reduce them, if food and shelter remain, the breeding rate of the survivors rises and new individuals migrate in from around to fill the vacancy. A brown rat bears 6-12 young per litter, breeds several times a year, and the young can breed in 3 months. Catching a few a day cannot keep up.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Traps reduce the rats that already exist, and removing food sets the number of rats there will be. Removing food and shelter comes first.

| What to remove | What it looks like in a disaster | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food | Exposed grain sacks, leftovers, food waste, livestock feed | Seal in metal, glass, or thick plastic. Bury or burn leftovers daily |
| Water | Leaking jugs, rainwater pooling, wet rags | Lids on jugs; remove standing water |
| Shelter | Debris piles, stacked planks, old furniture, overgrown grass | Raise at least 30cm off the ground and keep away from walls |
| Passages | Pipes, conduits, ivy, wall gaps | Block with materials that cannot be gnawed |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Brown rats must drink water separately, but house mice get by on the moisture in their food alone. Cutting off water is highly effective only against brown rats, and for house mice, sealing food is the only sure pressure.

---
### 4-2. Gap Dimensions and Traps
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The size of a gap a rat can pass through is set by the skull. If the skull gets through, the flexible body follows.

| Species | Smallest passable gap | Reference object |
| --- | --- | --- |
| House mouse | About 6mm | Thickness of a pencil |
| Brown rat | About 12mm | Thickness of a thumb |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A rat's incisors grow throughout its life, so it must gnaw constantly, and with that force it widens small gaps into passable holes. The blocking material must be not only the right size but also something that cannot be gnawed. A hole blocked only with wood, vinyl, sponge, or expanded polyurethane opens again in a few days. What actually holds is pressing in steel wool and covering it with cement, wire mesh of 6mm mesh or less, and thin metal plate. The traditional method of mixing pottery shards into clay and plastering is the same idea.

| Trap type | How it works | Properties |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Snap | A spring strikes instantly | Nearly instant kill so recovery is certain, but one animal at a time |
| Cage | A door that opens only inward | Catches several, but they are caught alive, leaving the problem of disposal |
| Bucket | A slippery ramp and rotating cylinder with water below | Catches repeatedly without resetting, and can be made of natural materials |

- A trap's success depends on position more than on bait. Rats travel with one side of the body against a wall and do not cross open spaces. Place the trap right against the wall with the trigger plate facing the wall. Grease marks on a wall, places where droppings gather, and gnaw marks show the runways.
- Brown rats are neophobic and avoid a new object in a familiar space for several days. Do not move the trap just because nothing is caught. Leave bait on it for several days without setting it to relax their wariness, and then set it. House mice are curious and approach right away.
- As bait, nuts, grain flour mixed with oil, and dried fish work better than cheese.

---
### 4-3. Rat Poison and Handling Carcasses and Droppings
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Anticoagulant-type rat poisons act slowly over several days. This design keeps the rat from linking the bait to death, but because of that delay the rat goes back to its burrow and dies. Carcasses inside walls and above ceilings cannot be recovered, and as they decay they give off a stench and become breeding sources for flesh flies and maggots.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There is also secondary poisoning. A rat slowed by the poison becomes the easiest prey for predators, and the cats, dogs, owls, and weasels that eat it are poisoned along with it. If the animals that were catching rats disappear first, the rat population actually recovers faster. The risk of children and livestock swallowing it is also large, and the vitamin K1 and blood transfusions needed for treatment cannot be obtained in a disaster.

> **Caution:** Rat poison leaves carcasses where hands cannot reach and also kills the animals that were catching rats. In a disaster, traps that guarantee recovery are better.

- Do not handle dead rats with bare hands. Wrap them in plastic or a wide leaf, or scoop them up with a shovel, soak them thoroughly with disinfectant, double-wrap them, and bury them deep or burn them.
- When a carcass cools, the fleas that were on it leave to find a new host. This is why the risk of plague is considered to rise after a large-scale rat eradication.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The cleaning method is set by the route by which the disease enters.

| Disease | Excreta | Route of entry | Forbidden actions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Leptospirosis | Urine | Contaminated water touches wounds or the mucous membranes of the eyes, nose, and mouth | Entering floodwater barefoot or bare-handed |
| Hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome (hantavirus) | Dried droppings and urine | Flies up as dust and is inhaled into the airways | Sweeping, shaking out, or vacuuming dried droppings |
| Salmonellosis | Droppings | Orally through contaminated food and hands | Using grain and cooking surfaces rats have passed over as they are |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Always clean rat droppings by a wet method.

1. Open windows and doors and ventilate for at least 30 minutes.
2. Spray the droppings and nesting material with dilute chlorine disinfectant or soapy water and let them soak for about 10 minutes.
3. Wipe them up with paper or cloth, put them in a bag, and bury or burn them.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sweeping or shaking out when dry scatters the pathogen into the air and creates an inhalation route yourself. While working, cover your nose and mouth with cloth and wrap your hands, and afterward wash your hands with soap. If you have a wound on your hand, cover it with waterproof material before starting. Leptospira cannot pierce intact skin, but a single small wound lets it in.
