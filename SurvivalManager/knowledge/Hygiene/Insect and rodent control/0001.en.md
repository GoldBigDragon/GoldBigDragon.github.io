## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In a disaster, insects and rodents are not a nuisance but carriers of infectious disease. Hygiene management alone does not cut this route. Mosquitoes breed even in clean water, and rats come anywhere there is food.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A disaster creates conditions favorable to vectors all at once.

- Floods: standing water increases and with it mosquito breeding sites.
- Power outages: refrigeration stops and food waste attracts flies and rats.
- Collapse and debris: they become hiding places for rats.
- Bathing and laundry stopping: lice spread.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Populations that the normal control system held down can multiply several times over in a single season. This document deals with the priorities of control on the premise that insecticides cannot be obtained. It sets out in terms of principle which methods in fact only repel rather than kill, and how far natural-material control works. Smoking is repellence, not insect killing; diatomaceous earth is useless when wet; and removing food comes before traps.
