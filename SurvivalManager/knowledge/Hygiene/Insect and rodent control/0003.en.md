## 2. Source Elimination and Repellence
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Source elimination targets the most vulnerable, immobile stages (eggs and larvae) in the insect life cycle. Adults fly around and scatter, but eggs and larvae are gathered in one place and cannot flee. The same effort removes far more individuals.

---
### 2-1. The One-Week Rule for Standing Water
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mosquitoes are born in water. From egg to adult generally takes 7-10 days at 25-30 °C in summer, and may take two weeks or more when the water is cooler.

> **If you empty or turn over standing water once a week, no mosquito grows up in that water and takes wing.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mosquito larvae can live only in water, so when you pour the water out, the larvae and pupae dry up and die at once. No insecticide, oil, or special treatment is needed. You only have to find every place where water collects. Aedes mosquitoes breed even in water collected in a bottle cap, so the problem is places people do not recognize as puddles.

| Commonly missed breeding sites | Why they are missed |
| --- | --- |
| Flowerpot saucers, empty pots | The standing water is not noticed |
| Discarded tires | Water remains inside and does not freeze even in winter |
| Clogged rain gutters and downspouts | Out of sight |
| Folds in tarps and plastic sheeting | A small amount, but water keeps being replenished |
| Uncovered water jugs and buckets | Meant for drinking, so no one thinks of emptying them |
| Discarded containers, cans, bottles | Buried in debris and invisible |
| Tree holes, bamboo stumps | Natural, so not thought of as sources |
| Septic tanks and sewer pooling | Hard to reach |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For water that cannot be emptied, use other methods.

- Water that has to be kept, such as in cisterns and rain barrels: cover it to prevent egg-laying itself.
- Wide water surfaces that cannot be covered: float a very thin film of vegetable oil or waste oil. Larvae and pupae cannot extend their breathing tubes at the surface and suffocate. This water cannot be used for drinking and pollutes surrounding soil and waterways, so use it only in a limited way on abandoned puddles.
- Tanks that can hold fish: small freshwater fish such as killifish and loaches eat up the larvae. This is the cleanest solution.

---
### 2-2. Smoke Is Not an Insecticide
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A smudge fire does actually reduce mosquitoes, but you need to know exactly how it works. There are three reasons smoke repels mosquitoes.

1. Smoke particles mask the mosquito's chemical sensors and scramble the scent trail of the carbon dioxide and body odor that people give off. When the scent trail is broken, the mosquito cannot find its target.
2. Depending on the plant, the volatile compounds mixed into the smoke themselves act as repellents. Examples are cineole and thujone in mugwort, terpenes in arborvitae and juniper, and pinene in pine needles.
3. Because of the rising air currents of the smoke, weakly flying mosquitoes find it hard to approach.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;None of the three is a killing action. When the smoke clears, the mosquitoes return, and even a little wind blows the smoke to one side and leaves the other side defenseless. A smudge fire cannot replace a mosquito net or source elimination; it is a supplementary measure during outdoor activity.

| Material | Main components | Characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mugwort (dried) | Cineole, thujone, camphor | Most widely used, strong fragrance, burns long |
| Arborvitae, hinoki, juniper leaves | Terpenes | Strong fragrance and thick smoke |
| Pine needles, pine cones | Pinene | Easy to get, but flames easily remain because of the resin |
| Citrus peel (dried) | Limonene | Strong fragrance even in small amounts |
| Green grass, green wood | Moisture | Works by the amount of smoke more than by components |

- Technique: let it smolder for a long time without producing flame. Covering well-dried material with less dry material or green grass keeps up incomplete combustion, so there is a lot of smoke and it lasts long. Lightly covering with soil to reduce the oxygen supply works on the same principle.
- Making a lot of smoke means causing incomplete combustion, and incomplete combustion makes carbon monoxide. Carbon monoxide has no color or smell and binds to hemoglobin more than 200 times more strongly than oxygen, blocking oxygen transport. It starts with a headache and drowsiness and leads to loss of consciousness, and accidents recur in which people fall asleep because of the drowsiness and die.

> **Caution:** Burning a smudge fire inside a closed tent or room kills people far more surely than mosquitoes. Smoking must always be done outdoors or in a completely open space, and indoors do not keep any kind of fire without ventilation.

### 2-3. Pyrethrum and Plant Repellents
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Among natural materials, the only plant with real killing power is pyrethrum (*Chrysanthemum cinerariifolium*). The pyrethrins in the flowers are the prototype of the pyrethroid family, a major pillar of modern insecticides.

- Action: it binds to the voltage-gated sodium channels of insect nerve cells and keeps the channels from closing. The nerve does not stop and sends signals repeatedly, so the insect immediately loses motor control and drops.
- Why mammalian toxicity is low: the sodium channels are structurally less sensitive, and ester-hydrolyzing enzymes in the liver and blood break down pyrethrins quickly.

| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Active part | The flower, with the components concentrated especially in the ovary |
| Harvest time | The content is highest when the flowers have fully opened and three or four rows of central disc florets are open |
| Drying | Always dry in the shade. Pyrethrins break down in light, so sun-drying destroys the active components |
| Storage | Keep sealed in a dark place. Potency drops with time |
| Use | Crush finely and sprinkle as powder, burn to make smoke, or steep in water, oil, or alcohol |
| Residue | Breaks down quickly in light, leaving almost no residue |

- The property of breaking down in light is both an advantage and a disadvantage. It does not stay in the environment and is relatively safe even around food, but it has almost no lasting effect and must be applied fresh each time.
- Pyrethrins are highly toxic to fish and cats. Be careful near aquariums or in spaces with cats.

| Other plants | Components and action | Real usefulness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neem | Azadirachtin. Inhibits feeding and disrupts growth | Does not kill immediately but slowly reduces the population |
| Cinnamon | Cinnamaldehyde. Repellent and larvicidal | Larvicidal effects have been reported |
| Clove | Eugenol. Repellent | Strong fragrance makes it useful for short-term repellence |
| Citronella, lemongrass | Citronellal, geraniol | The repellent effect is clear but the duration is short |
| Eucalyptus | Cineole | Used for repellence; the undiluted oil irritates skin |

> **Caution:** Tobacco leaf infusions are not recommended. Nicotine is a powerful insecticidal component, but it is also a strong neurotoxin to humans. It is readily absorbed through the skin, and merely handling the infusion with bare hands can cause nausea, dizziness, and heart rhythm abnormalities (green tobacco sickness in tobacco harvest workers). In a disaster, where there is no antidote and no way to gauge concentration, you lose far more than you gain.
