## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Deaths in a large-scale disaster come at two points in time. First, immediate deaths caused by the disaster itself, and second, a wave of diarrheal disease that arrives days to weeks later. The second curve lasts longer, and in uncontrolled refugee situations there are repeatedly recorded cases where it killed more people than the first. This curve is almost entirely a problem of human feces getting back into human mouths.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The amount needed is extremely small. Swallowing just 10-100 Shigella or rotavirus organisms, or a few dozen hepatitis A virus particles, causes infection. An amount invisible to the naked eye is enough. Conversely, building just one thin physical barrier properly breaks the chain.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This document assumes a situation in which electricity and water and sewage services are cut off, and covers barriers that can be built from materials available in nature. Rather than the order of building a latrine, it sets out as principles why 30 m, why 1.5 m above the groundwater table, and why spreading ash does not kill all the pathogens.
