## 2. Principles of the Improvised Latrine
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The function of a latrine is not to process feces but to isolate it. Among improvised latrines in disaster situations, almost none actually destroy pathogens, and most merely buy time and space. You must avoid the false reassurance of thinking that scattering ash made it safe.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For isolation to hold, three conditions must be met at the same time. It must not leak out through water paths, flies must not reach it, and people's hands and feet must not touch it. Whatever its form, it is evaluated by how it satisfies these three conditions.

---
### 2-1. Pit Latrine
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In a pit latrine, liquid seeps into the soil and is filtered, and solids remain in the pit, where anaerobic decomposition reduces their volume.

| Criterion | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distance from water sources | At least 30 m | The filtration distance needed for bacteria to be adsorbed and die while passing through the soil |
| Pit bottom and groundwater table | At least 1.5 m above | An unsaturated layer must remain for filtration to work |
| Up-and-down relation to water sources | Must be on the lower side of the water source | Groundwater flows along the slope |
| Distance from dwellings | Within 50 m | If too far, it goes unused at night and in rain |
| Depth | Generally 2-3 m | Deeper means a longer life but a greater risk of collapse |
| Number of users | 1 per 50 people at first, 1 per 20 people when stable | If the wait is long, people go back to open defecation |

- 30 m is the standard for ordinary soil. It is not enough in sand and gravel layers or heavily cracked bedrock, where water travels much farther without being filtered, while in clayey soil it is safe even at a much shorter distance. The criterion is not the distance but how fast water flows through the ground. The faster the ground drains after rain, the farther away you set it.
- 1.5 m above the groundwater table is even more decisive. When the pit bottom touches groundwater, the filtration zone disappears and the pit becomes not a sewage tank but an injection point for groundwater contamination. In regions where the groundwater table rises in the rainy season, use the rainy-season peak level, not the dry season.
- Service life is calculated by volume. The accumulation rate is generally 0.05-0.09 m³ per person per year. Assume less for a wet pit, where decomposition is faster, and more if anal cleansing materials other than paper are thrown in as well.

| Pit size | Effective volume | Service life for a family of 5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diameter 1 m, depth 2 m | About 1.6 m³ | About 4-6 years |
| 1 m×1 m, depth 3 m | About 3.0 m³ | About 7-12 years |
| 1 m×1 m, depth 1 m | About 1.0 m³ | About 2-4 years |

- When calculating, leave the top 0.5 m. If the pit fills nearly to the surface, flies and rainwater get in and there is a risk of overflow.
- Seal a pit at the end of its life by covering it with at least 30 cm of soil, and mark its location. Pathogens die over time, but roundworm eggs can remain for years, so do not dig the place up again.

---
### 2-2. Trenches and Cat Holes
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In the first few days, when there is neither time nor tools to dig a pit, use shallow structures. The principle is the same; only the scale differs.

| Method | Size | Principle and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cat hole | Diameter and depth 15-20 cm, single use | Microorganisms are most numerous in the shallow topsoil, so decomposition is fast. It must be covered completely every time |
| Shallow trench | Width 30 cm, depth 30-50 cm | Dig in a line and use from one end. Cover the used part immediately |
| Deep trench | Width 50-80 cm, depth 1.5-2 m | Used for several weeks to several months. Lay planks across so that the footing does not collapse |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Success or failure comes down to whether the covering soil is prepared beside it. If the soil is far away, people will not cover it, and an uncovered pit becomes a fly breeding ground and is actually more dangerous than open defecation.

---
### 2-3. Bucket Latrines and Absorbent Materials
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;On bedrock, where groundwater is high, and inside buildings, only the method of collecting in a container and carrying it away is possible. The key is not the container but the absorbent material covered over after each use. The absorbent does three jobs. It soaks up moisture to prevent leakage, covers the surface to block flies and odor, and supplies a carbon source to help decomposition. The odor is mostly hydrogen sulfide and ammonia produced by anaerobic decomposition, and when the surface is covered and moisture is reduced, both decrease greatly.

| Absorbent | Moisture absorption | Carbon supply | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sawdust, wood shavings | Very good | Very good | The best, but hard to get immediately in nature |
| Dry leaves, dry grass | Good | Good | The more finely crushed, the greater the effect |
| Rice husks, rice straw | Good | Good | Bulky, so must be emptied often |
| Dry soil | Moderate | None | The easiest to get, and its covering function is sufficient |
| Wood ash | Moderate | None | Alkaline, so it helps suppress odor, but decomposition slows |
| Dry sphagnum moss | Very good | Moderate | Can be obtained near wetlands |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The amount is enough that the excreta are completely invisible, which by volume is 1-2 times the excreta. If you skimp, the odor and flies return immediately. The quantity of absorbent you can secure sets the limit of what this method can accommodate.

### 2-4. Separating Urine and Feces
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The urine of a healthy person is almost sterile inside the bladder, and most pathogens in disaster hygiene are in the feces. The volume is overwhelmingly urine. These two facts are the basis for separation.

| Item | Urine | Feces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily output per person | About 1-1.5 L | About 100-250 g |
| Water content | About 95% or more | About 70-80% |
| Pathogens | Generally few | Very many |
| Main components | Urea, salts | Organic matter, microorganisms |

- When mixed, the feces absorb the urine's moisture and turn mushy. Wet organic matter turns anaerobic and produces foul-smelling substances such as hydrogen sulfide, its increased volume shortens the life of the pit, and because it cannot dry, the death of pathogens is slower too.
- When separated, the feces lose moisture quickly so drying and decomposition proceed, and the urine can be collected in a sealed container and handled separately.
- Fresh urine has almost no odor. When the urease enzyme of bacteria from feces breaks down urea to produce ammonia, a strong odor appears. The cause of urine odor is contact with feces, and a separating structure prevents this reaction itself and reduces odor.
- Separated urine is not harmless either. The urine of someone with a urinary tract infection may contain pathogens, and contamination with feces during excretion is the norm. When using it on a field, dilute it 5-10 times with water, and do not harvest for at least one month after spraying.

### 2-5. Ash and Quicklime
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sprinkling ash or lime over feces is an old custom and actually works. But misjudging the size and range of the effect is dangerous.

| Material | Chemical properties | Actual effect | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wood ash | Mainly potassium carbonate, pH about 10-12 | Covering, moisture absorption, raising surface pH, odor suppression | Acts only on the surface it touches and does not reach into the lump |
| Slaked lime (calcium hydroxide) | pH about 12.4 | Strong pH rise, effective in killing bacteria | Works only when moisture is present, and is slowly neutralized by carbon dioxide in the air |
| Quicklime (calcium oxide) | Reacts with water and gives off heat | Heat and drying, strong disinfection | High risk of burns to skin and eyes, and can spatter when it meets water |

- There are two reasons raising the pH disinfects. The strong alkali destroys the cell membranes and proteins of microorganisms, and it converts ammonium ions (NH₄⁺) into un-ionized ammonia (NH₃). Un-ionized ammonia passes through cell membranes and has far stronger disinfecting power. When lime is added to human waste, the share of this ammonia effect in killing bacteria and viruses is larger than that of pH.
- Sprinkled ash and lime cover only the surface and barely affect the layers below, so if disinfection is the purpose, mix it in.
- Roundworm eggs survive for a considerable time even in strong alkali.
- Using too much lime raises the pH too high, killing even the microorganisms responsible for decomposition, so the pit hardens and its volume does not shrink.

> **Caution:** Ash and lime are a covering and an odor suppressant, and do not judge that disinfection is complete merely because they were sprinkled. Quicklime gives off violent heat when it meets water and carries a burn risk.
