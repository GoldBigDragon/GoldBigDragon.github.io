## 4. Hunting Tools and Diseases from Wild Animals
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In situations traps alone cannot handle, such as open ground with no passages, waterside where birds gather, and environments without trap materials, tools for throwing and shooting are needed. Hunting tools are all a matter of how the energy made by human muscle is stored and how small an area it is concentrated onto.

---
### 4-1. Hunting Tools by Energy Transfer
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A human arm is short and slow. The speed of a stone thrown by bare hand is set by the length of the arm and its rotation speed and does not rise beyond that. Every projectile weapon gets past this limit by lengthening the lever or by storing energy beforehand and releasing it all at once.

| Tool | How it increases energy | Characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Throwing stick | Rotational inertia and a wide striking area | Easiest to make and effective on ground birds and small animals |
| Sling | Extends the radius of rotation by the length of the cord | Powerful but takes a very long time to master |
| Throwing spear | Uses the whole arm as a lever | Short range and easy to recover |
| Spear-thrower (atlatl) | Artificially extends the arm length | Greatly raises the spear's speed with the same force |
| Bow | Stores the force of drawing the string in the limbs | Releases the stored energy all at once |

- Spear-thrower: It increases the effective length of the arm. When a short rod hooked to the back end of the spear extends from the wrist, the tangential speed at the moment of release grows in proportion to the radius of rotation. If a person with a 70 cm arm uses a 50 cm spear-thrower, the effective radius becomes 1.7 times, and at the same angular velocity the speed is also 1.7 times. Kinetic energy is proportional to the square of speed, so the energy becomes about 3 times.
- Bow: The work done while drawing the string is stored as the elastic energy of the limbs and transferred to the arrow at the moment of release. The work stored is close to the area of draw force times draw length. With the same tension, a bow with a longer draw is stronger, and with the same draw length, a bow with greater tension is stronger. If the bow is short, even drawing the same distance bends the limbs too far and breaks them, so the draw length cannot be increased. This is why bows are made large.

| Element | Principle | Cause of failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Limb material | Must withstand both compression and tension | Dry wood splits on the tension side |
| Limb thickness | Thinner toward both ends | If the thickness is uniform, only the middle bends and breaks |
| Draw length | Stored energy rises as it gets longer | Dangerous beyond half the bow length |
| Bowstring | Must not stretch | A stretching cord absorbs energy |
| Straightness of the arrow shaft | If it bends, the direction goes off | Use bent twigs straightened with heat |
| Arrow weight | Heavier means more penetration, lighter means more speed | If too light, it breaks the bow |
| Fletching | Stabilizes attitude through rotation and drag | Without fletching the arrow flies sideways |

- For bow material use wood with high elasticity and straight grain, such as ash, mulberry, oak, and bamboo.
- Make the outer layer of the wood the back of the bow. The outer fibers are strong in tension and the inner ones are strong in compression. If you set it the opposite way, the bow almost certainly breaks.
- The bow is one of the hardest tools to make in a disaster. Drying and shaping the limbs takes several weeks, you must also have the string, arrows, and fletching, and hitting a target takes long practice. It is not the first thing a person who is starving right now should touch. Once traps have created a basic supply and you have some spare capacity, begin it as a long-term investment.

### 4-2. Diseases Passed from Wild Animals to People
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When handling wild animals there is a quiet danger far more common than gunshot wounds or bites: zoonotic disease. The route of infection directly determines cooking and handling.

| Disease | Main vector | Route of infection | Constraint on handling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rabies | Raccoon dogs, badgers, bats, canids | Bites, saliva through wounds | Do not catch or touch an animal that behaves strangely |
| Tularemia | Rabbits, rodents, ticks | Skin wounds, inhalation, undercooked meat | Do not skin with bare hands, and cook completely |
| Brucellosis | Wild boar, deer | Contact with blood and body fluids, eating raw | Keep body fluids from splashing into eyes and mouth when dressing |
| Leptospirosis | Rodents such as rats, contaminated water | Contact with urine through wounds and mucous membranes | If you have a wound, do not enter the water |
| Toxoplasmosis | Felids, meat | Cysts in undercooked meat | Killed by sufficient heating |
| Hantavirus | Rodents such as the striped field mouse | Inhaling dried droppings | Do not sweep burrows and nests dry |
| Trichinosis | Wild boar, bears | Meat with larvae in the muscle | Must be cooked through to the center |
| Avian influenza | Wild birds | Contact with feces and body fluids | Do not touch dead birds |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There are three recurring rules.

1. Body fluids and blood enter through skin wounds and mucous membranes. If you have a wound on your hand, do not dress animals, or cover it with something.
2. Most pathogens are killed by sufficient heating. Eat wild meat only completely cooked, without exception. Cooking only the surface has no effect on larvae in the muscle.
3. There is a route by inhaling dried droppings. Do not sweep or shake out an animal's burrow or nest site dry; spray it with water to settle the dust and then clear it.

- Do not catch animals that behave differently from usual. An animal that is nocturnal but wanders in the daytime, does not run even when it sees a person, or staggers or drools is as easy to catch as it is likely to be sick.
- Discard entirely any individual caught in a trap that is unusually thin with hair loss, has white spots on the liver and spleen, or has pus and a foul smell in the abdominal cavity. What you lose is far greater than the calories you gain by eating it.

> **Caution:** Eat wild meat only cooked through to the center, without exception. Discard an individual that behaves strangely or shows abnormalities in its organs, even if you are hungry.

### 4-3. Law and Ethics
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The content of this document cannot be carried out as is in ordinary times (see the legal caution in the Introduction). South Korea's hunting license is divided into Class 1, which uses firearms, and Class 2, which uses other tools, and you must pass an exam, a course, and a medical examination. Snares, traps, and pits are in principle prohibited because they catch regardless of target species, even endangered species.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Three principles remain even in a survival situation.

- Catch only as much as you need.
- Do not waste what you catch.
- When you leave, take up all the traps.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If you take every animal in an area, it becomes hard to get through the next season there, and the longer the disaster lasts, the more this loss falls back on the people themselves. How to use a caught animal completely continues in the Butchering document.
