## 1-1. Bosnian War
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I want to share my experience with you. Please be understanding if my English is not perfect. I come from a distant place.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I am from Bosnia, and from 1992 to 1995, it was like hell. Anyway, I survived for a year in a city of about 50,000 to 60,000 people without electricity, fuel, water, food supply, or any public authority. The city was under siege for a year, and it was a complete SHTF (Shit Hits The Fan) situation.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When the disaster began, some were prepared, but most families only had a few days' worth of food, and only a few people had firearms. Since there was no organized military or police, those with weapons had to fight to protect themselves and their families.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fortunately, my family was large. We had 15 people living in one house, with 5 or 6 handguns and 3 AK-47s. In a disaster situation, numbers meant power, and thanks to that, we survived. But no matter how well-prepared we were, if I had been alone, I would have been robbed and lost my life.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;After a month or two, the police completely disappeared, and armed robbers started committing extremely brutal crimes. The hospitals turned into slaughterhouses, and 80% of the hospital staff eventually went home.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The US Air Force dropped MREs (Meals Ready to Eat) every 10 days (which we were very grateful for), and that was a big help, but it was still absolutely insufficient. Some families grew vegetables in small gardens, but most people couldn’t do that.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;After three months, people began dying one by one from starvation and hypothermia, and rumors started spreading. We burned doors and window frames from abandoned houses for warmth, and I burned all my furniture to heat our home. Many people died from diseases, and many of them died from contaminated water. Two members of my family also passed away this way. We drank rainwater and sometimes ate pigeons and, once, a rat.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Money had no value. We traded goods through bartering, and the black market was booming. To give a few examples, you could get a woman for a few hours with a can of corned beef. It might be uncomfortable to hear, but that was the reality. Many of those women were desperate mothers. People needed candles, lighters, antibiotics, fuel, batteries, ammunition, and food, and they fought like beasts to get it. Most people became more and more brutal, and the situation was horrific.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Anyway, the war is over, and once again, I am grateful to the US. (And above all, I thank God.) It no longer matters which side was right in the war.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Now, nearly 30 years have passed since that event, but it still feels as vivid as if it happened yesterday. I remember all of those experiences, and I believe I learned a lot. My family and I are now prepared for any situation. I have enough weapons and supplies, and I’ve received the necessary training.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;No matter what happens, it’s not about what happens. Earthquakes, wars, tsunamis, alien invasions, terrorist attacks… the important thing is that something will inevitably happen. And from my experience, you can never survive alone. Power comes in numbers. Be with your family. Prepare with them. And choose your friends wisely, and prepare with them too.
