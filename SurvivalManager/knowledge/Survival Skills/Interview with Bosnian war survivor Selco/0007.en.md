# 6. Low Profile
---
## 6-1. Low Profile
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If most people are desperate, poor, and dirty, you should look like them. No one should know that you have a lot of food, ammunition, clean clothes, etc., at home. Appear and behave like everyone else. However, if someone attacks you or your family, that is when you should show that you are prepared.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When disaster strikes and you are left alone in the city, don’t lock yourself in a wall, and don’t install fancy bulletproof doors or windows. What I suggest is to break down walls and windows. Then, place old clothes and broken LCD TVs, and put dead animals (like puppies or cats) outside to create a stench.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In simple terms, make your house look like it has been robbed. Empty and of no interest. (Most people won’t go into an empty house, especially one with the smell of dead animals.)  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The most well-protected homes were the first to be attacked. Of course, we had some nice homes in the neighborhood, and they had walls, dogs, security equipment, and bars. But armed robbers attacked those homes first. Some homes succeeded in defense, but others did not. It depended on how many guns and people were inside.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;So, I think security is important, but it’s also crucial to maintain a low profile. Forget about security equipment. If disaster strikes in the city, it’s best to have a simple, uninteresting house with many guns and ammunition. Your house should maintain a low profile and be made unappealing.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;No one in the city wanted to look nice. Thieves might target valuable items. You also don’t want to walk around with fancy guns. Because it’s hard to get ammunition for them, and it attracts attention.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If disaster strikes tomorrow, I will try to look like everyone else. Afraid, desperate, confused, and probably screaming. I will maintain a low profile, be armed, well-prepared, and assess my options. If I need to go out at night, I’ll take all my gear and go with my closest friend or brother. It may sound ridiculous, but in my experience, it worked well. Be well-prepared. And don’t let anyone outside your home know about your preparedness.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;No matter how good your home security is, or how great your weapons are, if you give people a reason to loot your house, it’s only a matter of time. Never give them a reason to loot. Act unappealing.

## 6-2. Residence Security
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The defense of our dwelling was very primitive. The windows were broken, and the roof was mostly damaged by shelling. All the windows were blocked with something, and sandbags and stones were used to barricade them. Every night, I would block my yard door with debris from the street, and I used an old aluminum ladder to climb over the wall. When returning, I would call someone inside the house to bring the ladder back. After that, I would use the ladder to enter the house.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;One person on our street had a house that was completely destroyed with everything blocked off, but every night he came out through a hole connected to the neighboring house. In fact, he had a secret exit.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Of course, before doing all of this, invest in a good basement. Set up a hidden entrance and an air filtration system, and store supplies and weapons there. This is just a suggestion, and I’m not sure, but it might be better to move out of the city with friends or family.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I’ve now installed a security steel door on my apartment. But that’s just for withstanding the initial chaos. After that, I plan to move to the countryside with more armed people (family and friends). (I hope this happens.)

## 6-3. Movement
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I hardly used the car. Most roads were blocked by ruins, abandoned cars, destroyed houses, and debris, and fuel was as valuable as gold.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Whenever I had to go somewhere, I always traveled at night, and I never went alone. But I also never traveled with a large group (just 2 or 3 people). We were always armed, and we moved quickly, passing through the shadows. We never walked openly in the streets, always hiding among the ruins.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Always travel at night. Never go alone, take 2-3 people at most. Move very quickly and make sure no one notices you.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Almost no one went outside during the day. The reason was snipers. If there was something to be done, it had to be done at night. Whether exchanging goods, gathering firewood (which was very important in the city), searching for something, or meeting people (getting news was very important). Many people went somewhere to find out what was happening, but they ended up dead. Remember, with no news, radio, or TV, people lost many lives because of rumors.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;As an example of a rumor, there was talk of an elderly man several blocks away who was looking for someone to exchange canned goods for bullets or something else. Also, there were rumors of trade happening in the ruins of a large sports center at night, but that place was a very dangerous spot with no one guarding it.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To avoid snipers, you had to stay indoors during the day. Even at night, you had to move quickly and quietly through the ruins rather than walking on the road.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There was a large trading post about 5 miles from my street, but during the day there were snipers, and at night there was a high chance of being robbed, so going there was too risky. Therefore, I only went to that area when absolutely necessary.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I’ve never traveled in a large group. In disaster situations, large groups were like armed robbers.
