# 4. Items
---
## 4-1. Fire and Wood
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When the electricity disappeared, the trees in the city were quickly used for heating. After that, things like furniture, doors, and wooden floors remained, but they didn’t last long either.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;People took anything they could use to make fire or shelter. Even though I lived in a city with trees and parks, I had to tear up the floorboards in abandoned buildings.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Some people walked miles to light a fire on a tree and then brought it back home to cook and heat their houses. Lighters and matches were truly valuable resources, and most people couldn’t find enough firewood. Eventually, people had to constantly search for fire, wood, food, and bullets.

## 4-2. Disaster Preparedness Supplies
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The supplies needed vary depending on the situation, but it is hard to survive if you're only prepared for one scenario.  

### 4-2-1. Weapons
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It’s good to prepare simple weapons. I have two 7.62mm TT Russian pistols. Many people in this area use this gun, so it's easy to find ammunition.  
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In disaster situations, if you have something to trade, getting weapons is not difficult. But the important part is the initial stage of the disaster. During times of extreme confusion and fear, not being armed could put you in great danger.  
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I traded a car battery for two pistols. How much ammunition do you need? A lot. A lot, a lot. Try to gather as much as possible.  
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The first thing to prepare is weapons and ammunition. After that, everything else follows. It’s okay if you forget to pack something. There will be people wanting to trade. But if you forget the guns and ammunition, even going out to trade might be difficult.  

### 4-2-2. Food
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Salt was an important resource, but coffee and cigarettes were much more valuable. Of course, this is from my region, and in other areas, salt might be a more valuable resource. It’s best to prepare as many different items as possible.  
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If clean water is scarce, young children are the first to get sick. Without medicine or knowledge of treatment, even minor illnesses can be deadly.

### 4-2-3. Hygiene Supplies
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Many people lost their lives due to poor hygiene. Even simple items related to hygiene should be gathered. For example, trash bags can be used as multifunctionally as duct tape.  
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Cups, plates, paper, plastic bags were in high demand, but we had none of those. In my opinion, hygiene-related items might be even more important than food. Pigeons are easy to catch, and if your grandmother knows edible plants, that’s even better. But disinfectants, water purification tablets, cleaners, soap, bleach, gloves, and masks are not easy to obtain.  
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;All hygiene-related items were extremely valuable. Even toilet paper was rare. Over time, I think more people died from diseases caused by poor hygiene than from being killed by armed robbers. Be aware that infectious diseases were common.  
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Most things could be traded with others, but medicines and fuel were hard to trade.  
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When there was no toilet paper, we used rainwater or sometimes had to go to the river. But going to the river was very dangerous. Toilet paper was often unavailable, and if we had any, we used it for barter.  
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Many people died from infections due to small wounds. Diarrhea could kill someone in a few days without treatment, and it was particularly deadly to young children. Skin diseases and food poisoning were common, but there were few ways to treat them.  
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I had antibiotics for 3-4 doses, but they were only for my family. Basically, we treated diseases with herbs, and for wounds, we used strong alcohol and then looked for a way to get antibiotics.  

### 4-2-4. Energy Products
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Energy-related products like batteries and generators are important, but generators are noisy and could easily attract raiders.  

### 4-2-5. Small Items for Barter
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Prepare small items. Pocket knives, lighters, candles, matches, and other such items don't take up much space but can be useful. Preparing 1,000 disposable lighters might be a better choice than a generator. Generators make noise and can attract raiders, but lighters are cheap, take up little space, and are always good for trade.  
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Alcohol with high alcohol content, like whiskey, is also good for bartering. The type doesn’t matter. Even the cheapest kind is fine. We used alcohol for disinfection, but in desperate situations, people searched for alcohol ten times more than usual. Bartering with alcohol was no more dangerous than bartering with other goods.  
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If you have enough money, time, and storage space, it might be smarter to stock up on cigarettes, candles, batteries, antibiotics, and food for trading instead of alcohol.  
- &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The value of money collapsed quickly. In just a few weeks, or at most a few months, its value disappeared. Sometimes foreign currencies like dollars or German marks could be used, but the exchange rate skyrocketed to unbelievable levels. For example, a can of beans that used to cost 50 cents rose to 30-40 dollars. But such cases were rare, and most trades were done through bartering.

## 4-3. Bartering
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I have never bartered in front of my dwelling, and I have never traded a large amount of goods at once. Because of that, very few people knew what items I had and how much of them there were at my house.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Bartering has no intermediaries. For this reason, I kept a low profile and hid everything except my weapons. For example, even if I wore good boots, I would make them dirty by rubbing them in the dirt. If they didn’t look like good items, they wouldn’t attract the attention of robbers.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For bartering, I would wait for the dark of night, gather a pistol, knife, dark jacket, boots, and plenty of ammunition, and head out. I didn’t bring many goods to trade. The more items you have, the greater the risk. Also, to avoid becoming a target, I didn’t hide my weapons. I had to look like a poor but armed person. If I saw a large group at the bartering location, I would quietly hide.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Most deals were made through rumors. I would ask people if there was a place nearby to trade MREs (Meals Ready to Eat). Sometimes they knew, and other times they didn’t. If I judged that the terms of the trade were worth the risk, I would go and make the exchange.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Some bartering locations were dangerous, while others were relatively safer. There were no fixed rules for bartering, and the exchange rates would fluctuate. For example, if relief supplies dropped twice in a week, MREs would sometimes be traded at a lower price.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;All trades involved risks, so during the first few minutes, the seller and buyer would often hide behind rocks, about 30 to 40 meters apart, and talk. Only after confirming mutual trust would the trade begin.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The return journey after bartering was always tough. If you prepare a large storage space in advance and gather goods, you can reduce the problems that may arise from bartering.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To prepare barter items, you need to gather as many goods as possible, within your storage space and budget, and then assess what items are most needed depending on the situation. Of course, for me, ammunition and firearms would always be a priority, but no one can know for sure. A mask with a filter might become the second most popular item for trade.
