# 7. Disaster, Afterwards

## 7-1. Finance
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Speaking of banks, loans, and credit cards, the monetary system completely collapsed for about a year, and nothing worked. Even after nearly 20 years, people were suing banks in European courts because the banks refused to acknowledge the savings they had entrusted. During that time, many things happened, the currency name changed 2 or 3 times, and hyperinflation occurred. Records of savings and loans disappeared. During this period, some people became rich by seizing the opportunity, and they are still wealthy.   

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Back then, it was very difficult to prove people's property ownership. For example, my father owned a nice apartment, but had to leave it due to the war. After the war ended, he spent four years in court to prove that the apartment was his. The reason was political issues, and because my father did not take the relevant documents when he left the apartment. He had more pressing matters at the time and did not take the papers with him. Meanwhile, during the worst part of the war, people would simply move into abandoned houses and live there.

## 7-2. Public Safety
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Currently, the situation in my country is that crime rates are rising, unemployment is skyrocketing, people are sinking into despair, and the prices of food and fuel keep rising. Political corruption has also worsened, and drug use and armed robbery are still major problems. Therefore, saying that [someone will solve everything] is truly foolish. I strongly believe that a collapse or some form of explosion is highly likely.   

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Even after the peace agreements were signed, it took months for the streets to be safe to walk, for gunfire to cease, and for robberies to stop. This was possible only because of the intervention of foreign troops, and the reconstruction of the city proceeded very slowly. It was difficult to restore trust among the people. It may take generations to solve this problem.
