# 5. Learn and Search
---
## 5-1. Learn Anything
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;At that time, we were not prepared for war and knew nothing about prepping (disaster preparedness). As a result, in most situations, we were thrown back to the Stone Age.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;We made the most of what we had. I didn't use the gas can attached to the propane (or butane) stove for cooking or heating. It was too valuable. Instead, I connected the gas can to a hose with a friend, allowing us to refill disposable lighters. (Actually, they are not disposable; you just need to know how to refill them.) Later, if someone brought an empty lighter, I would refill it with gas in exchange for food, candles, or whatever they could offer.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Here's another example. I had a nursing qualification, and in such times, my knowledge could be a huge asset. That's why it’s important to always be learning and training. In those times, even just knowing something could be of great value. All supplies will eventually run out, but your knowledge can be a lifeline.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In other words, learn how to fix things. (Learn to fix shoes, people, anything.) My neighbor knew how to make oil for oil lamps, so he didn’t go hungry. He never taught me how to make oil. I think he used the trees behind his house or a small amount of diesel.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;My point is, learn. What people always need is someone who can fix things.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Learn how to treat small wounds, burns, or gunshot wounds. There may be no hospital, and even if you find a doctor, they may not have the medicine to prescribe or the goods to pay for treatment. Learn how and when to use antibiotics, and stock up on a lot of them. With good knowledge and enough medicine, you should have no trouble surviving.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I was fairly good at treating wounds, but long-term care was impossible. What I learned was hygiene and medication, especially antibiotics. You need to learn how to treat a lot of things. Study online, or get relevant certifications. Things like EMT or first aid. Everything changes in a disaster situation. Learn how to open an IV, when to use specific drugs or antibiotics.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Get a tetanus shot for yourself, prepare kits like a snake venom treatment kit, an adrenaline kit (for allergic reactions, etc.), a tick removal kit (learn how to remove ticks), and a rehabilitation kit (simple ones). Learn how to use them; it's really not hard. Of course, you can't use them regularly. Only certified EMTs, nurses, or doctors can. But in a disaster situation, no one will ask for certifications. Just learn and prepare medical supplies.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Not having a dentist was a really big problem. I knew a guy who lived a few blocks away, and he was pulling teeth out with an electric plier. He rinsed his mouth with strong alcohol, then mixed in herbs. Of course, after that treatment, antibiotics were absolutely necessary. The payment for this treatment was a few cigarettes, and he was a veterinarian.

## 5-2. Search for Yourself
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In the days leading up to the disaster, when politicians on TV said [everything is fine], the sky was falling, and we just grabbed what we could.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I’ve since gained the ability to sense when trouble is coming. When everyone says everything is going to be fine, I know it’s all going to collapse.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I no longer trust the government or authorities. When they are confident that everything is fine, I know bad things are going to happen. Don’t just trust them, investigate for yourself.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Before the disaster, there were definitely signs, but we either didn’t see them or didn’t want to see them. We knew important figures had left the city, but we couldn’t believe it. We couldn’t imagine that such things could happen to ordinary people.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I believe the most important thing is to accept that bad things can happen and that they could eventually become reality. Of course, I don’t trust mainstream media. I prefer to search for information online and distinguish between truth and lies.

## 5-3. If I Had 3 Months to Prepare for a Disaster
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I would probably flee abroad. (Just kidding.) Now I know that situations can turn very bad, so I’ve prepared 6 months' worth of food, hygiene supplies, and energy sources. I live in a somewhat secure apartment. There’s a house with a shelter about 5 miles away, and it has 6 months' worth of supplies as well. The village is a small community, mostly made up of my family. Most are prepared (as I learned from war). The house has 4 types of firearms with 2,000 rounds of ammunition for each. The house has a large garden and good knowledge of farming and gardening.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;From stories I’ve heard in other areas and from friends, things were much better in rural areas. The countryside had land, corn, wheat, fruit trees, and farms, and they were able to secure enough food. Of course, it was a tough time, but much better than in the city. One thing is certain: If we had a way to escape the city, we would have left for the countryside.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I read a novel a few years ago. In that book, I thought it accurately depicted how people’s mindsets change in a disaster situation and what they are ready to do to protect themselves. I recommend that book: **John Christopher - Death of Grass**.
