## 1. Why Fishing Comes Before Hunting, and How to Read Fish
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fishing and hunting are not two different tools serving the same purpose; they are two activities with fundamentally different calorie-balance structures. Many people fail to understand this difference, spend their time hunting, and end up starving, so this section starts by laying out the criteria for deciding which method to try first.

---
### 1-1. Return Relative to Calories Invested
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;An adult man's basal metabolic rate runs to roughly 1,500~1,700kcal a day, and walking rough terrain all day adds more than 2,000kcal on top of that. In a starved state, the body itself cuts activity level to shrink this budget, so any activity that burns a lot of calories carries a very large loss if it fails. Hunting has a low success rate, and calories spent on a failed hunt never come back, whereas passive fishing gear, once you have spent the calories to build it, costs you only the trip to go check on it afterward.

| Method | Nature of the Input | Does It Require a Person Present? | Loss on Failure |
| --- | --- | :---: | --- |
| Pursuit hunting | High calorie cost, long duration | Required | Total loss of calories spent |
| Rod fishing | Medium, long waiting time | Required | Loss of time; calorie loss is small |
| Traps, gillnets, longlines | Concentrated investment in building; low afterward | Not required | The gear itself remains |
| Eosal (fixed stake weir), dolsal (stone weir) | Very large initial investment | Not required | The structure remains in place and keeps yielding catch |
| Hand-catching (by feel) | Low | Required | Risk of losing body heat |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The value of passive gear lies not in the amount you recover, but in the reliability of recovering something. If you set out ten traps, a day when every single one is empty is rare — something comes out of at least one. Even if it doesn't fill your entire day's calorie needs, eliminating the days when protein intake drops to 0 matters far more when you are starving.

> **Gear keeps working while a person sleeps; hunting only works while a person is awake. The hungrier you are, the less time you spend awake, so the hungrier you are, the more the advantage shifts toward gear.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Passive gear does come with a trade-off, though. Gathering the cord and the material to weave it, and twisting it into gear, takes several days, so you need to start before the situation deteriorates. Once you are already weakened, you won't even have the strength left to build the gear.

---
### 1-2. The Principle Behind Reading Where Fish Are
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fish are not spread evenly through the water. Where fish gather is decided by the overlap of three needs: first, a place where food collects; second, a place to hide; and third, a place where little energy is needed to swim. Fish concentrate in the narrow zones where all three conditions are met at once, and you can spot those zones by eye from the shape of the terrain.

| Terrain Feature | Why Fish Gather There |
| --- | --- |
| A ledge where depth changes sharply | The deep side serves as shelter, the shallow side as a feeding ground, and the ledge becomes the passage between the two |
| Fallen trees, piles of rock, the edge of water weeds | There is structure to hide in, and small organisms live attached to it, making food plentiful |
| A spot where the current turns back on itself (an eddy) | The flow dies down there, so little effort is needed to swim, and food drifting down from upstream collects |
| Where two channels of water merge | Food and oxygen are supplied at once, and a boundary in current speed forms |
| Under bridge piers, weirs, or floodgates | Artificial structures create a current boundary and shade at the same time |
| Shaded water's edge | The surface temperature is lower, and it is hidden from predators looking down from above |
| Tide pools in the intertidal zone | Fish are left trapped there when the tide goes out |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A spot where the current speed breaks is especially important. In flowing water, a fish constantly burns calories just swimming to hold its position. So fish stay in the slow water right next to the fast current, picking off only the food the fast water carries past. Behind rocks, on the inside of a bend, and at the throat where a riffle ends and the water deepens are all places like this. The spot is exactly where the pattern on the water's surface suddenly turns smooth, or where it curls back on itself.

---
### 1-3. Water Temperature and Dissolved Oxygen
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fish are cold-blooded, so as water temperature rises, their metabolic rate rises and they eat more. But the amount of oxygen water can hold in solution falls as the temperature rises. Because these two curves move in opposite directions, warm summer water creates a situation where fish need more oxygen just as the supply shrinks, and fish crowd into whatever spots still have enough oxygen.

| Water Temp | Saturated Dissolved Oxygen in Freshwater (approx.) | State of the Fish |
| :---: | :---: | --- |
| 0°C | About 14.6mg/L | Metabolism at its lowest, barely eating |
| 10°C | About 11.3mg/L | Activity begins, stays in deep water |
| 20°C | About 9.1mg/L | The range where feeding is most active |
| 25°C | About 8.3mg/L | Still active, but oxygen margin shrinks |
| 30°C | About 7.6mg/L | Avoids the surface layer, moves to cold water, riffles, or spring seeps |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;As a general rule, fish come under stress once dissolved oxygen drops below 5mg/L, and most species die below 2mg/L. In stagnant midsummer water, fish poking their mouths above the surface and gasping is a sign of low oxygen, and in such a spot, even if you set gear there, the fish you catch will soon die and rot before long. In winter, by contrast, the water temperature in the deep water under the ice is the most stable, so fish gather there.

---
### 1-4. Time of Day and Season
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Most fish are most active at dawn and dusk because the amount of light affects predator and prey differently. As it starts to grow dark, there is a window when small fish can still see but birds looking down from above cannot see well; small fish come out into shallow water during this window, and the larger fish hunting them follow them out. At midday, by contrast, fish retreat into deep, shaded spots.

| Period | Fish Behavior | What It Means for Fishing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dawn, dusk | Come out into shallow water and feed actively | The best time for rod fishing and cast nets |
| Midday | Moves into deep water, under structure | Traps should be placed in deep water |
| Night | Active depending on species; catfish and eels are nocturnal | Why longlines and traps are set out at night |
| Spring spawning season | Crowds into shallow riffles and weed beds | The easiest time to catch fish, but the risk of depleting the stock is high |
| Summer | Water warms, fish avoid low-oxygen zones | Focus on spots where cold water flows in |
| Autumn | Feeds actively to prepare for winter | The period with the most stable catch |
| Winter | Metabolism drops, stays put in deep water | Fine to lengthen the gear-checking interval |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fish that crowd together during spawning are the easiest to catch, but one fish taken then is equivalent to wiping out hundreds of the next generation. If there is a chance the disaster will not be over in a few months, sweeping an entire spawning ground clean during spawning season is a choice that trades the catch you get right now for hunger the following year.
