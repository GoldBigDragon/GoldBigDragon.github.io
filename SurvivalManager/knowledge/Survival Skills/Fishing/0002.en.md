## 1. Calorie Balance of Fishing and Hunting, Where the Fish Are
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fishing and hunting are not different tools for the same purpose but activities whose calorie balance is fundamentally different in structure. We start by setting out the criteria for deciding which method to try first.

---
### 1-1. Recovery Relative to Calories Invested
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;An adult male's basal metabolic rate is 1,500-1,700 kcal a day, and walking all day over rough ground adds more than 2,000 kcal. When starving, the body cuts its activity on its own, so activities that use many calories carry a large loss on failure. Hunting has a low success rate, and even when it fails the calories spent do not come back. Passive gear costs calories once to make, and after that costs only the trip to check it.

| Method | Nature of input | Does a person need to be present | Loss on failure |
| --- | --- | :---: | --- |
| Hunting by pursuit | High calories, long duration | Must be present | Total loss of calories spent |
| Rod fishing | Moderate, long waiting time | Must be present | Loss of time, little loss of calories |
| Fish traps, gill nets, longlines | Concentrated input in making, low afterward | Not needed | The gear itself remains |
| Fish weirs, stone weirs | Very large initial input | Not needed | The structure remains and keeps being recovered |
| Catching by feeling with the hands | Low | Must be present | Risk of losing body heat |

- The value of passive gear is not the amount recovered but the stability of recovery. If you set ten traps, days when all are empty are rare, and something comes out of one. Even if it doesn't fill the day's calories, eliminating days when protein supply is zero matters far more in starvation.
- Gear works while people sleep, and hunting works only while awake. The more you starve, the fewer waking hours you have, so gear becomes more advantageous.
- The trade-off: obtaining and twisting the line and materials for gear takes days, so you must start before the situation worsens. After you have already weakened, you have no strength to build gear.

---
### 1-2. The Principle of Reading Where Fish Are
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fish are not spread evenly through the water. They gather in narrow zones where three conditions overlap: places where food collects, places to hide, and places where swimming takes little energy, and such zones can be read from the terrain by eye.

| Terrain feature | Why fish gather |
| --- | --- |
| A step where depth changes sharply | The deep side is a refuge and the shallow side a feeding ground, so it becomes the passage between the two |
| Fallen trees, rock piles, edges of aquatic plants | There is structure to hide in and small organisms live on it, so food is plentiful |
| Spots where the current bends and swirls back | The flow dies so swimming takes little effort, and food washed down from upstream collects |
| Where two streams join | Food and oxygen are supplied at the same time and a boundary of flow speed forms |
| Below bridge piers, weirs, and sluice gates | Man-made structures create a flow boundary and shade at the same time |
| Shaded banks | Surface temperature is lower and fish are hidden from predators looking down from above |
| Tide pools in the intertidal zone | Fish are trapped and left behind by the ebb tide |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The places where the current bends are especially important. To hold position in flowing water you must keep spending calories, so fish stay in slow spots right next to fast current and eat only the food the current carries. These include behind rocks, the inside of bends, and the neck where riffles end and the water deepens. The places where the pattern on the water surface suddenly smooths or swirls backward are such spots.

---
### 1-3. Water Temperature and Dissolved Oxygen
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fish are cold-blooded, so as water temperature rises their metabolic rate rises and they eat more. But the amount of oxygen dissolved in water decreases as temperature rises. In hot summer water, oxygen demand grows and supply shrinks, so fish crowd to places where oxygen is available.

| Water temperature | Saturated dissolved oxygen in fresh water (approx.) | State of the fish |
| :---: | :---: | --- |
| 0 °C | About 14.6 mg/L | Metabolism lowest, hardly eat |
| 10 °C | About 11.3 mg/L | Start to be active, stay in deep water |
| 20 °C | About 9.1 mg/L | Range of most active feeding |
| 25 °C | About 8.3 mg/L | Active, but oxygen margin shrinks |
| 30 °C | About 7.6 mg/L | Avoid the surface layer and move to cold water, riffles, and springs |

- When dissolved oxygen falls below 5 mg/L, fish are stressed, and below 2 mg/L most species die.
- In midsummer stagnant water, fish sticking their mouths out at the surface and gulping is a sign of oxygen shortage. In such places, even if you set gear, the caught fish soon die and rot.
- In winter, the water temperature in the deep water under the ice is the most stable, so fish gather there.

---
### 1-4. Time of Day and Season
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Most fish are most active at dawn and dusk. As darkness begins there is a window in which small fish can still see but birds looking down from above cannot see well, so small fish come out into the shallows, and the large fish that prey on them follow. In the middle of the day they retreat to deep, shaded places.

| Period | Behavior of fish | Meaning for fishing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dawn, dusk | Come out to the shallows and feed actively | Best time for fishing and cast nets |
| Midday | Move to deep water, under structures | Place fish traps in deep water |
| Night | Active depending on species. Catfish and eels are nocturnal | The reason longlines and fish traps are left in at night |
| Spring spawning season | Crowd into shallow riffles and plant zones | The easiest time to catch but a great risk of depleting stocks |
| Summer | Water temperature rises, avoid zones short of oxygen | Concentrate on places where cold water emerges |
| Autumn | Feed actively in preparation for winter | The time when the catch is most stable |
| Winter | Metabolism drops, stagnate in deep water | Fine to lengthen the gear recovery interval |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fish crowded at spawning are the easiest to catch, but one fish caught then destroys hundreds of the next generation. Considering the possibility that the disaster will not end within a few months, sweeping a spawning ground clean trades that spot's harvest for next year's hunger.
