# 2. Loops
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A knot-tying method that creates a fixed or adjustable loop capable of holding or securing objects.  

---
## 2-1. Figure Eight Loop
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Relatively simple to tie and secure, though it can become difficult to untie after being subjected to a heavy load.  

1. Fold the rope in half to form a bight.  
![image](./media/Figure_8_Loop_001.png)  
2. Wrap the working end on the right around the standing part once.  
![image](./media/Figure_8_Loop_002.png)  
3. Pass the working end through the loop created on the right.  
![image](./media/Figure_8_Loop_003.png)  
4. Hold both sides of the rope and pull tight.  
![image](./media/Figure_8_Loop_004.png)  
5. The completed Figure Eight Loop.  
![image](./media/Figure_8_Loop_005.png)  

---
## 2-2. Bowline
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A secure fixed loop knot widely utilized in maritime operations, rescue activities, and general ropework.  

1. Form a small loop in the standing part of the rope and pass the working end up through the loop.  
![image](./media/Bowline_Knot_001.png)  
2. Take the working end, wrap it completely around the standing part, and pass it back down through the loop.  
![image](./media/Bowline_Knot_002.png)  
3. Hold both the working end and the standing part, then pull to tighten.  
![image](./media/Bowline_Knot_003.png)  
4. The completed Bowline knot.  
![image](./media/Bowline_Knot_004.png)  

---
## 2-3. Lariat Loop
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A knot-tying method traditionally used by cowboys to construct a lasso for capturing cattle and horses.  

1. Create a small loop on the left side of the rope and pass the working end through it.  
![image](./media/Lariat_Loop_001.png)  
2. Take the working end that passed through to form a large primary loop.  
![image](./media/Lariat_Loop_002.png)  
3. Insert the working end into the small loop on the left, then hold both sections of the rope and pull tight.  
![image](./media/Lariat_Loop_003.png)  
4. The completed Lariat Loop.  
![image](./media/Lariat_Loop_004.png)  

---
## 2-4. Handcuff Knot
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Because the knot itself lacks an inherent locking mechanism, it is not practical to use for the restrictive purposes suggested by its name.  

1. Form a loop on the left side of the rope, and create another loop immediately adjacent to it.  
![image](./media/Handcuff_Knot_001.png)  
2. Pull the overlapping inner sections of the two loops through each other in opposite directions.  
![image](./media/Handcuff_Knot_002.png)  
3. The completed Handcuff Knot.  
![image](./media/Handcuff_Knot_003.png)
