## Introduction
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The muscle of a living animal is nearly sterile, and the bacteria that spoil meat come in from outside. There are three routes. The contents of the digestive tract, the hair and hide on the body surface, and human hands and knives. Butchering procedures such as bleeding, evisceration, skinning, and knife care are mostly there to block these three routes.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Depending on how an animal is butchered and dismembered, the calories of one rabbit caught in a trap (about 1,000 kcal) may actually get into a person's body, or may spoil and be thrown away within hours. This document covers the effect of bleeding and evisceration on keeping quality, the principles of rigor mortis and aging, the properties of meat by cut, and the use of parts other than lean meat.

> **Caution:** Meat obtained in the wild is always cooked completely through to the center, without exception. Zoonotic diseases and hunting laws are covered in the Trapping document, and the same constraints apply here. In ordinary times, act only within the scope set by the hunting laws of your country and the laws on livestock product hygiene.
