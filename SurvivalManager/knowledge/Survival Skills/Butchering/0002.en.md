## 1. Hygiene Principles of Butchering
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Butchering is essentially work to prevent contamination, and cutting is only the means. The muscle of a living animal is guarded by its immune system and is practically sterile. Meat spoils not because something inside woke up, but because bacteria from outside got in.

---
### 1-1. Three Routes by Which Contamination Enters
| Route | What comes in | How to block it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Digestive tract contents | Enteric bacteria such as E. coli, Salmonella, and Campylobacter, hundreds of millions per gram | Tie off the esophagus and rectum, don't rupture the intestines, and remove the viscera whole |
| Hair and hide | Dirt, feces, soil bacteria, ticks, mold spores | Skin by folding the hide outward so that hair does not touch the meat surface |
| Human hands, knives, work surfaces | Mostly things carried over from the two above | Always wash after touching a contaminated part, and change knives |

- The most dangerous is the digestive tract. 1 g of rumen contents of a ruminant contains over 10 billion bacteria, and the contents of the large intestine are similar. Even one drop touching the meat surface makes that spot a source of contamination at the level of millions of bacteria. Bacteria cannot burrow inward from the surface, but during cutting they spread over the whole meat on the knife.
- Do not wash contaminated parts; cut them out. Washing with water doesn't wash the bacteria away but spreads them over the whole surface, and a wet surface supports bacterial growth far better than a dry one. Use water sparingly and use dry cloth.
- A knife is the most efficient carrier of contamination. If you cut flesh with the same knife that skinned the hide, everything on the hide surface is carried into the muscle. If you have two or more tools, separate one for hide and one for meat; if you have only one, wipe it every time the stage changes.
- Industrial facilities disinfect knives by dipping them in 82 °C hot water. These are conditions under which vegetative cells on the surface die instantly. If you can boil water, you get the same effect by dipping the knife briefly in boiling water.

---
### 1-2. Effect of Bleeding on Keeping Quality
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The reason for draining the blood is not to brighten the color of the meat but to lengthen its keeping time. Blood is an almost perfect medium for bacterial growth. It has protein, iron, glucose, and a near-neutral pH, so if blood remains between blood vessels and tissue, that spot becomes the starting point of multiplication.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The second reason for bleeding is acidification. When muscle loses oxygen, it converts glycogen to lactic acid through anaerobic metabolism, and the muscle pH falls from around 7.2 to 5.4-5.7. This acidification is the meat's own defense against the growth of spoilage bacteria. If an animal is chased for a long time or becomes extremely excited before death, glycogen is used up in advance and acidification after death does not occur sufficiently. The result is dark, firm, dry meat that stays at pH 6.0 or higher, and this meat spoils much faster than normal meat.

| Condition | Final pH | Result |
| --- | :---: | --- |
| Properly bled meat | 5.4-5.7 | Normal color and texture, good keeping quality |
| Prolonged stress before death | 6.0 or higher | Dark and firm, bacteria multiply fast, hard to keep |
| Sudden stress and high temperature | Drops sharply to 5.4 or lower | Pale and soft, with heavy loss of juices |

- Bleeding is efficient only while the heart is still beating. After the heart stops, blood must be drained by gravity alone, and the recovery rate drops greatly. Place the head down so gravity helps, and open the large vessels in one go so the flow is not interrupted.
- If the windpipe and esophagus are cut together with the vessels when opening the neck, stomach contents flow back and contaminate the wound surface. Cut only the vessels precisely.
- The blood actually drained is about half of the total blood volume, and the rest stays in the organs and muscles. Bleeding is not work that eliminates the source of contamination but work that reduces it.
- Drained blood is a food rich in protein and iron. Its use is covered in Chapter 4.

### 1-3. Second Best When Water Is Scarce
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;If clean water cannot be used, the priorities of hygiene change. Washing with dirty water is worse than not washing. River water contains Leptospira, Giardia, and various enteric bacteria, which add a new source of contamination to the meat.

| Item | Best | Second best |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Handwashing | Soap and running water | Rub with ash, then wipe with a dry cloth |
| Knife disinfection | Dip in boiling water | Pass the blade lightly through a flame |
| Work surface | Washed and dried board | Peeled green wood, large leaves, clean stone |
| Meat surface | Don't wash, keep it dry | Cut out only the contaminated parts |
| Hand wounds | Stay out of the work | Cover completely with resin or clean cloth |

- Wood ash is the most realistic means for cleaning hands when there is no water. The potassium carbonate in ash is alkaline, so it breaks down grease to some extent, and the particles physically scrape off contaminants. Its disinfecting power falls short of soap, so it is second best.
- The most important principle is to keep the meat surface dry. Bacteria cannot multiply on a surface with low water activity. If hung in shade with good airflow, the surface dries thinly and forms a dry film, and this film protects the inside. It is the cheapest means of preservation available when logistics have broken down.
