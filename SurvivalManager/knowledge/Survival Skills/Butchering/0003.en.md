## 2. Timing of Evisceration and Order of Dismemberment
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The stage of butchering where time matters most is evisceration. Other tasks can be recovered even if delayed by a few hours, but meat whose viscera were removed late has no way back.

---
### 2-1. What Happens in the Digestive Tract After Death
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;While alive, the intestinal wall is guarded by the physical barrier of mucosal cells and by the immune system. When blood flow stops at the moment of death, the intestinal mucosal cells lose oxygen and begin digesting themselves (autolysis) within minutes. The wall grows thinner with time and eventually perforates.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Meanwhile, the bacteria in the gut move in the opposite direction. Body temperature is still warm, the immune system is gone, and food is unlimited. Under suitable conditions E. coli doubles every 20 minutes, becoming 8 times in an hour, 512 times in three hours, and 260,000 times in six hours. When bacteria pass through the thinned wall, the whole abdominal cavity and all muscles bordering it become contaminated.

| Time after death | Changes in the digestive tract | Effect on the meat |
| :---: | --- | --- |
| 0-30 minutes | Blood flow stops, autolysis of mucosal cells begins | None yet |
| 30 minutes-2 hours | Enteric bacteria surge, gas production begins | If the abdominal cavity is warm, odor begins |
| 2-6 hours | Wall permeability increases, bacteria begin to migrate | Muscles bordering the abdominal cavity become contaminated |
| 6-12 hours | Abdominal bloating, leakage of gut contents | Most parts are close to unusable |
| 12 hours or more | Whole-body decay proceeds | Inedible |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The times in the table vary greatly with temperature. In summer, when the temperature exceeds 30 °C, take the times in the table as half or less, and in freezing winter they can be stretched to several times as long. Evisceration is the only stage of dismemberment that time decides, and on hot days it comes before all other work.

- The purpose of removing the viscera also includes removing heat. The abdominal cavity is the thickest and best-insulated part of the body, so as long as the viscera are inside, the core stays near body temperature for hours. If you remove the viscera and leave the cavity open, air circulates, body temperature falls quickly, and the rate of bacterial growth falls too.
- Industrial facilities aim to lower the core temperature to 6-7 °C in 28-36 hours for cattle, 12-16 hours for pigs, and 24-30 hours for sheep.
- Without refrigeration, spreading the cavity open with a stick and hanging it in well-ventilated shade is the best means of cooling.

### 2-2. When the Gallbladder, Bladder, or Intestine Ruptures
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Failures of evisceration come almost entirely from rupturing three sacs.

| Ruptured organ | Contents | Damage left in the meat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gallbladder | Bile acids and bilirubin | A strong bitterness dissolves into the fat and does not wash out |
| Bladder | Urea and ammonia | A urine smell soaks in and remains even after drying and smoking |
| Stomach and intestine | Digestive juices and enteric bacteria | Bacterial contamination. The most dangerous for hygiene |

- Gallbladder: Bile acids dissolve well in fat through surfactant action and have a very low bitterness threshold. On meat touched by bile, the bitterness stays inside the fat layer and the only remedy is to cut it out. The gallbladder attached to the liver easily bursts when the liver is detached, so tie off the neck of the gallbladder first, or cut it out whole, leaving a generous piece of liver attached.
- Bladder: An animal that has not urinated has a full bladder. It sits inside the pelvis and bursts when pressure is applied while spreading the hind legs or splitting the pelvis. The rule is to tie the urethra side and take it out, and if there is nothing to tie with, merely pressing it empty by hand greatly reduces the risk.
- Rectum and esophagus: Cut around the anus in a circle, tie off the rectum, and push it inward. Tie the esophagus at the point where it joins the stomach. Industrial facilities, too, require that if the digestive tract is to be used as food, the esophagus, the junction of esophagus and stomach, and the junction of stomach and duodenum be tied off. Only when both ends are tied does the whole digestive tract become a closed sack and the contents not leak.

### 2-3. Dismemberment Order by Animal Size
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The order of dismemberment is not custom but the result of physical constraints that come from size. The criteria are two: whether the weight is one a person can handle alone, and how fast the heat inside the body escapes.

| Category | Approximate body weight | Logic of the order |
| --- | :---: | --- |
| Large livestock and wild animals | 50 kg or more | Bleeding, evisceration, skinning, splitting in half, cutting into parts. Cannot be handled unless hung, so proceed top to bottom while hanging |
| Medium animals | 10-50 kg | Bleeding, evisceration, skinning, cutting into parts. No need to split in half; cut the whole animal around the spine |
| Small game | 0.5-10 kg | Evisceration and skinning almost simultaneously. Heat escapes fast so there is less time pressure, but the risk of contamination by hair and feathers is actually greater |
| Birds | 0.5-2 kg | Pluck the feathers first, then remove the viscera. If you cut the belly open before plucking, feathers get into the cavity |
| Fish | All sizes | Remove the gills and viscera immediately. Digestive enzymes are strong, and if left, the belly flesh turns soft within hours |

- For large animals, evisceration comes before skinning. Because volume is large relative to surface area, heat in the abdominal cavity does not escape well, so if you do the skinning, which takes hours, first, bacteria multiply in the cavity in the meantime. Small animals lose heat quickly, so there is leeway in the order.
- For birds, pluck first. Feathers are light and scatter easily and get into an open abdominal cavity readily, and once in, they are very troublesome to remove. Dipping the bird briefly in hot water to soften the feather roots makes plucking easier, but if the water is too hot the skin cooks and tears.
- Fish are the most urgent. Digestive enzymes are highly active even at low temperatures, so they break down their own tissue even after death. Rigor mortis also passes within minutes to hours. Removing the gills and viscera right after catching and keeping the fish in cold water or shade is virtually the only response.
