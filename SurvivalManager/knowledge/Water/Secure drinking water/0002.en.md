## 1. Securing Water
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In the event of a water supply disruption or contamination emergency, it is highly recommended to prioritize verified safe water sources, such as unopened commercial bottled water, water purifier tanks, freezer ice, and canned food juices.

---
### 1-1. Alternative Water Sources
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When verified safe water sources are unavailable, water can be procured from the following alternative sources as a last resort. Please note that these items merely represent potential sources for gathering water in an emergency, and their potability cannot be guaranteed. Because standard purification or disinfection processes may fail to eliminate chemical contaminants, the safety of this water cannot be verified; therefore, whenever possible, it should be restricted to hygiene purposes such as washing and cleaning.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Avoid water that contains suspended solids, foul odors, or dark discoloration. Water contaminated with fuel, pesticides, toxic chemicals, or radioactive materials cannot be rendered safe through boiling, filtration, or chemical disinfection, and must never be collected. | Water Source | Remarks |
| --- | --- |
| Rainwater | Collect by routing into a rain barrel. When harvesting via rooftops and gutters, be aware that bird droppings, dust, and metallic components may become mixed into the water. |
| Rivers and Lakes | Prioritize collecting from points with rapid water velocity. However, if upstream contamination cannot be verified, the safety of the water cannot be guaranteed. |
| Bathtubs | Water filled from tap supplies immediately following a disaster carries a relatively low probability of contamination; however, water left stagnant for extended periods poses a risk of bacterial proliferation. |
| Dew | Prior to sunrise, collect dew from clean surfaces—such as plants free of dust or contaminants, metal plates, plastic sheeting, tarps, or plastic covers—using highly absorbent fabrics like cloths or towels. Avoid agricultural fields, roadsides, and areas near industrial facilities. |
| Water Heater Tanks | Deactivate the power and hot water supply lines first; discard initial sediment and utilize only the relatively clear water. However, if the setup cannot be verified as disconnected from space-heating water loops, collection is discouraged. |
| Residual Pipe Water | Open the faucet located at the absolute lowest point of the residence to salvage residual water. If the contamination status of the piping cannot be verified, do not collect. |
| Pet Water Bowls | High probability of bacterial contamination due to mixing with pet saliva. |
| Humidifier Tanks | High probability of bacterial and mold proliferation. |
| Swimming Pools | High risk of contamination from chlorine and pool chemicals; restrict collection to hygiene purposes such as washing and cleaning. |

### 1-2. Water Harvesting
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When alternative water sources are scarce in the surrounding environment, small quantities of water can be secured by exploiting natural evaporation and condensation phenomena or the transpiration process of plants. However, these harvesting techniques generally yield very limited volumes of water. Although the harvested water is typically cleaner than the raw source material, its safety cannot be guaranteed depending on installation variables and environmental contamination; thus, it should be subjected to purification and disinfection processes prior to consumption whenever possible.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Because water harvesting methods alone can rarely secure the daily hydration requirements for a single adult, they should be utilized as supplementary assets rather than primary water procurement means. Where feasible, multiple setups should be deployed simultaneously to maximize output volume.

| Method | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Transpiration Bag | Envelop a healthy, leafy, non-toxic tree branch with a transparent plastic bag and seal it tightly. Solar-driven plant transpiration causes water droplets to condense inside the bag. Yield varies based on meteorological conditions and plant health, but approximately 50 mL of water can be harvested per day. |
| Vegetation Bag | Place non-toxic grass, leaves, or other relatively safe plant materials inside a transparent plastic bag, seal tightly, and expose it to direct sunlight. Exercise caution as contaminants on the plant surfaces may mix into the condensate; approximately 50 mL of water can be harvested per day. |
| Solar Still | A method of securing water by utilizing plastic sheeting and a collection vessel to evaporate and condense moisture from soil or vegetation. A larger pit surface area expands the catchment zone, and introducing more moisture-rich materials inside maximizes the evaporation volume. Under favorable environmental conditions, roughly 250 mL of water can be harvested per day; however, yields may decrease significantly if soil moisture content is low or ambient temperatures are cold. Imperfect sealing of the plastic sheet drastically reduces efficiency, and repeated operation in the exact same location will deplete soil moisture, causing production to gradually decline. |
