## 2. Water Purification
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The optimal approach to water purification involves combining multiple techniques incrementally rather than relying on a single method, progressing from large particle removal to pathogen eradication.

| Stage | Content |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Sedimentation | If dealing with turbid or muddy water, transfer it to a container and allow large particulate matter to settle to the bottom. Afterwards, slowly decant only the relatively clear water from the upper layer. |
| 2. Filtration | Remove suspended solids and large contaminants using cloth, coffee filters, or an improvised filter composed of gravel, sand, and charcoal. |
| 3. Disinfection | Eliminate or inactivate pathogenic microorganisms—such as bacteria, viruses, and protozoa—using boiling, water purification tablets, or chlorine disinfection. |
| 4. Distillation (Optional) | If chemical contamination is not suspected, execute distillation to further eliminate pathogens, salts, and a wide array of contaminants. (Note that certain low-boiling-point chemicals, such as Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs), will not be removed). |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Generally, purification performance is rated in the order of Distillation > Boiling > Filtration; however, distillation does not eliminate all contaminants. While highly effective at removing microorganisms, salts, most heavy metals, and inorganic pollutants, low-boiling-point chemicals—such as Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs), certain pesticides, and fuel components—will vaporize alongside the steam and condense into the product water. Consequently, the safety of water that is suspected of chemical contamination or derived from an unknown source cannot be guaranteed even after distillation.  

---
### 2-1. Sedimentation
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This method utilizes gravity to settle suspended particles to the bottom of a vessel, yielding clearer water. It is highly beneficial for removing large particulates, particularly when treating muddy water prior to boiling. Sedimentation merely requires leaving the water undisturbed in a container for several hours; once the particles settle to the base, slowly decant only the relatively clear upper layer. Because sedimentation alone does not eradicate pathogens, the water must undergo subsequent purification stages.  

---
### 2-2. Filtration
#### 2-2-1. Improvised Filters
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Improvised filters can be constructed by layering readily available materials such as cloth, coffee filters, gravel, sand, and charcoal. While these filters effectively remove sediment and visible impurities, they cannot eradicate harmful bacteria or parasites; therefore, the water must undergo subsequent purification stages.

| Material | Function | Layer Depth |
| :---: | --- | :---: |
| Gravel | Positions as the top layer to eliminate the largest foreign debris. | 5 cm or more |
| Sand | Positions as the intermediate layer to filter out finer particles than gravel. | 5 cm or more |
| Charcoal | Aids in reducing odors and certain organic compounds to improve taste. Unlike activated carbon, regular charcoal features low adsorptive capacity; it only provides minor odor and organic reduction and cannot eliminate pathogens. | 5 cm or more |
| Sand | Secures the charcoal layer in place and provides supplementary filtration. | 5 cm or more |
| Cloth | Deploys at the absolute bottom layer to strain out minute particulate matter. | 3 layers or more |

#### 2-2-2. Portable Filters (e.g., LifeStraw)
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The use of commercial filters with an absolute pore size of 0.2 µm or less, or portable filters, ceramic filters, and hollow-fiber membrane filters certified to NSF P248 or NSF P231 standards is highly recommended. Note that standard portable filters likewise fail to eliminate chemical contaminants such as heavy metals or Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs).

| Certification | Description |
| :---: | --- |
| NSF P231 | Microbiological Water Purifiers (Eradication of bacteria, viruses, and protozoa) |
| NSF P248 | Military Operations Microbiological Water Purifiers (Includes high-turbidity testing) |
|   NSF 53   | Health Effects (Varies by specific certified item, e.g., lead, arsenic, PFAS) |
|   NSF 58   | Reverse Osmosis (RO) Drinking Water Treatment Systems (Reduces heavy metals, salts, and impurities) |
|   NSF 401  | Emerging Compounds / Incidental Contaminants (Trace pharmaceuticals, pesticides, herbicides, etc.) |

### 2-3. Disinfection
#### 2-3-1. Purification Chemical Agents
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Purification tablets or drops are chemical agents formulated to eradicate bacteria, viruses, and other pathogens in water. The dosage parameters and contact wait times specified in the manufacturer's instruction manual must be strictly obeyed.  

#### 2-3-2. Chlorine Bleach
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Standard liquid chlorine bleach (sodium hypochlorite) that is unscented, clear (not color-safe), and free of added cleaners, fragrances, or surfactants can be utilized for water purification. However, chlorine bleach features limited efficacy against *Cryptosporidium* and certain protozoan cysts. Therefore, turbid water must be thoroughly filtered prior to chemical disinfection. If *Cryptosporidium* or similar protozoan contamination is suspected, boiling or distillation methods must be prioritized.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Introducing chlorine bleach into high-temperature water can degrade its disinfecting efficacy. Furthermore, if the water is turbid, discolored, or extremely cold, the required dosage must be doubled; thus, utilize water kept at room temperature whenever possible.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Below is the standard protocol provided by the [CDC (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention)](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html). After adding the chlorine bleach, mix thoroughly and wait at least 30 minutes before consumption. If the water temperature is exceptionally low (e.g., 5°C or below), the disinfection process slows down; therefore, it is safer to extend the contact wait time to 60 minutes or longer.  

| Disinfectant | Concentration | Dosage per 1L of Water |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sodium Hypochlorite | 5–9% | 2 drops (approx. 0.1 mL) |
| Sodium Hypochlorite | 1% | 10 drops (approx. 0.5 mL) |

#### 2-3-3. Tincture of Iodine
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In the context of drinking water disinfection guidelines, "Tincture of Iodine" refers specifically to a solution prepared by dissolving elemental iodine and potassium iodide (or sodium iodide) in a mixture of water and ethanol (alcohol). It is not the same chemical product as "Lugol's solution" (a 5% or 10% concentration that typically uses water exclusively as the solvent).  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Tincture of iodine, commonly found in medicine cabinets or first aid kits, can be utilized for emergency water purification. However, like chlorine bleach, iodine features limited efficacy against *Cryptosporidium* and certain protozoan cysts. Turbid water must be filtered prior to disinfection; if protozoan contamination like *Cryptosporidium* is suspected, prioritize boiling or distillation.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Because iodine tincture disinfection is an emergency, short-term treatment method, it is unsuitable for pregnant individuals or those with thyroid disorders. Even healthy adults must not consume iodine-treated water continuously over an extended duration.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Below is the emergency disinfection guideline using tincture of iodine provided by the [EPA (Environmental Protection Agency)](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/emergency-disinfection-drinking-water). This applies strictly to a 2% Tincture of Iodine and cannot be applied to 5% or 10% Lugol's solutions. If the water is turbid, prioritize filtration; if filtration is impossible, double the dosage volume. After adding the iodine, mix thoroughly and wait at least 30 minutes. If the water temperature is exceptionally low (e.g., 5°C or below), extend the contact wait time to 60 minutes or longer to ensure safety.  

| Disinfectant | Concentration | Dosage per 1L of Water |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tincture of Iodine | 2% | 5 drops (approx. 0.25 mL) |

#### 2-3-4. Boiling
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Boiling represents the most reliable disinfection method in emergency scenarios. Under standard conditions, maintain the water at a vigorous rolling boil for at least 1 minute. However, at elevations above 2,000 meters, boil for 3 minutes; in extreme environments above 4,000 meters, maintain the rolling boil for a minimum of 5 minutes.  

#### 2-3-5. Solar Water Disinfection (SODIS)
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This technique exploits solar ultraviolet (UV) radiation to eradicate microorganisms. Fill clean, scratch-free transparent bottles with water, lay them horizontally, and expose them to direct sunlight for 6 hours (on clear days) or 2 days (on cloudy days). Because prolonged exposure to intense UV radiation and thermal energy can trigger chemical leaching from plastic containers, the use of glass bottles is preferred where possible; if using PET bottles, avoid reusing them excessively.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;When sufficient solar irradiance is secured, this method is highly effective at reducing most bacteria and viruses; however, its efficacy degrades sharply in water with high turbidity. Therefore, sedimentation and filtration must always be performed beforehand.

## 2-4. Distillation
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Distillation is a process that harvests the steam generated when boiling contaminated water to produce clean drinking water. Because it physically separates contaminants, salts, and minerals from the water, it is widely utilized to convert muddy water or seawater (brine) into freshwater. However, low-boiling-point chemicals—such as Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs), certain pesticides, and fuel components—will vaporize alongside the steam and condense into the product water. Consequently, the safety of water suspected of chemical contamination or derived from an unknown source cannot be guaranteed even after distillation.  

#### 2-4-1. Solar Distillation Methods
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;These methods leverage solar thermal energy to evaporate water and collect the condensed steam to obtain purified water. Placing the container of contaminated water over a black base surface accelerates the evaporation rate.  

![image](./media/Solar_Distillation.png)  

| Method | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Single-Slope Still | Pour contaminated water into a wide basin and install a transparent cover (glass, acrylic, or plastic sheet) at an incline. Position an empty vessel at the lower end of the incline to catch the condensing water droplets. |
| Pit Method | Dig a hole into the ground, place an empty collection vessel in the center, and arrange containers of contaminated water around it. Cover the pit with plastic sheeting, secure a small stone directly over the central collection vessel to create a cone shape, and allow solar heat to evaporate and condense water into the vessel. Exercise caution to avoid contamination if the plastic sheet is not perfectly sealed or if the collection vessel itself is dirty. |
| Water Cone | Cut off the upper section of a large plastic bottle and fold the cut edge inward by 2–3 cm to form a circular drainage trough, or create a collection trough inside a cone-shaped object like a transparent umbrella. Position the apparatus floating over seawater or covering a small container of contaminated water, and harvest the water that condenses into the internal trough. |

#### 2-4-2. Boiling Distillation Methods
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;These methods utilize fire or a heat source to boil water and collect the condensed steam to obtain purified water. Generally, utilizing standard PET bottles for thermal distillation carries high risks of container deformation and chemical leaching; therefore, heat-resistant glass or heat-resistant metal (such as stainless steel) vessels must be used.  

![image](./media/Fire_Distillation.png)  

| Method | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Inverted Lid Method | Utilizes a pot featuring a domed lid with a central handle, along with a collection cup. Place the empty cup in the center of the pot and fill the surrounding area with contaminated water to a level below the rim of the cup. Heat the pot with the lid placed upside down; steam will condense on the inverted lid, migrate toward the central handle, and drip into the cup. |
| Pressure Cooker Still | A method that connects a heat-resistant hose (piping) to the steam vent of a pressure cooker and cools it using cold water. Fill the pressure cooker with contaminated water and apply heat to direct the emerging steam into the hose. Routing the middle section of the hose through a container filled with ice or cold water rapidly condenses the steam into liquid water. |
| Dual-Bottle Connection | Fill a heat-resistant glass or metal bottle (Bottle A) with contaminated water. Take a heat-resistant plastic or punchable metal bottle (Bottle B), cut a hole in its base, and join it securely to the mouth of Bottle A. Invert the assembly so that the mouth of Bottle B points downward, then heat Bottle A to initiate distillation. |
| Sojutgori (Traditional Still) | A traditional three-tiered distillation apparatus featuring a lower boiling chamber for contaminated water, a middle condensation chamber shaped like an inverted kettle, and an upper reservoir for cold cooling water. Heated steam ascends, contacts the chilled upper boundary, condenses into pure water, and drains out through the collection spout. |
