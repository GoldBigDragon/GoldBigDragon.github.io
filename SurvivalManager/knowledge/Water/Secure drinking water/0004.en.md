## 3. Storing Drinking Water
---
### 3-1. Storage Container Selection
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Containers chosen for storing drinking water must be durable, shatterproof, and manufactured from food-grade materials. In particular, containers that previously held toxic substances—such as gasoline, pesticides, detergents, or motorized lubricants—must never be utilized for storing drinking water, regardless of how thoroughly they have been cleaned.  

| Container | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Commercial Water Bottles | Unopened commercial bottled water represents the safest and most reliable emergency water asset. However, thin PET bottles can deform or develop fractures over extended storage durations and are unsuitable for repeated reuse. |
| Food-Grade Containers | Containers explicitly rated as food-grade are optimized for the long-term storage of drinking water. |
| HDPE Containers | High-Density Polyethylene (HDPE) containers feature excellent durability and chemical resistance, making them widely favored for water storage. Verify that the specific product is certified for food use. |
| Blue Water Tanks | Large blue water tanks engineered specifically for potable water storage are ideal for long-term비축 and advantageous for stockpiling major volumes. |
| Glass Bottles | Glass carries zero risk of chemical leaching and is straightforward to sanitize, making it ideal for long-term storage. However, it features poor impact resistance and will fracture if the stored water freezes. |
| Stainless Steel Tumblers | Highly durable and blocks light completely, effectively suppressing algae growth. However, storing water disinfected with chlorine bleach long-term can induce metallic corrosion. |
| Beverage Bottles | Though manufactured for food use, any residual sugars or flavorings can foster rapid bacterial growth; they must be thoroughly sanitized and dried prior to use. |
| Ceramic Vessels | Advantageous for keeping water temperatures cool; however, achieving an airtight seal is difficult, and their weight complicates transport. |
| Milk Cartons and Jugs | Milk proteins and lipids adhere easily to the interior walls, rendering thorough sanitization exceptionally difficult. Due to high bacterial proliferation risks, they are discouraged for long-term storage. |
| Food-Grade Ziploc Bags | Suitable for short-term storage or freezing; however, they tear easily and are inappropriate as a primary storage solution. |
| Collapsible Water Bladders | Consumes minimal storage space when empty; however, the thin material renders them prone to punctures, and drying or cleaning the interior is challenging. |
| Kimchi Containers / Plastic Airtight Bins | Food-safe variants are secure and offer excellent seals; however, retained food odors can negatively affect the taste of the water. |
| Bulk Food-Grade Drums | Containers that previously held food items like vegetable oils or syrups can be repurposed, provided all odors and residues are completely eliminated. |
| Industrial-Grade Drums | Drums that previously contained detergents, lubricants, antifreeze, or industrial chemicals can retain toxic residues even after washing; they must never be used for storing drinking water. |

---
### 3-2. Sanitizing Storage Containers
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Prior to filling storage containers with drinking water, they must undergo a thorough washing and sanitization process to prevent microbial growth. For long-term emergency preservation, consider adding 2 drops of chlorine bleach (5–9% sodium hypochlorite) per 1L of water before sealing.  

| Stage | Content |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Washing | Clean the container thoroughly using dish soap and water, then rinse completely with clean water. |
| 2. Preparing Sanitizing Solution | Mix approximately 5 mL of liquid chlorine bleach (5% sodium hypochlorite free of fragrances, cleaners, or color-safe additives) per 1L of water to formulate a sanitizing solution of roughly 250 ppm. |
| 3. Sanitizing | Pour the sanitizing solution into the container, secure the lid, and shake vigorously to ensure the liquid contacts all internal surfaces completely. |
| 4. Contact and Drainage | Maintain contact for a minimum of 30 seconds, then drain the sanitizing solution out of the container. |
| 5. Drying and Filling | Allow the container to air dry, or rinse it thoroughly with clean water immediately after draining the solution. Fill with drinking water and seal tightly. |

### 3-3. Storage Environment Management
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Stored water can degrade over time due to ambient variables; therefore, maintaining appropriate storage conditions and performing regular inspections are vital to preserving water quality.  

| Parameter | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Temperature | Elevated temperatures accelerate plastic degradation and optimize conditions for microbial growth; store water in a cool location maintained between 10–21°C away from thermal fluctuations. |
| Light | Store containers in a dark environment away from direct sunlight, which degrades plastic polymers and fuels the growth of algae inside the water. |
| Positioning | While food-grade HDPE containers can withstand direct contact with concrete floors, it is best to elevate them on shelves or pallets to isolate them from floor moisture and potential contaminants. |
| Chemical Substances | Never store water near volatile chemicals such as gasoline, pesticides, paints, detergents, or fertilizers; chemical vapors can permeate through plastic container walls and contaminate the water. |
| Inspection | Document the storage date on each container and inspect them every 6 to 12 months. Replace any water that exhibits unusual odors or discoloration; rotated water can be repurposed for cleaning or agriculture. |
| Secondary Contamination | Never introduce bare hands or unsterilized cups directly into the storage container. Utilize containers fitted with integrated dispensing spigots, or allocate a dedicated, sanitized ladle to mitigate contamination risks. |
| Redundant Purification | Never assume that stored water remains permanently safe. To prepare for emergencies, maintain at least two distinct purification assets, such as boiling equipment, filtration units, or chemical purification tablets. |
| Re-Oxygenation | Water stored over extended durations can lose dissolved oxygen, causing it to taste flat or stale. Prior to drinking, pouring the water back and forth between two clean containers several times will re-oxygenate it and improve its taste. |
